1. SOURCES ACTUALLY READ (all four PDFs downloaded and text-extracted in full, 2026-08-30)
============================================================================
 [BNS06] Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard, "Econometrics of Testing for Jumps in Financial Economics
         Using Bipower Variation", J. Financial Econometrics 4(1), 2006, 1-30. Formulas transcribed:
         mu_1 = sqrt(2)/sqrt(pi) (Eq. 5); theta = pi^2/4 + pi - 5 ~= 0.6090 (Eq. 6); linear test
         G-hat (Eq. 10); ratio test H-hat (Eq. 11); adjusted ratio J-hat (Eq. 14).
 [HT05]  Huang & Tauchen, "The Relative Contribution of Jumps to Total Price Variance", J. Financial
         Econometrics 3(4), 2005, 456-499. RV and BV = mu_1^-2 (M/(M-1)) SUM |r_{j-1}||r_j| (p.459);
         nu_qq = 2, nu_bb = (pi/2)^2 + pi - 3 (p.460); tri-power quarticity TP (p.461 Eq. 3);
         RJ (Eq. 2) and z_TP,rm (Eq. 9) (p.462).
 [SAV12] Savor, "Stock Returns after Major Price Shocks: The Impact of Information", JFE 106(3),
         2012, 635-659.
 [JZ17]  Jiang & Zhu, "Information Shocks and Short-Term Market Underreaction", JFE 124(1), 2017,
         43-64. HONEST FLAG: full text is behind ScienceDirect and was NOT fetched; only the
         publisher/SSRN abstract was read, so it is cited for direction and horizon and nothing finer.
CONSTANTS ARE DERIVED, NOT TYPED: mu_a = E|Z|^a is implemented from its definition and unit-tested
against every value the two papers print (mu_1 = sqrt(2/pi), mu_2 = 1, mu_3 = 2 sqrt(2/pi), mu_4 = 3),
and theta is checked to agree between [HT05]'s nu_bb - nu_qq and [BNS06]'s printed 0.6090.

WHY z_TP,rm AND NOT THE OTHER FOUR [HT05] VARIANTS, quoted verbatim from [HT05] pp.469-470: "With the
exception of the z_TP,rm,t statistic, the sampling frequency has a significant impact on the size. As
the sampling frequency decreases, that is, the sampling interval increases, the actual sizes of all
statistics except z_TP,rm,t increase above the Monte Carlo confidence band". It is the one variant
their Monte Carlo finds size-robust as the sampling interval lengthens, and this work lengthens the
sampling interval to its extreme.

2. THE DAILY ADAPTATION — WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT COSTS
============================================================================
LEE & MYKLAND (2008) IS NOT IMPLEMENTED. Their statistic needs a local SPOT volatility and its Gumbel
critical values come from in-fill asymptotics inside a locally constant volatility regime; feeding it
daily bars as if they were intraday returns would produce a statistic its tabulated critical values do
not describe. No claim about it is made anywhere in this family.
WHAT IS DONE INSTEAD: BNS/HT is a WITHIN-PERIOD test. Here the period is a rolling window of w trading
days and the M "intra-period" returns are the w DAILY log returns in it. The algebra is unchanged; the
asymptotic regime is not. delta is pinned at one trading day and cannot shrink, so the N(0,1) limit is
an approximation whose finite-sample properties are NOT the ones [HT05] validated. Unit-tested
consequence, on a purely continuous synthetic path: the limit does hold well at these window sizes
(mean z within 0.10 of 0, sd within 0.10 of 1 at both w=21 and w=63) — but see the measured size below.
DAY-LEVEL ATTRIBUTION IS THIS MODULE'S OWN AND IS LABELLED AS SUCH. No source was found that localizes
a single day inside a rejected BNS/HT window. The rule (window rejects AND |r_d| is the window max) is
unsourced, deterministic, strictly backward-looking, and falsified-or-confirmed by unit test, not by
citation.

5. COSTS — AND THE BIGGEST SINGLE CAVEAT IN THIS RUN

config.cost_model = "edge_spread": per-ticker trailing effective HALF-spread from
spread_estimator.build_edge_half_spread_frame (Ardia, Guidotti & Kroencke, JFE 2024, EDGE estimator over
daily OHLC, full spread / 2) at its COST_MODEL_WINDOW_DAYS = 63 production window. cost_bps = 5.0 is the
per-ticker fallback; fallback notional was 0.0-3.0 out of 256-1147 total turnover, i.e. essentially
never used. financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 (harness default; this project has no sourced securities-borrow
feed — a known OPEN paid-data gap).
REALIZED AVERAGE COST RATE, derived per spec as total_cost_drag / total_turnover: 38.8 to 45.7 bp
one-way, mean 41.3 bp. That is ~8x this project's flat 5bp convention.
MEASURED SEPARATELY, AND THIS IS A FINDING ABOUT THE COST MODEL RATHER THAN ABOUT JUMPS: EDGE at the
63-day production window was run over 29 large/mega-caps (2014-2026, 87,447 ticker-days). Median
half-spread 23.3bp, mean 31.8bp; per-ticker medians run PG 11.9bp, KO 13.4bp, JNJ 14.1bp, VZ 15.7bp at
the tight end and TSLA 42.1bp, AMD 43.6bp, MRNA 50.4bp, ENPH 59.9bp at the wide end. Real quoted
half-spreads for Procter & Gamble and J&J are on the order of 1bp. EDGE is therefore overstating this
universe's spreads by roughly an order of magnitude — the same upward bias in the tight large-cap regime
that spread_estimator.py's own docstring documents at window=21 ("a true 10bps spread recovers as
~21bps"), but much larger at window=63 on real data than that note implies. On flagged jump days the
median EDGE half-spread is only 1.12x the all-day median, so the 41bp charged here is the estimator's
LEVEL, not a post-jump selection effect.
CONSEQUENCE, STATED PLAINLY: the net Sharpes in section 7 are charged a cost that is very probably far
too high for this universe. That is why section 7 also reports the zero-cost attribution — and why the
conclusion rests on the GROSS numbers, which are the ones that are not contaminated by this.
NOT MODELED at all: market impact, commissions, short borrow, and post-jump spread widening (EDGE reads
a trailing 63-day window, so it prices normal-times spreads).
CROSS-CUTTING: cross_sectional_patterns.py (Round C) is the only other family using cost_model=
"edge_spread". This measurement applies to it too and is worth an independent look there.

    carry the complementary sign declared a pattern. It fired once in this run: w=21 / alpha=0.001 /

 2. SECTION 6'S p-VALUES ARE TOO SMALL — the most important correction here. The pre-registered
    bootstrap resamples non-jump days INDEPENDENTLY WITHIN EACH TICKER, but the events are heavily
    CLUSTERED IN CALENDAR TIME: the 3,304 down-jump events at w=21/alpha=0.001 fall on 1,424 dates,
    78 of them on 2025-04-03 alone, and 7.7% of all events on eight dates. Under a DATE-CLUSTERED
    bootstrap (resample whole event dates, 4000 draws) the standard error inflates 1.4x-2.8x, worst
    at the SHORT horizons where same-day cross-sectional correlation dominates:
        h      abnormal    i.i.d. t   clustered t   SE infl.   clustered p   (section 6 reported p)

        20     -0.6176%      -3.06       -2.20        1.39x       0.027           0.0015
