SCHEDULED EARNINGS-ANNOUNCEMENT PREMIUM — PRE-REGISTRATION
Written 2026-09-01, BEFORE any return, Sharpe, DSR, event-study number or
placebo result existed for this family. Nothing below may be revised after
results are seen. The run report (earnings_announcement_premium_2026-09-01.txt)
is the AFTER document; this is the BEFORE document, and the two are meant to
be diffed.

WHAT HAD ALREADY BEEN RUN WHEN THIS WAS WRITTEN, STATED PLAINLY SO THE
DISCIPLINE IS AUDITABLE RATHER THAN ASSERTED:
  (a) the SEC EDGAR filing-calendar fetch (data acquisition only), and
  (b) the PREDICTOR-ACCURACY calibration reported in section 3.
Neither computes a return of any kind. (b) measures how well a calendar
heuristic locates real announcement DATES — a data-quality property of the
input, with no price, no position and no P&L anywhere in it — and the build
brief explicitly required it to be measured before the design was fixed. It
is reported here in full, including the variants that lost, so that the
choice it drove is checkable rather than merely claimed. NO RETURN SERIES
WAS COMPUTED BEFORE THIS DOCUMENT WAS COMMITTED.

============================================================================
0. WHAT IS BEING TESTED
============================================================================
Buying a firm BECAUSE IT IS SCHEDULED TO ANNOUNCE EARNINGS SOON — before the
announcement happens, with no knowledge of what it will say — and shorting
the rest of the index against it.

H_EAP  A book that is long the point-in-time S&P 500 members predicted to
       announce inside a short upcoming window, and short every other
       point-in-time member, earns a positive net Sharpe.

This is NOT post-earnings-announcement drift, which this codebase already
screened as cross_sectional_pead.py and reported as an honest negative. PEAD
conditions on the REALIZED announcement return and trades the drift
afterwards. This family is fully determined BEFORE the announcement: the
long leg is selected from a firm's own past filing calendar, and the entry
and exit rows are both fixed the moment the window opens. Nothing in
position formation reads what an announcement said or how the stock reacted.

A null is a fully acceptable and (see section 8) a genuinely likely outcome.

1. THE ACADEMIC BASIS — INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED, AND TWO OF THE BUILD
   BRIEF'S OWN CLAIMS DID NOT SURVIVE
============================================================================
An adversarial verification pass re-fetched every source on 2026-09-01
rather than trusting the build brief. Tier labels: PRIMARY = the paper's own
text was read and quoted; ABSTRACT = publisher-deposited abstract retrieved,
body NOT retrieved; RECORD = bibliographic record only; SECONDARY = the
figure comes from a summary of the paper, not the paper.

 [SW16]  Savor & Wilson, "Earnings Announcements and Systematic Risk",
         Journal of Finance 71(1), 2016, pp. 83-138, doi:10.1111/jofi.12361.
         ABSTRACT (Crossref publisher-deposited). Verbatim opening:
         "Firms scheduled to report earnings earn an annualized abnormal
          return of 9.9%. We propose a risk-based explanation for this
          phenomenon, whereby investors use announcements to revise their
          expectations for nonannouncing firms, but can only do so
          imperfectly. Consequently, the covariance between firm-specific
          and market cash flow news spikes around announcements, making
          announcers especially risky."
 [SW11]  The December 2011 Wharton draft of the same paper (retrieved and
         read; "This version: December 2011"; sample 1974-2009). PRIMARY.

  *** CORRECTION TO THE BUILD BRIEF, #1 ***
  The brief treated [SW11] and [SW16] as interchangeable ("the mechanism is
  stable across versions"). The MECHANISM is; THE HEADLINE NUMBER IS NOT.
  [SW11] states verbatim: "A portfolio strategy that buys all announcing
  firms in a given week and sells short all the non-announcing firms earns
  an annualized abnormal return of 20%." That 20% is the EQUAL-WEIGHTED
  book, 1974-2009. The published abstract's figure is 9.9%. WHY the headline
  moved was NOT established — the published body is behind Wiley (HTTP 403)
  and was not retrieved — so NO reconciliation is asserted. THE CITABLE
  PUBLISHED NUMBER IS 9.9%. The 20% is a superseded draft figure and is not
  quoted anywhere in this family as the paper's result. This pre-registration
  records the distinction now so that a result near either number cannot
  later be presented as "matching the paper" by picking whichever fits.

  Two mechanism nuances read directly from [SW11], both load-bearing here:
   (i)  the premium requires IMPERFECT signal extraction and dies at BOTH
        corners — if investors learn nothing about non-announcers the news
        is idiosyncratic and unpriced; if they learn as much as about
        announcers, the differential is exactly zero;
   (ii) the claim is that the excess return is LARGER than the higher market
        beta explains ([SW11] puts the announcement portfolio's market beta
        at 0.12), so this is not a "beta rises during announcements" story.

 [LF07]  Lamont & Frazzini, "The Earnings Announcement Premium and Trading
         Volume", NBER Working Paper 13090, May 2007, doi:10.3386/w13090.
         PRIMARY (NBER PDF retrieved and read). THE DIRECT PRECEDENT, because
         it is the EX-ANTE TRADEABLE version — it conditions on PREDICTED
         announcement timing, exactly as this family must. From p.1, verbatim:
         "we find monthly strategies earning excess returns of between 7% and
          18% per year, with Sharpe ratios larger than other popular
          anomalies. The premium is strong in large capitalization stocks, is
          not only confined to the three-day window around the announcement,
          and appears consistently since 1927."
         (NBER's own title page orders the authors Lamont, Frazzini; [SW16]
         cites it Frazzini-first. NBER's order is used.)
         Their proposed mechanism is LIMITED ATTENTION and small-investor
         buying pressure — explicitly NOT risk.
 [B13]   Barber, De George, Lehavy & Trueman, "The earnings announcement
         premium around the globe", Journal of Financial Economics 108(1),
         2013, pp. 118-138, doi:10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.10.006 (the DOI the
         brief did not supply). ABSTRACT retrieved verbatim; 20 countries
         analysed within-country, NINE with a significantly positive premium.
         HONEST FLAG: THE PUBLISHED ABSTRACT CONTAINS NO MAGNITUDE. The 2011
         working paper says "by over 11 percent annually"; a CFA Institute
         digest of the published version reports 59.7bp/month (7.16%/yr) —
         SECONDARY, and it disagrees with the working paper's own 66.8bp raw
         / 95.4bp adjusted. The international magnitude is recorded as
         UNSETTLED at this project's level of access, and no figure from it
         is used to calibrate anything here.
 [BEA68] Beaver, "The Information Content of Annual Earnings Announcements",
         Journal of Accounting Research 6, 1968, pp. 67-92. RECORD. The
         ancestor: volume and volatility spike at announcements.
 [BK91]  Ball & Kothari, "Security Returns Around Earnings Announcements",
         The Accounting Review 66, 1991, pp. 718-738. RECORD (verified from
         Kothari's own MIT CV). Cited for the OPPOSITE reason to the rest:
         per [B13]'s text, "Ball and Kothari (1991) find that the short-window
         announcement premium is not explained by systematic risk."
 [CDLS07] Cohen, Dey, Lys & Sunder, "Earnings Announcement Premia and the
         Limits to Arbitrage", Journal of Accounting and Economics 43(2-3),
         2007, pp. 153-180, doi:10.1016/j.jacceco.2007.01.008. RECORD.
         Explanation: LIMITS TO ARBITRAGE, not risk.

  *** CORRECTION TO THE BUILD BRIEF, #2 — THE IMPORTANT ONE ***
  The brief states the premium "is a risk premium, not an inefficiency, so —
  unlike most anomalies — there's no strong a priori reason it should decay
  once known." THE LITERATURE DOES NOT SUPPORT THAT AS SETTLED. The
  risk-based reading is [SW16]'s PROPOSAL and is one of at least three live
  explanations: [BK91] finds the short-window premium is NOT explained by
  systematic risk; [CDLS07] attributes it to limits to arbitrage; [LF07]
  attributes it to retail attention. Two of those three ARE inefficiency
  stories, and inefficiency stories decay. This family therefore does NOT
  get to treat "it shouldn't decay" as a premise, and a negative result here
  is NOT evidence that something went wrong in the implementation.

2. THE UNIVERSE — POINT-IN-TIME, AND WHAT THAT STILL DOES NOT FIX
============================================================================
sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over(MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START,
membership_coverage_end()) supplies the candidate pool, and a per-day
point-in-time membership frame from get_universe_as_of gates BOTH legs on
every single day. ticker_universe.SCREENING_UNIVERSE (a present-day snapshot)
is NOT used and is not imported.

Dates after membership_coverage_end() are masked ALL-FALSE rather than
filled with today's roster — substituting present-day membership at the
recent end is precisely what would silently reintroduce survivorship bias.
Formations therefore stop at that boundary.

Screening window: formations 2016-01-04 .. 2026-06-30. The start is one
full year after MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START (2015-01-07) because the predictor
needs a prior year of each firm's own filing calendar before it can place a
single prediction. Filings and prices are fetched from 2014-06-01 for that
warm-up; no window with an entry before 2016-01-04 is ever traded.

RESIDUAL SURVIVORSHIP, DISCLOSED IN ADVANCE AND MEASURED, NOT FIXED:
 (a) 162 of the 768 point-in-time tickers (21%) DID NOT RESOLVE A CIK in
     SEC's company_tickers.json, because that file lists only CURRENT
     registrants. The unresolved names are exactly the acquired and renamed
     ones — AABA, ABC, ABMD, ADS, AET, AGN, ALTR, ALXN, ANDV, ANTM, ARNC,
     ATVI, AVP and 149 more. They contribute NO announcements at all.
 (b) yfinance sells no delisted price history, so even a resolved name that
     died is unpriceable.
Both effects remove the same population — companies that stopped existing.
DIRECTION, stated before any result: this is a LONG-SHORT book in which the
short leg is the whole index, so the missing names would have appeared on
BOTH sides at different times, and the net direction of the bias is NOT
cleanly signable a priori. What IS certain is that the sample is thinner and
more survivor-heavy than the real index was, and no number in the run report
should be read as if it were not.

3. THE PREDICTOR, AND ITS MEASURED ACCURACY
============================================================================
THE PROBLEM. This family must know a firm's next announcement date BEFORE it
happens. This project has no analyst-estimate or earnings-calendar vendor
feed. The only available source is the firm's OWN past 8-K Item 2.02 filing
history from SEC EDGAR.

THE RULE, fixed here. Each ACTUAL announcement generates exactly ONE
prediction, EAP_PREDICTOR_LAG_DAYS = 364 calendar days later. 364 = 52 weeks
exactly, which PRESERVES DAY OF WEEK; earnings releases are scheduled on
weekdays, so a 365-day shift walks the prediction one weekday per year.
Because each prediction is generated by an announcement roughly a year before
the position it justifies, the entire predicted calendar is point-in-time by
construction — a prediction can never read a filing that had not yet been
made. The module asserts this rather than trusting it.

Day 0 of an actual announcement is derived by
cross_sectional_pead.announcement_day0, REUSED UNCHANGED (acceptance before
16:00 ET -> that session; at/after 16:00 ET, on a non-trading day, or with no
timestamp -> the next session — the never-early direction).

THE MEASUREMENT (2026-09-01, formation window only, NO returns computed).
Real EDGAR data: 29,199 Item 2.02 filings for 606 CIK-resolved tickers,
de-duplicated to 28,696 announcements across 603 tickers (503 dropped as
re-files within 5 trading rows), median 49 announcements per ticker. Signed
error is (actual - predicted) in TRADING days, matched to the nearest actual
announcement by the same ticker:

   lag   n_pred  unmatched  medAbsErr  meanAbsErr  meanSigned   ±0     ±1     ±3     ±5     ±7
   364   24,528     0.4%       1.0        2.17       +1.18    46.5%  61.2%  76.2%  92.1%  94.8%
   365   24,528     0.4%       1.0        2.37       +0.20     9.4%  62.1%  78.4%  91.5%  95.2%
   371   24,520     0.4%       5.0        4.50       -3.58     9.9%  14.5%  27.8%  84.6%  92.4%

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT.
 * The day-of-week hypothesis is CONFIRMED EMPIRICALLY, not assumed: the
   364-day lag hits the EXACT trading day 46.5% of the time against 9.4% for
   365. That is the entire reason 364 is the declared lag. Beyond ±1 the two
   are near-identical, so this choice matters for tight windows and washes
   out for wide ones — which the grid in section 4 spans deliberately.
 * 371 days (53 weeks) is clearly worse and is reported only to show the
   comparison was actually run rather than asserted.
 * The predictor is GOOD: 92.1% of predictions land within ±5 trading days
   and only 0.4% match no real announcement at all.
 * MEAN SIGNED ERROR IS +1.18 TRADING DAYS. Firms drift LATER year over
   year. PRE-DECLARED CONSEQUENCE, written before any return exists: of the
   four (days_before, days_after) pairs in section 4, the LATE-SKEWED pair
   (1,5) should mechanically capture the most real announcements and the
   EARLY-SKEWED pair (5,1) the fewest. If (1,5) wins, that is an expected
   mechanical consequence of calendar drift and NOT independent evidence of
   a premium; this is recorded now so it cannot be presented as a discovery
   later.
 * IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH that a caught announcement earns anything. The
   per-spec caught_actual_fraction reports the realized share of each spec's
   windows that actually contained the firm's real announcement, and it is a
   DIAGNOSTIC — never a filter, because an ex-ante family cannot know it
   missed.

4. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 8 SPECS, FROZEN
============================================================================
Three dimensions, fully crossed. This grid is frozen as of this document.

  D1  days_before   2 levels: 1, 5    trading days before the PREDICTED
                    announcement row at which the position opens. 1 = the
                    minimal genuinely-ex-ante entry; 5 = a full week of
                    accumulation, which is what a real book with a noisy
                    calendar would need.
  D2  days_after    2 levels: 1, 5    trading days after the predicted row at
                    which it closes. The window must SPAN the announcement
                    for the premium to be earned at all, and section 3 shows
                    the predictor is not exact, so this axis is what buys
                    tolerance for calendar error. The (1,1) and (5,5) corners
                    bracket roughly 61% and 92% expected catch rates.
  D3  leg_weighting 2 levels: equal, ann_vol
                    "equal" is the harness's own equal mode. "ann_vol"
                    weights each announcer PROPORTIONALLY to its own trailing
                    announcement-window risk — the mean ABSOLUTE excess-of-SPY
                    return over its last 8 three-day (-1,+1) announcement
                    windows. This is the weighting [SW16]'s mechanism implies:
                    compensation scales with risk BORNE. Note it is the
                    OPPOSITE of a generic inverse-vol scheme. It is a
                    MAGNITUDE, so the sign of a past announcement never
                    reaches the weights.

  2 x 2 x 2 = 8 = EAP_N_TRIALS, asserted in code against the built list and
  used as the DSR n_trials denominator. 8 >= deflated_sharpe.MIN_TRIALS_FOR_DSR (5).

  pattern_id format: eap_b{1|5}_a{1|5}_{equal|ann_vol}

THE SHORT LEG IS NOT AN AXIS. It is always every OTHER point-in-time member
with a usable return that day, EQUAL-WEIGHTED. That is not a fresh judgment:
cross_sectional.py's own docstring states the rule for its universe hedge —
"The universe-hedge side itself stays equal-weighted always — there is no
rank cutoff for 'the whole eligible universe' to weight members' distance
from." Reused, not re-decided.

WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED, AND IS FIXED NOW AT ONE VALUE EACH:
  predictor lag                364 days (section 3)
  slot-filled suppression      30 calendar days
  duplicate-filing gap         5 trading rows
  min prior announcements      4, applied UNIFORMLY to all eight specs —
                               including the equal-weighted ones, which do
                               not need it — so every spec trades the
                               IDENTICAL population and differs only in
                               window and weights. Without that uniformity
                               the sigma_SR feeding the DSR would measure
                               universe differences rather than spec
                               differences. This is asserted by a test.
  ann_vol basis depth          last 8 announcements, (-1,+1) window
  benchmark                    SPY
  delisting imputation         ON (Shumway, via the harness's own
                               _compute_delisting_positions)

5. COST MODEL — THE MOST LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION HERE
============================================================================
This book is TURNOVER-HEAVY BY CONSTRUCTION: the long leg is rebuilt every
few days as firms rotate through their announcement windows. Cost is
therefore the assumption most able to decide the result, and it is fixed in
advance rather than chosen afterwards.

Cost is charged as ONE-WAY bps on the L1 turnover of the NET book every day.
The ladder is adopted VERBATIM from this project's own sourced calibration in
data/research_runs/edge_cost_reaudit_corrected_PREREGISTRATION.txt section 2
— it was NOT re-derived here, and the sources behind it (Hagstromer JFE 2021
Table 1; Mackintosh/Nasdaq 2024 and 2021) were read and cited by that
document, not by this one:

   0.0bp  no-cost reference, to separate a gross null from a cost kill
   1.0bp  2015-regime tight bound
   2.0bp  BEST ESTIMATE for an equal-weighted S&P 500 book across 2015-2026
   3.5bp  conservative bound (vol spikes, departed-member illiquidity skew)
   5.0bp  = cross_sectional.DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS, the project's existing
          control rate — AND THIS FAMILY'S DECLARED HEADLINE.

The HEADLINE Sharpe, the DSR, and the persisted DB rows all use 5.0bp. The
harsher end is deliberately the headline; the other four columns are reported
for every spec so a reader can see exactly where cost flips the verdict.

Financing: 0.0bp/yr — the harness default and the value every other equity
family in this project uses, carrying the SAME disclosed optimism documented
in cross_sectional.py: this project has no sourced securities-borrow feed (a
known OPEN paid-data item). The short leg here is the broad index rather than
a hard-to-borrow tail, which makes the optimism smaller than in some sibling
families, but it is optimism and it is disclosed.

NOT MODELLED, declared in advance: market impact, commissions, borrow, and
the fact that announcement-window spreads are WIDER than a stock's
normal-times spread. That last one is this family's specific cost hazard and
it biases reported costs DOWNWARD. True costs are HIGHER than every column
of the ladder.

6. PRE-DECLARED DIAGNOSTICS (reported always; NOT specs; NOT in n_trials)
============================================================================
 1. THE PLACEBO CALENDAR — the falsification control. The identical
    machinery is run on a calendar shifted +45 calendar days, so every
    window lands in the quiet middle of a reporting cycle where nothing is
    scheduled. PRE-DECLARED READING: if the placebo earns what the real
    book earns, then whatever is being measured is NOT about announcements,
    and the family is reported as a null regardless of its headline Sharpe.
 2. caught_actual_fraction, per spec: the share of traded windows that
    actually contained the firm's real announcement. Computed with
    hindsight, used for REPORTING ONLY, never consulted in formation.
 3. The full cost ladder of section 5, per spec.
 4. Gross (0bp) Sharpe alongside net, per spec, so a cost kill is
    distinguishable from a gross null.
 5. Subperiod Sharpes over three equal contiguous thirds, per spec. The
    post-2004-decay allegation in section 8 predicts no time trend in
    particular, but a family that lives entirely in one third is fragile
    whatever the literature says.
 6. Long-leg size distribution (mean / min / max) and the count of
    one-sided days. Earnings cluster into four reporting seasons; a spec
    whose long leg is routinely tiny is single-name noise, not a
    cross-section.

7. THE DECISION RULE — FIXED BEFORE THE RESULT
============================================================================
This project's own standard, as already implemented in
cross_sectional_eigenportfolio.py and cross_sectional_correlation_risk_premium.py,
applied to the BEST DSR across the eight specs at the 5.0bp headline:

   DSR >= 0.90   clears this project's significance standard
   0.50 <= DSR < 0.90   possibly interesting, well short of the standard
   DSR < 0.50    an HONEST NEGATIVE by this project's pre-declared rule

A spec that clears on DSR but fails the placebo control (diagnostic 1) is
NOT reported as a pass. The placebo is a veto, not a tiebreak.

8. WHAT WOULD MAKE A POSITIVE RESULT HERE FAKE
============================================================================
 1. THE POST-2004 DECAY ALLEGATION. There is NO peer-reviewed published
    study establishing whether this premium survived post-2013
    out-of-sample. The one directly on-point study — Heitz, Narayanamoorthy
    & Zekhnini, "Filings of Material Information and the Disappearing
    Earnings Announcement Premium" (SSRN 3296537, doi:10.2139/ssrn.3296537)
    — is an UNPUBLISHED working paper whose text could NOT be retrieved
    (SSRN 403, Rotman mirror HTTP 500, no abstract at Crossref or OpenAlex).
    Its existence, authorship and title are verified; its numbers are NOT,
    and none are quoted here or anywhere in this family. Its CLAIM,
    consistently reported across independent summaries and stated here as
    the paper's claim rather than as fact, is that the US premium
    disappeared after 2004 because the 2004 Form 8-K disclosure regime
    pre-empted the information. THIS FAMILY'S ENTIRE SAMPLE IS POST-2004.
    If that claim is right, the expected result is zero, and a strong
    positive should be disbelieved before it is believed.
 2. GENERAL POST-PUBLICATION DECAY. McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic
    Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", Journal of Finance
    71(1), 2016, pp. 5-32, doi:10.1111/jofi.12365 (abstract retrieved
    verbatim) measure returns "58% lower post-publication" across 97
    predictors. GENERAL, not EAP-specific; used as a prior only.
 3. THE CALENDAR-DRIFT ARTIFACT of section 3: (1,5) beating (5,1) is
    mechanically expected from the +1.18-day drift and is not evidence of a
    premium.
 4. A MARKET-BETA ARTIFACT. If the long leg is systematically higher-beta
    than the index short, part of any positive number is just beta. [SW11]'s
    own claim is that the premium EXCEEDS what beta explains (its
    announcement portfolio beta is 0.12). This family does not estimate
    betas, so a positive result would carry an unresolved beta confound and
    must say so rather than claim the risk premium.
 5. TRIAL COUNT. n_trials = 8 covers only THIS grid. It does not cover the
    literature scan that nominated this family, nor the ~28 other families
    this project has screened. Every DSR reported is an UPPER BOUND on the
    honest one.
 6. OVERLAPPING RETURNS. Earnings cluster into four reporting seasons and
    windows overlap heavily, so consecutive daily returns share most of
    their constituents. The daily observation count feeding the Sharpe and
    the DSR OVERSTATES the independent information by a large and
    unquantified factor. This is a SEPARATE caution from the n_trials
    correction and neither substitutes for the other.

9. STOPPING RULES
============================================================================
 * The 8-spec grid in section 4 is FROZEN. If, after seeing results, a
   further spec looks attractive, the temptation is to be DOCUMENTED IN THE
   RUN REPORT and not acted on.
 * The predictor lag is FROZEN at 364 by section 3's measurement, which was
   made on dates alone with no return in sight. It will not be re-tuned
   against returns.
 * The headline cost is FROZEN at 5.0bp. The ladder is reported in full;
   the headline is not moved to a friendlier column after the fact.
 * n_trials will NOT be shrunk after the fact on any argument that some
   specs are near-mirrors of each other.
 * If the placebo control earns what the real book earns, the family is
   reported as a null regardless of its Sharpe.
 * An honest negative is a complete and acceptable result. No spec will be
   added, no window widened, and no cost lowered to avoid one.
