  (b) THE BLACKOUT-PERIOD RESULT IS GONE FROM THE PUBLISHED ABSTRACT. [HS22]'s

1.2 THE MECHANISM, IN THE PUBLISHED PAPER'S OWN WORDS ([HS25] abstract,
    verbatim, and the only [HS25] text this build could read):

    "We demonstrate that predictable uninformed cash flows forecast aggregate
     market stock returns. Buying pressure from dividend payments (announced
     weeks prior) predicts higher value-weighted market returns, with returns

WHY THE IDENTIFICATION IS UNUSUALLY CLEAN, [HS22] Section III.A verbatim: "the

implications." And: "Once the dividend is declared, companies have a legal
obligation to pay it."

2. THE PAPER'S OWN CONSTRUCTION, QUOTED FROM [HS22]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 THE DATA AND THE TIMING CONVENTION (Section II, verbatim)

"Stock return data is from the Center for Research in Securities Prices
(CRSP), including returns, dividend amounts and payment dates. We limit our
return data to ordinary common shares (codes 10 or 11) listed on the NYSE,
NASDAQ or AMEX. For dividend amounts we examine ordinary cash dividends. We
examine data from 1926 through 2018."

"Unless otherwise noted, when we refer to the timing of a dividend, we mean
the payment date. For example, when we refer to a dividend date or a dividend
yield, we are referring to date or yield based on the payment date."

THAT SENTENCE IS THE WHOLE REASON SECTION 3.1 EXISTS. The payment-date choice
is deliberate, is defended on economic grounds ("lacks economically meaningful
news or tax implications", against an ex-date that resolves tax consequences),
and is the one input this project's existing dividend infrastructure does not
have.

2.2 THE DEPENDENT VARIABLE (Section III.A, verbatim)

"To consider the broadest and most liquid set of test assets, we take as our
main dependent variable the value-weighted market portfolio from CRSP."

2.3 THE PAYMENT YIELD AND THE PRIMARY REGRESSION (Section III.A / Table I,
    verbatim)

"The independent variable is the dividend payment yield on a payment day t,
which is the daily total dividend payments, divided by the previous day's total
market capitalization."

    MktRet_t = a + b * PayYield_t + eps                                     (I)

Column 1 lag-by-lag: "a coefficient on payment yield of 55.76 for day t
(t-statistic of 1.74), 60.04 for day t-1 (t-statistic of 2.73). Days t-2 and
t-3 are smaller and statistically insignificant, though day t-4 is also larger
and marginally significant (t-statistic of 1.89)."

THE MAIN VARIABLE, columns 3-4, verbatim: "the cumulative dividend payment
yield on the payment date and the day before. Column 3 finds similar effects to
column 1 - dividend payout from day t and day t-1 positively and significantly
predicts value-weighted market returns on day t (coefficient of 59.50 and
t-statistic of 3.32). When year-by-month fixed effects are added in column 4,
the results are again stronger, with coefficients increased to 67.07 and a
t-statistic of 3.47. A one standard deviation increase in payout yield over
days t and t-1 (.0004711) predicts higher daily returns of 3.2 b.p. on day t."

NOTE THE TIMING, because it is what makes this tradeable rather than a
contemporaneous correlation: the regressor for day t's return uses dividends
paid on day t AND day t-1. Using day t's own dividend is NOT look-ahead — the
amount and date were declared on average 43 days earlier and are legally owed.
That is the paper's central identifying claim, not a modelling liberty.

2.4 THE PLACEBO (Table I columns 5-6, verbatim)

"If the returns reflect price pressure from investing a dividend payment, then
there is less reason to expect positive return predictability before the
payment is made. Thus the period directly prior to dividend payment offers a
placebo where price pressure does not make strong predictions about returns. To
examine this period, Table I columns 5 and 6 examines the influence of future
dividends (on day t+1 and t+2) on current (day t) returns. The coefficients are
economically small and insignificant."

2.5 THE PRICE-INDEPENDENT ALTERNATIVE (Table II Panel A, verbatim)

"we construct an abnormal dividend yield which is dividends on days t and t-1
divided by the average daily dividend paid over the prior year."

Footnote 12, verbatim: "We calculate this average using the trading days from
t-20 to t-272. The average year contains 252 trading days and we skip about a
month (20 trading days) to ensure the denominator excludes any recent market
information."

Result, verbatim: "the results are positive and significant at the 1% level ...
Taking the value-weighted estimate in column 2, a one standard deviation
increase in the dividend yield is associated with an increase in expected
returns of 2.7 b.p., SIMILAR TO THE 3.2 B.P. FOUND FROM THE SAME SPECIFICATION
NORMALIZING BY MARKET CAPITALIZATION." That last clause is the paper's own
market-cap-versus-prior-year-average agreement claim, and fidelity check F6
reproduces it here.

2.6 THE QUINTILE FIGURE (Figure 1, verbatim)

"We split days into quintiles based on the abnormal dividend and take the
average of the value-weighted market returns in each quintile. The graph shows
a monotonically increasing relationship between payment yield and returns. The
returns in the lowest two quintiles are about 2 basis points. For the next two
quintiles this value increases to roughly 4 basis points. The top quintile of
abnormal dividends is associated with value-weighted market returns of about 8
basis points."

This is the source of both abstracts' "top quintile of payment days four times
higher than the lowest" (8bp against 2bp).

2.7 THE PAPER'S OWN EX-ANTE TRADABLE RULE (Table II Panel B, verbatim) — THE
    CONSTRUCTION THIS FAMILY TRADES

"The graph suggests that the largest effect is concentrated in more extreme
dividend payment dates. To test this, AND TO USE A SIMPLE RULE THAT IS EASY TO
INTERPRET AND EX-ANTE TRADABLE, we examine whether or not a given day's
dividend payment is high relative to the payments made in the previous 252
days. Specifically, we regress market returns on dummy variables equal to one
if the cumulative dividend payment on day t and t-1 is in the top 2 weeks (10
days), quarter (63 days), third (84 days) or half (126 days) of days in the
past year, and equal to zero otherwise."

Results, verbatim: "with no controls, the top ten days have higher
value-weighted returns by 9.8 b.p., with a t-statistic of 2.39. In column 2,
with a year-by-month fixed effect, this increases slightly to 11.6 b.p. In the
specifications including year-by-month fixed effects, the top quarter of days
experienced returns 7.1 b.p. higher (column 4, with a t-statistic of 4.27) and
the top third of days experienced returns 5.7 b.p. higher (column 6, with a
t-statistic of 3.71). Column 8 shows that days with above median dividend
payments experience returns 4.7 b.p. higher with a t-statistic of 3.15. The
constant indicates that below average days earn returns of 1.7 b.p."

2.8 THE PAPER'S OWN ANNUALIZED MAGNITUDES (Table III, verbatim) — THE NUMBERS
    SECTION 10.2's POWER ARITHMETIC IS BUILT FROM

"Column 1 shows that investing in the top 2 weeks earns an average annual
return of 1.4%, while investing on all other days earns a return of 10.2%. This
means that the top 2 weeks per year (3.8% of trading days) earn about 12% of
the equity premium. This ratio of 3.13 indicates that the top 2 weeks account
for roughly triple the amount of the equity premium compared to the number of
days they are in the market. The final two columns, examine being above or
below the median dividend payment. The above median days experience average
annual returns of 7.7% while the below median days experience average returns
of 3.5%."

2.9 A DATA FACT USED AS A COVERAGE CHECK (Section III.A, verbatim): "while
there is some seasonality in dividend payments, over 90% of trading days
involve a dividend payment."

3. DEVIATIONS FROM THE SOURCE, EVERY ONE NAMED AND REASONED ABOUT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 DEVIATION D1 — THE PAYMENT DATE. THE BIG ONE, AND THE ONE THE PAPER IS
    MOST EXPLICIT ABOUT.

THE GAP, CONFIRMED LIVE 2026-09-06 RATHER THAN ASSUMED. This project's existing
dividend infrastructure (cross_sectional_dividend_month.py, DividendEvent,
build_ex_date_calendar, data/dividend_month_ex_date_calendar.json) is built
entirely on EX-DATES. That is not an oversight in this project; it is a
property of the feed. YFinanceProvider.get_dividend_history's own docstring
states it as a fact about the source: "THE INDEX IS THE EX-DIVIDEND DATE, NOT
THE PAYMENT DATE ... Yahoo's actions feed carries one date per distribution and
it is the ex-date ... A caller that needs PAYMENT dates does not have them here
and must say so; nothing in this method can recover one."

RE-VERIFIED DIRECTLY AGAINST THE LIVE ENDPOINTS, not taken on the docstring's
word: yf.Ticker("AAPL").dividends and .actions return a single date index with
columns ['Dividends','Stock Splits'] and no payment-date field of any kind.

WHAT WAS FOUND INSTEAD, AND IT IS WHY THIS FAMILY IS BUILDABLE AT ALL. Two free
sources DO carry payment dates, and both were tested live before this document
was written:

 (i) yfinance's PER-TICKER `.calendar` endpoint carries BOTH 'Ex-Dividend Date'
     and 'Dividend Date' (the PAYMENT date), for a ticker's most recent or
     upcoming distribution — ONE observation per ticker, not a history.
     Measured live over this project's own point-in-time S&P 500 candidate
     pool: 486 of the 496 dividend-paying tickers (98.0%) return a usable
     (ex, pay) pair. On a 50-name random pre-check the implied lag distribution
     was min 7, median 21, mean 21.2, max 34 calendar days — WHICH MATCHES
     [HS22]'s OWN STATED "on average 22 days after the ex-date" almost exactly,
     on this project's own universe and a modern window. That agreement is an
     independent live corroboration of the paper's stated gap, and it is the
     evidence this imputation is not a guess.

 (ii) NASDAQ'S OWN dividend-history API (api.nasdaq.com/api/quote/<T>/dividends)
     returns REAL HISTORICAL (ex-date, record date, DECLARATION date, PAYMENT
     date) tuples. IT CANNOT BE THE PRIMARY SOURCE, and the reason is
     structural rather than transient: it answers, verbatim and repeatably,
     "Dividend History for Non-Nasdaq symbols is not available" for
     NYSE-listed names. Measured live on a random 60-name sample of this
     project's own universe: 9 of 60 covered (15.0%), 38 refused as
     non-Nasdaq, 4 "no dividend", 9 other-empty, 0 HTTP errors. A market-wide
     dividend aggregate built on a source that structurally excludes the NYSE
     would be missing most of the dividend dollars in the market, so this
     source is used ONLY as INDEPENDENT GROUND TRUTH (fidelity check F7).

THE ADOPTED CONSTRUCTION, FIXED HERE:

    payment_date(i, k) = next trading day on or after [ ex_date(i, k) + L_i ]

where L_i is ticker i's OWN calendar-day ex-to-pay lag from source (i), and
where a ticker with no usable lag takes the UNIVERSE MEDIAN lag, computed from
the same source over the same pool. The roll-forward exists because a payment
landing on a weekend or holiday cannot be a trading day in a daily return
panel; it is never rolled BACKWARD, which would move cash earlier than it
could have arrived.

WHY THE IMPUTATION IS NOT SIMPLY ASSUMED TO WORK — F7. Nasdaq's real historical
pay dates are used to MEASURE it: for every covered distribution, the imputed
date is compared against the true one and the signed error distribution, the
exact-hit rate and the within-1/2/5-trading-day rates are reported. THE
PRE-DECLARED READING, fixed here: this is a DATA-QUALITY measurement containing
no return of any kind, so running it before the grid is frozen is not a
p-hacking route (the same reasoning cross_sectional_dividend_month.py's
forecast-accuracy table records). IT IS REPORTED WHATEVER IT SHOWS, and a poor
result is a headline finding of this build, not a reason to change the
construction after the fact.

WHICH DIRECTION THE RESIDUAL ERROR BIASES THE RESULT, reasoned rather than
assumed. Three separate channels, and they do not all point the same way:

 (a) ATTENUATION IS THE FIRST-ORDER EFFECT, AND IT RUNS AGAINST THIS FAMILY.
     A payment-date series with per-event timing error is the true series
     convolved with an error kernel. The signal here is a RANK of a
     two-day-cumulative dollar total, so an event misdated by d trading days
     moves its dollars out of the correct (t, t-1) bucket and into a wrong one.
     That is classical timing/measurement error in the regressor: it dilutes
     the correlation between the proxy and the true pressure day and biases the
     coefficient TOWARD ZERO. A null here is therefore WEAK evidence against
     the paper, and section 9 pre-commits to saying so rather than reporting a
     null as a refutation.
 (b) THE ERROR IS MOSTLY A LEVEL SHIFT PER FIRM, AND A CONSTANT PER-FIRM SHIFT
     IS THE BENIGN CASE. If L_i is right for firm i on average and wrong only
     by within-firm drift, the aggregate is a correctly-timed series with a
     modest per-event jitter. The Nasdaq check in F7 measures exactly the size
     of that jitter, so the benign case is a MEASURED claim in the results
     document, not an assumption here.
 (c) THE CHANNEL THAT COULD MANUFACTURE A FALSE POSITIVE, AND IT IS THE
     EX-DATE ARM'S PROBLEM SPECIFICALLY. The EX-DATE has its own large,
     long-documented price effects that the PAYMENT date does not: the ex-day
     price drop, and the run-up and reversal around it that the SAME TWO
     AUTHORS document in Hartzmark & Solomon (JFE 2013) and that THIS PROJECT
     has already measured on its own data (+12.84bp run-up into the ex-day,
     -18.08bp reversal over the following 40 days;
     cross_sectional_dividend_month.py section 5). An ex-date-anchored
     aggregate signal can therefore pick up a REAL effect that is NOT the
     payment-date reinvestment mechanism. That is why the ex-date arm is
     carried as an explicitly COUNTED grid arm rather than as the primary, and
     why a positive that appears ONLY under `ex_date` dating is pre-declared
     (section 9) to be reported as evidence for the WRONG mechanism.

THE EX-DATE ARM IS NOT DISCARDED, AND WHY. It is what this project's existing
infrastructure natively provides, so a successor needs to know what it gives;
and carrying it as a counted arm means resolving the payment-versus-ex
question costs trials instead of being a free choice made after seeing which
one worked. This is the same device rebalancing_pressure_timing.py used for
its one-sided/two-sided trigger ambiguity.

3.2 DEVIATION D2 — UNIVERSE. [HS22] uses ALL CRSP ordinary common shares
(codes 10/11) on NYSE/NASDAQ/AMEX, 1926-2018. This build uses this project's
POINT-IN-TIME S&P 500 candidate pool (768 tickers over the membership
coverage window, 625 of them priced, 496 with a dividend history, 24,858
ex-dates).

DIRECTION, and it is genuinely more favourable here than it was for this
project's OTHER Hartzmark-Solomon family. cross_sectional_dividend_month.py
had to disclose that a large-cap-only universe is where a CROSS-SECTIONAL
price-pressure effect should be SMALLEST (the paper's own liquidity evidence).
That argument does not transfer: this family's object is an AGGREGATE
market-wide dollar flow, and the S&P 500 is the large majority of US market
capitalization and of US dividend dollars. The S&P 500 aggregate is a
reasonable proxy for the CRSP aggregate in a way that an S&P 500 cross-section
is NOT a proxy for the CRSP cross-section. It is still a proxy, not the thing:
excluded small- and mid-caps pay dividends on their own calendars, and their
absence adds timing noise to the aggregate. No adjustment is made.

SURVIVORSHIP: the pool is point-in-time by construction
(sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over), so a firm enters the aggregate
only while it was actually an index member.

3.3 DEVIATION D3 — DEPENDENT VARIABLE. [HS22] uses the CRSP value-weighted
market portfolio. This build uses SPY total return, from this project's own
point-in-time price store — the same instrument and the same call
vol_regime_timing.py and rebalancing_pressure_timing.py already use. SPY is a
value-weighted S&P 500 total-return proxy, consistent with the universe in D2.
It is net of a ~9bp/yr expense ratio, a constant drift the regression intercept
absorbs freely and which cannot manufacture predictability from a
mean-reverting dividend-timing signal.

3.4 DEVIATION D4 — WINDOW, AND IT IS THIS BUILD'S SEVEREST LIMITATION.
[HS22]: 1926-2018, ~24,000 daily observations. This build: FORMATIONS FROM
2018-01-02 to the end of point-in-time membership coverage, roughly 2,100
trading days — about ONE ELEVENTH of the paper's sample.

WHAT BINDS THE START, precisely, because it is not the obvious thing: the
point-in-time S&P 500 membership data begins 2015-01-07, but the SEC
point-in-time share counts this build needs to turn per-share dividends into
DOLLARS begin at frame CY2017Q3 and carry a 90-day visibility lag
(sec_shares_outstanding_provider.EARLIEST_FRAME, VISIBILITY_LAG_DAYS), so no
dollar aggregate exists before roughly 2017-12-29. THE WHOLE GRID USES THAT ONE
WINDOW, including the specs that need no share counts, so that sigma_SR
measures spec differences rather than universe differences — the uniformity
rule cross_sectional_dividend_month.py's DMP_MIN_PRIOR_EX_DATES note states.

THE POWER CONSEQUENCE IS COMPUTED IN ADVANCE IN SECTION 10.2 AND IS SEVERE.

3.5 DEVIATION D5 — SHARE COUNTS ARE SEC-FILED, NOT CRSP. Dollar dividends and
aggregate market capitalization are built from
dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding via this project's own
SecSharesOutstandingProvider, forward-filled as a STEP function from each
count's own visibility date. A ticker whose CIK SEC cannot resolve contributes
NO dividend dollars and NO market cap; the count of such tickers is reported
rather than absorbed silently.

SPLIT-BASIS HAZARD, NAMED BECAUSE GETTING IT WRONG WOULD SILENTLY SCALE THE
NUMERATOR. yfinance dividend amounts are SPLIT-ADJUSTED into today's share
units; SEC share counts are the RAW counts filed at the time. Multiplying them
directly understates a pre-split firm's dividend dollars by exactly its
cumulative split factor. Both series are therefore put on ONE basis with
cross_sectional_ivol.split_adjust_share_counts — the same function
cross_sectional_buyback.py already uses for the identical hazard — before any
product is formed. F8 checks the result against an economically meaningful
known quantity (the implied aggregate dividend yield) rather than trusting it.

3.6 DEVIATION D6 — NO YEAR-BY-MONTH FIXED EFFECTS IN THE TRADED BOOK.
[HS22]'s stronger columns all add year-by-month fixed effects. A TRADED
PORTFOLIO CANNOT HAVE FIXED EFFECTS: they are estimated using the whole
sample, including the future. The regressions reported as fidelity checks
report BOTH the no-FE and the FE specification, labelled, because both are the
paper's; the TRADED specs use no controls of any kind. This is why the
CALENDAR CONTROL SPEC of section 5 exists instead — the paper's own stated
purpose for its year-by-month FE is that it "controls for specific calendar
effects", and a portfolio has to defend against those structurally.

3.7 DEVIATION D7 — "ORDINARY CASH DIVIDENDS" IS NOT SEPARATELY IDENTIFIABLE.
[HS22] restricts to ordinary cash dividends using CRSP distribution codes.
Yahoo's actions feed has no distribution code, so a special dividend is
indistinguishable from an ordinary one and IS INCLUDED. Specials are large and
rare, so they push the aggregate's extreme right tail — precisely the top-10-day
bucket this family trades. Direction: a special is genuinely a cash payment
that gets reinvested, so including it is arguably faithful to the MECHANISM
even where it is unfaithful to the paper's SAMPLE. Not adjusted; disclosed.

3.8 NON-DEVIATIONS, stated so they are not later mistaken for choices made
here. The four dummy thresholds (top 10 / 63 / 84 / 126 of the trailing 252),
the two-day (t, t-1) cumulation window, the abnormal-yield denominator window
(trading days t-20 to t-272), and the quintile split of Figure 1 are ALL taken
VERBATIM from [HS22] sections 2.5-2.7 above. None is tuned here, none is
searched here, and none was chosen with any knowledge of how it performs on
this project's data.

4. WHY THIS IS NOT THE DIVIDEND-MONTH-PREMIUM FAMILY WEARING A DIFFERENT HAT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project already ran cross_sectional_dividend_month.py — the SAME TWO
AUTHORS' 2013 JFE dividend-month premium — to an honest negative. The
distinction is recorded before the run, and the overlap that IS real is priced
by the pooled DSR denominators of section 6, not argued away.

  UNIT        DMP: individual stocks, a cross-sectional long/short of ~144
              names against ~119-226. HERE: ONE instrument (SPY). There is no
              cross-section at all; the signal is a single scalar per DAY
              belonging to no ticker.
  DATE        DMP: the EX-date, and its own docstring records that as a
              correction it had to make ("Dividend months refer to months with
              an ex-date"). HERE: the PAYMENT date, ~21 days later, chosen
              because it is the date that carries NO tax or news content.
  HORIZON     DMP: monthly formation, month-long holds. HERE: daily, a one-day
              prediction.
  MECHANISM   DMP: dividend-SEEKING investors buying AHEAD of the ex-day, a
              cross-sectional demand story. HERE: dividend-RECEIVING investors
              reinvesting cash AFTER it arrives, an aggregate flow story. The
              two predict pressure on opposite sides of the ex-date.
  WHAT KILLED DMP: cost. Every one of its 12 specs was positive gross and
              negative net of 5bp, on a book turning over 0.14-0.37 daily.
              THIS family trades ONE of the cheapest instruments in existence
              and holds a directional position, so cost is a far smaller part
              of the story here — which is itself a reason the two are
              genuinely different tests rather than the same test twice.

WHAT IS SHARED AND CANNOT BE ARGUED AWAY: two authors, one broad research
programme, one project, and a universe and price store in common. n_trials
cannot see that this is this project's SECOND Hartzmark-Solomon family. Both
DSRs are upper bounds and section 9 says so.

THE STATE VARIABLE, common to every dividend spec, is [HS22] section 2.7's own:

    S_t   = D_t + D_{t-1}        (the two-day cumulative dollar dividend)
    x_t   = scale(S_t)           (one of the three scalings below)
    high_t = 1 if x_t ranks in the top N of {x_{t-251}, ..., x_t}, else 0

SIGNAL DEFINITIONS (4):
  raw          x_t = S_t. [HS22] Table II Panel B LITERALLY: "whether or not a
               given day's dividend payment is high relative to the payments
               made in the previous 252 days". No scaling at all. This is the
               paper's own most literal ex-ante rule and is the PRIMARY
               definition.
  mktcap       x_t = S_t / MktCap_{t-1}. [HS22] Table I's payment yield, whose
               denominator is "the previous day's total market capitalization".
  abnormal     x_t = S_t / mean(D over trading days t-20 .. t-272). [HS22]
               Table II Panel A and footnote 12 verbatim. PRICE-INDEPENDENT,
               and the paper's own answer to any circularity concern about the
               market-cap denominator.
  future_ph    x_t = D_{t+1} + D_{t+2}. [HS22] Table I columns 5-6's PLACEBO,
               TRADED. **PRE-DECLARED TO FAIL.** The paper's own finding is
               that these coefficients "are economically small and
               insignificant"; under the price-pressure mechanism, cash that
               has not yet arrived cannot be reinvested. It is in the grid as
               the matched negative control for the three real definitions, not
               as a candidate. IT IS NOT POINT-IN-TIME TRADEABLE and is not
               claimed to be: it reads dividends dated after the formation. It
               is a falsification device, and the results document says so
               wherever its number appears.

DATINGS (2), the D1 axis:
  payment      the imputed payment date of section 3.1. PRIMARY.
  ex_date      the raw ex-date, i.e. what this project's existing dividend
               infrastructure natively provides. A DEVIATION carried as a
               counted arm so that resolving D1 costs trials.

THRESHOLDS (4), inherited verbatim from [HS22] section 2.7, not searched:
  N = 10 (top 2 weeks), 63 (top quarter), 84 (top third), 126 (top half).

SIZING (1) — DEMEANED, and there is exactly one because the alternative is a
disguised equity-premium bet:

    position_t = high_t - N/252

A long-only "hold SPY on high-dividend days" book is long the market a
predictable fraction of the time, so its Sharpe is dominated by the EQUITY
PREMIUM rather than by anything the paper claims. Subtracting the
PRE-DECLARED unconditional frequency N/252 makes the book market-neutral BY
CONSTRUCTION and in expectation, and makes it the exact traded analogue of the
Table II Panel B dummy COEFFICIENT — which is the number the paper reports.
N/252 is not estimated from the sample; it is the definitional frequency of a
top-N-of-252 rank. The long-only Sharpe is still REPORTED, as a diagnostic, so
a reader can see how much of it is the equity premium.

CONTROL SPECS (2) — THE MOST IMPORTANT SPECS IN THE GRID, and they contain no
dividend data whatsoever:
  tom_ctrl_4d  position_t = tom_t - E[tom], where tom_t = 1 on the LAST trading
               day of a month and the FIRST THREE of the next — the
               Lakonishok & Smidt (1988) / Ariel (1987) turn-of-the-month
               window, one of the oldest documented calendar anomalies in
               equities. ~4 of ~21 trading days (19%), comparable to the
               N = 63 (25%) dividend cell.
  tom_ctrl_1d  the same with a ONE-DAY window (the first trading day of the
               month alone), ~4.8% of days, comparable to the N = 10 (4.0%)
               dividend cell.

  WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT CONTROL, AND IT IS NOT GENERIC. Dividend payments are
  heavily clustered on the 1st, 10th, 15th and month-end — so a "high aggregate
  dividend day" indicator is, mechanically, substantially a CALENDAR indicator.
  The turn-of-the-month effect is a large, independently documented equity
  return regularity that would produce exactly the pattern this family is
  looking for, with no dividend mechanism at all. [HS22] defends against it
  with year-by-month fixed effects, whose stated purpose in the paper's own
  words is that it "controls for specific calendar effects"; a traded portfolio
  cannot do that (D6), so it needs a matched calendar spec instead.

  PRE-DECLARED READING, FIXED HERE: if either control spec matches or beats the
  dividend specs, this family measured the TURN-OF-THE-MONTH EFFECT wearing a
  dividend costume, and the results document says so REGARDLESS OF THE DSR.
  This is the direct analogue of rebalancing_pressure_timing.py's
  trailing21_ctrl and vol_regime_timing.py's vix_level control.

DIRECTION IS NOT A FREE PARAMETER. Every dividend spec trades
DIVIDEND_PRESSURE_DIRECTION = +1 (high dividend payments -> BUYING pressure ->
HIGHER market returns), fixed by [HS22]'s abstract before any return is
computed. Specs whose realized sign is the opposite will print negative Sharpes
and will NOT be flipped. A flipped family is a second 34-trial search and must
be counted as one.

WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED, and therefore not separately in the denominator: the four
thresholds (inherited, 3.8), the two-day cumulation (inherited, 3.8), the
abnormal-yield denominator window (inherited, 3.8), the traded instrument (SPY,
inherited from vol_regime_timing.py), the turn-of-month window (Lakonishok &
Smidt's own), the demeaning constant N/252 (definitional), and the formation
start (data-bound, 3.4).

envelope over D'Avolio (2002) Table 3's 17bp value-weighted GC mean. SPY is
general collateral by any reading of D'Avolio p.273.

THIS FAMILY IS SHORT SPY MOST OF THE TIME BY CONSTRUCTION, which is unusual and

holds -0.5 on half of them. The charge is small in absolute terms (34bp/yr on
|position| <= 0.5) but it is a PERMANENT one-directional drag on a book whose
expected gross is itself small, so it is priced rather than waved off.

10. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 WHY THE SOURCE IS THE BEST OF THE SIX

Peer-reviewed in the American Economic Review, a top-five journal, in September
2025 — the only one of this session's six flow candidates that cleared
refereeing. The mechanism is mechanical rather than behavioural: a declared
dividend is a LEGAL OBLIGATION, so the cash genuinely arrives, and nobody has
to be making a mistake for the flow to exist. The identification is unusually
clean and the paper's own placebo (F2) and price-independent alternative (F6)
are exactly the two checks a sceptic would demand. The effect is documented in
the single instrument this project can trade most cheaply. And the paper's own
tradeable rule (2.7) was designed by its authors to be simple and ex-ante
implementable, so this build is not inventing a portfolio the paper never
contemplated.

10.2 AND WHY IT SHOULD STILL BE EXPECTED TO FAIL — THE ARITHMETIC, DONE IN
     ADVANCE FROM THE PAPER'S OWN NUMBERS

THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PARAGRAPH IN THIS DOCUMENT, and it is written before
any backtest exists so that it cannot be retrofitted to whatever comes out.

 (a) THE EFFECT, TAKEN AT FACE VALUE, IS TOO SMALL FOR THIS PROJECT'S BAR.
     From [HS22] Table III (2.8), above-median dividend days earn 7.7% a year
     and below-median days 3.5%. A demeaned long/short book at N = 126 is
     +0.5 on half the days and -0.5 on the other half, so its expected gross
     return is 0.5 * (7.7% - 3.5%) = 2.1% a year, carried at 0.5 units of
     market exposure, i.e. ~8% annualized volatility.
         EXPECTED SHARPE ~ 2.1 / 8 = 0.26.
     The same arithmetic at N = 10, using Table III's 1.4% on the top 2 weeks
     against 10.2% on all other days and a demeaned position of +0.96 / -0.04:
     expected return 0.96*1.4% - 0.04*10.2% = 0.94% a year on an expected
     volatility of ~3.1%, i.e.
         EXPECTED SHARPE ~ 0.30.
     BOTH ARE BELOW THIS PROJECT'S 0.50 SCREENING FLOOR AND FAR BELOW ITS 0.95
     DSR BAR. **A PERFECT, UNDECAYED, ZERO-COST REPLICATION OF THE PAPER'S OWN
     REPORTED MAGNITUDE WOULD STILL NOT PASS.** That is not a criticism of the
     paper — it reports a real economic effect and never claims a tradeable
     Sharpe — it is a statement about what this family can possibly conclude.

 (b) THE SAMPLE IS ~1/11 OF THE PAPER'S, SO THE t-STATISTIC IS ~1/3.
     [HS22] has ~24,000 daily observations; this build has ~2,100. Since t
     scales with the square root of the sample, the paper's headline
     t = 3.32 (2.3) implies an expected t of 3.32 * sqrt(2100/24000) = 0.98
     here. **AN EXACTLY-TRUE EFFECT OF EXACTLY THE PAPER'S SIZE WOULD PRODUCE
     AN INSIGNIFICANT REGRESSION IN THIS BUILD.** Every F-check number must be
     read against that expectation, and a t of ~1 with the right sign is the
     SUCCESS case for F1, not a failure.

 (c) THE PAYMENT-DATE IMPUTATION CAN ONLY ATTENUATE (3.1a). Whatever F7
     measures, the residual timing error dilutes the signal toward zero.

 (d) THE CALENDAR CONFOUND IS STRUCTURAL, NOT HYPOTHETICAL (section 5). Firms
     pay on the 1st, 10th, 15th and month-end. The single most likely way this
     family produces a positive is by rediscovering the turn-of-the-month
     effect.

 (e) PUBLICITY. Circulated since November 2022, published in the AER in
     September 2025, about a flow that is public and known 43 days in advance.
     McLean & Pontiff (2016) measure 58% post-publication decay across 97
     predictors; preservation_score already applies that 0.42 retention factor
     here. Applied to (a), a decayed expected Sharpe is ~0.11-0.13.

 (f) EVERY PRIOR FLOW CANDIDATE THIS SESSION CAME BACK NEGATIVE, four of five
     on this project's own DSR bar rather than on data availability. The base
     rate in this project is what it is.

10.3 WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS BUILD WORTH HAVING DONE EVEN THOUGH IT IS EXPECTED TO
     FAIL THE BAR

The DSR verdict is nearly foreordained by 10.2(a) and is the LEAST interesting
output. What is genuinely unknown, and what this build exists to find out, is:

  * Does the paper's effect appear AT ALL in a modern, large-cap, free-data,
    2018-2026 window, at the right SIGN and roughly the right MAGNITUDE (F1,
    F4, F5)? Nobody has checked, and the published sample ends in 2018.
  * Does the paper's own placebo hold on this data (F2)?
  * Does the effect survive the turn-of-the-month control that the paper's
    fixed effects handled but a portfolio cannot (F10, section 5)?
  * Is a payment-date dividend calendar buildable for free at all, and how
    accurate is it (F7)? That answer is REUSABLE by any future family and is
    arguably the most durable thing this build produces.

AN HONEST NEGATIVE IS A COMPLETE, USEFUL OUTCOME AND IS THE EXPECTED ONE. The
failure mode this pre-registration exists to prevent is not a negative — it is
a positive manufactured by choosing the threshold, the scaling, the dating, the
cumulation window, the sizing, the window or the cost model after seeing which
one worked. Every one of those is fixed above.
