Part 1: Introduction 
 
The Stock Exchange of Thailand ("SET") has reviewed the rules related to securities trading on both 
SET and mai  across various aspects to ensure that they remain appropriate for the current market 
environment and trading conditions, without imposing unnecessary obstacles for investors, and in line with 
international stock exchange practices. Therefore, SET deems it appropriate to further amend these rules as 
follows: 
1. Board Lot Adjustment: The board lot size will be reduced to 10 shares per Board Lot 
specifically for securities with a closing price of 50 baht or higher continuously for a period of 6 
months, in order to align with the ticket size of retail investors (The Board Lot size for general 
securities will remain unchanged at 100 shares).  
2. Ceiling & Floor Adjustment for New Listings: The floor level for newly listed securities will be 
reduce to 0.5 times the IPO price , while the Ceiling will remain unchanged at no more than 3 
times the IPO price). 
In this regard, SET is seeking input from the stakeholders to gather information that will be beneficial 
in considering further revisions to related regulations. Please submit your comments or any suggestions to 
the Exchange by June 13, 2025, via the following channel: https://forms.gle/KudnKA6oLyQh6exYA 
For further inquiries , please contact the Securities Trading Operations Department via Email: 
SecuritiesTradingDepartment@set.or.th

Part 2: Proposed Amendments 
2.1) Board Lot Adjustment 
SET has established trading rules requiring investors to specify the number of securities based on a 
trading unit (Board Lot) to ensure appropriate transaction sizes. Currently, 1 Board Lot equals 100 shares 1, 
except for securities with a closing price of 500 baht or higher continuously for a period of 6 months, where 
1 Board Lot equals 50 shares, or calculated as a minimum ticket size of 25,000 baht. 
Most international stock exchanges in the region, such as BURSA, SGX, and IDX, set the Board Lot 
size for common stocks at 100 shares, similar to SET, except for HKEX, which sets different Board Lot 
sizes depending on the issuer's policy. 
SET has studied data regarding the trading behavior and trends of retail investors and stock prices. 
The conclusions are as follows: 
• Retail Investor Trading Behavior  
- The ticket size of retail investors has been trending downward, with most having a ticket size of 
no more than 5,000 baht.  
- The proportion of retail investors with a ticket size not exceeding 500 baht has been continuously 
increasing, with 16% in 2024 (up from just 9% in 2019).  
• Majority of Stock Prices  
- Approximately 95% of securities priced below 50 baht, as well as SET100 stocks, are priced 
between 5 and 50 baht  
 Proposal:  
 To align the Board Lot size with the trading behavior of retail investors, particularly the new generation 
of investors, most of whom have an average ticket size of no more than 5,000 baht, and to enhance 
investment opportunities across a wider range of price levels, SET proposes the following adjustments to the 
Board Lot requirements:

2.2) Ceiling & Floor Adjustment for New Listings 
Currently, SET applies wider price limits (Ceiling & Floor) for the first trading day of common stocks 
compared to those applied during regular trading days. The Ceiling for the first trading day is set at no more 
than 3 times the initial public offering price (IPO) and the floor is set at no lower than 0.01  baht (equivalent 
to 1 time the IPO price). This price limit structure aims to provide a sufficiently wide price range to facilitate 
appropriate price discovery by investors, while still maintaining market stability and preventing excessive price 
volatility. 
International stock exchanges, such as BURSA and KRX, apply different Ceiling & Floor limits for 
newly listed common stocks compared to SET. Specifically, these exchanges allow for a wider Ceiling range 
while imposing narrow Floor limits. BURSA and KRX set the Floor at 30% and 60% of the IPO price, 
respectively. 
The price movements of newly listed common stocks (New Listings) on SET over the past five years 
reveal that, among 175  New Listings, only 4%  reached prices close to the Ceiling level. In contrast, some 
stocks recorded trading prices declined by as much as 45% below their IPO price. 
 Proposal:  
 To reduce the impact of price declines in New Listings on the first trading day, thereby helping to limit 
investment risks for investors and aligning with practices of international stock exchanges, which generally apply 
narrower Floor limits for first-day trading, SET proposes the following amendments to the trading price limits for 
New Listings as outlined below:
