# Does trading more often reduce the chance of losing? — measured

Owner's question. Predictions written first in `PREDICTION_2026-09-15.md` (committed `927a106`,
before any number existed). Script: `run_frequency_experiment.py`.

## Answer: NO. Frequency by itself does nothing, and with costs it is actively harmful.

But one of my four predictions was **refuted**, and I found and fixed a bug in my own simulation
mid-run. Both are below.

---

## ARM 1a — frequency alone, at a fixed annual edge and zero cost → **P1 CONFIRMED**

Annual Sharpe held at 0.60, annual volatility 15%, the SAME annual edge split across N trades
(so each trade carries mu/N of drift and sd/√N of risk — this preserves annual Sharpe by
construction, which is the point). 200,000 simulated years per cell.

| trades per year | P(losing year) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 27.29% |
| 4 | 27.45% |
| 12 | 27.54% |
| 52 | 27.52% |
| 252 | 27.26% |
| 1,000 | 27.68% |

Spread across a 1,000× range of frequency: **0.42 percentage points**, i.e. simulation noise
around the theoretical Φ(−0.60) = **27.43%**.

**Trading 1,000 times a year instead of once gives exactly the same chance of a losing year.**

## ARM 1b — the same, with a per-trade cost → **P2 CONFIRMED**

P(losing year):

| trades/year | 0 bp | 1 bp | 5 bp | 20 bp | 50 bp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27.4% | 27.3% | 27.5% | 27.8% | 28.5% |
| 12 | 27.3% | 27.8% | 28.9% | 33.1% | 42.4% |
| 52 | 27.5% | 28.7% | 33.5% | 53.6% | 87.0% |
| 252 | 27.5% | 33.2% | **59.6%** | **99.7%** | 100% |
| 1,000 | 27.3% | 52.5% | **99.7%** | 100% | 100% |

At 5 bp a trade, a 252-times-a-year strategy with a genuinely good annual Sharpe of 0.60 loses
money in **60% of years**. At 1,000 trades, essentially always.

## ARM 1c — the only case where frequency helps → **P3 CONFIRMED** (after a bug fix, below)

Here each trade carries its OWN fresh edge: the per-trade SHARPE is held constant (0.05 — a tiny
edge), so annual Sharpe grows as 0.05·√N. **This is the Medallion case.**

| trades/year | gross edge per trade | gross annual SR | P(lose) | net SR @1 bp | P(lose) | net SR @5 bp | P(lose) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 75.00 bp | 0.05 | 47.8% | 0.05 | 48.1% | 0.05 | 48.3% |
| 12 | 21.65 bp | 0.17 | 43.3% | 0.17 | 43.5% | 0.13 | 44.6% |
| 52 | 10.40 bp | 0.36 | 36.0% | 0.33 | 37.5% | 0.19 | 42.7% |
| 252 | **4.72 bp** | 0.79 | **21.3%** | 0.63 | 26.6% | **−0.05** | **52.0%** |
| 1,000 | **2.37 bp** | **1.58** | **5.7%** | 0.91 | 18.1% | **−1.75** | **96.1%** |

**This one table contains the whole answer.**

Frequency helps enormously — P(losing year) falls from 47.8% to 5.7% — **but only because the
edge renews.** And renewal comes with a price written into the same row: **the gross edge per
trade shrinks as 1/√N**, from 75 bp to 2.37 bp. So the fast strategy's edge marches straight down
toward the cost line.

- At **1 bp** cost, 252 trades a year still works (net SR 0.63).
- At **5 bp**, the same strategy is **negative** (net SR −0.05).
- Our measured cost for the relevant universe is **46–62 bp one-way** (SEC/Collver). At that level
  even **12 trades a year** is under water: the edge per trade is 21.65 bp against a 46 bp cost.

**Medallion can trade 100,000 times a day because their cost per trade is near the 1 bp column.
We are three columns to the right of that, and no amount of frequency fixes it.**

### The bug I found in my own simulation, and fixed mid-run

The first version of `arm1_fresh_edge` set `mu_t = s·vol/n` with `sd_t = vol/√n`, which gives an
annual Sharpe of `s` at **every** n — i.e. it silently re-ran Arm 1a. The symptom was visible in
the output: the "annual Sharpe" column printed 0.60 at every frequency, which is exactly what a
fresh-edge model must NOT do. A fresh edge per trade means the per-trade **Sharpe** is what stays
constant, so `mu_t = s·sd_t` and annual Sharpe = `s·√n`. Fixed, documented in the function, rerun.

## ARM 2 — the real 195 predictors → **P4 REFUTED**

Sealed windows, non-overlapping calendar years, descriptive only, no verdict.

| cadence | predictors | full years | losing years, GROSS | losing years, with the project's haircut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| monthly | 97 | 1,885 | **38.2%** | **48.0%** |
| quarterly | 7 | 167 | 34.7% | 38.9% |
| semi-annual | 2 | 45 | 42.0% | 42.0% |
| annual | 86 | 1,717 | **44.0%** | **44.7%** |
| 3-yearly | 1 | 21 | 52.4% | 52.4% |

**GROSS: monthly 38.2% vs annual 44.0% → monthly is better by 5.8 points. I predicted it would
not be. I was wrong.**

**WITH COST: monthly 48.0% vs annual 44.7% → monthly is WORSE by 3.3 points. The ranking inverts.**

### What the refutation does and does not mean

It does **not** rescue the claim that frequency reduces loss probability, because Arm 1a proves
frequency at a fixed edge changes nothing at all. So the monthly group's gross advantage must come
from those predictors having **better edges**, not from their being faster.

How much better? The 2026-09-13 holding-period cut measured median gross Sharpe 0.291 monthly
against 0.255 annual, which predicts Φ(−0.291)=38.6% vs Φ(−0.255)=39.9% — a **1.3 point** gap.
The measured gap is **5.8 points**. **So roughly a quarter of the difference is explained by the
Sharpe difference and the rest is not explained here** — candidates are distribution shape, or the
median being unrepresentative of the group. Flagged, not explained.

And the inversion is measured at the project's own **20/P bps** haircut, which the 2026-09-15 audit
found to be built on an unsourced constant that is **9–12× too low** for an equal-weighted
universe. At the sourced cost, the monthly group's disadvantage is far larger than 3.3 points.

## What to tell the owner, in one line each

1. **Trading more often does NOT reduce the chance of losing.** Proven: a 1,000× change in
   frequency moves it by 0.42 points.
2. **What reduces it is more INDEPENDENT bets — and frequency only creates those if the edge
   itself renews.** If you slice one annual edge into 250 pieces you have 1 bet, not 250.
3. **When the edge does renew, the edge per trade shrinks as 1/√N and runs into the cost.** That
   collision, not the frequency, is what decides everything.
4. **Medallion sits in the 1 bp column. We sit at 46–62 bp.** Same physics, opposite answer.
