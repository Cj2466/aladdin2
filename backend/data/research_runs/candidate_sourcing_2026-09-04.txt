THE BRIEF, AND WHY IT IS SHAPED THIS WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The effective-N measurement of 2026-09-04 (commit a521c6b) settled an argument
this project had been having on intuition: effective N is about 9 across 42
DSR>=0.5-qualifying specs in 7 families, clusters recover family lines from
correlations alone, and — the sharper finding — 4 of this project's screening
modules all screen the IDENTICAL seeded 200-ticker sample, so even their low
mutual correlations describe orthogonal signals inside ONE opportunity set, not
genuinely different opportunities. More variants buy almost nothing. The two
clear past wins on the axis that does pay are lazy_prices (a different INPUT:
filing text) and xc_btcbeta (a different ASSET CLASS: crypto).

So the search below was run on two axes only: a genuinely different signal type,
or a genuinely different universe. Everything else was out of scope by design.

HOW EACH CANDIDATE WAS CHECKED, so none of this rests on recall
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

tested by a live call made tonight, not assumed. Where a check killed a

#1 — COUNTRY EQUITY INDEX VALUE AND MOMENTUM.  RECOMMEND BUILDING.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOURCE. Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, "Value and Momentum Everywhere", Journal
of Finance 68(3), June 2013, 929-985. Fetched in full from
w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf and read.

(a) EVIDENCE — the strongest of anything considered tonight. Table I, Panel B,
country indices, 01/1978-07/2011, 18 developed markets, equal-weighted terciles,
annualized:

    momentum P3-P1     mean  8.7%   t = 4.14   stdev 11.9%   Sharpe 0.73
                       alpha 8.2%   t = 4.00
    value    P3-P1     mean  6.0%   t = 3.45   stdev  9.8%   Sharpe 0.61
                       alpha 5.9%   t = 3.45
    50/50 combination  Sharpe 1.16  t = 6.62
    correlation(value, momentum) = -0.34

    Momentum's Sharpe of 0.73 is the highest single-signal Sharpe of any
    non-stock asset class in the paper's Panel B; currencies, the next
    closest comparison, manage 0.34. 33.5 years, t above 4, and the effect is
    independently established for this exact asset class by Asness, Liew &
    Stevens (1997), which AMP cite.

    A NUMERICAL COINCIDENCE IS FLAGGED HERE SO NOBODY LATER TREATS IT AS A
    FINDING: AMP's currency Sharpe of 0.34 and this project's FX family's best
    DSR of 0.3397 are nearly identical numbers measuring completely different
    things. The FX family's best SHARPE was +0.158. Do not compare the two.

(b) FEASIBILITY — verified live tonight, and it is the best in this project.
A yfinance pull of iShares MSCI single-country ETFs for AMP's 18 markets
returned continuous daily history from 1996-03-18 (the WEBS/iShares launch) for
FOURTEEN of them — EWA EWO EWK EWC EWQ EWG EWH EWI EWJ EWN EWP EWD EWL EWU — plus
SPY for the US from 1993-01-29. That is 30.5 years of free daily data on 15 of
the paper's 18 markets; the other three are accounted for immediately below.

    Three do not make it, and each is disclosed rather than quietly dropped:
      * Norway (ENOR) starts 2012-01-24 and Denmark (EDEN) 2012-01-26 — too
        short to share the common panel.
      * Portugal (PGAL) ran 2013-11-12 to 2024-03-05 AND THEN LIQUIDATED.
        Global X announced the liquidation on 2024-01-19, last trading day
        2024-02-16, liquidated on or about 2024-02-23, one of 19 Global X
        funds closed in that batch. (yfinance carries prints to 2024-03-05,
        slightly past the stated last trading day — a small data-quality
        note, not a contradiction of the closure.)

    THE PGAL CLOSURE IS THE MOST USEFUL THING THIS FEASIBILITY CHECK FOUND. The
    bonds and commodities families both use fixed_universe_membership and both
    disclose, in the abstract, a residual hindsight bias: "an ETF that had
    closed before today would never have made this hand-written list." PGAL is
    that abstraction happening, in this exact universe, 19 months ago. The
    country-index family must carry the disclosure as a MEASURED fact.

    Statistical power is the decisive advantage. A common formation start set
    by the slowest signal's lookback (the 5-year value proxy, so ~2001-09)
    through 2026-09 gives ~25 years. At the paper's own monthly cadence that is
    roughly 300 non-overlapping formations; even at h126 it is ~50. Every
    equity family in this project runs on 24-47 formations, and the buyback
    family's self-critique named exactly this as its binding constraint: "9-18
    of them is not a sample from which a Sharpe of 0.4 can be distinguished
    from 0." This universe is the first one that escapes that.

    Legs are clean too: terciles over 15 names give max(1, int(15/3)) = 5 per
    leg, disjoint at 10 <= 15, which clears DEFAULT_MIN_NAMES_PER_LEG = 5
    without the waiver that FX and commodities both had to take.

(c) INDEPENDENCE — good, with one honest qualification. The universe has zero
overlap with the seeded 200-ticker S&P 500 sample that 4 screening modules
share, zero with crypto, zero with the 8-ETF bond basket and the 11-commodity
basket. It is a genuinely new opportunity set, which is the axis a521c6b said to
prioritize. The qualification: US-listed country ETFs are UNHEDGED, so their USD
returns are local equity return plus currency, and this project's FX family
already ranks currencies. The overlap is real and must be measured, not assumed
away — see the mandatory confound check in the pre-registration.

THE CASE AGAINST, stated before any build rather than after a bad result:
  * McLean & Pontiff's 58% post-publication decay is the single biggest
    concern and it is quantified: 13 years post-publication on a Sharpe of
    0.73 implies an expected ~0.31, which is BELOW this project's own 0.50
    screening floor before a single line is written. This is a reason to
    expect a negative, and it should be written into the pre-registration as
    the honest prior, not discovered afterwards.
  * The signal is price momentum, which this project has already run and
    rejected on five universes (Round C large-cap, small-cap S&P 600,
    commodity ETFs, G10 FX, crypto). A sixth is not a fresh hypothesis class.
    What is fresh is the universe, and the fact that this is the one universe
    where the literature's own numbers are strong.
  * The value leg is NOT paper-faithful and must never be presented as if it
    were. AMP use the MSCI country index's BE/ME, which is a paid dataset. The
    substitute is their own long-horizon-reversal measure — the negative of
    the 5-year return — which they apply to commodities and currencies and
    justify by citing Fama-French (1996) on its high correlation with BE/ME
    portfolios. Using it for country indices is a DEVIATION from what they did
    for THIS asset class. Given that mechanism fidelity has now killed
    asset_growth, residual_momentum, best_ideas_13f and si_dtc in this
    project, the deviation is labelled loudly in advance and the value leg
    carries a lower prior than the momentum leg for exactly that reason.
  * Country ETF spreads are wider than SPY's, especially EWO/EWK/EWI, and the
    short leg's borrow is assumed at zero per standing convention. Both are
    cost channels that killed earlier families here.

STATUS: a pre-registration was written tonight, BEFORE any result exists —
data/research_runs/country_index_value_momentum_PREREGISTRATION.txt. NOTHING WAS
RUN. No panel was built, no Sharpe computed, no DSR computed.

#2 — OPTIONS-IMPLIED VOLATILITY SPREAD (DEVIATIONS FROM PUT-CALL PARITY).
     DEFER. The best long-run target here, and it is not buildable yet.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOURCE. Cremers & Weinbaum, "Deviations from Put-Call Parity and Stock Return
Predictability", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 45(2), 2010,
335-367. Headline: stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform stocks with
relatively expensive puts by 50 basis points per week, measured as the
difference in implied volatility between matched call/put pairs. Corroborated by
a separate strand (Xing, Zhang & Zhao, JFQA 2010, the volatility smirk).

(a) EVIDENCE. Strong and from a different market entirely. 50bp/week is large,
the sample is 1996-2005, and the authors report the predictability is larger
when option liquidity is high and stock liquidity low.

(b) FEASIBILITY — this is where it stands or falls, and tonight's live checks
moved it from "assumed impossible" to "not yet, and here is the exact date":

    * Options data IS reachable on the account this project already has.
      A read-only GET to data.alpaca.markets/v1beta1/options/snapshots/AAPL
      returned live chains with quotes and daily bars. This had never been
      checked before tonight and the standing assumption that options data is
      simply unavailable for free was WRONG.
    * BUT the history is ~1.6 years. Probing per-contract daily bars, the
      earliest bar for SPY241220C00500000 and SPY251219C00500000 — both LEAPS
      that certainly traded well before 2024 — is 2024-01-18 and 2024-01-19
      respectively, and AAPL270115C00200000 begins at its own 2024-09-16
      listing. The floor is the data vendor's, not the contracts'.
    * No implied volatility and no greeks are returned at this tier: a
      snapshot's keys are exactly dailyBar / latestQuote / latestTrade /
      minuteBar / prevDailyBar. Cremers-Weinbaum's measure IS an implied-vol
      difference, so IV would have to be computed from price, and doing it
      correctly means American exercise with discrete dividends — which is
      what OptionMetrics does for the paper and is not a detail that can be
      skipped for a put-call-parity measure specifically.
    * The historical chain cannot be enumerated read-only. GET
      api.alpaca.markets/v2/options/contracts returned {"code":40110000,
      "message":"request is not authorized"} — that endpoint lives on the
      TRADING host. It should stay unauthorized: this project's Alpaca client
      deliberately talks to data.alpaca.markets only and contains no order
      path, and that guardrail is worth more than the convenience. Chains
      would have to be reconstructed by enumerating OCC symbols.

(c) INDEPENDENCE — the best available, by a distance. Options-market
information is orthogonal by construction to prices, fundamentals, filing text,
13F positioning and short interest. If any single addition would raise this
book's effective N materially, it is this one.

VERDICT: DEFER, with a date. ~1.6 years supports about 85 non-overlapping weekly
formations, and a weekly-rebalanced long-short at option-market spreads is
exactly the cost profile this project's edge-cost work has killed repeatedly. A
five-year panel arrives around 2029. NOTHING NEEDS TO BE BUILT OR COLLECTED
TODAY TO PRESERVE THIS — unlike futures curves, the vendor retains the history,
so this is a wait, not a race. Recheck the available window annually.

#3 — INNOVATIVE EFFICIENCY (PATENTS PER R&D DOLLAR).  DECLINE, on power.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOURCE. Hirshleifer, Hsu & Li, "Innovative efficiency and stock returns",
Journal of Financial Economics 107(3), 2013, 632-654. Fetched in full and read.

Construction, quoted: Patents/RDC is "the ratio of firm i's patents granted in
year t scaled by its R&D capital [the 5-year cumulative R&D expenses assuming an
annual depreciation rate of 20%] in fiscal year ending in year t-2", i.e.
R&D(t-2) + 0.8*R&D(t-3) + 0.6*R&D(t-4) + 0.4*R&D(t-5) + 0.2*R&D(t-6). Portfolios
are formed "at the end of June of year t from 1982 to 2007" on IE in year t-1
and held July of year t to June of year t+1.

(a) EVIDENCE — weaker than its journal suggests, once the long-short is
isolated. The monthly Carhart alphas of the low / middle / high Patents/RDC
portfolios are 19, 22 and 27 basis points, so the high-minus-low alpha is about
8bp per month. The EMI1 factor's average monthly return is 0.41% at an ex post
monthly Sharpe of 0.23, and the paper itself flags that "ex post Sharpe ratio
estimates are upward biased" and reports it falling to 0.21 when the year 2000
is dropped.

(b) FEASIBILITY — the killer, and it is arithmetic. Formation is strictly
annual. The denominator needs R&D expense for fiscal years t-2 through t-6, and
this project's free R&D source is EDGAR XBRL, whose S&P 500 coverage only
becomes usable around 2009-2011 as the mandate phased in; add the five-year
R&D-capital window and the two-year application-grant lag and the first sound
formation lands around 2016. That is roughly TEN annual formations to 2026.
Ten non-overlapping bets cannot separate an 8bp/month long-short from zero, and
this project has already written that lesson down twice. Separately, matching
USPTO/PatentsView assignees to CIKs is an unsolved, error-prone crosswalk, and
the XOM successor-shell CIK bug is a standing reminder of what identity-matching
bugs cost here.

(c) INDEPENDENCE — genuinely good; patents are a data type nothing here uses.
Not enough to rescue ten formations.

#4 — DAILY SHORT-SALE VOLUME.  DECLINE. Killed by the authors' own cost table
     and, decisively, by a measure the free data cannot construct.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOURCE. Diether, Lee & Werner, "Short-Sale Strategies and Return
Predictability", Review of Financial Studies 22(2), 2009, 575-607; the November
2005 working paper version was fetched in full and read.

DATA IS EXCELLENT AND FREE, and was verified live tonight: FINRA's consolidated
daily short-volume files at cdn.finra.org/equity/regsho/daily/CNMSshvol<date>.txt
return HTTP 200 with per-symbol Date|Symbol|ShortVolume|ShortExemptVolume|
TotalVolume|Market rows. Probing the archive boundary, 2018-08-01 returns 200 and
2018-07-30 returns 403 (403 is simply "no file" — 2018-09-03, Labor Day, also
403), so the archive runs from about 2018-08-01 to the present, roughly eight
years of daily data on every US symbol. That is a genuinely valuable free
dataset and this project should know it exists.

IT STILL FAILS, on two independent grounds, both from the paper itself.

  1. COST. The strategy is a daily-rebalanced quintile sort on relative short
     volume. Gross: "a statistically significant daily average return of 0.128
     percent (2.6 percent per month)", rising to 2.9%/month at a five-day hold.
     Then the authors price it: assuming only a quarter of the portfolio turns
     over daily, "Our estimate of the effective spread is 62 basis points. Thus
     execution costs for the Low-High portfolio would be roughly 6.2 percent
     per month (not including commissions)", plus roughly 32.6bp/month of
     borrow. Costs exceed gross by more than two to one. The working paper's
     abstract states the conclusion outright: the strategy "generates
     significant returns, but incurs costs large enough to wipe out any
     profits."

  2. MECHANISM FIDELITY — the decisive one, and it is the si_dtc shape found
     before building anything. The paper decomposes by trade size. Small short
     sales carry the result (0.149%/day, monotone across quintiles). Panel C:
     "The long-short strategy based on large short-sales generates returns that
     are insignificantly different from zero both for the one-day and the
     five-day holding periods." All the predictability is in small trades. The
     free FINRA file publishes ONE aggregate ShortVolume column with no
     trade-size breakdown, so the only measure it can build is the aggregate
     one — the version the paper shows does not work. A separate, well
     documented problem points the same way: a large share of published short
     volume is market-maker hedging rather than directional shorting, so the
     aggregate column is contaminated in precisely the direction that dilutes
     the informed small-trade signal.

INDEPENDENCE would also have been only moderate: same universe and the same
economic theme as the already-registered si_ratio_hedged_h21, a flow measure
beside a stock measure.

#5 — CRYPTOCURRENCY SIZE FACTOR.  DECLINE. The effect lives in a universe this
     project excludes on purpose.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOURCE. Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", Journal of
Finance 77(2), 2022, 1133-1177; NBER w25882 fetched in full and read.

(a) EVIDENCE. Top journal, and the number is enormous: a weekly-rebalanced
quintile sort on log market capitalization, long smallest and short largest,
earns about 3.4% PER WEEK over 2014-2018.

(b) AND THAT IS THE PROBLEM. Their universe is 1,707 coins filtered only at a
market capitalization above $1,000,000, and its median daily dollar volume is
$103,890. This project's crypto family gates eligibility at a trailing 90-day
MEDIAN DOLLAR VOLUME OF $25,000,000, which is what leaves it 28-71 tradeable
coins. The paper's median coin is about three orders of magnitude below this
project's liquidity floor. The size effect is a microcap-tail effect, and
running it on the liquid segment would not be a replication — it would test a
claim the paper never makes. The authors also concede the shorting problem
directly: "a natural criticism of our findings is that short selling is either
not possible or limited for most of the coins."

(c) INDEPENDENCE would have been mediocre anyway: it reuses the crypto universe
xc_btcbeta already occupies, and it would enter the same family's
multiple-comparisons denominator, where the expected best of 28 zero-edge trials
(1.058) already exceeded the best Sharpe actually observed (+0.944).

One finding from this paper IS worth keeping, though it is not actionable here:
LTW report momentum works BETTER among larger coins (4.2%/week above the median
size versus an insignificant 0.6% below it), which is the opposite of the size
result's direction and is the segment this project can actually trade. This
project's crypto family already tested six momentum specs on its liquid universe
and returned an honest negative, so this is a corroborating note, not a new
candidate.

CANDIDATES CONSIDERED AND DROPPED BEFORE RANKING
================================================================================
COMMODITY FUTURES CURVE CARRY (Koijen/Moskowitz/Pedersen/Vrugt-style basis).
Dropped on a constraint this repository had already measured and written down:
futures_curve_collector.py exists precisely because free sources cannot
reconstruct curve history — Yahoo's continuous futures tickers are a naive
front-month splice whose chained returns fabricate the roll yield they would be
needed to measure (+28.4%/yr on NG=F against the investable proxy), and expired
contracts are purged from the feed. The collector is accumulating forward. There
is nothing to backtest and nothing new to decide.

GOOGLE TRENDS / SEARCH ATTENTION (Da, Engelberg & Gao, JF 2011). Dropped without
a full fetch, and the reason is recorded honestly: the data is sampled and
revised by the vendor, so a series pulled today is not the series that existed at
the formation date. This project has just spent an entire night fixing exactly
that class of bug — yfinance retroactively restating adjusted prices between
fetches (commits 24f0974 and 77e77d7..61bd307) — and deliberately adopting a
second, worse instance of it would be perverse.
