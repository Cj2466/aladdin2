THE SIX PRE-DECLARED FIDELITY CHECKS, ANSWERED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  LOSS/GAIN ASYMMETRY — PASSES, cleanly, at [PW01]'s own window.
    At h=6 on the S&P 500 the truncated LOSS construction beats the signed one on every window:
    full_year +0.4965 vs +0.2922, jul_dec +0.4965 vs +0.3310, jan_jun +0.2417 vs +0.0049. The edge
    lives on the loser side and adding a short-the-winners leg subtracts from it, which is exactly
    what [PW01] Table III's near-zero GAIN coefficients predict and what a symmetric reversal story
    does not. This is the check the pre-registration said would decide whether the family had
    measured reversal wearing a calendar. It answered in the mechanism's favour.

F2  CALENDAR PLACEBO — SPLIT, AND THE ADVERSE HALF IS THE ONE THAT DECIDES THE VERDICT.
    At h=6 the placebo behaves as a placebo should: the June-anchored lossonly cell earns -0.15% a
    year (t = -0.22, 6/12 positive) against the December-anchored one's +1.59% (t = +3.02, 9/11
    positive) on the identical ranking variable. At h=21 it inverts completely — the June placebo
    is the single best-scoring cell in the entire 32-cell build, in BOTH universes.
    The post-hoc check in tax_loss_selling_verification_2026-09-06.txt P3 is what that best cell
    actually is: drop its one largest event (2026-06-29, +41.85%) and its mean falls from +5.92% to
    +2.65% and its t from 1.57 to 1.30. That event is a long book of 2026 H1 software/services
    losers against a short book of memory and semiconductor winners, held through July 2026, in
    which the long leg gained 11.7% and the short leg lost 16.6%. It is real data and it is one
    month of one sector reversal. It is not a finding, and a family that reported its best DSR cell
    without saying so would be reporting an artifact.

F3  DOSE-RESPONSE MONOTONICITY — PASSES, and this is the strongest mechanism evidence in the build.
    [PW01]'s equation (2) is linear in LOSS, so the tax story predicts a monotone gradient, not a
    top-decile jump. S&P 500, averaged over 11 anchors, excess 6-day return by loss bucket:
        -0.64  -0.60  -0.27  -0.16  -0.48  -0.10  +0.23  +0.30  +0.45  +1.30   (%)
    Spearman(bucket rank, mean excess) = 0.9636. S&P 600 gives the same shape at 0.9152, with the
    extreme-loss bucket at +1.39%. Both universes show a graded relationship between how much
    tax benefit there is to realize and how much the stock recovers, in the right direction, over
    the exact window [PW01] measures. Nothing in the construction forces this: the buckets are the
    whole eligible cross-section, and the middle buckets could have been anywhere.

F4  SIZE STRATIFICATION — FAILS, AND IT FAILS BACKWARDS. This is the single most important negative
    finding in the build and it is the one that most directly contradicts the source literature.
    [PW01] Table V's largest size decile shows no significant effect in any of its three tax
    regimes, and [SK14]'s abstract calls the turn-of-the-year effect a small-capitalization
    phenomenon. The prediction is therefore small-cap STRONGER than large-cap. Measured:
        tls_dec_full_year_lossonly_h6   S&P 500  Sharpe +0.4965  ann.mean +1.59%  t 3.02  hit 9/11
                                        S&P 600  Sharpe +0.2853  ann.mean +1.23%  t 1.01  hit 4/6
        tls_dec_jul_dec_lossonly_h6     S&P 500  Sharpe +0.4965  ann.mean +1.54%  t 3.59  hit 8/11
                                        S&P 600  Sharpe +0.2283  ann.mean +0.98%  t 0.75  hit 3/6
    The effect is WEAKER in small caps on every December lossonly cell.
    THIS IS NOT A COST ARTIFACT, and that was checked rather than assumed (verification P4): the two
    universes were pre-declared with different turnover models, so the S&P 500 grid was re-run at
    the SMALL-CAP 15bp flat cost. The large-cap headline falls from +0.4965 to +0.3526 and still
    beats the small-cap +0.2853 on a like-for-like charge. The size ordering survives cost-matching.
    NOR IS IT PURELY A PERIOD ARTIFACT, though period-matching does shrink it a lot and that is
    stated rather than buried. The two universes span different windows — the S&P 600's
    point-in-time membership only starts 2020-01-01 — so the raw comparison is partly
    2015-2019-versus-2020-2025. Restricting the S&P 500 to the S&P 600's own six anchors
    (verification P5):
        tls_dec_full_year_lossonly_h6   S&P 500 matched 6: +1.384% (t 1.93)   S&P 600: +1.225% (t 1.02)
        tls_dec_jul_dec_lossonly_h6     S&P 500 matched 6: +1.364% (t 2.33)   S&P 600: +0.982% (t 0.75)

    0.38 percentage points rather than the 0.36 and 0.55 the unmatched comparison showed. So roughly
    half of the apparent size gap in the headline table is the DIFFERENT WINDOW, not size. What does
    not change under any of these cuts is the thing that matters: small caps are never STRONGER.
    Two further honest qualifications, neither of which rescues the prediction: six events is a
    near-uninformative sample in either direction; and the S&P 600 is not the micro-cap tail where
    [RK76]-era January effects were largest, so this measures "small" relative to "large", not
    "small" in the literature's sense. But the direction is wrong, and a mechanism whose own papers
    locate it in small caps producing its cleanest signal in the S&P 500 is evidence against the
    mechanism reading, not for it.

F5  REGIME-III WITHIN-YEAR SPLIT — FAILS. [PW01] p.360, verbatim: "when the long-term holding
    period is 12 months in Regime III, we would expect b3 = b4 < 0." Current US law IS Regime III
    (verified against IRS Topic 409 as reviewed 25-Feb-2026), so July-December and January-June
    losses should matter about equally today. Measured on the S&P 500 at h=6 lossonly:
        jul_dec  Sharpe +0.4965, ann.mean +1.54%, t 3.59
        jan_jun  Sharpe +0.2417, ann.mean +0.59%, t 0.98
    Recent losses matter roughly two and a half times as much as first-half losses. That is [PW01]'s
    REGIME II pattern under REGIME III law. It is what a short-horizon reversal story predicts and
    not what the tax story predicts under today's holding-period rules. On eleven observations the
    two are not statistically separable — but the point estimate points the wrong way, and it is
    recorded as a failed check rather than as noise.

F6  NO NATURAL EXPERIMENT — CONFIRMED UNAVAILABLE, as pre-declared. [PW01]'s identification is the
    1969 and 1976 Tax Reform Acts, decades before any daily price history reachable here. The two
    modern candidates named in this family's brief (JGTRRA 2003, ATRA 2013) both predate
    MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START. Within 2015-2026 none of the three parameters [PW01] identifies off
    changed: TCJA 2017 left the holding period, the long-term loss deduction fraction and the loss
    limit alone (its mandatory-FIFO securities provision was dropped from the final bill), and IRS
    Topic 409 still states the more-than-one-year rule and the $3,000/$1,500 limit with
    carryforward. So the part of [PW01] that establishes the effect is TAX-driven rather than merely
    seasonal cannot be run here, and none was manufactured. Everything above is consistent with the
    tax story; none of it identifies it.

THE POST-HOC BETA DECOMPOSITION, AND WHY IT IS REPORTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT pre-declared. Added because the largest year-to-date losers are systematically high-beta names
and the turn of the year is on average an up window, which would produce a positive loser-vs-universe
spread with no tax content at all. It can only weaken the candidate, which is why adding it after
seeing a positive number is honest rather than the cherry-picking the pre-registration prevents.

Regressing the eleven equal-weighted loser-leg excess returns on the eligible universe's own 6-day
return (verification P2):
    excess = +0.01027 + 0.3812 * universe_6d ;  R2 0.443 ;  corr 0.666
The loser leg carries about 0.38 of extra market beta. Roughly a fifth of the raw +1.29%
equal-weighted excess (+0.26%) is compensation for that beta in a window that averaged +0.69% for
the universe. The residual alpha is +1.03% with t = 2.57 on eleven observations. So beta explains
44% of the VARIANCE and about 20% of the MEAN; it does not explain the effect away, but any reader
of the +1.59% headline should know that a meaningful slice of it is a directional market bet that a
dollar-neutral book was not supposed to be taking.

Leave-one-year-out on the same spec (verification P1): the t-statistic ranges 2.58 to 3.69 across
all eleven single-year deletions. The result is not one year's artifact. That is a real robustness
property and it is stated here beside the reasons it still does not clear the bar.

WHAT THIS BUILD ACTUALLY FOUND, STATED WITHOUT STRETCHING IT IN EITHER DIRECTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The honest summary is neither "nothing here" nor "an edge". It is this:

A December-anchored, loss-truncated, universe-hedged decile held over exactly Poterba and
Weisbenner's six-day turn-of-the-year window earned +1.59% a year on the S&P 500 over eleven years,
positive in nine of them, with a monotone dose-response across loss buckets (Spearman 0.96), a clean
loss-versus-gain asymmetry, and a matched calendar placebo that earned nothing at the same window.
Those four things together are the most mechanism-consistent result any family produced in this
session, and they were all pre-declared as the checks that would have to pass.

It is nevertheless a DEFINITE_NEGATIVE, and the reasons are not close calls:
  * DSR 0.5109 at the MOST LENIENT denominator, against a 0.95 bar. It does not clear the informal
    0.50 screening floor by enough to mean anything either, and it is 0.19 at the pooled N.
  * preservation_score 0.0685. The worse half of the sample does not carry the edge.
  * ELEVEN OBSERVATIONS. This was declared in advance as the binding constraint and it is. A
    once-a-year strategy cannot be distinguished from luck on eleven draws by any amount of
    daily-return arithmetic, and the ~2,700-day series is 77 nonzero days with excess kurtosis 209.
  * THE SIZE PREDICTION FAILED BACKWARDS (F4) and the regime-III split failed (F5). Two of the four
    substantive predictions the source literature makes came out wrong.
  * ~20% of the raw excess is market beta, not flow.
  * The best-DSR cell in the whole build is a placebo carried by one month.
  * The trade is 100% concentrated in the most crowded, most publicized calendar window in US
    equities, priced here at a 2bp median half-spread with NO market impact and NO window-specific
    spread widening modelled. That is an unmeasured optimism pointing the wrong way, and this family
    has no free data with which to measure it.

The one thing that would change this verdict is more turn-of-year events, which is a property of the
calendar and cannot be bought, borrowed or engineered. Fifty more years of the effect at this
strength would still be the same +1.59%; it would simply be measurable. That is a genuinely
different reason for a negative than this session's other three flow candidates had — TSMOM was
blocked on paid data, N-PORT flow was measured and empty at DSR 0.341, options-dealer gamma had no
free historical archive at all — and it is worth recording as such rather than collapsing into
"another negative".

SCOPE OF THE NEGATIVE, so it is not over-read: 11 turn-of-year events on the S&P 500 and 6 on the
S&P 600, against [PW01]'s 34 years of NYSE/AMEX including the small and micro caps where the effect
is reported to live. 143 of 768 S&P 500 point-in-time tickers and 218 of 1,088 S&P 600 tickers
resolved no price data at all (this project's standing delisted-securities gap). This is evidence
that the turn-of-the-year tax-loss effect is not harvestable at DSR-clearing confidence in S&P 500
and S&P 600 constituents over 2015-2026 as implemented here. It is NOT a refutation of Poterba &
Weisbenner (2001).
