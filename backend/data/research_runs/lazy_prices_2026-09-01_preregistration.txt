LAZY PRICES — FILING-LANGUAGE CHANGE AS A CROSS-SECTIONAL SIGNAL — PRE-REGISTRATION
Written 2026-09-01, BEFORE any screening run, before any Sharpe, DSR or portfolio
number existed, and before the family module was written. The TEXT PIPELINE was
built and validated against real filings first (section 3 records exactly what that
found, including two defects it caught); the SPEC GRID, the direction, the universe
and the pass/fail threshold below are frozen as of this document and may not be
revised after results are seen. The run report (lazy_prices_2026-09-01.txt) is the
AFTER document; this is the BEFORE document, and the two are meant to be diffed.

============================================================================
0. WHAT IS BEING TESTED
============================================================================
Does the SIMILARITY OF A FIRM'S 10-K TO ITS OWN IMMEDIATELY-PRIOR 10-K predict
that firm's future stock returns, cross-sectionally, among S&P 500 members?

  H_LAZY  Firms whose annual-report LANGUAGE changed materially versus their
          own previous annual report ("changers", LOW similarity) underperform
          firms whose language stayed largely unchanged ("non-changers", HIGH
          similarity). A portfolio long non-changers and short changers earns
          a positive net Sharpe.

H_LAZY is the paper's own direction and is the ONLY direction screened. The
opposite sign is NOT declared as a sibling hypothesis, deliberately and unlike
jump_drift_2026-08-30, which declared both: there, the literature genuinely
pointed both ways at different horizons. Here it does not — Cohen, Malloy &
Nguyen report one sign, and pre-declaring the reverse would be inventing a
hypothesis nobody holds purely to keep a post-hoc sign flip available. If the
measured result is negative, that is a refutation of H_LAZY and will be
reported as one; it will NOT be re-presented as confirmation of a
"reversal" hypothesis this document declined to declare.

This is the FIRST text/NLP-based family in this codebase. Every other family
ranks on a numeric series (a return, a ratio, a rate). Sections 2 and 3 exist
because that novelty concentrates the risk in the text pipeline rather than in
the portfolio machinery, which is reused unchanged.

============================================================================
1. THE SOURCE — INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED, AND ONE HEADLINE FIGURE CORRECTED
============================================================================
 [CMN20] Lauren Cohen, Christopher Malloy & Quoc Nguyen, "Lazy Prices",
         THE JOURNAL OF FINANCE, Vol. 75, No. 3 (2020), pp. 1371-1415,
         doi:10.1111/jofi.12885. Also NBER Working Paper 25084 (September
         2018, revised March 2019).

VERIFICATION PROVENANCE. The build brief for this family quoted a headline of
"up to 188 basis points per month (~22%/year)" concentrated in "MD&A, the
executive-team section, and litigation/risk-factor language". That claim was
NOT taken on trust. The NBER working paper PDF was fetched from nber.org and
text-extracted IN FULL this session, and every quote below is from that
extracted document. Two corrections came out of it, and both change what this
family should expect and how it should be described:

 CORRECTION 1 — 188bp IS NOT THE BASE CASE. It is real, but it is an "up to",
 EQUALLY-WEIGHTED, RISK-FACTORS-SECTION-ONLY figure. Verbatim from the
 abstract: "a portfolio that shorts 'changers' and buys 'non-changers' earns
 up to 188 basis points in monthly alphas (over 22% per year) in the future".
 The paper's own stated headline, verbatim from the introduction: "Our key
 finding is that a portfolio that goes long 'non-changers' and short
 'changers' earns a statistically significant 34-58 basis points per month —
 up to 7% per year (t=3.59) - in value-weighted abnormal returns over the
 following year." THE BASE CASE IS 34-58bp/month VALUE-WEIGHTED, ~7%/yr — not
 22%/yr. Quoting 22%/yr as this strategy's expected return would misrepresent
 the source, and this document will not do it.

 CORRECTION 2 — MD&A IS WHERE CHANGES CONCENTRATE, NOT WHERE THE RETURNS ARE.
 The brief grouped MD&A with risk factors and litigation as equally
 return-informative. The paper explicitly separates the two, verbatim: "We
 show that firms' reporting changes are concentrated in the management
 discussion (MD&A) section... However, in terms of return-rich content, we
 find that while changes in MD&A section wording do predict large and
 significant abnormal returns, changes in text in the Risk Factors section are
 even more informative for stock returns." And on magnitude: MD&A's effect
 "ranging between 11-22 basis per month" is "often smaller than" Legal
 Proceedings, Item 7a, "and particularly the 'Risk Factors' section (Item
 1A)". So the PRE-REGISTERED EXPECTATION ACROSS SCOPES is risk_factors
 strongest, mda weakest, full document in between. That ordering is written
 down now so that whichever scope wins cannot be narrated afterwards as
 "the one the paper predicted".

 The authors' own hedge on the section table, verbatim, and it applies to this
 family's scope axis too: "we view these results as providing suggestive but
 not definitive evidence on where our return results are most pronounced."

VERIFIED AND RELIED ON:
 * Sample 1995-2014, "the complete history of regular quarterly and annual
   filings by U.S. corporations".
 * NO ANNOUNCEMENT EFFECT — the mechanism's own evidence. Verbatim: "we find
   an economically and statistically zero announcement day return... in the
   full sample", and "Their stock prices exhibit little to no reaction at the
   time of public filing by the firm, even though there is a robust and
   systematic relationship... with the information only being impounded into
   price in the future." Also verbatim: these returns "continue to accrue out
   to 18 months, and do not reverse." A caveat the paper adds and this document
   records rather than suppresses: a significant short-run announcement effect
   DOES appear for the subset of firms whose investors execute multi-year
   filing downloads.
 * SIMILARITY MEASURES: four — Sim_Cosine, Sim_Jaccard, Sim_MinEdit,
   Sim_Simple. This family implements the first two (section 4).

NOT VERIFIED, AND FLAGGED: the published Journal of Finance text itself was
never retrieved (Wiley returned HTTP 403). Bibliographic record (volume,
issue, pages, DOI) was confirmed independently at RePEc and the AFA issue
listing. Every substantive quote above is from the NBER working paper, which
may differ from the published version; the published abstract as rendered by
RePEc does differ from the working-paper abstract (it drops the sample period
and the accrual clause). Treat all quotes as NBER w25084, not as JF 75(3).

2. WHY THE HONEST PRIOR IS WEAK — STATED BEFORE ANY RESULT
============================================================================
 1. UNIVERSE MISMATCH, AND IT IS THE BIG ONE. [CMN20] runs on the COMPLETE
    universe of U.S. filers — thousands of firms, most of them small, thinly
    covered, and exactly the population an inattention anomaly should live in.
    This family runs on S&P 500 members: ~500 of the most intensively
    analyzed, most heavily arbitraged securities in existence, each covered by
    dozens of analysts who are paid to read exactly these documents. The
    paper's own mechanism is investor INATTENTION. Large caps are where
    inattention is scarcest.
 2. POST-PUBLICATION ATTENUATION. The paper's sample ends 2014; it circulated
    as an NBER working paper from 2018 and was published in the Journal of
    Finance in 2020. This family's sample is 2015-2026, i.e. almost entirely
    AFTER the paper existed and was widely read. Filing-diff products are now
    commercially available. A widely-documented pattern (not independently
    re-verified this session, and flagged as such) is that published anomalies
    attenuate after publication.
 3. ANNUAL-ONLY SIGNAL. [CMN20] uses 10-K AND 10-Q, so a firm's signal
    refreshes roughly quarterly. This family uses 10-K only (section 5), so a
    firm's signal refreshes ANNUALLY and is carried for up to a year. That is
    strictly less information than the paper had.
 4. WEIGHTING MISMATCH. The paper's 34-58bp headline is VALUE-weighted. This
    family weights equal and inverse-vol (section 6), because point-in-time
    market cap would require a separate share-count pipeline this family does
    not build. The comparison to the paper's headline is therefore inexact in
    a way that is disclosed, not corrected.

An honest negative is the expected outcome and is a complete, successful
result. Roughly 25 of ~28 families tried in this codebase have come back
honest negatives; this one has four named reasons to join them.

3. THE TEXT PIPELINE — BUILT AND VALIDATED ON REAL FILINGS FIRST
============================================================================
This section is written BEFORE the screening run but AFTER the text pipeline
was built and checked against live EDGAR, because a spec grid over a pipeline
that does not work would be worthless. Nothing here is a return or a Sharpe.
Everything here is a measured property of the pipeline, and TWO REAL DEFECTS
WERE FOUND AND FIXED THIS WAY.

3.1 DATA PATH. app/services/market_data/edgar_filing_text_provider.py is a
    genuinely new module. edgar_xbrl_provider.py was read in full first and
    CANNOT serve this: it reads structured XBRL numeric facts, and the one
    place it touches an archived document it deliberately reads only the first
    16KB SGML header via an HTTP Range request, never the body. The new module
    reuses that one's EdgarFetchError, build_edgar_user_agent (SEC's
    declared-bot UA convention and its SEC_EDGAR_CONTACT override), throttle
    interval and retry constants unchanged.

3.2 DEFECT FOUND #1 — INLINE-XBRL CONTAMINATION. Modern filings are Inline
    XBRL: the .htm carries an <ix:header>/<ix:hidden> machine dump of every
    tagged fact. Stripping tags without removing those blocks first yields
    text beginning (Apple FY2025 10-K, measured live):
      "aapl-20250927 false 2025 FY 0000320193 P1Y P1Y P1Y P1Y
       http://fasb.org/us-gaap/2025#LongTermDebtNoncurrent ..."
    — ~14,000 characters of duration codes and taxonomy URIs before any
    English. That block's content varies with how many facts the filer tagged
    that year, so it manufactures "language change" that has nothing to do
    with language. It is removed first, unconditionally.

3.3 DEFECT FOUND #2 — SECTION EXTRACTION THAT LOOKED RIGHT AND WAS WRONG.
    This is the one worth recording in detail, because size checks did not
    catch it and only a CONTENT check did. A first implementation matched
    heading words anywhere in the flattened text and took the longest
    plausible span. Measured live on 43 real 10-Ks it reported 89-100%
    "coverage" with reasonable-looking section sizes (24-30% of document).
    The content was wrong:
      * Apple's "risk_factors" began mid-sentence at '." The Company assumes
        no obligation to revise or update any forward-looking statements' —
        the forward-looking-statements disclaimer, reached via a
        CROSS-REFERENCE to risk factors rather than the Item 1A heading.
      * J&J's "mda" ENDED inside Item 5, at '...symbol JNJ. As of February 4,
        2026, there were 108,358 record holders of Common Stock' — spanning
        entirely the wrong region.
    An earlier, cruder version of the same rule additionally returned spans of
    87-98% of the whole document for GE and Exxon — the document wearing a
    section's name.
    FIX, structural rather than a looser pattern: html_to_text now preserves
    block-level structure as NEWLINES, and section headings must be LINE-
    ANCHORED. A real Item heading occupies its own block element and starts a
    line; a cross-reference sits mid-sentence. Two size guards remain as
    backstops (>= 1,000 chars, <= 50% of document).
    RE-MEASURED after the fix, over 42 real 10-Ks from 15 S&P 500 filers:
    every extracted section opens with genuinely correct prose ("An investment
    in the Company's common stock or debt securities involves risks and
    uncertainties"; "The following summarizes factors that could have a
    material adverse effect on the Company's business"), sizes are stable
    year-over-year for the same filer (Caterpillar risk factors 53,582 then
    53,565 characters), and doc-share medians are 14.1% (risk_factors) and
    16.3% (MD&A).
    COVERAGE FELL from a false 89-100% to a true 86% / 86%. THAT TRADE IS THE
    POINT and is pre-registered as acceptable: correct text at 86% is worth
    more than wrong text at 100%. GE's integrated report matches neither
    pattern and contributes no section observation at all. A filing whose
    section cannot be located yields NO section-scope observation for that
    firm-year — it is refused from ranking (NaN), never silently treated as
    unchanged. Realized coverage will be reported per scope in the run report.

3.4 THE SPOT-CHECK THE BRIEF DEMANDED — DOES A REAL EVENT MOVE THE NUMBER?
    Boeing, whose FY2019 10-K (filed 2020-01-31) covers the 737 MAX grounding
    and whose FY2020 10-K (filed 2021-02-01) covers COVID plus the continuing
    MAX crisis, against its own routine recovery year:
      BA FY2019 -> FY2020 (crisis):  MD&A cosine 0.9474, Jaccard 0.7240
      BA FY2020 -> FY2021 (routine): MD&A cosine 0.9809, Jaccard 0.8420
    The crisis pair scores markedly LOWER similarity than the same firm's
    following routine year, on both metrics. Across 20 consecutive real 10-K
    pairs from AAPL/KO/JNJ/BA/GE, whole-document Jaccard ranged 0.775-0.959.
    The pipeline detects real language change and does not fire on routine
    re-filing. THIS IS A SANITY CHECK, NOT EVIDENCE FOR H_LAZY: it shows the
    metric measures what it claims to measure, and says nothing whatever about
    whether that measurement predicts returns.

3.5 MEASURED, AND IT DECIDED A DESIGN CHOICE — COSINE NEEDS STOPWORDS GONE.
    On whole documents, raw term-frequency cosine over 20 real consecutive
    pairs ranged 0.9965-0.9998: a spread of 0.0033, dominated by function
    words identical in every filing. This is exactly the boilerplate-dilution
    failure the brief warned about, and it is real. Removing a standard
    English stopword list widens the spread ~11x, to 0.9676-0.9976. Jaccard,
    being set-based, was never affected (0.775-0.959 either way). Stopword
    removal is therefore part of the frozen tokenization (section 4), decided
    on measurement rather than assumed.
    RESIDUAL, DISCLOSED: even after stopword removal, whole-document cosine
    sits close to 1.0. The strategy ranks CROSS-SECTIONALLY, so the level does
    not matter and the ordering does — but a compressed level means the
    ordering is carried by a small numerical difference, and the run report
    must show the realized cross-sectional dispersion of every scope/metric
    combination so a reader can judge whether it is ranking on signal or on
    parse noise.

3.6 TRANSFER COST, MEASURED because it decided feasibility: mean 0.23s and
    0.256 MB ON THE WIRE per filing over 43 real 10-Ks, against 2.6 MB of raw
    HTML — a 10x saving from gzip, which SEC honors on Archives documents. A
    full-universe run is therefore ~1 hour and ~2.6 GB inside the throttle,
    not the tens of GB a naive estimate implied.

============================================================================
4. THE SIGNAL — FROZEN DEFINITION
============================================================================
TOKENIZATION (frozen): lowercase; tokens are runs of >= 2 ASCII LETTERS
(regex [a-z]{2,}); all digits and mixed alphanumerics dropped; a fixed English
stopword list removed. Dropping numbers is deliberate and matters: every
filing's figures change every year mechanically, and the hypothesis is about
LANGUAGE, not about arithmetic churn.

SIMILARITY between a filing and its predecessor, over term-count vectors of
those tokens:
    Sim_Cosine (a, b)  = <a, b> / (||a|| ||b||)          on raw term counts
    Sim_Jaccard(a, b)  = |A INTERSECT B| / |A UNION B|   on term SETS
Both are the paper's own named measures. Sim_MinEdit and Sim_Simple are NOT
implemented: minimum edit distance over ~30,000-token documents is
quadratic-time and would dominate the run, and Sim_Simple is defined in the
paper by running Microsoft Word's Track Changes or Unix diff, which is not a
reproducible library primitive. Their absence is a declared deviation.

TF-IDF IS DELIBERATELY NOT USED, and this is a point-in-time decision rather
than a stylistic one. An IDF vector fitted over the sample corpus would embed
information from EVERY document in the corpus — including filings from the
future of any given formation date — into that date's similarity score. That
is look-ahead by construction. Pairwise cosine on raw counts needs no corpus
and cannot leak. The build brief suggested TF-IDF; it is declined for this
reason, stated in advance.

PAIRING (frozen): a filing is compared ONLY to that same firm's immediately
prior filing OF THE SAME FORM TYPE, ordered by filing date. A 10-K is never
compared to a 10-Q. Amendments (10-K/A, 10-Q/A) are excluded entirely rather
than mapped onto their base form: an amendment is usually a partial re-filing
of one item or an exhibit, so treating it as a full annual report would
manufacture an enormous spurious language change for whichever firm filed one.
A firm's FIRST observed filing has no predecessor and yields no signal.

DIRECTION: signal = similarity, used as-is. The harness's convention is
top-of-ranking-is-long, so HIGH similarity (non-changers) goes LONG and LOW
similarity (changers) goes SHORT — [CMN20]'s own direction.

============================================================================
5. POINT-IN-TIME AVAILABILITY — THE SAFETY-CRITICAL RULE
============================================================================
A filing's similarity score may be used ONLY from the date the filing actually
became public, derived from the submissions API's acceptanceDateTime
(UTC -> US/Eastern via zoneinfo):
    accepted before 16:00 ET on day D   -> available on D
    accepted at/after 16:00 ET on day D -> available on D+1
    no/unparseable acceptance timestamp -> filing_date + 1 (conservative)
A tz-NAIVE timestamp is stamped UTC rather than passed to astimezone(), which
would silently assume the HOST machine's timezone — the exact latent bug an
adversarial pass found in cross_sectional_pead.announcement_day0 on
2026-08-28.

IT IS NEVER KEYED TO reportDate (the fiscal period end). MEASURED on 481 real
10-K filings from 20 S&P 500 companies: availability_date minus report_date
has median 53 days and maximum 107 days. Keying the signal to the period end
would let a backtest read, on the fiscal year-end date, language that did not
exist for another ~53 days. That is the single largest look-ahead hazard in
this family. availability_date() takes a FilingRef and has NO code path that
can reach report_date at all; a unit test pins this.
ALSO MEASURED: 244 of those 481 filings (51%) are accepted at or after 16:00
ET, so the after-close shift is material, not cosmetic.

STALENESS BOUND: 455 calendar days, reusing cross_sectional_quality.py's
FUNDAMENTAL_MAX_STALENESS_DAYS and its reasoning unchanged (one annual refresh
cycle plus the SEC's own 60-90 day 10-K filing window). A firm whose next 10-K
is later than that has stopped filing on schedule, and carrying a year-old
similarity as "current" would be a dead-series masquerade. Beyond the bound
the value goes NaN and the firm is refused from ranking.

The signal panel is built as a FORWARD-FILLED STEP FRAME on the price index —
visible from the availability date, never interpolated, never backfilled —
via the same construction cross_sectional_quality.build_point_in_time_factor_
frame uses, and rides CrossSectionalData.fundamental_signal. Formation-time
look-ahead is then structurally impossible: the harness truncates the history
view to rows <= the formation date before the signal function ever sees it.

FORMS: 10-K ONLY for this run. 10-Q is supported by the provider and the
pairing logic (and the same-type-only guard is unit-tested on mixed 10-K/10-Q
input, which is where that test has teeth), but is not screened here — adding
it would roughly quadruple the EDGAR document footprint, and 10-Q risk-factor
sections are typically a single "no material changes" cross-reference rather
than a comparable body of text. This is a declared deviation from [CMN20],
which uses both, and it is reason 3 in section 2's weak prior.

6. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 36 SPECS, FROZEN
============================================================================
Four dimensions, fully crossed. Frozen as of this document.

  D1  similarity metric   2: cosine, jaccard        ([CMN20]'s two implemented measures)
  D2  document scope      3: full, risk_factors, mda
  D3  holding period      3: 21, 63, 126 trading days (~1, 3, 6 months)
  D4  leg weighting       2: equal, inverse_vol

  2 x 3 x 3 x 2 = 36 = LAZY_PRICES_N_TRIALS, asserted in code against the
  built list so a size drift is a loud import-time failure rather than a
  silent change to every future run's DSR denominator. 36 >= MIN_TRIALS_FOR_DSR (5).

  pattern_id format: lazy_{cosine|jaccard}_{full|rf|mda}_h{21|63|126}_{eq|ivol}

RATIONALE PER AXIS. D1 and D2 are the paper's own axes (its Table VII reports
by measure and by section), and section 1's verified reading gives a
pre-registered ordering across D2 (risk_factors > full > mda) that the results
can contradict. D3 spans one to six months inside the horizon over which
[CMN20] says returns "continue to accrue out to 18 months". D4 is the two
non-signal weighting modes available without a share-count pipeline.

WHAT IS FIXED AT ONE VALUE AND IS NOT AN AXIS:
  rank_fraction     0.20 (quintiles — [CMN20] sorts into quintiles)
  portfolio         long_short
  direction         high similarity long (section 4); the sign is NOT an axis
  staleness bound   455 calendar days
  forms             10-K only
  tokenization      frozen in section 4, including stopword removal
Widening any of these into an axis after seeing results would be exactly the
search this project's DSR machinery exists to punish.

7. UNIVERSE, DATA AND COSTS
============================================================================
UNIVERSE: point-in-time S&P 500 via sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over
(start, end) for the candidate pool, with the harness's default was_member gate
applied at every formation date. ticker_universe.SCREENING_UNIVERSE (today's
snapshot) is NOT used and is not imported. Window: 2015-01-07
(MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START) to 2026-08-31.

FILING HISTORY DEPTH: the provider walks filings.files — EDGAR's paginated
pre-`recent` history — not just filings.recent. This is a deliberate departure
from cross_sectional_pead.py, which fetches `recent` only and discloses
truncation. Here truncation is not merely a shorter sample: a firm whose
predecessor filing falls off the edge loses its FIRST usable observation
entirely. Measured: paging yields 481 10-Ks across 20 tickers reaching back to
2000, against roughly 8 per firm from `recent` alone.

RESIDUAL SURVIVORSHIP, disclosed in advance and NOT fixed by the gate:
sp500_membership_history's own KNOWN LIMITS record that yfinance returns no
price history for ~48% of tickers that left the index in the trailing 5 years —
the acquired and failed names. SEC's ticker->CIK map compounds this: it maps
CURRENT tickers only, so a departed member resolves no CIK even though its
filings still exist. The point-in-time gate removes the LOOK-AHEAD half of
survivorship bias; it cannot manufacture prices or CIKs no free source sells.
DIRECTIONALLY: a deteriorating firm is exactly the kind expected to rewrite its
risk factors and litigation language, i.e. a "changer" belonging in the SHORT
leg. Their absence should, if anything, WEAKEN the measured short side and thus
work AGAINST H_LAZY. Counts will be reported first-class.

COSTS: config.cost_model = "edge_spread" — each ticker's own trailing effective
HALF-spread from spread_estimator.build_edge_half_spread_frame (the validated
Ardia, Guidotti & Kroencke JFE 2024 EDGE estimator over daily OHLC) at
COST_MODEL_WINDOW_DAYS = 63, charged on traded notional at each formation.
Reused, not reinvented, matching cross_sectional_jump_drift.py. cost_bps = 5.0
(DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS) is the per-ticker fallback where no EDGE estimate
resolves; fallback notional is reported.
financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 — the harness default and every equity family's
value here, carrying this project's standing, disclosed short-borrow optimism:
no securities-borrow feed exists (a known OPEN paid-data gap), so the short
leg's real borrow cost is NOT charged. Disclosed, not modeled.
NOT MODELED: market impact, commissions, borrow. Turnover here is structurally
LOW — an annual signal held 1-6 months — so costs are expected to be a smaller
factor than in any event-driven family this project has run, and a marginal
result must still not be read as tradeable.

8. PASS / FAIL — DECIDED NOW, NOT AFTER
============================================================================
PASS requires, on the pre-declared n_trials = 36:
    at least one spec with sharpe_annualized > 0 AND dsr >= 0.95.
Anything else is an HONEST NEGATIVE and will be reported as one, in full,
with all 36 specs listed rather than the best few.

Even a PASS is explicitly NOT a validated edge and will not be described as
one. n_trials = 36 covers THIS GRID ONLY. It does not cover the literature
scan that nominated this family, nor the ~28 other families this project has
screened, nor the pipeline choices in section 3 that were made before the grid
was frozen. Every DSR reported is an UPPER BOUND on the true multiple-
comparisons correction.

THIS FAMILY WILL NOT BE REGISTERED FOR FORWARD VALIDATION under any result,
and multi_signal_combination.py and cross_sectional_forward_registry.py will
not be touched. Those are separate, later decisions.

============================================================================
9. WHAT WOULD MAKE A POSITIVE RESULT HERE FAKE
============================================================================
 1. LOOK-AHEAD VIA THE PERIOD END. Keying similarity to reportDate instead of
    acceptance would inject a median 53-day advantage (measured, section 5).
    A test pins that availability_date cannot read report_date.
 2. CROSS-TYPE PAIRING. Comparing a 10-K to a 10-Q would produce a huge
    spurious "change" for every firm, correlated with the filing calendar
    rather than with news. A test pins same-type-only pairing on mixed input.
 3. IDF / CORPUS LEAKAGE. Any corpus-fitted weighting embeds future documents
    into past scores (section 4). Not used, by construction.
 4. PARSE NOISE MASQUERADING AS SIGNAL. If a scope's realized cross-sectional
    dispersion is tiny, its ranking may be sorting on formatting churn rather
    than language. Dispersion per scope/metric is a required run-report number
    precisely so this can be judged, not assumed.
 5. SECTION-COVERAGE SELECTION. Section-scope specs rank only the ~86% of
    firm-years whose section could be located, and extraction failure is not
    random across filers (GE's integrated report never matches). A
    section-scope result is therefore computed on a differently-composed
    cross-section than the full-document one, and the two are NOT directly
    comparable. Coverage per scope is a required run-report number.
 6. THIN LEGS. Quintiles of a cross-section thinned by staleness, missing
    prices and section-coverage failure can be few names.
    avg_names_per_leg and n_skipped_formations are reported for every spec.

10. STOPPING RULES
============================================================================
 * The section-6 grid is FROZEN. If a further spec looks attractive after
   results, the temptation is to be DOCUMENTED IN THE RUN REPORT and not acted
   on.
 * The direction is FROZEN (section 0). A negative result is a refutation of
   H_LAZY, not evidence for an undeclared reverse hypothesis.
 * n_trials will NOT be shrunk after the fact on any argument — including that
   the two metrics or the three scopes are correlated rather than independent
   trials. Post-hoc trial-count laundering is a thing this project has
   identified and rejected before.
 * The empirical run MUST be against real live EDGAR filing text. A
   synthetic-fixture-only result would not validate this family's premise and
   would not be reported as a result at all.
 * An honest negative is a complete and acceptable outcome. No spec will be
   added, no scope introduced, no horizon moved, and no threshold relaxed to
   avoid one.
