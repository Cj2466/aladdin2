# Test A — protocol, written BEFORE any number exists

## The hypothesis under test (mine, stated 2026-09-15)

Every return series this project uses is measured **close to close**. In US equities the official
closing price comes from the **closing auction**, a single-price crossing in which no bid-ask
spread is crossed. If that is what our `close` column contains, then a book rebalanced with
market-on-close orders **never pays the spread**, and the 46–62 bp one-way half-spread A2 charged
(SEC/Collver) is a cost we do not incur.

**This hypothesis, if true, makes our results look better. That is exactly why it gets a
pre-declared rule and a falsification-first design.**

## The measurement

Roll (1984): if recorded prices alternate between the bid side and the ask side, successive
measured returns zigzag, producing negative first-order autocovariance even when the efficient
price has not moved. The implied effective (full) spread is

    s = 2 * sqrt( -cov(r_t, r_{t-1}) )      when the covariance is negative

computed per ticker on daily close-to-close returns, then summarised by liquidity quintile.

## Why this design falsifies better than it confirms — stated before the result

Negative autocovariance has more than one cause. Genuine short-horizon reversal produces it too.
So **Roll's number is an UPPER BOUND on the spread component**, and the two directions are not
symmetric:

- **A small Roll estimate is DECISIVE.** A 46–62 bp one-way (92–124 bp full) spread being paid at
  every close cannot hide inside a small autocovariance. If it is small, we are not paying it.
- **A large Roll estimate is AMBIGUOUS.** It could be spread, or reversal, or both.

## Internal calibration check, declared now

The most liquid quintile is a control with a known answer: published sources put the S&P 500
one-way half-spread at **1.3–2.3 bp**, i.e. a **2.6–4.6 bp full** effective spread
(Hagstromer JFE 2021 Table 1; Mackintosh/Nasdaq 2024). If Roll returns roughly that on the liquid
quintile, the estimator is behaving and its micro-cap reading can be believed. **If Roll is wildly
off on the quintile whose answer we already know, the whole test is void and I will say so rather
than keep the part I like.**

## The decision rule — written now, binding

Compare the Roll-implied FULL effective spread in the least-liquid quintile against the SEC
sourced micro-cap figure of **92.2–123.5 bp full** (Collver 2014, median relative effective
spread 0.922%–1.235% for sub-$100M caps).

| measured Roll spread, Q1 | verdict |
|---|---|
| **< 1/3 of the SEC figure** (< ~31 bp) | **A SUPPORTED** — closing prices do not carry the spread; every cost number in this project must be recomputed |
| **> 2/3 of the SEC figure** (> ~61 bp) | **A REFUTED** — closing prices carry the spread; we pay it; close the question and move to test B |
| **between** | **UNRESOLVED** — not decidable on daily bars; needs real quote data, which is paid |

Additionally, if the calibration check fails (liquid-quintile Roll estimate outside roughly
1–15 bp full), **the verdict is VOID regardless of what Q1 shows.**

## Scope

Universe, window and quintiles are taken unchanged from A2 (`a2_universe_2026-09-15/
clean_universe.csv`, common stock only, evaluation from 2025-09-01) so the two are directly
comparable. Price data only; the sealed OSAP box is not opened.

## Known limitations, written before the result

- Roll on daily bars is a noisy estimator; a meaningful share of tickers will show POSITIVE
  autocovariance, for which the estimator is undefined. That share is reported, not hidden, and
  those tickers are treated as "no measurable bounce", which is the conservative choice **against**
  my own hypothesis only if they are excluded — so both treatments are reported.
- A single stock's estimate is unreliable; only the quintile summaries are read.
- This tests what the RECORDED closing price contains. Whether a retail market-on-close order
  actually receives that price is a separate question, not tested here.
