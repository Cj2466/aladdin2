MOMENTUM
Frozen 2026-09-04, BEFORE any overlaid return series was computed.

This document is committed BEFORE the module that implements it is run. The
hypothesis, the base specs, the overlay definitions, the trial count, the
registration-eligibility rules and the pass/fail bar are all fixed here and are
not restated afterwards as though they had been decided later.

The one thing that WAS measured before this document was written is the
frequency of Daniel & Moskowitz's bear-state indicator itself (section 4). That
is a market-state variable computed from index returns alone; it touches no
strategy return series and no Sharpe. Measuring it first is a POWER ANALYSIS,
which a pre-registration is worse without, not a peek at the outcome. The exact
numbers are recorded in section 4 and they materially shaped the eligibility
rules in section 7 — which is the point of doing it first.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE SOURCE, VERIFIED FROM THE PUBLISHED PAPER, NOT FROM MEMORY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Daniel, Kent & Tobias J. Moskowitz, "Momentum crashes", Journal of Financial
Economics 122(2), December 2016, pp. 221-247, doi:10.1016/j.jfineco.2015.12.002.
Fetched as the published JFE PDF from the first author's own site
(https://www.kentdaniel.net/papers/published/jfe_16.pdf) and text-extracted for
this build. Every construction detail below is quoted from that file. Verbatim
from the abstract:

    "Despite their strong positive average returns across numerous asset
     classes, momentum strategies can experience infrequent and persistent
     strings of negative returns. These momentum crashes are partly
     forecastable. They occur in panic states, following market declines and
     when market volatility is high, and are contemporaneous with market
     rebounds. [...] An implementable dynamic momentum strategy based on
     forecasts of momentum's mean and variance approximately doubles the alpha
     and Sharpe ratio of a static momentum strategy and is not explained by
     other factors."

THE FOUR CONSTRUCTIONS, QUOTED EXACTLY.

(a) THE BEAR-MARKET INDICATOR. Verbatim, from the Table 4 notes (p. 230) and
    repeated identically in section 3.2's variable list (p. 228):

        "IB,t-1 is an ex ante bear market indicator that equals one if the
         cumulative CRSP VW index return in the past 24 months is negative and
         is zero otherwise."

    Note what it is NOT: not a drawdown, not a moving-average cross, not an
    excess return. It is the plain cumulative 24-month TOTAL return on the
    CRSP value-weighted index, and the threshold is exactly zero.

    Paper footnote 6, p. 228: "Of the 1,035 months in the 1927:01-2013:03
    period, IB,t-1 = 1 in 183". That is 17.7%, and section 4 below uses it as a
    correctness check on this build's implementation.

(b) THE MARKET VARIANCE. Eq. (4), p. 230: "sigma-hat^2_m,t-1 is the variance of
    the daily returns of the market over the 126 days prior to time t." The
    Table 5 notes say "measured over the 126 days preceding the start of month
    t"; the bullet list on p. 231 says "the variance of the market excess return
    over the preceding 126 days". THE PAPER IS INTERNALLY INCONSISTENT ON
    TOTAL-vs-EXCESS here and this build discloses that rather than silently
    picking one: it uses TOTAL daily returns, matching Eq. (4)'s own wording,
    and the choice is immaterial because a daily risk-free rate of ~1e-5 shifts
    a 126-day variance in the sixth decimal.

(c) THE PANIC STATE is not a separate indicator. It is the INTERACTION of (a)
    and (b): "in periods of high market stress, as indicated by bear markets and
    high volatility, future momentum returns are low" (p. 230). The normalized
    form the paper regresses on is IBsigma^2 = (1/vbar_B) * IB,t-1 *
    sigma-hat^2_m,t-1, where 1/vbar_B is a pure display normalization "to give
    the coefficients a simple interpretation" (p. 231). No normalization is used
    here: it cancels out of the weight, which is scale-free (section 5d).

(d) THE DYNAMIC WEIGHT. Eq. (6), p. 233, verbatim in structure:

        w*_{t-1} = ( 1 / (2*lambda) ) * mu_{t-1} / sigma^2_{t-1}

    "where mu_{t-1} = E_{t-1}[R_WML,t] is the conditional expected return on the
    (zero-investment) WML portfolio over the coming month, sigma^2_{t-1} =
    E_{t-1}[(R_WML,t - mu_{t-1})^2] is the conditional variance of the WML
    portfolio return over the coming month, and lambda is a time-invariant
    scalar that controls the unconditional risk and return of the dynamic
    portfolio."

    The mean forecast, Fig. 7 caption p. 236, verbatim:

        mu_{t-1} = gamma-hat_{0,t-1} + gamma-hat_{int,t-1} * IB,t-1 *
                   sigma-hat^2_m,t-1

    "only now the gamma-hat_{0,t-1} and gamma-hat_{int,t-1} are the estimated
    regression coefficients not over the full sample, but rather from a
    regression run from the start of our sample (1927:07) up through month t-1".
    That is the IMPLEMENTABLE, out-of-sample version and it is the only version
    this build treats as a candidate. The in-sample version uses full-sample
    coefficients and is not implementable; it is not built here at all.

    The variance forecast for the out-of-sample strategy, Table 7 notes p. 236:
    "sigma^2_{t-1} is the realized variance of the daily WML returns over the
    preceding 126 trading days." (The in-sample version additionally blends a
    GJR-GARCH forecast; the out-of-sample one, which is what is replicated here,
    does not.)

    NO CAP AND NO FLOOR ON THE WEIGHT. The paper's own weight reaches 5.37 and
    goes NEGATIVE in 82 months of its sample (p. 233). This build imposes no
    cap, because the paper imposes none, and reports the realized range.

(e) THE TWO SIMPLER SCALINGS the paper benchmarks against, Table 7 notes p. 236,
    verbatim: "cvol is the constant volatility strategy, in which the WML
    returns each month are scaled by the realized volatility of the daily WML
    returns over the preceding 126 trading days. For the variance scaled
    portfolio, the WML returns each month are scaled by the realized variance of
    the daily WML returns over the preceding 126 trading days." cvol is
    attributed in the paper to Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015).

    THE PAPER'S OWN RESULT, Table 7, annualized Sharpe over 1934:01-2013:03:
        WML                 0.682
        cvol                1.041
        variance scaled     1.126
        dyn, out-of-sample  1.194
        dyn, in-sample      1.202

    READ THAT TABLE HONESTLY BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING ON IT: of the +0.512
    Sharpe improvement from WML to the full dynamic strategy, +0.359 (70%) comes
    from the plain volatility scaling that uses NO bear indicator at all, and
    only +0.153 from everything else combined. D&M's headline is the dynamic
    strategy; the bulk of their measured gain is Barroso-Santa-Clara's
    volatility scaling.

(f) WHAT THE PAPER CLAIMS THE MECHANISM IS, which is what section 8's
    capital-preservation test is against, p. 234: "much of the improved
    performance of the constant volatility, and especially dynamic strategy,
    over the static WML portfolio is the amelioration of big crashes." The
    claim being tested is CRASH MITIGATION. A Sharpe improvement that does not
    come with a drawdown improvement has not reproduced this paper's mechanism,
    whatever it has done to the Sharpe.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE HYPOTHESIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H1 (volatility scaling). Scaling a residual-momentum long-short book by the
inverse of its own trailing 126-day realized volatility (or variance) reduces
its maximum drawdown, and does not reduce its Sharpe ratio.

H2 (bear-state gate). Standing flat in Daniel-Moskowitz bear states further
reduces maximum drawdown.

H3 (the D&M-vs-BHM tension, the reason a total-return arm is in this grid).
Blitz/Huij/Martens's own abstract claims residual momentum's whole point is that
"conventional momentum strategies exhibit substantial time-varying exposures to
the Fama and French factors [and] these exposures can be reduced by ranking
stocks on residual stock returns". D&M's crash mechanism IS a time-varying
market-beta effect (loser-leg betas above 3 after market declines, p. 228). If
BHM are right, D&M's overlay should help residual momentum LESS than it helps
total-return momentum. This grid measures that directly and it is the sharpest
mechanism test available here.

3. THE BASES — AND WHY THE PRIMARY IS THE PAPER-FAITHFUL ONE, NOT THE BEST ONE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All three bases are EXISTING specs from this project's already-run 18-spec
residual-momentum grid (residual_momentum_2026-09-02.txt), taken unchanged, all
at h21 — BHM's own monthly rebalance and the cadence D&M's monthly weighting
requires. Holding period is NOT a search axis in this grid: one value, fixed by
both source papers.

    key    spec                              persisted Sharpe / DSR(n=18)
    ----   -------------------------------   ----------------------------
    ctrl   rm_total_return_control_ls_h21    -0.021 / 0.198
    ff3    rm_ff3_residual_ls_h21            +0.057 / 0.279     <-- PRIMARY
    ff3n   rm_ff3_residual_neutral_ls_h21    +0.326 / 0.630

THE PRIMARY BASE IS `ff3`, THE BHM-FAITHFUL RAW RESIDUAL SORT. It is not the
family's best spec. It is a third of the way to the screening floor. It is
chosen anyway, and this is the central judgment call of this build:

  (i) `ff3n` WAS DECLINED FOR FORWARD VALIDATION EARLIER TONIGHT (commit
      dd288f9), on the finding that industry neutralization — a step BHM never
      take — supplies 82.7% of its Sharpe. Building the overlay on `ff3n` would
      stack a second unvalidated design choice on top of the first. Any DSR that
      came out would still be four-fifths attributable to the axis that was just
      rejected, and registering it would launder a declined spec through a new
      wrapper. That decision's own section D.6 forbids exactly this in advance:
      an industry-adjusted construct "cannot be created by relabelling this
      grid's audit leg as its finding after seeing the numbers."

 (ii) D&M'S OWN OBJECT IS PLAIN MOMENTUM. Their WML is a Jegadeesh-Titman
      total-return decile sort. It is not residual, and it is certainly not
      industry-neutral. Applying their overlay to a residual sort is already one
      step of extrapolation from the source; applying it to an industry-neutral
      residual sort is two. One step is a defensible test. Two is a different
      strategy wearing two papers' citations.

(iii) THE COST OF THIS CHOICE IS STATED PLAINLY RATHER THAN DISCOVERED LATER.
      The primary base's Sharpe of +0.057 over 11.62 years is a naive t-statistic
      of about +0.19. It is statistically indistinguishable from zero. An overlay
      cannot be expected to lift that to a registrable DSR, and this
      pre-registration does not expect it to. What the overlay CAN be measured on,
      even against a null base, is H1's drawdown claim — which is the claim
      D&M actually make (section 1f) and the one this project's standing
      capital-preservation objective cares about. That measurement is the
      deliverable here; a registrable Sharpe would be a surprise.

`ff3n` and `ctrl` are still screened, for the mechanism reasons in section 7 and
H3, and both are PRE-COMMITTED AS NON-REGISTRABLE regardless of what they score.

4. THE POWER CHECK, RUN BEFORE THIS DOCUMENT WAS WRITTEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D&M's IB was implemented exactly as quoted in 1(a) — 24-month cumulative total
return on the CRSP value-weighted index, from Ken French's own monthly market
factor (mkt_rf + rf IS the CRSP VW index return; French's library builds it from
CRSP) — and first run over D&M'S OWN SAMPLE as a correctness check:

    1927:01-2013:03 ..... IB = 1 in 183 of 1035 months = 17.7%
    the paper's footnote 6 ... "IB,t-1 = 1 in 183" of "1,035 months"  MATCH.

An exact reproduction of the paper's own count, to the month. The implementation
is correct.

THEN OVER THIS PROJECT'S BACKTEST WINDOW, and this is the finding that shapes
everything below:

    2015-01 .. 2026-08, CRSP VW ... IB = 1 in   8 of 138 months = 5.80%
    2015-01 .. 2026-08, SPY proxy .. IB = 1 in   1 of 140 months = 0.71%

The eight CRSP VW bear months are 2020-03, 2022-12, 2023-04, 2023-05, 2023-08,
2023-09, 2023-10 and 2023-11 — that is TWO episodes, not eight independent
observations. This project's window carries less than a third of D&M's own base
rate, and the entire small-cap-driven 2023 cluster does not appear at all in the
large-cap SPY series (94.9% agreement between the two indicators, disagreeing on
exactly the months that matter).

CONSEQUENCES, FIXED HERE IN ADVANCE:

  * Any arm whose distinguishing input is IB is testing a hypothesis against
    two episodes. Its Sharpe is not a 2,929-day measurement whatever the DSR
    machinery says, because the DSR deflates for sample length, skew, kurtosis
    and trial count, and NOT for the effective independence of the underlying
    bets. This is the same gap dd288f9 identified in the industry-neutral
    result and it applies with more force here.
  * The `bear` and `dyn` arms are therefore declared NON-REGISTRABLE IN ADVANCE,
    whatever they score (section 7). They are run because the measurement is
    itself the finding, not because a good number would mean anything.
  * PREDICTED AND RECORDED BEFORE THE RUN, so it can be checked afterwards: with
    IB on in 8 of 138 months, D&M's `dyn` weight should collapse to very nearly
    the `vscale` weight times a slowly-drifting constant, because mu_{t-1}
    reduces to gamma-hat_{0,t-1} in 130 of 138 months. The correlation between
    the two arms' weight series is reported as a check on this prediction.

5. THE OVERLAY DEFINITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let r_d be the base spec's REALIZED DAILY NET RETURN series, exactly as this
project's harness produces it (net of the family's 5 bps one-way turnover cost).
Weights are set monthly, on the last trading day of month m-1, and applied to
every trading day of month m — D&M's own cadence ("the WML portfolio weights
each month are multiplied each month by w*").

Let, all measured on the 126 trading days ending on the last trading day of
month m-1:
    sigma_hat      = std(r_d) * sqrt(252)          own annualized volatility
    sigma_hat_sq   = var(r_d) * 252                own annualized variance
and, from the market:
    IB             = 1 if the CRSP VW cumulative return over the 24 months
                     ending month m-1 is negative, else 0
    sigma_m_sq     = var(daily SPY total return over the 126 trading days
                     ending the last trading day of month m-1) * 252

FOUR ARMS, fixed:

  cvol    w = 1 / sigma_hat                        D&M Table 7 "cvol"
  vscale  w = 1 / sigma_hat_sq                     D&M Table 7 "variance scaled"
  bear    w = 0 if IB = 1, else 1                  ABLATION, see below
  dyn     w = mu_hat / sigma_hat_sq                D&M Eq. 6 + Fig. 7 caption
          mu_hat = g0 + gint * IB * sigma_m_sq, with (g0, gint) from an OLS of
          the base's own MONTHLY returns on [1, IB * sigma_m_sq] estimated on an
          EXPANDING window from the start of the replay through month m-1.

`bear` IS AN ABLATION, NOT A D&M CONSTRUCTION, and is labelled as such wherever
it is reported. D&M never propose a binary gate; they propose a continuous
weight in which IB enters through mu_hat. The arm exists because it is the only
way to isolate the bear axis from the volatility axis, which is what this
project's mechanism-fidelity standard requires. Its gate level is 0 (fully flat
in bear states) — pre-declared, the maximally capital-preserving reading, and
NOT searched over. No other gate level is tried.

WARMUP. w = 1 (i.e. the base, unmodified) for every day before the first month
boundary with 126 prior trading days of base returns, and for `dyn` also for
every month before 36 months of base history exist or before gint is identified
(which requires at least one IB = 1 month in the expanding window). This is
deliberate: base and overlay then share an IDENTICAL day set, so the base's
Sharpe reproduces the persisted number exactly and every difference between the
two series is the overlay and nothing else. The warmup day count is reported.

(d) LEVERAGE NORMALIZATION, AND WHY IT CANNOT BUY A BETTER SHARPE. Each raw
weight series is multiplied by one constant k, chosen so the overlaid pre-cost
series has the same full-sample annualized volatility as its own base. This is
D&M's own convention (p. 233: "we scale the weights of both the constant
volatility and the dynamic strategy so as to make the full sample volatility of
each return series equal to that of the baseline WML strategy"). It is stated
here that k is a FULL-SAMPLE quantity. A constant multiplier cannot change a
Sharpe ratio, so k has exactly zero effect on every Sharpe, PSR and DSR in this
run. It does affect the overlay's turnover cost and the percentage depth of a
drawdown, so those are reported at the declared k and ALSO at k/2 and 2k as a
sensitivity. Nothing is selected on k.

(e) THE OVERLAY'S OWN TRADING COST IS CHARGED, not waved away. D&M note (p. 233)
that "an actual implementation of the dynamic strategy would certainly incur
higher transaction costs" and leave it there. Changing the book's leverage from
w_{m-1} to w_m means trading |w_m - w_{m-1}| x 2 units of one-way gross notional
(the base is $1 long / $1 short, so gross notional is 2 per unit of weight), and
that is charged at the family's own 5 bps on the first trading day of each
month. The base's own rebalancing cost is already inside r_d and is scaled by
w_m along with the returns, which is correct: cost scales with notional.

(f) FINANCING IS NOT CHARGED, and that is a DISCLOSED OPTIMISM that is strictly
worse here than for the base. The family's config carries
financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 — this project's standing short-borrow disclosure.
An overlay whose weight exceeds 1 is levered, and levered gross notional is
exactly where a 0 bps borrow assumption is least defensible. The realized mean
and maximum of |w| are reported for every arm so the size of this optimism is
visible rather than implied.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE GRID, AND THE TRIAL DENOMINATOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3 bases x 4 overlays = 12 new specs. One holding period (h21), one gate level,
one warmup rule, one normalization rule — none of them searched.

n_trials = 30 = the 18 already-run residual-momentum specs + these 12.

WHY THE PRIOR 18 ARE CARRIED, rather than treating this as a fresh 12-trial
grid. The 18 base Sharpes were computed, persisted and READ before this grid was
designed; the choice of which three of them to build on was made with those
numbers in view. That is a search dimension and pretending otherwise would be
the trial-count laundering this project has already identified and rejected once
(cross_sectional_patterns_round_d.py). The declined decision's own section D.6
instructs any follow-up to "carry these 18 trials into the denominator". 30 is
that instruction, applied.

WHY THE EXISTING 18 PERSISTED ROWS ARE NOT RE-DEFLATED TO 30. They were computed
and published under a pre-registration that fixed 18, and rewriting a persisted
result to a denominator chosen after the fact is exactly the kind of retroactive
edit this project's append-only convention exists to prevent. The enlarged
denominator applies to the NEW rows, which is where the enlarged search actually
lands.

sigma_SR is pooled over all 30 Sharpes — the 18 persisted base Sharpes plus the
12 new overlaid ones — for the same reason run_residual_momentum_screening pools
over 18 rather than per-arm: sigma_SR is meant to describe the dispersion of the
whole searched family. The direction of this choice is not knowable in advance
and it is applied unconditionally, before any number is inspected.

7. REGISTRATION ELIGIBILITY — FIXED NOW, NOT AFTER THE NUMBERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EXACTLY TWO of the twelve specs are eligible to be proposed for forward
validation: `ff3_cvol` and `ff3_vscale`. Every other spec is declared
NON-REGISTRABLE HERE, IN ADVANCE, WHATEVER IT SCORES:

  * all four `ff3n_*` — the base was declined tonight (section 3(i));
  * all four `ctrl_*` — total-return momentum is BHM's comparison baseline, not
    this family's hypothesis, and its own base spec has a NEGATIVE Sharpe;
  * `ff3_bear` and `ff3_dyn` — their distinguishing input fires in 8 of 138
    months across two episodes (section 4). No DSR computed on 2,929 daily
    observations describes a bet taken twice.

AN ELIGIBLE SPEC IS REGISTERED ONLY IF ALL FOUR HOLD:

  (i)   DSR >= 0.5 at n_trials = 30 — this project's informal screening floor,
        the same one the four live registrations cleared;
  (ii)  MECHANISM FIDELITY: the overlay, not the base, supplies the edge. Made
        concrete in advance: the overlaid Sharpe must exceed the base's own
        Sharpe by a margin that survives the arm's own trading cost, and the
        improvement must not be reproducible by a constant leverage change
        (which, being scale-free, cannot move a Sharpe at all — so any Sharpe
        movement that is not attributable to the TIME VARIATION of w is an
        implementation bug and is checked for as one);
  (iii) THE PAPER'S OWN CLAIM MUST HOLD: maximum drawdown must be REDUCED versus
        the base at matched full-sample volatility. D&M's mechanism is crash
        mitigation (section 1f). An overlay that raises Sharpe while deepening
        drawdown has not reproduced this paper's mechanism and is not registered
        on this paper's authority, whatever its DSR;
  (iv)  preservation_score is computed and reported. It is reported for all
        twelve specs regardless; for an eligible spec it is a required input to
        the decision, not a footnote.

IF NO SPEC CLEARS, THE FAMILY IS DECLINED AND THE RESULT IS WRITTEN UP AS AN
HONEST NEGATIVE. Given section 3(iii) and section 4, that is the outcome this
pre-registration considers most likely. Nothing in this build is permitted to be
re-scoped afterwards into a positive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT IS REPORTED FOR EVERY SPEC, WHATEVER THE VERDICT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sharpe (net), DSR at n=30, PSR vs 0, n observations, max drawdown, Calmar,
first-half and second-half Sharpe, preservation_score and its no-stability
variant, realized mean/min/max of the weight series, the overlay's own turnover
cost, the count of warmup days, and the number of months the arm's weight
differed from 1 by more than 1%.

Plus the three structural diagnostics fixed in advance:
  * the base-vs-overlay drawdown delta, which is H1's actual test;
  * corr(dyn weight, vscale weight), which tests section 4's prediction;
  * the H3 comparison: the overlay's Sharpe and drawdown improvement on `ctrl`
    versus on `ff3`.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. WHAT THIS BUILD WILL NOT CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It will not claim to have tested Daniel & Moskowitz. Their sample is 1927-2013,
1,035 months, a broad CRSP universe, a 17.7% bear base rate and a static WML
Sharpe of 0.682 to work with. This build is 138 months of a 200-name large-cap
sample with a 5.8% bear base rate and a base Sharpe of +0.057. A null here is a
statement about this universe and this window, and about whether their overlay
rescues THIS family — nothing more.

It will not claim, if `ff3n_*` scores well, that industry-neutral residual
momentum with a crash overlay is a finding. Per dd288f9 section D.6 that would
need its own pre-registered family grounded in the Moskowitz-Grinblatt /
Grundy-Martin industry-momentum literature, with its own bar. This grid cannot
create it and will not be used to.
