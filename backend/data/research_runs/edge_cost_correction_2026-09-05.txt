THE EDGE COST-MODEL CORRECTION, MEASURED ON lazy_prices
2026-09-05. Two defects fixed; the family re-run under four cost scenarios.
==============================================================================

WHAT WAS WRONG, AND WHY THIS IS NOT A NEW FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Commit df8a933 (2026-08-30) established that spread_estimator's EDGE
half-spread frame overstates liquid large-cap trading costs by roughly 10-40x,
and dd34094 re-audited two families against a properly sourced calibration
rather than the estimator's own level. Neither commit changed the estimator or
the frame any family actually trades on. lazy_prices — a LIVE forward
registration since 2026-09-03 — has been screening and forward-ticking on the
uncorrected frame ever since.

This run fixes two separable defects and measures what each does.

  DEFECT 1, an implementation deviation from the source package.
  build_edge_half_spread_frame calls bidask.edge_rolling with the package
  default sign=False, which returns sqrt(|s^2|) — a negative squared-spread
  estimate is FOLDED to a positive spread rather than truncated. The package's
  own documentation (bidask.eguidotti.com, FAQ, retrieved 2026-09-05) states
  the trade-off directly: the absolute-value default is "generally a good
  option if you are interested in point estimates" but "may create a
  small-sample bias if the estimates are used for averaging or regression
  studies", while computing "signed estimates with the argument sign=True and
  reset negative values to zero" is what "reduce[s] this source of bias". A
  cost model is the averaging case, not the point-estimate case.

  HOW MUCH OF THE FRAME THIS TOUCHES, measured on the real panel rather than
  argued: 738,741 of 1,830,000 cells (40.4%) carry a negative squared-spread
  estimate. Every one of them was being folded into a positive charge.

  DEFECT 2, the level, which is the source paper's own disclosed limitation
  and cannot be fixed inside the estimator (Ardia/Guidotti/Kroencke: post-2005
  mid and large cap spreads "have become too small to be reliably estimated
  from a monthly sample of daily data"). The RANKING is what remains
  trustworthy, so the level is not estimated — it is pinned by one pooled
  scalar to published spread statistics, exactly the RC-edge-ranked scenario
  frozen in edge_cost_reaudit_corrected_PREREGISTRATION.txt §3.

  DEFECT 3, closed although it was never a defect: a US equity's quoted spread
  cannot be below one $0.01 tick, so the one-way half-spread cannot be below
  0.005 / price. Exact arithmetic, applied per cell.

WHAT THE CORRECTED FRAME DOES, ON THE REAL PANEL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
625 tickers x 2,928 dates = 1,830,000 cells, S&P 500 point-in-time universe,
2015-01-07 .. 2026-08-28, from the family's own frozen price snapshot (no live
fetch: the "before" number has to be the registered one).

  ONE-WAY HALF-SPREAD, POOLED OVER EVERY CHARGED CELL
                                     median        mean
    raw EDGE frame (as registered)   24.41 bp    31.11 bp
    corrected frame                   2.00 bp     2.83 bp

  A 24.41bp one-way half-spread is a 48.8bp FULL spread on the S&P 500
  cross-section. Hagstromer (JFE 2021) Table 1 measures the actual S&P 500
  median full effective spread at 2.53bp. The raw frame is therefore ~19x
  high on this panel — an independent reproduction of the documented 10-40x
  band, on a different universe and window from the one that produced it.

  CALIBRATION, recorded so the scalar is auditable:
    window 63d; pooled median over positive cells 24.47bp; scale 0.08174
    (i.e. divide by 12.2) to the 2.00bp target; realized median after the
    tick floor 2.00bp.
    921,099 cells estimated / 738,741 truncated to zero / 170,160 with no
    estimate at all / 311,106 raised to the tick floor.

  THE TARGET IS SOURCED, NOT CHOSEN. 2.0bp one-way for an equal-weighted S&P
  500 cross-section over 2015-2026, from edge_cost_reaudit_corrected_
  PREREGISTRATION.txt §2: Hagstromer JFE 2021 Table 1 (median stock 2.53bp
  full, p75 3.78, p95 6.99, Dec 2015) plus Nasdaq/Mackintosh 2024
  (cap-weighted whole-index "just over 4.5 bps" full) plus Mackintosh 2021 on
  post-2018 regimes running wider.

  ONE DESIGN CALL, MADE ON REAL DATA AND WORTH FLAGGING. A cell truncated to
  zero is charged the CALIBRATED TARGET, not the tick floor. At 40.4% of
  cells this moves the realized median by roughly a factor of two (1.56bp if
  those cells take the tick floor, 2.00bp as implemented). A squared-spread
  estimate at or below zero is a statement about the estimator's precision at
  this window length, not a claim that the stock trades for free; charging
  ~0.3bp there would price 40% of the book at almost nothing on the strength
  of a small-sample artifact. Cells with NO estimate at all (170,160) are left
  NaN so the harness's counted flat fallback still fires.

WHAT MOVED, IN WORDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * The turnover cost charge falls by 9.8x (8.27% -> 0.84% of the best spec's
    cumulative return), which is the whole point: it was charging a 48.8bp
    round-trip on S&P 500 large caps.
  * Best-spec net Sharpe rises 0.5946 -> 0.7456 (+0.151, +25%).
  * Seven specs that were net-negative under the raw frame are net-positive
    under the corrected one (29/36 -> 36/36). Nothing about the family's
    RANKING changed: the best spec is the same one.
  * DSR rises at every denominator, and STILL does not clear this project's
    0.95 validated-edge bar at any of them. Under Policy D the verdict against
    the 0.95 bar is DEFINITE_NEGATIVE both before and after the fix — the
    correction changes the number, not the answer.
  * Against the 0.50 forward-registration SCREENING floor the picture is
    different and worth stating: the control clears at all three denominators
    but only barely at the top (0.5083 at N=857), while the corrected frame
    clears comfortably (0.6609). The registration was closer to its own floor
    than anyone knew.

THE BORROW FINDING, WHICH IS THE LARGER ONE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S2 vs S3 is not a small sensitivity. Charging the short leg general collateral
(34bp/yr — Beneish/Lee/Nichols 2015 p.15, DCBS=1) costs 0.078 Sharpe and
leaves the family where it was. Charging it the specials rate (430bp/yr —
D'Avolio 2002 Table 3) takes the whole family NEGATIVE: 0/36 specs positive,
best net Sharpe -0.2118, financing drag 0.5007.

The true rate is near the GC end — D'Avolio p.273: "S&P 500 constituents,
provided in excess supply by indexing lenders, are almost always general
collateral" — so S2 is the realistic reading and S3 is a bracket. But the
bracket is what shows how much work the standing financing_bps_per_year = 0.0
assumption has been doing, in a family nobody would have called
borrow-sensitive. See borrow_cost.py for every rate's source and for the
still-open paid-feed gap (PENDING_PAID_DATA_DECISIONS.md, entry P1).

WHAT WAS NOT DONE, DELIBERATELY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO FAMILY'S DEFAULT COST MODEL WAS CHANGED, and no registration's status was
touched. cross_sectional_lazy_prices.default_lazy_prices_config() still builds
the raw frame; the live lazy_prices_jaccard_full registration still ticks on
it. Two reasons, both deliberate:

  1. Swapping the frame changes an accumulating forward track record WITHOUT
     changing spec_fingerprint or config_fingerprint — the half-spread frame
     is data the adapter builds, not a config field. Doing that silently is
     precisely the drift class that live_registration_dependencies.json was
     added (same commit series) to make visible. It is the repo owner's call.
  2. build_edge_half_spread_frame is left byte-identical so every persisted
     run under cost_model="edge_spread" stays exactly reproducible.

THE DECISION THIS LEAVES OPEN, stated plainly rather than implied: whether
lazy_prices_jaccard_full should switch to build_calibrated_half_spread_frame,
and whether any equity family should adopt a non-zero
financing_bps_per_year. Both change live numbers. Neither is an agent's call.

REPRODUCTION AND CONTROL DRIFT, DISCLOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S0 reproduces the production configuration but NOT, exactly, the persisted
production numbers. Best-spec Sharpe for lazy_jaccard_full_h126_ivol:

    0.6035   run_tag lazy_prices_2026-09-01   (DSR 0.7540 at n_trials 36)
    0.5887   run_tag global_effective_n_2026-09-04 (DSR 0.7474)
    0.5946   this run's S0 control            (DSR 0.7533)

This family's non-monotonic reproduction drift is a known, open finding — its
ticker->CIK resolution is not point-in-time (see project notes on the
lazy_prices confound; the XOM-fix hypothesis for it was REFUTED in 3af7904 and
the real cause is still open). The three numbers straddling each other is
consistent with that and is NOT explained by anything in this commit: S0
changes no code path the earlier runs used. It is recorded here rather than
smoothed over, and it is why every comparison above is S0-vs-S1 on ONE data
build rather than corrected-vs-persisted across runs.

SELF-REPRODUCTION, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSUMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The whole comparison was run TWICE end to end, in two separate processes, the
second under different machine load. Every one of the four scenarios' logged
figures is identical to the digits reported above. Comparing the two runs'
full per-spec payloads field by field: 139 of 576 compared floats differ, with
a maximum RELATIVE difference of 3.2e-14 — i.e. agreement to about 14
significant digits, floating-point summation-order noise and nothing else. The
largest single case is one 1-ULP difference in a cost-drag accumulator
(0.08269524405872185 vs ...86). No Sharpe, DSR or clearing count moves at any
reported precision. The frame statistics and the calibration scalar are
bit-identical between runs.
