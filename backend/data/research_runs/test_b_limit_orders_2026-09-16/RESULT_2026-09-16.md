## The verdict the pre-declared rule returns, and why I am not reporting it as a pass

Section 7's rule returns **L1**: primary marketable reformation cost **30.55 bp** against the
**30.6 bp** break-even.

**That margin is 0.05 bp — 0.16%. It is a tie, not a pass, and I am recording it as a tie.**

Two reasons, both structural, neither discovered after the fact:

1. **The "primary" statistic is the median of three bucket medians, so with three buckets it is
   literally Q2's value and nothing else.** {35.96, 7.64, 4.93} → 7.64 → ×4 → 30.55. One bucket
   decides it. That is a weakness in the reduction I wrote, and it is disclosed rather than
   repaired after seeing the number.
2. Landing within 0.16% of a line drawn independently is a coincidence, not a finding.

**The per-bucket table is the result. The single number is not.** And the table says something the
single number hides:

> **The break-even line falls between Q2 and Q3.** Q3, Q4 and Q5 clear it with room
> (23.7 / 10.7 / 4.5 bp against 30.6). Q1 fails by 5.7×. Q2 sits on the line.

This is a **different** conclusion from either of the ones on offer before today. Not "the book
passes" and not "the micro-cap route is closed", but: **the cost problem is confined to the
bottom one or two quintiles, and it is severe there.**

**Prediction B2 CONFIRMED** — a full session does give a resting order a majority fill, comfortably.

### The adverse selection is real, and enormous, and it still loses the argument

The days a midpoint buy filled returned **−128.21 bp** marketable; the days it did not fill
returned **+89.40 bp**. **A gap of 217.6 bp.** You fill on the bad days. That effect is exactly
what I argued for in conversation all week, and it is bigger than I would have guessed.

**And it does not matter, because the spread saved is bigger still:**

```
0.8402 × 65.16 bp saved  −  0.1598 × 89.40 bp foregone  =  +39.1 bp
```

**Prediction B3 REFUTED.** I predicted the saving would be "substantially smaller than half the
quoted spread" and "would not be surprised by zero". It is **+39 bp per ticker-day**, essentially
the whole saving. **Prediction B4 REFUTED** — I predicted L3, the closed route; the rule returned
L1 and the honest reading is a tie with a liquidity split underneath.

## THE CAVEAT THAT GOVERNS ALL OF PART 3, stated before anyone acts on it

**There is no signal in this test.** Every ticker-day is bought and sold unconditionally. That
makes the P&L *levels* meaningless — buying 1,189 illiquid names at the ask and marking them to
the last trade is structurally negative, which is why arm M shows −92.76 bp. Only the differences
between arms carry information.

More important, it makes **the +39.1 bp an upper bound, and the 89.40 bp a lower bound**:

> A real strategy buys because it expects the price to RISE. The days a resting buy fails to fill
> are the days price ran away — **which are precisely the days a working signal was right.** For a
> strategy with a genuine edge, the foregone return on unfilled days is larger than it is for this
> signal-free sample, by roughly the size of the edge.

**So passive execution cannot be adopted on this evidence.** What this test establishes is that
the mechanism is real and measurable and that its sign is favourable **absent a signal**. Whether
it survives contact with a signal is a different measurement, and it is not this one.

## What changes, and what does not

1. **A2's estimator bracket is replaced by a measurement** for the first time. 11.1–112.4 bp
   becomes a per-bucket table anchored next to the SEC's own figure.
2. **The Set-2 FAIL AS BUILT verdict is NOT overturned here.** The rule returned L1 on a 0.16%
   margin from a statistic that is one bucket. What is now established is narrower and more
   useful: the failure is concentrated in Q1, and Q3 upward clears the line.
3. **Time of day is a free lever nobody had measured.** Q2 costs 12.61 bp across the session and
   7.64 bp at midday. Q3: 5.92 vs 4.93.
4. **Passive execution stays unadopted**, pending a measurement that includes a signal.
5. Ten sessions, one regime. Nothing here is a forward claim.

## For the owner, one line each

1. **Trading costs are much lower than our tool said** — and the measurement agrees with the US
   regulator's own published figure, which is the best evidence we have that it is right.
2. **The problem is not "small stocks", it is the smallest fifth.** Above that, costs clear the
   line.
3. **Waiting for a good price does save money — a lot — but you only get filled on the bad days**,
   and the gap between fill days and no-fill days is 217.6 bp. On this test the saving still wins.
4. **Do not act on point 3 yet.** This test had no opinion about which stocks to buy. With a real
   opinion, the days you miss are the days you were right, and that is not measured here.
