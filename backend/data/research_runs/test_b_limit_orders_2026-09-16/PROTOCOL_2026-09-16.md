# Test B protocol — what a trade actually costs when it can be worked across a full session

**Written before any measurement exists.** Binding. The decision rules in section 7 were fixed
before the first number was produced, and the verdict is whatever they return.

## 1. Why this test replaces Test A rather than continuing it

Test A (`../test_a_closing_price_2026-09-16/`) asked whether the CLOSING price carries the spread.
Two corrections appended to its result on the same day change what comes next:

- **Its stated reason for UNRESOLVED was false.** It said quote data is paid. It is not; this
  account returns real NBBO back to at least 2016 for 40 of 40 tickers sampled across all five
  A2 liquidity buckets, including 8 of 8 in the least liquid.
- **Its question was mis-framed** (raised by the owner): *"ปกติการขายเราไม่จำเป็นต้องล๊อกราคาปิด
  หรือราคาเปิดนิ เรามีเวลา 6 ชม ครึ่ง ในการซื้อและขายต่อวัน"*. Nothing requires us to transact at
  the close. Which price the close is only matters under an assumption we never had to make.

So the question is not *which price the close is*. It is: **what does an order cost when it can be
worked anywhere inside a 6.5-hour session, and does working it passively actually save money once
the days you fail to fill are counted?**

## 2. What is being decided

A2 (`../a2_cost_2026-09-15/RESULT_A2_2026-09-15.md`) left the Set-2 book **UNRESOLVED on cost**,
because its estimator returned a bracket of **11.1 – 112.4 bp** per full reformation that straddles
the **30.6 bp** break-even. The bracket is wide because it is an ESTIMATE from daily bars, produced
by a tool this project has twice caught manufacturing spread out of volatility.

**Part 1 of this test replaces that estimate with a measurement.** That is the larger prize here,
and it exists only because of the Test A correction. Parts 2 and 3 then ask whether passive
execution moves whatever Part 1 returns.

## 3. Sample — fixed here, before any measurement

**Tickers.** Deterministic, no randomness, no discretion: within each `liquidity_bucket`, sort by

| buckets | N per bucket | role |
|---|---|---|
| Q1, Q2, Q3 | 40 | **primary** — the contested zone, where A2's bracket straddles the line |
| Q4, Q5 | 10 | **calibration anchor only** (section 5), not part of the verdict |

**Days.** The 10 most recent complete regular sessions ending 2026-09-11 (A2's last bar date).
Fixed list written into the run's output JSON before collection begins.

**Session.** Regular hours only, 14:30:00Z – 21:00:00Z. No pre/post market.

## 4. Definitions, each cited, none invented

- **Quoted half-spread**, bp = `(ask − bid) / 2 / mid × 10,000`, `mid = (ask+bid)/2`, **time-weighted**
  across the session.
- **Effective spread** = twice the absolute deviation of a trade price from the NBBO midpoint
  prevailing at the time of the trade — the SEC Rule 605 construction. **The exact regulatory text
  must be fetched and quoted in the result before any effective-spread number is reported.** If it
  cannot be verified, the statistic is reported as quoted-spread only and the effective-spread
  claim is dropped. (Test A's lesson, applied to a definition rather than a formula.)
- **Carry-forward is mandatory.** The vendor returns quote UPDATES, not a continuous state: KOSS
  on 2026-09-11 has no update at all in the 15:00–15:01 window while being continuously quoted.
  The NBBO at any instant is the last update at or before it. A pipeline that ignores this
  under-samples exactly the illiquid names the test is about.
- **Round trip** = 2 × one-way. **Full reformation** of a long-short book = 4 × one-way half-spread
  (both legs, in and out) — the same convention A2 used, kept so the numbers are comparable.

## 5. VALIDATION GATE — run first, and the test stops here if it fails

Before any Q1–Q3 number is believed, the pipeline is run on the Q4/Q5 anchor sample and must
reproduce published large-cap spreads:

> S&P 500 effective spreads of **1.3 – 2.3 bp** one-way (Hagstromer JFE 2021 / Mackintosh 2024, as
> already cited in A2's sourced-anchor table).

**Pass condition, declared now: the Q5 median one-way half-spread must land in 0.5 – 6 bp.**
Outside that band, the pipeline is wrong and **no other number from this run is reported** — the
result is a pipeline failure, not a market finding.

This gate exists because Test A's whole lesson was that an unvalidated measurement device returns
a confident number with no relationship to the quantity asked for.

## 6. The three measurements

**Part 1 — what the spread actually is.** Per ticker-day: time-weighted quoted half-spread,
effective half-spread (subject to section 4), median and dollar-volume-weighted, by bucket.
Compared directly against A2's 11.1 / 77.8 / 112.4 bp bracket and against the SEC 2013 sourced
46.1 – 61.8 bp for sub-$100M caps.

**Part 2 — what a resting limit order fills at.** Order posted at the first NBBO update at or after
14:30:00Z, limit price fixed at posting, resting until 21:00:00Z. Three arms:

| arm | buy limit | sells |
|---|---|---|
| **M** marketable | the posted ask — always fills | the posted bid |
| **X** midpoint | `mid₀` | `mid₀` |
| **P** passive | the posted bid | the posted ask |

**Fill rule, conservative and pre-declared:** a buy limit at X fills iff `min(ask)` over the session
`≤ X`; a sell limit at X fills iff `max(bid) ≥ X`. Rationale: `ask ≤ X` means a seller was offering
at or below X, so execution is certain. The looser rule (`bid ≤ X`, i.e. price merely touched)
assumes queue priority we cannot observe, and is reported only as an upper bound, never as the
headline.

**Part 3 — the days it does not fill.** This is the part Test A's framing omits entirely, and the
part that decides the answer. Two policies compared over the SAME ticker-days:

- **Policy M**: fill at the marketable price every day. P&L to session close = `(close − ask₀)/ask₀`.
- **Policy X**: fill at `mid₀` only when the fill rule says so; hold nothing otherwise.

Reported: mean P&L per ticker-day for each, **and** mean P&L per unit of exposure, because Policy X
takes fewer positions and a raw comparison would flatter it for being absent. Both directions
(buy and sell) measured on every ticker-day, reported separately — no strategy or signal is used,
so nothing here consumes a look at sealed data.

**The number that settles it:** `saving on filled days − return foregone by not holding on unfilled
days`. If that is negative, passive execution is an accounting illusion.

## 8. My predictions, recorded before any number exists

**B1.** Measured effective spreads will come in **materially BELOW** the EDGE estimator's 112.4 bp
high end, because effective spreads sit inside quoted spreads and the estimator manufactures
spread from volatility. I expect Q1 one-way to land nearer the SEC's sourced 46–62 bp than the
estimator's 106 bp.

**B2.** Policy X (midpoint) will fill on a **majority** of ticker-days given a full 6.5-hour
session — the owner's point that time is on our side is correct on the fill-rate side.

**B3.** **Most of the midpoint saving will be given back by fill selection.** I expect Policy X's
net advantage over Policy M to be positive but **substantially smaller than half the quoted
spread**, and I would not be surprised by zero.

**B4.** L3 is the most likely outcome overall.

**What would refute me:** B3 coming out at or near the full half-spread saving, i.e. fill selection
costing nothing. That would mean a resting order is close to a free option, and I would have to
withdraw the adverse-selection argument I have used repeatedly in conversation without ever
measuring it.
