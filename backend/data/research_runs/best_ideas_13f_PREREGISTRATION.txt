1. HYPOTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H: Stocks that represent the LARGEST ACTIVE BET of many institutional managers
   — the names those managers hold at a far greater weight than a benchmark
   would imply — earn higher subsequent returns than stocks that are no
   manager's top bet, on the point-in-time large-cap S&P 500 universe over
   2015-2026, USING ONLY INFORMATION PUBLICLY AVAILABLE AT EACH FORMATION.

Direction: high best-idea intensity = long. The harness's top-is-long
convention puts heavily-conviction-weighted names in the long leg.

MECHANISM, and it is an AGENCY story, not a pure mispricing story. The claim
is not that markets are broadly inefficient. It is that fund managers are paid
to gather costly information but face asset-gathering incentives and
tracking-error constraints that push them to overdiversify — so whatever real
skill they have is concentrated in their few largest active tilts, and is
diluted to nothing across the long tail of "portfolio-rounding" positions.

THIS FAMILY TESTS THE OUTSIDER'S VERSION OF THE CLAIM, WHICH IS STRICTLY
HARDER THAN THE PAPER'S PRIMARY TEST. The source paper's headline result
identifies best ideas "as of the true holding date of the fund manager's
portfolio", explicitly because it is "primarily interested in whether managers
have stock-picking ability, not whether outsiders can piggyback on the
information content in managers' holdings data" (verbatim, section IV). We
cannot and do not test that. We can only see a portfolio ~45 days after the
quarter it describes, so this family tests the piggyback version — which the
paper addresses only in a footnote and one figure (see section 2). A negative
here is therefore NOT a negative on the paper's own headline claim.

VERIFIED — THE HEADLINE CLAIM, from the retrieved abstract, verbatim:
    "We find that the stocks in which active mutual fund or hedge fund
     managers display the most conviction towards ex-ante, their 'Best ideas,'
     outperform the market, as well as the other stocks in those managers'
     portfolios, by approximately 2.8 to 4.5 percent per year, depending on
     the benchmark employed. The vast majority of the other stocks managers
     hold do not exhibit significant outperformance."

VERIFIED — THE EXACT RANKING STATISTIC. The paper's Figure 4 caption states
the information-ratio measure in closed form, verbatim (subscripts are lost in
PDF text extraction and are restored here in brackets):
    "Best ideas are determined within each fund as the stock with the maximum
     value of the following information ratio measure, IR[ift] = sigma[it]
     (lambda[ift] - lambda[iMt]) where lambda[ift] is manager f's portfolio
     weight in stock i, lambda[iMt] is the weight of stock i in the market
     portfolio, and sigma[it] is the most-recent estimate of a stock's
     CAPM-idiosyncratic volatility."
This is the load-bearing formula for this family and it is quoted, not derived
from memory. It also matches the algebra the paper's section III sets out
(alpha = k Sigma_t tilt, IR = alpha/sigma, hence IR proportional to
sigma_it x tilt_ift).

VERIFIED — THE THREE TILT MEASURES, from section III and section V.E:
  * market alpha:    tilt[ft] = lambda[ft] - lambda[Mt]     (vs market weights)
  * portfolio alpha: tilt[ft] = lambda[ft] - lambda[fVt], where lambda[fVt] is
    "the value-weight portfolio consisting only of stocks the manager actually
    holds" (verbatim)
  * conviction:      alpha[ft] = lambda[ft], best = arg max lambda[ift]
    — introduced specifically for the paper's 13F HEDGE FUND sample, because
    "hedge funds arguably care less about benchmarks and tracking error than
    mutual funds" (verbatim). THIS IS THE MEASURE THE PAPER ITSELF APPLIES TO
    13F DATA, which is the only data this family has.

VERIFIED — THE LICENSE TO DROP SIGMA. From section III, verbatim:
    "For similar reasons, we confirm that robustness checks based on the
     assumption that all stocks have equal idiosyncratic risk provide
     qualitatively similar results."
This matters enormously for what is constructible here (section 4): under
equal idiosyncratic risk, IR ordering collapses to TILT ordering, so the
market and portfolio measures become computable from 13F data alone, with no
per-stock volatility estimate for the thousands of names outside our price
universe. This family uses that assumption and it is the PAPER'S OWN stated
robustness variant, not this project's invention.

VERIFIED — THE PAPER'S OWN 13F/HEDGE-FUND RESULTS, Table V Panel A, verbatim
figures: five-factor alphas of "31 basis points (t-statistic of 3.21), 31 basis

VERIFIED — THE PIGGYBACK CLAIM IS A FOOTNOTE, NOT A HEADLINE. Footnote 4,

3. THE HONEST PRIOR, STATED BEFORE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A tradeable edge here is UNLIKELY, for five reasons fixed on the record now:

 (i)  EVIDENTIARY BASE. Seventeen years in open circulation, five years frozen
      at one draft, no peer review, no marked revise-and-resubmit (section 2).
      Every other family this project has tested rested on a published JF/JFE/
      RFS paper. This one does not.

 (ii) WE TEST THE HARD VERSION. The paper's own headline uses TRUE holding
      dates. We are restricted to public filing dates, ~45+ days stale, which
      the paper supports only by footnote assertion (section 2).

 (iii) 13F IS PUBLIC, FREE, AND ALREADY MINED. Whale-watching 13F portfolios is
      one of the most widely-replicated retail and institutional activities in
      US equities, with commercial products, ETFs and free websites built on
      exactly this file. If a simple public-date 13F sort worked on mega-caps,
      it would be the most crowded trade in this codebase.

 (iv) DISPERSION KILLS POWER ON A LARGE-CAP UNIVERSE. The paper's own section
      V.A says 72% of best ideas belong to exactly one manager, i.e. best
      ideas are spread thinly across the WHOLE CRSP universe. Restricted to
      ~500 S&P 500 names we see only the large-cap slice, where conviction
      bets are least distinctive.

 (v)  OUR MANAGER UNIVERSE IS DIRTIER THAN THE PAPER'S BY CONSTRUCTION
      (section 5). We cannot exclude banks and mutual-fund complexes the way
      the paper's hand-classified hedge-fund sample does.

The two interesting outcomes are (a) an honest negative, or (b) an apparent
positive that is really a size/momentum/crowding artifact. The grid in
section 6 is built so (b) cannot be reported as a success by accident.

There is no result this pre-registration is hoping for.

4. DATA — REAL SOURCES, CONFIRMED LIVE 2026-09-02
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SOURCE A — SEC FORM 13F STRUCTURED DATA SETS. Index page
https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets was
fetched live; 53 quarterly ZIP archives were enumerated from its own links,
running 2013q2 through the 01mar2026-31may2026 window. Files were downloaded
directly from sec.gov with a real User-Agent under SEC's fair-access policy.
Each archive holds tab-separated tables; the ones this family reads are:
  * SUBMISSION.tsv  — ACCESSION_NUMBER, FILING_DATE, SUBMISSIONTYPE, CIK,
                      PERIODOFREPORT
  * COVERPAGE.tsv   — FILINGMANAGER_NAME and address/report metadata
  * INFOTABLE.tsv   — one row per reported position: NAMEOFISSUER, TITLEOFCLASS,
                      CUSIP, FIGI, VALUE, SSHPRNAMT, SSHPRNAMTTYPE, PUTCALL,
                      INVESTMENTDISCRETION, VOTING_AUTH_*

  THE FILE ORGANISATION IS BY FILING DATE, NOT REPORT PERIOD, AND THIS WAS
  MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED. In the real 2016q1 archive, FILING_DATE falls
  entirely in Jan/Feb/Mar 2016 (4,953 FEB, 1,041 JAN, 78 MAR) while
  PERIODOFREPORT is 31-DEC-2015 for 5,870 of ~6,072 submissions, with a long
  tail of late filers reporting older quarters. That is the 45-day statutory
  lag visible directly in the data, and it is why this family keys everything
  on FILING_DATE (section 7).

  SUBMISSIONTYPE in that same real archive: 4,268 13F-HR, 1,523 13F-NT,
  271 13F-HR/A, 10 13F-NT/A. 13F-NT are notice filings carrying NO holdings
  and are dropped. Amendments are handled point-in-time (section 7).

  THE RAW DATA IS DIRTY AND THIS IS MEASURED, NOT WISHED AWAY. The very first
  INFOTABLE row of the real 2016q1 archive has NAMEOFISSUER "0", TITLEOFCLASS
  "0", CUSIP "0        " and VALUE 0. Malformed rows like this are refused by
  counted reasons, never silently coerced.

SOURCE B — CUSIP -> TICKER, from SEC's own Fails-to-Deliver files. 13F reports
CUSIPs and nothing else usable as an identifier (the FIGI column exists but was
empty in the 2016q1 sample). This project has no CUSIP licence and no
commercial security master, so the mapping must come from a free, official,
dated source. SEC's fails-to-deliver files
(https://www.sec.gov/files/data/fails-deliver-data/cnsfails<YYYYMM><a|b>.zip,
enumerated live from https://www.sec.gov/data/foiadocsfailsdatahtm) are
pipe-delimited with the header, verbatim from the real 201603a file:
    SETTLEMENT DATE|CUSIP|SYMBOL|QUANTITY (FAILS)|DESCRIPTION|PRICE
so every row is an official SEC-published (CUSIP, SYMBOL) pair stamped with a
settlement date. ~60,000 rows per semi-monthly file.

  KNOWN LIMITATIONS OF THIS MAPPING, stated before it is used:
   * COVERAGE IS INCIDENTAL, NOT A REGISTRY. A security appears only if it
     had a settlement fail. For S&P 500 names over a multi-year window this
     should be near-complete, but it is NOT guaranteed, and realized coverage
     is MEASURED per run and reported, never assumed.
   * IT IS A SECURITY-LEVEL, NOT ISSUER-LEVEL, MAP. Multi-class issuers
     (GOOG/GOOGL, BRK.B) get distinct CUSIPs, which is correct here but means
     a manager's stake can be split across classes.
   * TICKERS AND CUSIPS BOTH GET REUSED over time on corporate actions. The
     map is therefore built PER-PERIOD from dated files rather than as one
     flat all-time dictionary, and a (cusip, ticker) pair is only applied to
     13F filings in a bounded window around the file that evidences it.
   * NO CHECK-DIGIT VALIDATION IS TREATED AS AUTHORITY. CUSIPs are normalised
     (upper-cased, whitespace-stripped, 9-char) and obviously-malformed values
     are refused by counted reason.

SOURCE C — PRICES. yfinance daily history, the same provider every S&P 500
equity family here uses.

SOURCE D — POINT-IN-TIME UNIVERSE. The existing S&P 500 point-in-time
membership panel (sp500_membership_history.was_member), gated per formation,
identical to every sibling family.

NO SYNTHETIC OR SIMULATED DATA IS USED ANYWHERE IN THE PRODUCTION RUN. Test
fixtures are synthetic by necessity and are never read by the run.

5. MANAGER UNIVERSE — WHAT WE DO, AND THE ONE THING WE CANNOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

APPLIED, following the paper's own rules where the data permits:
  * 13F-HR (and 13F-HR/A) only; 13F-NT notice filings dropped.
  * Equity long positions only: PUTCALL must be blank (option positions are
    notional and would corrupt portfolio weights), SSHPRNAMTTYPE must be SH.
  * At least 5 recorded holdings           [paper: "at least 5 recorded holdings"]
  * Total reported equity value > $5m      [paper: "exceeding $5 million"]
  * NAME-BASED PASSIVE/INDEX SCREEN on FILINGMANAGER_NAME, the closest
    available analogue of the paper's fund-name screen.
  * TOP 25% ACTIVENESS CUT, per formation: a manager is eligible only if its
    maximum position-level tilt is in the top quartile of all managers'
    maxima visible at that date [paper: "top 25% most active"].

THE THING WE CANNOT DO, AND IT IS THE BIGGEST WEAKNESS OF THIS FAMILY.
The paper's 13F results rest on Agrawal-Jiang-Tang-Yang's MANUAL
classification of every 13F institution into hedge funds vs banks vs mutual
fund companies, yielding 1,662 hand-identified pure-play hedge funds. That
dataset is hand-built, academic, and not available to this project. There is
no free, official, machine-readable registry of "which 13F filers are
pure-play hedge funds" — 13F filings themselves carry no such field.

  WHAT WE SUBSTITUTE: the mechanical screens above. A name screen catches
  obvious index and ETF sponsors but CANNOT distinguish a discretionary
  long-only equity boutique from a bank trust department from a quant shop.

  WHY THE SUBSTITUTE IS PARTIALLY DEFENSIBLE, stated without overclaiming: the
  top-25%-activeness cut is not a name filter but a BEHAVIOURAL one, and it
  mechanically demotes exactly the filers the paper excludes — a hugely
  diversified index-tracking or bank-trust filer has tiny maximum position
  weights and therefore tiny maximum tilts, so it fails the activeness cut on
  its own portfolio's arithmetic. That is a real mitigation. It is NOT a
  replacement for hand classification, and this family will not claim it is.

  DISCLOSED CONSEQUENCE: our manager universe is dirtier than the paper's.
  Any negative result from this family is therefore confounded with universe
  quality and CANNOT be read as a clean refutation of the paper's hedge-fund
  finding. This is written here, before results, precisely so it cannot be
  offered later as an excuse for a bad number.

TWO FURTHER 13F-SPECIFIC HAZARDS, disclosed:
  * 13F is filed by the INSTITUTION, not the fund. A multi-strategy complex
    reports one combined portfolio, so genuine per-fund conviction is blurred.
    The paper says this too ("Since 13F data is for the institution rather
    than the fund"). We cannot fix it.
  * 13F shows LONGS ONLY. A manager's largest active bet may be a short we
    never see. The measured "best idea" is the best LONG idea.

6. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 36 DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    best-idea measure  {conviction, market_tilt, portfolio_tilt}     3
  x aggregation        {count, share}                                2
  x holding period     {63, 126, 252} trading days                   3
  x rank fraction      {decile 0.1, quintile 0.2}                    2
  = 36 definitions, portfolio = long_short throughout.

n_trials = 36 for the deflated Sharpe. This family's OWN denominator, with no
carried trials from any other family: it is a fresh hypothesis from an
independent literature, and the entire grid is fixed here before any number
exists. THE DENOMINATOR IS THE HONEST COUNT OF WHAT IS SEARCHED. 36 is a large
denominator and it raises this family's bar; that is the correct consequence of
searching 36 definitions and it is accepted deliberately rather than trimmed.

THE THREE MEASURES, all computed per manager-filing over that manager's FULL
reported portfolio (not just our universe), then aggregated to our universe:

  conviction     best_f = arg max_i lambda[ift]
                 The paper's own 13F/hedge-fund measure (section 2). Needs no
                 benchmark and no volatility estimate: exact, not a proxy.

  market_tilt    best_f = arg max_i ( lambda[ift] - lambda[iMt] )
                 The paper's market measure under its OWN stated equal-
                 idiosyncratic-risk robustness assumption (section 2), which
                 removes sigma from the ordering.
                 lambda[iMt] IS A DISCLOSED PROXY: the AGGREGATE 13F PORTFOLIO
                 — total reported value in stock i across all filers visible
                 at t, divided by total reported value across all stocks and
                 filers. It is NOT the CRSP value-weighted market. It is the
                 institutionally-held market. Chosen because it is the only
                 market-weight vector that is (a) real, (b) point-in-time, and
                 (c) defined over the SAME universe as lambda[ift] — a market
                 weight computed over only our 500-name price universe would
                 have a different denominator from the manager weight and the
                 difference would be arithmetically meaningless.
                 ITS BIAS, STATED IN ADVANCE: stocks with low institutional
                 ownership (founder- or insider-controlled firms) carry
                 understated market weights, which mechanically inflates every
                 manager's apparent tilt toward them.

  portfolio_tilt best_f = arg max_i ( lambda[ift] - lambda[ifVt] )
                 The paper's portfolio measure, again under equal idiosyncratic
                 risk. lambda[ifVt] is the value weight of stock i within the
                 value-weighted portfolio of ONLY the stocks manager f holds,
                 with the same aggregate-13F value used as the capitalisation
                 proxy and renormalised over f's held set.

THE TWO AGGREGATIONS, from manager-level best ideas to a cross-sectional signal:

  count   signal_i(t) = number of eligible managers whose most recent VISIBLE
          13F names i as their best idea. This IS the paper's own aggregation
          ("if more than one manager considers a stock a best idea, we
          overweight accordingly", section V.B).

  share   signal_i(t) = count_i(t) / (number of eligible managers holding i at
          t). THIS IS THIS PROJECT'S OWN VARIANT AND IS LABELLED AS SUCH — it
          is NOT in the paper. Rationale, fixed in advance: a widely-held
          mega-cap is mechanically somebody's top position more often than a
          thinly-held name, so `count` is partly a popularity/size proxy.
          `share` asks the popularity-controlled question — of the managers
          who hold this name, what fraction have it as their single highest-
          conviction bet. Including BOTH is what makes the size-artifact
          reading testable rather than a post-hoc excuse.

HOLDING PERIODS {63, 126, 252}. 63 trading days is the paper's own cadence
("rebalanced on the first day of every quarter") and the natural one, since
13F refreshes quarterly. 126 and 252 are robustness variants supported by the
paper's Figure 4 finding that best-idea outperformance "does not revert in the
months or even years after first appearing in the portfolio". A 21-day hold is
EXCLUDED up front: the signal cannot change more often than quarterly, so a
monthly hold re-pays turnover on an unchanged ranking.

MANAGER-VIEW STALENESS: a manager's filing stops counting after 200 calendar
days, so a filer who stops filing does not haunt the panel forever. Fixed here;
not searched over.

COSTS: DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS (5 bps one-way), identical to every S&P 500 equity
family here so Sharpes stay comparable. financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 — this
project's standing DISCLOSED optimism about short borrow, not an estimate.

UNIVERSE: the point-in-time S&P 500 union universe gated per formation by
was_member, matching the sibling families.

7. POINT-IN-TIME DISCIPLINE — THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT CORRECTNESS PROPERTY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE RULE, and it admits no exception: a 13F filing contributes to the signal on
and after its own FILING_DATE, and never one day earlier. Not its
PERIODOFREPORT, not the statutory 45-day deadline, not a quarter-end.

WHY FILING_DATE AND NOTHING ELSE. It is the date the document became public on
EDGAR, so it is the date an outsider could have acted on it. It is also robust
to every anomaly in the real data: late filers reporting a two-year-old quarter
(58 such submissions for 30-SEP-2015 sit in the real 2016q1 archive), and
implausible same-day filings (a real row reports PERIODOFREPORT 31-MAR-2016
with FILING_DATE 31-MAR-2016). Under a period-keyed rule those rows would be
look-ahead or would need special-casing; under a FILING_DATE-keyed rule they
are simply correct, because a document IS knowable the day it is filed.

AMENDMENTS. A 13F-HR/A restates an earlier report. Point-in-time honesty means
the ORIGINAL is what was visible between its filing date and the amendment's
filing date, and the amendment takes over only from ITS OWN filing date. The
panel is therefore built by replaying filings in FILING_DATE order and letting
the latest-filed record for a given (manager, period) win as of the date it
was filed — never retroactively. Restatements never rewrite history.

STRUCTURAL GUARANTEE. The cross-sectional signal is supplied to the harness as
CrossSectionalData.fundamental_signal, a daily step panel the harness slices to
rows <= the formation date, so formation-time look-ahead is structurally
impossible on top of the filing-date construction.

THIS WILL BE TESTED DIRECTLY AND THE TEST IS PRE-DECLARED HERE:
 (a) a filing dated D contributes 0 to the panel on every date < D and a
     non-zero contribution on D (a boundary test on the exact day, not "around"
     it);
 (b) taking a REAL filing from the cached SEC data and artificially moving its
     FILING_DATE later removes its contribution from every date in between,
     and moving it earlier adds it — i.e. the panel is a function of
     FILING_DATE and not of PERIODOFREPORT;
 (c) the realized minimum gap between PERIODOFREPORT and the date a value
     first appears in the production panel is MEASURED across the whole real
     run and reported; it must never be negative, and is expected to sit at or
     above the statutory ~45 days for the overwhelming majority of filings;
 (d) an amendment filed after the original does not change any panel value
     dated before the amendment's own filing date.

8. PASS / FAIL THRESHOLD — FIXED NOW, BEFORE ANY RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This family is reported as a VALIDATED EDGE only if BOTH hold:

  (i)  the best spec's deflated Sharpe (DSR, computed at n_trials = 36)
       exceeds 0.95; AND
  (ii) that spec's result is not an artifact of the `count` aggregation alone
       — i.e. its `share` counterpart at the same measure, holding period and
       rank fraction must also show a materially positive Sharpe rather than
       collapsing toward zero.

Condition (ii) is this family's version of the sibling NOA family's
sector-artifact trap: `count` is partly a popularity/size proxy, so a `count`
positive that vanishes under `share` is a size bet wearing a best-ideas
costume, and this family will say so plainly rather than reporting the count
number.

Anything else is an honest negative, or an honest artifact, and is written up
as such. A well-documented honest negative is a FULLY SUCCESSFUL OUTCOME by
this project's standards, and roughly 25 of this codebase's ~28 tested families
have been exactly that.

EVERY ONE of the 36 specs is persisted to cross_sectional_trial_results under
family_key "best_ideas_13f" regardless of outcome — winners, losers and nulls
alike. No spec is dropped, re-run, or re-specified after seeing its number. If a
bug is found after the run, the fix is re-running the WHOLE grid and replacing
the whole run tag, never patching individual rows.

9. WHAT A NEGATIVE WILL AND WILL NOT LICENSE US TO SAY

Written now so the results document cannot quietly overclaim later.

A negative here will NOT refute Antón-Cohen-Polk. Four reasons, all structural
and all known in advance:
  * They identify best ideas at TRUE holding dates; we are restricted to
    public filing dates ~45+ days later (section 1).
  * They use a hand-classified pure-play hedge fund universe; ours is
    mechanically screened and admits banks and fund complexes (section 5).
  * They rank over the whole CRSP cross-section; we rank ~500 large caps,
    where the paper's own 72%-single-manager dispersion statistic says best
    ideas are least distinctive (section 3).
  * They measure factor-model alpha on a long-only best-ideas portfolio; we
    measure a long-short Sharpe on a cross-sectional rank. These are different
    statistics and a null in one does not imply a null in the other.

A negative also will NOT establish that 13F holdings data is uninformative in
general.

What a negative WILL establish is the only question that matters for this
project: this construction, on this universe, at public filing dates, with
this manager universe, is not tradeable by us now — and that 13F best-ideas
should not be re-tested here without genuinely new data (a wider cross-section,
a real hedge-fund classification, or true holding dates), carrying these 36
trials into the denominator of anything that does.
