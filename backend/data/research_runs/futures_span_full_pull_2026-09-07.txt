A REAL DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED (not hypothetical -- this broke the first
full-data run): 3 of 31 instruments (LE, ZF, ZT) failed to chain with
"contract X is not priced on both date A and date B; a roll needs an
overlap day" -- chained_contract_daily_returns correctly refusing to guess
rather than silently producing a fabricated return. Root cause, found by
hand-inspecting the actual failing rows: CME's SPAN archive prints a
placeholder Type-82 record for a not-yet-fully-configured far contract
month, carrying settle 0.0 AND a self-referential expiration_date equal to
its own trade date --
  ZT/ZF contract_month "201612" on 2015-09-25 and 2015-09-28:
    settle 0.0, expiration_date "20150925" (the SAME day as the first
    occurrence). The REAL 201612 contract, with its real expiration
    (20161230) and real prices, does not start appearing until 2016-04-01.
  LE contract_month "201710" on 2016-06-14/15: identical pattern.
Because the stub's fabricated expiration_date is the smallest of all
candidates quoted that day, the calendar roll rule treated it as the new
front month and tried to roll into a contract with no real price and no
prior-day quote.

FIX: exclude settle == 0.0 rows from roll-rule candidacy (they are still
kept in the underlying data and counted in diagnostics
n_zero_settle_candidate_exclusions). Justified because a genuine settlement
of exactly 0.0 does not occur for any instrument in this 31-root universe
-- checked by hand for all 72 zero-settle rows in the full pull (see JOB 2
above): the other 69 are all either already-expired as of their own trade
date (excluded by the pre-existing filter regardless) or far-deferred
months nowhere near that date's minimum expiration. Only the 3 ZT/ZF/LE
stub rows carry both conditions (zero settle AND a self-referential
expiration date) that make them look like "expiring today" under the
calendar rule alone.

RESULT AFTER THE FIX: 31/31 instruments chain successfully across the full

listing history). Before the fix: 28/31. Manifest:
