MECHANISM, in the paper's own words. Abstract, verbatim: "Margin credit,
defined as the excess debt capacity of investors buying securities on the
margin, is a very strong predictor of aggregate stock returns."

The economic story, p.2 verbatim: "A credit in the margin account is typically
posted when a levered long position appreciates in value and the investor
decides not to reinvest the gain... Hence, a decision not to reinvest the gain
results in excess debt capacity. This is a 'hold' signal coming from winning
investors. That is, the investors who are ex-post correct about their past
beliefs now have pessimistic view about future returns. We thus expect an
inverse relationship between margin credit and future returns."

2. MECHANISM HONESTY — WHAT KIND OF CANDIDATE THIS ACTUALLY IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS NOT A MECHANICAL FORCED-FLOW TEST, AND WILL NOT BE WRITTEN UP AS ONE.
The source paper is explicit that its measure is VOLUNTARY and INFORMATIONAL,
and it goes out of its way to distinguish itself from the forced-deleveraging
story. Section 2.3, p.9 verbatim, about margin DEBT (the measure it rejects):
"In case of such forced deleveraging, margin debt balance drops AFTER the fall
in price, and hence is not useful as a predictive signal for future price
movements." And p.10 verbatim, about margin CREDIT (the measure it adopts):
"margin credit only results from appreciation of value of long positions
indicating that the investors with margin credit have been correct in the past.
This focus on winning investors potentially allows margin credit to extract
beliefs of relatively sophisticated investors."

Section 1, p.6 verbatim, on how it differs from the margin-constraint
literature: "While this literature focuses on the impact of margin requirements
or capital constraints, we empirically show that VOLUNTARY reduction in
leverage by margin investors has information about future returns."

So the correct placement is: this candidate belongs to the "aggregate investor
POSITIONING REVEALS INFORMATION not yet in price" tradition — the same broad
family as Lou (2012) flow-induced trading, Frazzini-Lamont "Dumb Money", and
Rapach-Ringgenberg-Zhou (2016) aggregate short interest (which [DKP16] itself
uses as its closest comparison predictor) — and NOT to the purely mechanical
forced-flow tradition (Coval-Stafford fire sales, options-dealer gamma hedging,
index-inclusion pressure). Lou (2012) and the Dumb-Money construction have
BOTH already been tested in this project. That overlap is a reason to be MORE
sceptical of a positive here, not less, and it is priced into section 10.

The honest one-line characterisation, which is the one that goes in the report:
a smart-money SENTIMENT/BELIEF signal read off a regulatory balance-sheet
aggregate — flow-adjacent, not flow-mechanical.

3. THE PAPER'S OWN CONSTRUCTION, QUOTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 THE TWO RAW SERIES (Section 3, p.11, verbatim)

"We construct scaled margin debt and scaled margin credit from monthly data on
the value borrowed by all investors with NYSE member organizations and the
amount held by the same investors which could be withdrawn. The data are end of
month values and FINRA rule 4521 requires that these numbers be reported for
only investor accounts used to take long positions on margin."

Footnote 6, p.11 verbatim: "Rule 4521(d) requires that a member must only
include free credit balances in cash and securities margin accounts in the
report. Balances in short accounts and in special memorandum accounts... are
not considered free credit balances."

3.2 THE CONCEPTUAL DEFINITION (Section 2.2, p.8, verbatim)

    Margin Credit = (Position Value) * (1 - Margin Requirement) - Margin Debt.

"(1 - Margin Requirement) is the maximum debt the investor can take as a
fraction of the position value... Once we subtract the debt already taken, we
get margin credit which is nothing but excess debt capacity."

THIS IS THE EXPOSITORY IDENTITY, NOT THE EMPIRICAL RECIPE. The empirical
measure (Section 3, p.11) is the REPORTED aggregate free-credit-balance series,
not a quantity recomputed from position values. This build uses the reported
series, as the paper does. The identity is implemented anyway, in
`margin_credit_identity`, purely so the paper's own four-row worked example
(p.7-8: 10 shares, 60% margin requirement, price 100 -> 250 gives margin credit
600) can be pinned in a unit test — a formula-from-memory guard, not a
production code path.

3.3 SCALING (Section 3, p.11, verbatim)

"The raw margin statistics numbers are reported in millions of dollars. We
scale these values so they are relative to the size of the economy by dividing
by nominal GDP."

3.4 THE REPORTING LAG (Section 3, p.11, verbatim)

"The data is available at the NYSE and FINRA websites with a two month delay.
To account for the two month reporting delay, we use margin debt and credit
numbers that are two months old to avoid look-ahead bias. For example, we use
the June 1995 numbers for August 1995."

IMPLEMENTED EXACTLY: the signal formed at the end of month t uses the FINRA
reading for month t-2. Getting this wrong is a look-ahead bug and is asserted
in the test suite against the paper's own June-1995/August-1995 example.

3.5 THE GDP VINTAGE RULE (Section 3, p.12, verbatim)

"For the purposes of in-sample testing we take the values announced in Q4 2015
which have the fourth, usually final, revisions for the numbers through Q4
2014. For out of sample testing, the GDP numbers that are available to
investors at that time of making a prediction are used to avoid any look-ahead
bias. So for making a prediction in August 1997, we use the numbers available
in the Q2 1997 announcement. The GDP numbers used are further lagged by taking
the Q1 1997 GDP value from Q2 1997 announcement. This last adjustment is done
because there seems to be the largest change in value from the first to second
revision in GDP announcements."

3.6 THE DETRENDING REGRESSIONS (Section 3, p.16, verbatim, displayed equations)

    MarginCredit_t / GDP_t = alpha_c + beta_c * t + u_t
    MarginDebt_t   / GDP_t = alpha_d + beta_d * t + v_t

"The residuals from these regressions u_t and v_t are our predictors, MC and
MD, respectively... these measures are standardized with mean zero and standard
deviation 1... For out-of-sample tests, MC and MD are computed RECURSIVELY
using only the data available up to time t to avoid look-ahead bias."

THAT LAST SENTENCE IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT ANTI-LOOK-AHEAD DETAIL IN THE
WHOLE BUILD and it is why the grid below carries `insample` and `recursive` as
two separate, separately-counted, separately-reported arms that are NEVER
averaged and NEVER reported as one number. A full-sample detrend used inside
something labelled "out-of-sample" would be exactly the class of bug this
project's zero-fabrication rule exists to catch.

The paper's justification for detrending at all (p.15-16): Perron-Yabu trend
test t-statistic 3.36 (significant at 1%), Ng-Perron unit-root test rejects a
unit root against trend-stationarity at 10% (statistic -2.58, critical value
-2.57), KPSS rejects at 5% (statistic 0.1614, critical value 0.146). THIS
BUILD RE-TESTS THE TREND ON ITS OWN SAMPLE rather than assuming it: the
reported diagnostics include the OLS trend slope, its HAC (Newey-West)
t-statistic and an ADF test on the detrended residual, for every definition
arm. The paper's tests were run on 1984-2014 NYSE data; this build's sample is
different (section 4.1) and a trend that exists there is not automatically
present here.

3.7 THE DEPENDENT VARIABLE (Section 3, p.12-13, verbatim)

"we measure this excess return as the log of the return to the S&P 500 minus
the log of the return to a one month Treasury bill."

3.8 THE PREDICTIVE REGRESSION (Section 4.1, Eq. (1), p.18, verbatim)

    r_{t:t+H} = alpha + beta * x_t + eps_{t:t+H}                              (1)

"where r_{t:t+H} is the AVERAGE MONTHLY S&P 500 log excess return for month
t+1 to month t+H, and x_t the predictor variable which part of investors'
information set at time t." H in {1, 3, 6, 12}. Expected sign: beta < 0.

3.9 THE TRADEABLE STRATEGIES (Section 5, p.26-28)

MEAN-VARIANCE, Eq. (9), p.26 verbatim:

    w_t = (1 / gamma) * (r_hat_{t+1} / sigma_hat^2_{t+1})                     (9)

"where gamma is the risk-aversion coefficient, r_hat_{t+1} is the out-of-sample
forecast of the simple excess return, and sigma_hat^2_{t+1} is the variance
forecast. We follow Campbell and Thompson (2008) and estimate sigma_hat^2_{t+1}
using monthly returns over a 10 year moving window. As in Rapach, Ringgenberg,
and Zhou (2016), we restrict w_t to lie between -0.5 and 1.5 and consider
gamma = 3."

LONG-ONLY, p.28 verbatim: "a long only investor that invests either 100% in the
equity market or 100% in the risk-free asset. The investments weights are
determined by the prediction of one month ahead excess log return to the S&P
500. THE INVESTMENT WEIGHT IS 1 IN S&P 500, WHEN THE PREDICTION IS POSITIVE AND
0 OTHERWISE. Buy and hold corresponds to the investor passively holding the
market portfolio."

NOTE WHAT THIS MEANS, because it is easy to get wrong and would change the
whole build: the weight is NOT a direct function of MC's level or sign. It is a
function of the sign of a RECURSIVELY-ESTIMATED PREDICTIVE-REGRESSION FORECAST
r_hat_{t+1} = alpha_hat_t + beta_hat_t * MC_t, where alpha_hat_t and beta_hat_t
use data through t only. The signal enters only through that forecast.

4. DEVIATIONS FROM THE SOURCE, EVERY ONE NAMED AND REASONED ABOUT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 DEVIATION D1 — THE BIG ONE. THE FREE MARGIN-CREDIT SERIES HAS A CONFIRMED
    DEFINITIONAL BREAK AT FEBRUARY 2010, AND THE PAPER'S EXACT MEASURE DOES NOT
    EXIST FOR FREE BEFORE THAT DATE.

    Established by DIRECT INSPECTION of FINRA's own file and FINRA's own
    footnote, both fetched 2026-09-06, not inferred and not assumed.

    THE FILE. https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2021-03/margin-statistics.xlsx
    (HTTP 200, 20,426 bytes, sha256 af947a17abf21df44b2d3657bd4e9fe7ea0c30db7cb367ab0483e394148fbf05).
    One sheet, "Customer Margin Balances", 355 monthly rows 1997-01 .. 2026-07,
    reported in $ millions, four columns:
        A Year-Month
        B Debit Balances in Customers' Securities Margin Accounts
        C Free Credit Balances in Customers' Cash Accounts
        D Free Credit Balances in Customers' Securities Margin Accounts
    Column B is complete for all 355 months. COLUMN D IS EMPTY FOR THE FIRST
    157 MONTHS (1997-01 .. 2010-01) and populated for the last 198 (2010-02 ..
    2026-07).

    FINRA'S OWN EXPLANATION, verbatim from the margin-statistics page:
    "Through January 2010, NYSE and FINRA each independently collected similar
    margin data from their respective member firms but COMBINED THE FREE CREDIT
    BALANCES IN BOTH CASH AND MARGIN INTO A SINGLE AMOUNT." And: "As of
    February 2010, data are collected pursuant to FINRA Rule 4521 and are
    aggregated across all member firms, regardless of whether the firm was
    designated to NASD or the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) before the
    consolidation..."

    So there are TWO breaks at the same date: a DEFINITIONAL one (combined ->
    split) and a POPULATION one (per-SRO membership -> all FINRA members).
    Both were measured rather than assumed. At the seam:
        2010-01  debit 266,455   free credit (combined) 281,035
        2010-02  debit 263,657   free credit  106,131 cash + 164,624 margin
                                            = 270,755 combined
    i.e. debit moves -1.05% and the RECONSTRUCTED COMBINED free credit moves
    -3.66% across the seam, both ordinary month-on-month moves. The population
    change is therefore immaterial at this resolution, and the combined total
    is continuous across the seam; only the SPLIT is new.

    THIS IS THE SAME CLASS OF PROBLEM THE AUTHORS THEMSELVES HIT AT THE OTHER
    END OF HISTORY and it is handled the same way they handled it. [DKP16]
    p.12 verbatim: "Margin statistics are available from January 1959. However
    revisions to Reg T in June 1983 make post-1983 margin credit incomparable
    to pre-1983 margin credit. To insure comparability of data across time we
    begin our sample in 1984."

    THE RESOLUTION, FIXED HERE, BEFORE ANY RESULT: two definition arms, both
    built, both reported, neither allowed to stand in silently for the other.

      ARM `faithful`  MC = column D alone, free credit balances in SECURITIES
                      MARGIN accounts. This is [DKP16]'s measure as written —
                      "the amount held by the same investors [who borrowed]
                      which could be withdrawn" — with no definitional break
                      anywhere inside it. COST: the sample starts 2010-02, so
                      the first tradeable month is 2010-04 after the two-month
                      reporting lag. SHORT.

      ARM `proxy`     MC = the reconstructed COMBINED free credit balance:
                      column C alone through 2010-01 (where C *is* the combined
                      figure), column C + column D from 2010-02 on. Continuous
                      by construction and continuous in fact (-3.66% at the
                      seam). COST: it is NOT the paper's measure. It pools free
                      credit belonging to CASH-account holders, who by
                      definition are not levered, and the paper's entire
                      mechanism is about LEVERED winners choosing not to
                      reinvest. Labelled a PROXY everywhere it appears.

    MARGIN DEBT (MD) has no such problem: column B is one continuous series
    1997-01 .. 2026-07. MD is therefore run on BOTH arms' windows, which makes
    it the clean control on whether any difference between the arms is about
    the DEFINITION or merely about the WINDOW.

    WHY THE PAPER'S OWN SUMMARY STATISTICS COULD NOT ADJUDICATE WHICH COLUMN
    THEY USED, stated because it was actually attempted and actually failed:
    [DKP16] p.17 says the peak MC/GDP is "2.6%, occurring in October of 2008"
    and that "all of the highest 10 values" occur in 2008. Column C in October
    2008 is $383.4bn against a same-quarter nominal GDP of $14,608.2bn = 2.625%,
    which matches the level — but under column C the 2008 MAXIMUM is AUGUST
    (3.558%), not October, so the match is a coincidence and column C is not
    their series. Reading Figure 1 (p.38) confirms the mismatch in the other
    direction: it shows MC/GDP around 0.002 in 1997 where column C gives
    0.0085. CONCLUSION, STATED AS A LIMITATION RATHER THAN PAPERED OVER: this
    build COULD NOT REPRODUCE [DKP16]'s exact MC series from free data, because
    the NYSE series they used (nyxdata.com factbook, now defunct) reported the
    cash/margin split historically and FINRA's free file does not. Neither arm
    below is claimed to BE their series.

4.3 DEVIATION D3 — S&P 500 EXPOSURE IS TRADED AS SPY, NOT AS THE INDEX OR AS
    FUTURES. The paper regresses on the S&P 500 index total return. SPY is the
    tradeable object this project can actually replay from its own committed
    point-in-time price store (data/price_store/v1/SPY.csv.gz, 8,458 rows,
    1993-01-29..2026-09-04), it is what the long-only strategy would actually
    be executed in, and it removes the futures-roll problem that blocked TSMOM

    Consistent with rebalancing_pressure_timing.py, which trades SPY for the

4.4 DEVIATION D4 — RISK-FREE RATE. Paper: "the log of the return to a one
    month Treasury bill". This build: the Fama-French monthly RF series from
    the committed backend/data/fama_french_factors_monthly.csv, whose own
    header states "The 1-month TBill rate data until 202405 are from Ibbotson
    Associates. Starting from 202406, the 1-month TBill rate is from ICE BofA
    US 1-Month Treasury Bill Index." Same instrument, different custodian, and

4.5 DEVIATION D5 — GDP VINTAGES COME FROM ALFRED, NOT THE PHILADELPHIA FED.
    Paper: Philadelphia Fed real-time dataset, real 2009 dollars converted to
    nominal. This build: FRED/ALFRED series `GDP` (Gross Domestic Product,
    Billions of Dollars, Seasonally Adjusted Annual Rate), queried with
    realtime_start = realtime_end = the signal date so the value returned is
    exactly what stood published on that date.
      * VERIFIED LIVE 2026-09-06 that this is sufficient and free: `GDP`
        exposes 414 vintage dates from 1991-12-04 onward, which covers the
        whole FINRA sample with five years to spare. NO PAID-DATA GAP IS
        CREATED BY THE GDP SIDE OF THIS BUILD.
      * FRED's `GDP` is ALREADY NOMINAL, so the paper's real-2009-dollars ->
        nominal conversion step is not needed. This is a deviation that
        REMOVES a step and a possible error, not one that adds an assumption.

4.6 DEVIATION D6 — THE "FURTHER LAG" IN 3.5 IS AMBIGUOUS IN THE WORKING PAPER,
    AND IS RESOLVED HERE RATHER THAN AFTER SEEING RESULTS. Two readings of
    "we use the numbers available in the Q2 1997 announcement... taking the Q1
    1997 GDP value from Q2 1997 announcement" are possible: (a) the vintage
    published in Q2 1997, whose most recent quarter is Q1 1997; (b) the vintage
    current in August 1997, from which one steps back past the most recent
    quarter to Q1 1997. BOTH READINGS SELECT THE SAME REFERENCE QUARTER — Q1
    1997 — and differ only in WHICH REVISION of that quarter is used. Reading
    (b) is implemented, because it uses the estimate an investor standing in
    August 1997 would actually have had, and because it matches the paper's own
    stated justification (avoid the first-to-second revision).
    IMPLEMENTED RULE: as of the month-end signal date, take the SECOND-most-
    recent GDP quarter present in that day's vintage.
    VERIFIED AGAINST THE PAPER'S OWN WORKED EXAMPLE at fetch time: querying
    ALFRED as of 1997-08-31 returns Q2 1997 as the latest quarter and Q1 1997
    as the second-most-recent — reproducing "the Q1 1997 GDP value" exactly.
    Pinned in the test suite.
    For the `insample` arms, which the paper builds from final revisions
    instead, each margin month is paired with the latest-vintage GDP for the
    quarter CONTAINING THAT MONTH — both describing the same economic period.

4.7 DEVIATION D7 — THE SHARPE IS COMPUTED ON MONTHLY RETURNS
    (periods_per_year = 12), NOT DAILY. This family's signal changes at most
    once a month. Marking the same monthly bet daily would multiply the
    observation count feeding PSR/DSR by ~21 without adding one extra
    independent draw of the signal's information, which would inflate
    statistical confidence in whatever direction the point estimate happens to
    point. This is the CONSERVATIVE choice and it makes the DSR bar HARDER to
    clear, which is the correct direction for a deviation to run. It differs
    from vol_regime_timing.py and rebalancing_pressure_timing.py, whose signals
    genuinely do update daily.

4.8 DEVIATION D8 — H = 1 ONLY. The paper tests H in {1, 3, 6, 12} using the
    Britten-Jones/Neuberger/Nolte transformation for overlapping observations.
    This build tests the ONE-MONTH horizon only. Reason: the tradeable object
    declared in section 3.9 is a one-month-ahead switch, the overlapping-
    observation machinery is a second published method that would itself need
    validating against a known answer before it could be trusted, and adding
    three more horizons would triple the grid for a claim this build is not
    making. The paper's larger annual-horizon R-squared is therefore NOT
    tested here and no claim about it is made either way.

4.9 NON-DEVIATION, STATED SO IT IS NOT LATER MISTAKEN FOR ONE. gamma = 3, the
    [-0.5, 1.5] weight clip, and the 120-month variance window are INHERITED
    from [DKP16] Eq. (9) verbatim. They are not searched, not tuned, and not
    counted as grid arms.

5. THE GRID — 24 PRE-DECLARED SPECS, n_local = 24
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fully crossed, fixed here, and not extended after any result is seen:

  PREDICTOR (3)      mc          the paper's headline predictor
                     md          margin debt; the paper's own weaker one
                     histmean    CONTROL: forecast = the recursive/full-sample
                                 historical mean excess return, no margin data
                                 at all. This is the Campbell-Thompson /
                                 Goyal-Welch benchmark [DKP16] itself measures
                                 against, and it is the spec that says how much
                                 of any result is the signal versus the
                                 machinery around it.

  DEFINITION ARM (2) faithful    MC = free credit in margin accounts (col D),
                                 window 2010-02+
                     proxy       MC = reconstructed combined free credit,
                                 window 1997-01+
                     (md and histmean run on both arms' WINDOWS, which is what
                     isolates window effects from definition effects.)

  ESTIMATION (2)     insample    full-sample detrend, full-sample standardize,
                                 full-sample predictive regression. LOOKS AHEAD
                                 BY CONSTRUCTION. Reported as the paper's
                                 in-sample analogue and NEVER as evidence of
                                 tradeable edge.
                     recursive   expanding window for ALL THREE of the detrend,
                                 the standardization and the predictive
                                 regression, using data through t only. THE
                                 ONLY HONEST OUT-OF-SAMPLE ARM.

  STRATEGY (2)       longonly    w in {0, 1}; w = 1 iff r_hat_{t+1} > 0  (p.28)
                     meanvar     w = clip((1/3) * r_hat / sigma_hat^2, -0.5, 1.5),
                                 sigma_hat^2 from a 120-month trailing window (Eq. 9)

  3 x 2 x 2 x 2 = 24 specs. n_local = 24.

BURN-IN IS FIXED, NOT SEARCHED: 120 months, matching the paper's own 10-year
initial estimation window (their sample starts 1984-01 and their out-of-sample
starts 1994-01). It is NOT a grid arm, because a burn-in length chosen after
seeing results is a free parameter that flatters. The consequence is severe and
is accepted here in advance: the `faithful` arm's first signal is 2010-04, so
its first tradeable month is roughly 2020-04 and it will have on the order of
75 realized months. THAT IS THIN, IT IS EXPECTED TO BE THIN, AND A WEAK RESULT
ON THE `faithful` ARM IS THEREFORE UNINFORMATIVE ABOUT THE PAPER rather than
evidence against it. The `proxy` arm carries the statistical weight.

PRE-DECLARED EXPECTATION ABOUT md, so that a weak md result costs nothing in
surprise: [DKP16] p.2 verbatim — "MD, quite popular among the practitioners and
the financial press, is a strong negative predictor of the aggregate market
return in-sample. But its performance out of sample is weak. However MC,
largely ignored until now, is a significant predictor of market returns."
A weak `md` result is therefore CONFIRMATORY, not a red flag. It is `mc` —
specifically `mc` x `recursive` — that carries the verdict.

6. THE TRADED STREAM, AND WHY THE VERDICT IS READ OFF THE OVERLAY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two return streams are computed for EVERY spec and BOTH are always reported:

  strategy_t+1 = w_t * (R_SPY,t+1 - RF_t+1)  - costs      [DKP16]'s own object;
                                                          Sharpe comparable to
                                                          their long-only 0.92
  overlay_t+1  = (w_t - 1) * (R_SPY,t+1 - RF_t+1) - costs the INCREMENTAL,
                                                          self-financing bet
                                                          against buy-and-hold

THE VERDICT IS FIXED HERE TO READ OFF THE `overlay` STREAM. Reason, decided
before any number exists: the long-only strategy is long equities most of the
time, so a DSR computed on `strategy` would be measuring the equity risk
premium — an exposure available for free by doing nothing — and calling it a
signal. `overlay` is exactly what the timing decision adds or destroys relative
to passively holding the market, which is the only thing this family could
honestly be said to have discovered. `strategy` Sharpe and buy-and-hold Sharpe
over the identical window are reported beside it for comparability with the
paper, and a spec whose `strategy` Sharpe beats buy-and-hold BUT whose
`overlay` DSR fails is reported as a FAIL.

DEGENERACY GUARD: an overlay stream whose standard deviation is zero (a spec
that never deviates from w=1 — the expected fate of some `histmean` controls)
reports Sharpe 0.0 rather than a ratio of floating-point dust, following
vol_regime_timing.RESIDUAL_DEGENERACY_RATIO's precedent.

8. COSTS — INSIDE THE DSR, NOT BESIDE IT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three cost arms, screened separately; the BASELINE arm is the only verdict
input, per CLAUDE.md's rule that cost realism feeds the DSR calculation itself.

  cost_free  0 bp, 0 borrow.  GROSS signal, for attribution only. NEVER a
                              verdict input.
  baseline   2.0 bp one-way per unit of gross notional traded, plus 34 bp/yr
             general-collateral borrow (borrow_cost.GENERAL_COLLATERAL_BPS_PER_YEAR,
             Beneish/Lee/Nichols 2015 p.15) accrued on any SHORT notional the
             meanvar clip produces. THE VERDICT ARM. 2.0 bp is well above SPY's
             own quoted half-spread and is the same figure
             rebalancing_pressure_timing.py uses, kept identical so the two
             families' numbers stay comparable.
  stress     10.0 bp one-way, same borrow. A deliberate UNSOURCED stress.

TURNOVER IS EXPECTED TO BE LOW — at most one switch per month between SPY and
cash, and in practice far fewer — so costs are expected to be a small drag
here, unlike the daily-turnover families. That is a reason to report the
breakeven cost, not a reason to skip the arm.

9. MECHANISM-FIDELITY CHECKS, FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  THE TWO-MONTH REPORTING LAG. Assert the signal at month t uses the FINRA
    reading for month t-2, against the paper's own June-1995 -> August-1995
    example (p.11). A unit test, not a comment.
F2  THE GDP VINTAGE RULE. Assert that as of 1997-08-31 the selected GDP quarter
    is 1997-Q1, reproducing the paper's own worked example (p.12).
F3  THE MARGIN-ACCOUNTING IDENTITY. Assert margin_credit_identity(position=2500,
    margin_requirement=0.60, margin_debt=400) == 600 and that situations 0, 1
    and 3 of the paper's table (p.7-8) give 0 — the paper's own four-row worked
    example, pinned so the identity in 3.2 cannot drift from memory.
F4  RECURSIVE ARMS MUST NOT SEE THE FUTURE. A synthetic test in which the
    series after a cut date is replaced by garbage must leave every `recursive`
    signal value at or before the cut date BIT-IDENTICAL. This is the direct
    test for the look-ahead bug that section 3.6 warns about, and it is the
    single most important test in the file.
F5  DETREND RE-VALIDATION ON THIS SAMPLE. Report the OLS trend slope, its
    Newey-West t-statistic, and an ADF statistic on the detrended residual, per
    definition arm — rather than assuming the paper's 1984-2014 trend finding
    carries over.
F6  SIGN CHECK. Report the sign and HAC t-statistic of beta in the full-sample
    predictive regression of next-month excess return on MC. The paper predicts
    beta < 0 with a 1-s.d. effect near -1.1 pp/month (p.2). A POSITIVE and
    significant beta would be a finding in its own right and is reported as
    such rather than quietly absorbed.
F7  THE SEAM. Report the month-on-month change in every series across the
    2010-01/2010-02 break, so the continuity claim in 4.1 is a measured number
    in the output and not just a sentence in this document.
F8  CONFOUND. For every spec report mean weight, fraction of months long,
    number of switches, the buy-and-hold Sharpe over the identical window, and
    the OLS beta of the strategy stream on the buy-and-hold excess stream.

10. PASS / FAIL — FIXED BEFORE RESULTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy D, two-tier, applied to the `overlay` stream of the BEST spec by DSR at
n_local = 24 within the BASELINE cost arm:

  DSR at n_local = 24 < 0.95                        -> DEFINITE_NEGATIVE
  DSR >= 0.95 at n_local but < 0.95 at n = 857      -> UNRESOLVED (needs
                                                       forward validation; NOT
                                                       a pass)
  DSR >= 0.95 at the most conservative N measured   -> PASS

0.95 is the VALIDATED-EDGE bar used by every family this session. 0.50 is the
informal screening floor used only to describe whether a spec is worth
mentioning; it is not a verdict.

NO REGIME-CONDITIONAL DSR. CLAUDE.md restricts regime-conditional testing to
sources whose claim is explicitly conditional. [DKP16]'s headline claim is
UNCONDITIONAL — MC predicts returns at all horizons and in both subsamples —
and its recession/expansion Sharpe split (1.42 / 0.96) is presented as evidence
that the effect is not confined to recessions, i.e. the opposite of a
conditional claim. Recession-window Sharpes are therefore reported as
DESCRIPTIVE diagnostics only and are not a separate gate.

11. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Low probability of a real pass. Reasons, all of them known in advance:

  * The claimed effect is ENORMOUS. An out-of-sample monthly R-squared of 7.45%
    for the aggregate equity premium is far outside anything in the
    Welch-Goyal (2008) universe, where the honest benchmark result is that
    essentially nothing beats the historical mean out of sample. Extraordinary
    claims of this size in return predictability have a poor survival record.
  * The paper's own out-of-sample period ENDS IN 2014, and the working paper is
    from 2016. This build's `faithful` arm is entirely post-publication and its
    `proxy` arm is roughly 40% post-publication. Published aggregate predictors
    decaying after publication is the modal outcome, not the exception.
  * MC's own peak is October 2008 and the paper's Figure 2 shows almost the
    whole of MC's cumulative out-of-sample advantage arriving in a single step
    in late 2008. A predictor whose entire edge is one crisis is a predictor
    with an effective sample size close to one.
  * The `faithful` arm — the only definitionally correct one — will have
    roughly 75 realized months after the fixed 120-month burn-in. That is not
    enough to clear a 0.95 DSR bar at n = 24 except by luck, and if it DOES
    clear it that should increase suspicion rather than confidence.
  * This project has already tested and declined two close cousins of this
    mechanism (Lou 2012 flow-induced trading via N-PORT; Frazzini-Lamont
    "Dumb Money").

  Against all that: the source is PEER-REVIEWED in a top field journal, which
  is more than can be said for the last three candidates; the data is free,
  the construction is simple, and the real-time-vintage discipline the paper
  demands is fully reproducible here. It is worth the 24 trials.

  MOST LIKELY OUTCOME, RECORDED SO IT CANNOT BE RETROFITTED: DEFINITE_NEGATIVE
  on the `overlay` verdict, with the `strategy` stream's Sharpe looking
  superficially respectable purely because it is mostly long equities.
