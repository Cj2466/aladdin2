1.1 THE MECHANISM, IN THE SOURCE'S OWN WORDS.
[CKMR99] p.3, verbatim: "The share price of an open-end mutual fund is not
negotiated in a market. It is inferred from the negotiated prices of the fund's
holdings, typically their closing prices on their primary exchanges. And while
there are obvious benefits from pricing off these arms-length transactions,
there is a potential cost from the bid/ask spread. The location within the
spread of a stock's closing trade has little or no intrinsic significance, but
it passes straight through to the prices of the funds positioning it."

p.5, verbatim: "In SEC terminology, 'marking the close' is 'the practice of
attempting to influence the closing price of a stock by executing purchase or
sale orders at or near the close of the market,' (SEC release #36556, December
6, 1995). One explanation offered by Zweig (1997), which we call the marking-up
model, is that fund managers mark the close of their holdings with buy orders.
The policy of most equity funds is to value each holding at the last reported
trade price on its primary exchange."

p.6, verbatim: "Because of the way fund managers are compensated, and because
of the flow-performance relation in mutual fund assets, the obvious target for
this strategy is the calendar-year return... The convexity of the new
investment/annual return relation (Ippolito (1992), Sirri and Tufano (1998)),
combined with the weakness of performance persistence (Hendricks, Patel and
Zeckhauser (1993), Brown and Goetzmann (1995), Carhart (1997)), suggests an
especially strong incentive for the year-to-date's best performers to increase
their year-end portfolio value at the expense of the next year's return."

This is flow/positioning, not fundamentals: a deliberate, mechanical,
calendar-triggered order-flow event with no information content, which is
exactly the class this session has been searching.

1.2 AN ERROR IN THE WORKING PAPER'S OWN ABSTRACT, RECORDED BECAUSE IT CHANGES
WHICH DEFINITION THIS BUILD FOLLOWS. The abstract (p.2) says, verbatim: "The

month-end. Section III p.8 defines the dummies verbatim as "quarter-end (other

caption is unambiguous: "QENDd is 1 if d is the last trading day of March, June
or September, and ... MENDd is 1 if d is the last day of January, February,
April, May, July, August, October or November."

THIS BUILD FOLLOWS THE BODY, THE CAPTION AND THE CONCLUSION, not the abstract:
QUARTER-END means the last trading day of March, June or September (never
December), and MONTH-END means the last trading day of one of the other eight
months. Whether [CKMR02] fixed the abstract is unknown to this build.

    ARITHMETIC THAT FOLLOWS DIRECTLY FROM THOSE SENTENCES, and that this
    pre-registration treats as the single most important fact about the design
    (see section 6.2). Writing r0 for the day-0 close-to-close return and r1
    for the day-1 close-to-close return of the WINNER quintile:
      full cross-section  r0 = +81bp, r0+r1 = +2bp  =>  r1 = -79bp
      smallest-cap        r0 = +109bp, r1 = +6bp
      largest-cap         r0 = +37bp, r1 = -144bp
    and for the LOSER quintile in the full cross-section, r0 = r1 = +250bp.
    Therefore, AT THE DAILY CLOSE, IN THE PAPER'S OWN DATA, the day-0
    winner-minus-loser spread is 81 - 250 = **MINUS 169bp**, and the day-1
    winner-minus-loser spread is -79 - 250 = **MINUS 329bp**. Both are negative

    effect, which the paper names in the same paragraph (p.15: "This is exactly
    the turn-of-the-year effect already documented in earlier years - big
    returns for small stocks, especially recent losers (e.g. Givoly and Ovadia
    (1983), Sias and Starks (1997))").
    The quantity that ISOLATES the marking signature is the DIFFERENCE of the
    two days, r0 - r1: +160bp for full-cross-section winners, +103bp for
    smallest-cap winners, +181bp for largest-cap winners, and ~0bp for
    full-cross-section losers. That difference is [CKMR99]'s own Section V
    measure, and it is why this build's primary arm is built on it (section 5).

WHY BOTH, cited rather than assumed. [CKMR99] p.10: "A fund's opportunity
depends on its weight in large-spread stocks, a good match to the bigger shifts
in AG funds here and in small-stock funds in Zweig (1997)." And Section IV

winner quintile shows a large day-0 move and NO next-day reversal, the
largest-cap winner quintile shows a small day-0 move and a LARGE next-day

calling it the paper's result. This is the same decision, for the same reason,
that tax_loss_selling_PREREGISTRATION §4 made.

WHAT NEITHER UNIVERSE FIXES: neither reaches the micro-caps where the
turn-of-the-year literature puts the largest effects, and the small-cap
membership module documents that its own price coverage gap is worse than the
S&P 500's. This build does not claim to test the mechanism where it is largest.

4.2 THE EVENT CALENDAR — [CKMR99]'s OWN DUMMY DEFINITIONS, TABLE I CAPTION p.23.
Let d(0) be the event day, d(-1) the trading session before it and d(1) the
session after it, all read off the loaded price index and never constructed as
calendar dates.

  year_end       d(0) = the last trading day of December.        [CKMR99] YEND
  quarter_end    d(0) = the last trading day of March, June or
                 September.  NEVER December.                     [CKMR99] QEND
  month_placebo  d(0) = the last trading day of January,
                 February, April, May, July, August, October or
                 November.                                       [CKMR99] MEND

A calendar month with fewer than 15 sessions in the loaded index is TRUNCATED BY
THE DATA WINDOW rather than short, and is skipped and counted, never clamped —
the same rule and the same threshold cross_sectional_tax_loss_selling.py already
uses, for the same reason (anchoring on a "last trading day" that is an artifact
of where the fetch ended is a silently-wrong answer, not a missing one).

4.3 THE RANKING VARIABLE, PERF — TWO PRE-DECLARED WINDOWS, BOTH SOURCED.
Both are TOTAL-RETURN (dividend-inclusive) price ratios, matching [CKMR99] p.14
("a total return from CRSP"), and both are measured with data up to AND
INCLUDING the close of d(-1) and never later. Higher PERF = better recent
performer = "winner".

  perf_h126   the trailing 126-trading-day total return ending at d(-1).
              [CKMR99]'s OWN STOCK-LEVEL SORT: p.14, "a total return from CRSP
              from the end of June to day -1"; Table V caption, "the previous
              six-months' return not including last day of the year". For a
              year-end, 126 sessions back from 31 December lands in the first
              days of July, i.e. the paper's own window. DEVIATION, named: the
              paper anchors on a CALENDAR date (end of June) and this uses a
              FIXED TRADING-DAY COUNT, so that the identical definition applies
              at a March, June, September or ordinary month-end where "end of
              June" has no analogue.
  perf_ptd    the PERIOD-TO-DATE total return: from the last close BEFORE the
              period opened, to the close of d(-1). For year_end that is the
              calendar-year-to-date return; for quarter_end the
              quarter-to-date; for month_placebo the month-to-date.
              [CKMR99] p.6, "the obvious target for this strategy is the
              calendar-year return", and p.12, "If we sort funds by their
              year-to-date return going into the last day, we should find by
              this argument that the best performers evidence above-average
              inflation of year-end valuations." This is the MOTIVE variable:
              the number the manager is trying to improve is this period's, so
              at a quarter-end the mechanism's own logic points at the
              quarter's return, not at a fixed six months.

  This is a SEARCHED AXIS with two values and it is counted as such in the
  denominator. It is not resolved by argument; both readings are defensible and
  the honest thing is to run both and pay for both.

4.4 A DELIBERATE TIMING CHOICE, DECLARED BEFORE THE RUN. The day-1 (reversal)
arm forms at the close of d(0) but ranks on PERF measured through d(-1),
EXCLUDING d(0)'s own return. [CKMR99]'s fund-level Table II/IV specification
ranks on the window ending d-1 for a dependent variable on d, which for d = d(1)
would mean a window ending d(0). This build refuses that for the stock-level
test: a cross-sectional sort that includes d(0)'s own return would be sorting
partly on "which stocks printed high at the close", which at the daily stock
level is indistinguishable from bid-ask bounce and one-day reversal, and would
manufacture a next-day reversal out of microstructure alone. Excluding d(0) is
the conservative choice — it can only weaken a measured reversal, never create
one. It also makes the day-0 and day-1 arms a matched pair on ONE ranking, which
is what the OVAL arm (section 5) requires to be the paper's own measure.

5. THE GRID — 18 SPECS PER UNIVERSE, 36 TRIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
        period {year_end, quarter_end, month_placebo}
    x   arm    {day0, day1, oval}
    x   perf   {perf_h126, perf_ptd}
    = 18 specs, asserted at import against QEM_SPEC_CEILING = 18,
    x   2 universes
    = n_local 36.

THE THREE ARMS, and what each one is.

  day0   THE MARKUP LEG. Forms at the close of d(-1) with signal = +PERF, holds
         one day, so it realizes d(0)'s close-to-close return. LONG the winner
         quintile, SHORT the loser quintile. [CKMR99]'s prediction for the
         marking mechanism is POSITIVE (Table IV's b1 hypothesis, p.13;
         Section IV's "we should see the recent winner stocks closing high on
         day 0", p.14).
  day1   THE REVERSAL LEG. Forms at the close of d(0) with signal = -PERF (PERF
         still measured through d(-1) — see 4.4), holds one day, so it realizes
         d(1)'s close-to-close return. LONG the loser quintile, SHORT the
         winner quintile. [CKMR99]'s prediction is that winners underperform on
         d(1) (Table IV's b2 hypothesis; Section IV's "44 of them were gone 30
         minutes into day 1"), so this arm's pre-declared expected sign is
         POSITIVE as constructed.
  oval   THE PRIMARY ARM, AND THE PAPER'S OWN MEASURE. One continuously managed
         book: +PERF at the close of d(-1), flipped to -PERF at the close of
         d(0), liquidated at the close of d(1). Its realized return is exactly
         [the day-0 return of (winners - losers)] minus [the day-1 return of
         (winners - losers)], which is [CKMR99] p.18's OVAL, "the day 0 return
         minus the day 1 return", applied to a winner-minus-loser spread
         instead of to a single stock.

NO DIRECTION AXIS. Every arm's sign is fixed above by the source's own
prediction and is never chosen after the fact. A long/short quintile spread is
antisymmetric under direction reversal up to identical costs, so a "reversed"
variant would double the denominator while adding zero information — the same
reasoning cross_sectional_patterns.py, cross_sectional_seasonality.py and
cross_sectional_tax_loss_selling.py all state.

WHY ALL THREE ARMS ARE IN THE DENOMINATOR EVEN THOUGH oval = day0 + day1 IN
RETURN TERMS. They are three cells from which a maximum could be reported, so
all three are counted. They are also mechanically correlated by construction,
which makes 36 a conservative (larger-than-independent) denominator rather than
an over-count of independent evidence. Both facts are disclosed; neither is used
to shrink the number.

WHAT IS FIXED AND NOT SEARCHED, each with its citation.
  RANK FRACTION 0.20 (QUINTILES), not this project's usual deciles.
    [CKMR99] p.15 sorts stocks "into performance-quintile portfolios"; Table V's
    sorts are quintiles. Following the source beats following the house default.
  LEG WEIGHTING "equal", not the harness default "magnitude". [CKMR99] p.15:
    "calculate the equal-weighted average of r_s,y,d,t for all d and t within
    each portfolio."
  PORTFOLIO "long_short" on every spec — the winner quintile against the loser
    quintile. Table IV's dependent variable is "the winner return minus the
    loser return" (caption, p.26); Section IV compares the winner and loser
    portfolios directly.
  HOLDING DAYS 1 on every spec. The event is one session. There is no holding
    axis, and adding one would be holding a prediction about a window that has
    already closed.

HARNESS NOTE, declared in advance so it is not discovered later. holding_days=1
makes the harness's formation cadence one trading day, so EVERY session is a
formation date and exactly one staggered sleeve exists (sleeve_scale == 1).
Unlike cross_sectional_tax_loss_selling.py there is NO sleeve blending and NO
1/holding_days rescaling of returns, costs or annualized figures: every number
the harness reports for this family is the real book's number. A unit test pins
that rather than assuming it. The signal panel is NaN on every non-event
session, so those formations skip, the book goes flat, and the round-trip
turnover is charged where it really occurs.

6. THE PLACEBO, AND THE ONE-SIDED OVERRIDE IT CARRIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 THE PLACEBO IS THE SOURCE'S OWN CONTROL, NOT AN INVENTION. [CKMR99] p.9,
verbatim: "There is no evidence of the price shifts for month-ends that aren't
quarter-ends." p.9 again: "Other month-ends show nothing (despite many more
observations), ruling out month-end explanations for the effect, such as the
monthly transfer from paychecks to pensions or other savings plans." Table I
confirms it (3.1). The `month_placebo` period runs the IDENTICAL grid — same
ranking variable, same arms, same universe, same costs — on the eight months
that are neither quarter- nor year-ends, and it has roughly eight times as many
events as the year-end arm, so it is the better-powered arm of the two.

BINDING OVERRIDE (i) — THE PLACEBO OVERRIDE. If the best `month_placebo` spec's
Sharpe and per-event mean return are of the SAME SIGN and COMPARABLE MAGNITUDE
to the best `year_end` or `quarter_end` spec's, this family is reported as a
NEGATIVE ON MECHANISM GROUNDS whatever the real arms' DSR, because the effect
would then not be attributable to period-end marking at all. Same wording
convention as ipo_lockup_expiration_PREREGISTRATION §12(i) and the same
one-sidedness: this rule can only make the verdict worse, never better.

BINDING OVERRIDE (ii) — THE QUARTER-END ASYMMETRY IS PRE-WEIGHTED, NOT
DISCOVERED. [CKMR99] p.9 says the quarter-end pattern is real but "on a reduced
scale" and that "the p-values for QBEG generally exceed the usual rejection
levels", and Table I bears that out (QEND p = 1.82%, QBEG p = 12.69%). The
`quarter_end` x `day1` cells therefore carry an explicitly LOWER prior than the
`year_end` cells before any number exists, and a strong quarter-end reversal
result must be reported as SURPRISING RELATIVE TO THE SOURCE rather than as
confirmation of it. Table IV complicates this in the opposite direction (its ALL
row has an insignificant QEND and a significant QBEG, 3.3), which is itself a
reason to treat any single quarter-end cell as weak evidence.

7. COSTS — INSIDE THE DSR, NOT BESIDE IT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BASELINE, UNIVERSE 1 (S&P 500): cost_model="edge_spread" against the CALIBRATED
per-ticker half-spread frame (spread_estimator.build_calibrated_half_spread_
frame at its SP500_TARGET_MEDIAN_HALF_SPREAD = 2.0bp one-way default), with the
flat fallback at DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS = 5.0. Same basis lazy_prices, nport_flow
and tax_loss_selling adopted. The raw uncalibrated EDGE frame is ~19x too
expensive on this project's own panel and is not used.

BASELINE, UNIVERSE 2 (S&P 600): cost_model="flat_bps" at SMALL_CAP_COST_BPS =
15.0, the figure cross_sectional_small_mid_cap.py already pre-declared for this
exact universe. The calibrated frame is NOT reused there, for the reason
spread_estimator's own docstring gives ("THIS NUMBER IS A UNIVERSE PROPERTY, NOT
A CONSTANT OF NATURE"); this project has no sourced small-cap median
half-spread and will not invent one for cosmetic symmetry.

BORROW: financing_bps_per_year = 17.0 = borrow_cost.financing_bps_for_long_
short_book(34.0) — the D'Avolio (2002) general-collateral rate of 34 bps/yr on
the short leg alone. It is charged, but it is arithmetically irrelevant here and
that is stated rather than left to look like diligence: a one-day hold at 34
bps/yr is under 0.01bp. NO BORROW LADDER IS RUN, because a ladder on a quantity
that cannot move the answer is decoration. The cost ladder below is the one that
matters.

WHAT ACTUALLY COSTS MONEY, and the arithmetic done in advance. A long/short
quintile book has gross notional 2.0. The day0 and day1 arms each pay entry
(2.0 of one-way notional) plus exit (2.0), i.e. 4.0 per event. The oval arm pays
entry (2.0), a full sign flip (4.0) and exit (2.0), i.e. 8.0 per event. At a
5bp one-way flat charge that is 20bp and 40bp per event respectively; at the S&P
600's 15bp it is 60bp and 120bp. Against [CKMR99]'s own largest-cap winner OVAL
of +181bp (3.4) that is not hopeless a priori, which is the only reason this
family is worth running at all — but see section 9(1).

PRE-DECLARED COST LADDER — Sharpe AND DSR recomputed on the SAME grid with the
SAME denominator; NO new trials, exactly the pattern tax_loss_selling's borrow
ladder used:
    x0.0  no turnover charge at all. Reported so the report can say whether
          the mechanism is ABSENT or merely UNTRADEABLE. A x0.0 result that is
          itself flat settles the question in one number, and a x0.0 result that
          is strong while x1.0 is not is a cost story, not a signal story.
          x0.0 IS NOT A RESULT THIS FAMILY MAY BE JUDGED ON. It cannot pass
          anything; it is a diagnostic.
    x1.0  the baseline above. THE ONLY ARM THE VERDICT IS COMPUTED ON.
    x2.0  double the turnover charge (for Universe 1, the calibrated half-spread
          frame scaled by 2 and the flat fallback doubled; for Universe 2,
          30.0bp flat). A stress, not a forecast.
A verdict that flips anywhere on this ladder is reported as flipping.

CROWDING AND IMPACT, disclosed and not modelled (informational, not gating,
since no capital is live). Every trade in this family executes at a period-end
or period-start close — the single most concentrated, most surveilled liquidity
events in the US equity calendar, and the sessions with the largest closing
auctions of the year. The backtest charges the universe's ordinary median
spread and models NO market impact and NO period-end widening or auction
imbalance. That is an optimism this family cannot measure with free data and
does not claim to have measured.

8. MECHANISM-FIDELITY CHECKS AND DIAGNOSTICS, FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All four are DIAGNOSTICS computed from the same price data and the same replays.
None adds a spec and none adds a trial.

F1  QUINTILE DOSE-RESPONSE — the direct analogue of Figures 2A-2C. At every
    event of every period type, the eligible cross-section is sorted into FIVE
    PERF quintiles and each quintile's mean d(0) return, mean d(1) return and
    mean OVAL (= d(0) minus d(1)) is recorded, alongside the equal-weighted
    eligible universe's own. [CKMR99] p.15: "The in-between quintiles are in
    between, in exact order." PREDICTION: OVAL increasing monotonically from the
    loser quintile to the winner quintile. A top-quintile jump with a
    non-monotone middle is a weaker fact than the paper's and is reported as
    such. This is the most informative single output of the build and is
    directly comparable to the numbers in 3.4.

F2  TABLE I ANALOGUE (aggregate). [CKMR99]'s Table I regresses an equal-weighted
    fund index's return IN EXCESS OF THE S&P 500 on the six period dummies. No
    fund data exists here, so TWO regressions are run and reported side by side,
    both on the same six dummies with an intercept:
      (a) SPY's own daily return. This is NOT the paper's dependent variable and
          is not a replication of anything. It answers one question: is there a
          MARKET-WIDE period-end/period-start pattern that a long/short book
          would be exposed to? [CKMR99] p.5 flags exactly this confound in
          [ZW97]'s numbers ("This is an odd fit with the price shifts of
          small-cap indices, which generally beat the market on both days").
      (b) the equal-weighted return of the point-in-time eligible universe MINUS
          SPY's return. This is the closest available analogue of Table I's
          dependent variable — an equal-weighted book of index constituents
          against the cap-weighted index — and it is labelled an ANALOGUE, not
          a replication, in the report. An equal-weighted portfolio of S&P 500
          members is not a mutual fund.

F3  TABLE II ANALOGUE (cross-sectional lag slope). For each session d, regress
    the cross-section of eligible stocks' day-d returns on the same stocks'
    day-(d-1) returns and save the slope b_d; then regress the b_d series on
    YBEG, QBEG and MBEG with an intercept. [CKMR99]'s prediction is a negative
    YBEG and QBEG loading against a positive intercept (3.2).
    DECLARED IN ADVANCE, because it will otherwise look like a failure: at the
    STOCK level the intercept is expected to be NEGATIVE, not positive, because
    daily stock returns cross-sectionally reverse (bid-ask bounce and short-term
    reversal) while daily FUND returns do not. The testable part is whether the
    YBEG/QBEG dummies make the slope MORE negative than the baseline, not the
    baseline's own sign.

F4  SIZE STRATIFICATION. Section 4.1's two universes, same grid, doubled
    denominator. PREDICTION FROM 3.4: the largest-cap winner quintile should
    show the LARGEST OVAL (+181bp in the paper) but the SMALLEST day-0 move
    (+37bp), and the smallest-cap winner quintile should show a large day-0 move
    (+109bp) with NO day-1 reversal (+6bp). A result that is strong in the day0
    arm on the S&P 500 and absent in the oval arm would be backwards relative to
    the paper's own figures and must be reported as such rather than banked.

  PASS                clears 0.95 at every N measured.

POLICY D DENOMINATOR LADDER, all three reported for every spec:
    36   n_local (= dsr_n_trials(36); the pooled ONC estimate sits at its
         structural floor of 2, so the max() is the local number)
    481  n_specs_clustered from global_effective_n.json
    857  raw_pooled_distinct_trials from global_effective_n.json

sigma_sr is the ddof=1 standard deviation of the SIBLING SHARPES within each
universe's own 18-spec screening pass — the harness's own convention. The two
universes are screened separately and therefore have separate sigma_sr;
disclosed, not hidden.

0.50 is the informal screening floor this project uses to decide whether a
result is worth further work. It is not a verdict and not a pass.

THE TWO BINDING OVERRIDES OF SECTION 6 APPLY, and both can only make the verdict
worse: (i) the placebo override, (ii) the pre-weighted quarter-end asymmetry.

preservation_score (app/services/research_lab/preservation_score.py) is computed
for EVERY spec in BOTH universes, with NO exceptions, and reported alongside. It
is a secondary check, not a gate.

AN ADDITIONAL, DELIBERATELY MORE HONEST STATISTIC IS REPORTED ALONGSIDE THE DSR
AND IS THE ONE A READER SHOULD WEIGH MOST: the PER-EVENT realized return of each
spec, its mean, its standard deviation across events, the naive t-statistic on
those events, the hit rate and the event count. The Sharpe machinery will report
~2,700 daily observations per spec of which all but a handful are exact zeros
(the book is flat on every session that is not adjacent to a period end). The
EFFECTIVE independent sample is the EVENT COUNT: about 11 year-ends and 33
quarter-ends on the S&P 500 (2015-2026), about 6 and 18 on the S&P 600
(2020-2026), against about 88 and 48 placebo month-ends respectively. No amount
of daily-return arithmetic creates independent events. This is declared here so
that a large-looking DSR on a zero-inflated daily series cannot be reported as
if it rested on 2,700 draws.

NO REGIME-CONDITIONAL DSR. CLAUDE.md restricts regime-conditional testing to
sources whose claim is explicitly conditional. [CKMR99]'s claim is
UNCONDITIONAL — it is a calendar claim, not a state-of-the-world claim.

NO FORWARD-VALIDATION REGISTRATION is created by this run under any outcome, and
no live registration's operational status is touched. That is the project
owner's call under CLAUDE.md §6.6 and is not made here.

D9  THE TRADEABLE OBJECT IS NOT THE PAPER'S. [CKMR99]'s own prescription is a

D10 THE PUBLISHED ARTICLE WAS NOT READ. Section 1, [CKMR02].

11. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERY LOW — pre-registered LOWER THAN tax_loss_selling's and roughly at
ipo_lockup_expiration's, which was itself the lowest of this session's first
eight. Nine reasons, each stated so that a positive result would have to survive
all of them.

 (1) THE EFFECT MAY BE THE SPREAD ITSELF, AND A TRADER MUST CROSS THE SPREAD
     TWICE TO REACH IT. This is the single strongest reason and it is the
     paper's own framing, p.3: "The location within the spread of a stock's
     closing trade has little or no intrinsic significance, but it passes
     straight through to the prices of the funds positioning it." If the day-0
     close is high because the last print landed at the ASK rather than at the
     mid, then a fund's NAV is inflated but nothing is transferable: a trader
     who bought at the d(-1) close and wants to sell into the d(0) close sells
     at the BID, and the entire measured shift disappears at the point of
     execution. The mispricing accrues to whoever is already holding, not to
     whoever trades. A strategy that must pay the spread to reach a phenomenon
     that IS the spread is arithmetically disadvantaged before any data is
     seen.
     WHAT WOULD ARGUE THE OTHER WAY, stated fairly: the paper's Section IV
     traces the move through the trading day (46 of 81bp in the last hour, 44
     gone within 30 minutes of the next open, p.15) which is more shape than a
     static within-spread mark would produce. But [CKMR99] does NOT report a
     quote-midpoint version of Figure 2 anywhere in the pages read, so how much
     of that shape is genuine price movement versus a shift in where prints sit
     inside the spread IS NOT SETTLED by this source. That uncertainty is
     itself a reason for a low prior, not a reason to assume the favourable
     reading.
 (2) THE PAPER'S OWN STOCK-LEVEL DAILY-CLOSE NUMBERS SAY TWO OF THE THREE ARMS
     SHOULD LOSE. From 3.4's arithmetic, the day-0 winner-minus-loser spread in
     [CKMR99]'s own full cross-section is MINUS 169bp and the day-1 spread is
     MINUS 329bp, both swamped by the turn-of-the-year small-cap loser effect
     that this project has ALREADY tested and closed as an honest negative
     (tax_loss_selling, DEFINITE_NEGATIVE, best DSR 0.5553; and
     cross_sectional_patterns_d2's long-horizon reversal). The `day0` and
     `day1` arms are therefore expected to fail, and — this is the point — a
     LARGE POSITIVE result in the `day1` arm would most likely be a
     rediscovery of that same already-negative reversal effect rather than
     evidence of marking. The report must read it that way.
 (3) REGULATORY ATTENTION PREDATES THE PAPER'S OWN SAMPLE. [CKMR99] p.5 quotes
     an SEC release from 6 December 1995 that already defines "marking the
     close" as a named practice — before the end of the paper's own fund-return
     sample. The behaviour has been an enforcement target for thirty years. That
     is a materially stronger decay argument than the ordinary post-publication
     one, and it applies on top of it.
 (4) THE CLOSE IS STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT NOW, AND THIS IS FLAGGED AS REASONING,
     NOT AS A CITED FACT. [CKMR99]'s mechanism describes a continuous-trading
     close in which one determined buyer can place "the last reported trade
     price". The modern US close is dominated by centralized closing auctions
     with published imbalance information. THIS BUILD DID NOT VERIFY ANY
     QUANTITATIVE CLAIM ABOUT CLOSING-AUCTION VOLUME SHARE OR MECHANICS, and no
     number about it is used anywhere. The argument is recorded as a directional
     prior only, and a reader should treat it as the author's inference rather
     than as evidence.
 (5) McLEAN & PONTIFF. [CKMR02] is a Journal of Finance article from 2002 with a
     memorable title, i.e. about as public as a finance result gets. McLean &
     Pontiff (2016) measure 58% post-publication decay across 97 predictors;
     preservation_score already applies that 0.42 retention factor.
 (6) THE SOURCE ITSELF SAYS THE QUARTER-END REVERSAL IS THE WEAK PART. Table I's
     QBEG p-value is 12.69% and the text says the QBEG p-values "generally
     exceed the usual rejection levels" (p.9). Two of six real (non-placebo)
     period x arm cells are therefore weakly predicted even in 1987-1996 data.
 (7) SMALL EVENT COUNTS. About 11 year-end events on the S&P 500 and 6 on the
     S&P 600. That is the whole independent sample for the arm the paper's
     evidence is strongest on, and it is a property of the free-data window, not
     of the implementation.
 (8) THE UNIVERSE IS WHERE ONE OF THE SOURCE'S OWN TABLES SAYS THERE IS NOTHING.
     Table V's largest-capitalization x winner-performance cell is -0.0005 with
     p = 67.17% (3.5). Universe 1 sits inside that cell.
 (9) THE PAPER'S OWN TRADEABLE PRESCRIPTION IS A DIFFERENT TRADE, AND THE
     AUTHORS EXPECT IT TO BE BLOCKED (3.6). Nobody in the source claims the
     stock-level version of this is profitable; this build is the first place
     that question is being asked in this form, which is a reason for humility
     about the answer, in either direction.

AN HONEST NEGATIVE IS THE EXPECTED OUTCOME AND IS A COMPLETE, USEFUL RESULT.
The failure mode this pre-registration exists to prevent is not a negative — it
is a positive manufactured by choosing the period type, the arm, the ranking
window, the universe, the cost model or the quintile cut after seeing which one
worked, or by reading a rediscovered turn-of-the-year loser effect as evidence
of marking the close.
