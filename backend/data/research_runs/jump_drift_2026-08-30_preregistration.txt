JUMP-DETECTION + POST-JUMP DRIFT/REVERSAL — PRE-REGISTRATION
Written 2026-08-30, BEFORE any real-data screening run, before the module was
implemented, and before any Sharpe, DSR or event-study number existed. Nothing
below may be revised after results are seen. The run report
(jump_drift_2026-08-30.txt) is the AFTER document; this is the BEFORE document,
and the two are meant to be diffed.

============================================================================
0. WHAT IS BEING TESTED
============================================================================
When a stock's daily price path makes a statistically DETECTED discontinuous
jump — as opposed to an ordinary large move drawn from its own fat-tailed
continuous volatility — does it CONTINUE in the jump's direction or REVERSE
over the following 1 to 20 trading days?

Two pre-declared, mutually exclusive directional hypotheses (see section 5):
  H_CONT  post-jump CONTINUATION: long recent up-jumpers, short recent
          down-jumpers earns a positive net Sharpe.
  H_REV   post-jump REVERSAL: the opposite book earns a positive net Sharpe.
Both are declared here, in advance, with neither favoured by the spec grid;
both are screened; the result is whatever it is. A null on both is a fully
acceptable and (see section 8) a genuinely likely outcome.

1. THE JUMP TEST — SOURCES ACTUALLY READ, AND THE EXACT FORMULAS
============================================================================
Both primary sources were downloaded and text-extracted IN FULL this session
(2026-08-30) and the formulas below are transcribed from them, not recalled.

 [BNS06] Ole E. Barndorff-Nielsen & Neil Shephard, "Econometrics of Testing
         for Jumps in Financial Economics Using Bipower Variation", JOURNAL
         OF FINANCIAL ECONOMETRICS, Vol. 4, No. 1 (2006), pp. 1-30,
         doi:10.1093/jjfinec/nbi022. Read from the author-hosted PDF at
         public.econ.duke.edu/~get/browse/courses/883/Spr15/COURSE-MATERIALS/
         Z_Papers/BNSJFEC2006.pdf (30 pages, extracted in full).
 [HT05]  Xin Huang & George Tauchen, "The Relative Contribution of Jumps to
         Total Price Variance", JOURNAL OF FINANCIAL ECONOMETRICS, Vol. 3,
         No. 4 (2005), pp. 456-499. Read from the author-hosted PDF at
         public.econ.duke.edu/~get/wpapers/rcj.pdf (45 pages, extracted in
         full).

VERBATIM from [HT05] p.459 (its equations for RV and BV, and its definition
of the moment constants):

    RV_t = SUM_{j=1..M} r_{t,j}^2

    BV_t = mu_1^{-2} (M/(M-1)) SUM_{j=2..M} |r_{t,j-1}| |r_{t,j}|
         = (pi/2)  (M/(M-1)) SUM_{j=2..M} |r_{t,j-1}| |r_{t,j}|

    mu_a = E(|Z|^a),  Z ~ N(0,1),  a > 0

so mu_1 = sqrt(2/pi) = 0.7978845608 and mu_1^{-2} = pi/2 = 1.5707963268.
[BNS06] Eq. (5) states mu_1 = sqrt(2)/sqrt(pi) independently — the two
sources agree, and pi/2 IS the scaling constant, as the build brief guessed.

VERBATIM from [HT05] p.460, the joint asymptotic variance matrix of (RV, BV)
under the no-jump null:

    nu_qq = 2,   nu_qb = 2,   nu_bb = (pi/2)^2 + pi - 3

therefore  nu_bb - nu_qq = (pi/2)^2 + pi - 5 = 0.6089937539.
[BNS06] Eq. (6) gives exactly this constant independently, calling it
theta:  "W = (p2/4) + p - 5 ~= 0.6090"  (i.e. pi^2/4 + pi - 5). Two sources,
same number, computed here to 10 digits rather than quoted from memory.

VERBATIM from [HT05] p.461, Eq. (3) — realized tri-power quarticity, the
jump-robust estimator of the integrated quarticity that scales the test:

    TP_t = M mu_{4/3}^{-3} (M/(M-2))
             SUM_{j=3..M} |r_{t,j-2}|^{4/3} |r_{t,j-1}|^{4/3} |r_{t,j}|^{4/3}

with mu_{4/3} = 0.8308609250 and mu_{4/3}^{-3} = 1.7434720745 (computed from
the mu_a definition above; not quoted).

VERBATIM from [HT05] p.462, Eq. (2) and Eq. (9) — the relative jump measure
and the RATIO-plus-MAX-adjusted z-statistic, which is THE statistic this
family implements:

    RJ_t = (RV_t - BV_t) / RV_t

    z_TP,rm,t = RJ_t / sqrt( (nu_bb - nu_qq) (1/M) max(1, TP_t / BV_t^2) )

    -> N(0,1) as M -> infinity under the null of no jumps.

[BNS06]'s own "adjusted ratio jump test" J-hat (its Eq. 14, transcribed from
p.8 of the PDF) is the identical statistic with the opposite sign convention
and quad-power in place of tri-power quarticity:
    J-hat = d^{-1/2} (mu_1^{-2}BPV/QV - 1)
            / sqrt( theta max(t^{-1}, QP/BPV^2) ),
rejecting when significantly NEGATIVE. [HT05] p.460 says so explicitly:
"An equivalent statistic, -RJ_t, called the ratio statistic, is proposed and
studied by Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard (2006)." This module uses the
[HT05] orientation (positive under jumps) and one-sided upper-tail critical
values. That is a sign convention, not a different test.

WHY z_TP,rm AND NOT ANY OF THE OTHER FOUR VARIANTS — this is the single most
important methodological choice here and it is made on the source's own
evidence, quoted verbatim from [HT05] p.469-470:

    "With the exception of the z_TP,rm,t statistic, the sampling frequency
     has a significant impact on the size. As the sampling frequency
     decreases, that is, the sampling interval increases, the actual sizes
     of all statistics except z_TP,rm,t increase above the Monte Carlo
     confidence band"
    "Note that z_TP,rm,t in the bottom panel appears to have the best size
     property among the five statistics."

z_TP,rm is the ONE variant Huang & Tauchen find to be size-robust as the
sampling interval lengthens. This work lengthens the sampling interval to its
extreme (see section 2), so it is the only defensible choice of the five.

2. THE DAILY-DATA ADAPTATION, AND ITS HONEST STATISTICAL COST
============================================================================
THE DATA CONSTRAINT. This project's only equity price source is
app/services/market_data/yfinance_provider.py: DAILY OHLCV bars via
yf.download(auto_adjust=True). There is a get_intraday_bars method, but its
shortest interval is 60m and yfinance's intraday history is a rolling ~60-day
window — unusable for an 11-year cross-sectional replay. There is no tick,
trade or 5-minute data anywhere in this project.

WHY LEE-MYKLAND (2008) IS NOT IMPLEMENTED. Their statistic standardizes each
return by a LOCAL spot volatility estimated from a short trailing bipower
window, and its Gumbel-limit critical values are derived under in-fill
asymptotics where the sampling interval goes to zero within a fixed local
volatility regime. Feeding it daily bars as though they were the intraday
returns it was designed for would produce a statistic with critical values
that do not describe it. It is not implemented, and no claim about it is made.

WHAT IS BEING DONE INSTEAD, STATED PRECISELY. The BNS/HT test is a
WITHIN-PERIOD test: it takes M returns sampled inside one period and asks
whether that period's quadratic variation contains a jump component. Here the
PERIOD is a rolling window of w TRADING DAYS and the M "intra-period" returns
are the w DAILY log returns inside it. The algebra is unchanged (a
semimartingale does not know what a calendar unit is); what changes is the
asymptotic regime.

THE COST, STATED BEFORE THE RUN AND NOT NEGOTIABLE AFTERWARDS:
 (a) The in-fill CLT requires delta -> 0 with the period length fixed. Here
     delta is pinned at ONE TRADING DAY and cannot shrink; the limit is
     approached only by lengthening the window w. The N(0,1) limit is
     therefore an APPROXIMATION whose finite-sample accuracy is not the one
     [HT05] validated. Their Monte Carlo runs M = 12 to 288 intraday returns
     per day; w = 21 and w = 63 sit inside that RANGE of M, which is the
     reason to expect the approximation to be usable at all — but their
     returns are minutes apart inside one volatility regime, and w = 63 daily
     returns span a quarter, across which volatility genuinely moves.
 (b) CONSEQUENCE: the nominal significance level is NOT trustworthy as a
     literal false-positive rate. Daily equity returns are fat-tailed and
     volatility-clustered relative to the null's locally-constant-volatility
     Brownian semimartingale, and both push the test toward OVER-rejection.
     The z-threshold is therefore treated as a declared TUNING PARAMETER with
     a nominal Gaussian label, never as a calibrated p-value. The realized
     jump frequency will be MEASURED and reported against the nominal alpha
     precisely so a reader can see the size distortion rather than infer it.
 (c) The window-level test says a jump occurred SOMEWHERE in the w-day
     window. It does not locate the day. Section 3 states the attribution
     rule that does, and it is this module's own construction, not a
     published one.

3. DAY-LEVEL ATTRIBUTION RULE — THIS PART IS NOT FROM A PAPER
============================================================================
Declared plainly: no source was found that locates a single day inside a
rejected BNS/HT window. The rule below is this module's own, chosen for being
deterministic, backward-looking, and directly falsifiable by unit test.

    Day d is flagged a JUMP DAY iff BOTH hold for the window of the w daily
    log returns ENDING AT AND INCLUDING d:
      (i)  z_TP,rm(window ending at d) > z_crit         [the window contains
                                                         a jump]
      (ii) |r_d| = max_{j in that window} |r_j|          [and d is that
                                                         window's dominant
                                                         move]
    The jump's signed size is r_d itself.

Condition (ii) is what turns a window verdict into a day verdict. Because the
window ENDS at d, both conditions read only returns up to and including d —
the flag is strictly backward-looking and cannot be revised by later data.
The unit tests must prove, on synthetic ground truth, that this rule fires on
a known injected jump day and does NOT fire on an ordinary high-volatility
day with no injected discontinuity. If it cannot be made to do both, the
family is reported as undeliverable rather than shipped.

RETURNS are LOG returns, ln(C_t / C_{t-1}), on the dividend/split-adjusted
close. Log is required, not stylistic: RV/BV/TP are discretizations of the
quadratic variation of the log-price semimartingale in both source papers
([HT05] p.459 defines r_{t,j} as a difference of p, its log price).

4. PRIOR FINDINGS ON POST-JUMP DRIFT/REVERSAL — SOURCES ACTUALLY READ
============================================================================
 [SAV12] Pavel Savor, "Stock Returns after Major Price Shocks: The Impact of
         Information", JOURNAL OF FINANCIAL ECONOMICS 106(3), 2012, pp.
         635-659. Read from the author's Wharton-hosted May 2012 final
         version (faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/
         Stock-Returns-After-Major-Price-Shocks---May-2012---Final.pdf, 62
         pages, extracted in full).
 [JZ17]  George J. Jiang & Kevin X. Zhu, "Information Shocks and Short-Term
         Market Underreaction", JOURNAL OF FINANCIAL ECONOMICS 124(1), 2017,
         pp. 43-64. HONEST FLAG: the full text is behind ScienceDirect and
         was NOT fetched. What was read is the publisher/SSRN abstract
         material only, so [JZ17] is cited below for its headline direction
         and horizon and for nothing finer.

[SAV12] is the directly relevant one because its horizons ARE this family's
horizons. Its Table 4 regresses post-event cumulative abnormal return on the
event-day abnormal return AR_0; the coefficient on AR_0 is the drift(+) /
reversal(-) coefficient. Transcribed from the PDF:

    Panel A, Full sample (N = 120,221):
        AR_{1,5}   -0.062  [t = -13.2]
        AR_{1,10}  -0.065  [t =  -6.0]
        AR_{1,20}  -0.081  [t =  -7.1]
        AR_{1,40}  -0.115  [t = -12.2]
    Panel B, Unreported (no-analyst-report, "no-information") sample:
        AR_{1,5}   -0.095  [t = -15.2]   ... AR_{1,40} -0.157 [t = -12.3]
    Panel C, Reported ("information") sample:
        AR_{1,5}   -0.003  [t =  -0.4]   ... AR_{1,40} -0.025 [t =  -1.9]

and from the body: "While no-information stocks experience large reversals
(amounting to 9.6% of the initial price shock), information ones do not and
even exhibit drift."

[JZ17], from its abstract: jumps are used as a proxy for large information
shocks; "Strategies long (short) stocks with positive (negative) lagged jump
returns earn significantly positive returns over the next one- to three-month
horizons" — i.e. CONTINUATION, but at ONE TO THREE MONTHS.

THE PRE-REGISTERED PRIOR, fixed now: the literature does not point one way,
it points BOTH ways at DIFFERENT horizons. Unconditional on any information
proxy — which is all this project can do, having no analyst-recommendation
data — [SAV12]'s full-sample coefficients are NEGATIVE and strongly
significant at exactly 5, 10 and 20 days, the horizons screened here. So the
LITERATURE-IMPLIED PRIOR AT THIS FAMILY'S HORIZONS IS REVERSAL, while
[JZ17]'s continuation lives at horizons this family does not reach. This is
written down now so that a reversal result cannot later be presented as a
surprise, and so that a continuation result is recognized as contradicting
[SAV12] rather than confirming [JZ17].

5. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 24 SPECS, FIXED
============================================================================
Four dimensions, fully crossed. This grid is frozen as of this document.

  D1  z-threshold z_crit   2 levels: 3.0902  (nominal one-sided alpha=0.001)
                                     2.3264  (nominal one-sided alpha=0.010)
                           Nominal labels only — see section 2(b).
  D2  window w             2 levels: 21 trading days (~1 month)
                                     63 trading days (~1 quarter)
  D3  holding horizon h    3 levels: 5, 10, 20 trading days
  D4  direction            2 levels: cont (H_CONT), rev (H_REV)

  2 x 2 x 3 x 2 = 24 = JUMP_SPEC_CEILING, asserted in code against the
  literal, and used as the DSR n_trials denominator.

  pattern_id format: jump_{cont|rev}_w{21|63}_a{001|010}_h{5|10|20}

ON DECLARING BOTH DIRECTIONS, given that cross_sectional_illiq.py explicitly
declines to: a long-short book is antisymmetric under a sign flip UP TO
COSTS, so H_REV's gross return is exactly -1 times H_CONT's while the cost
drag is SUBTRACTED from both. The two are therefore near-mirrors, not
independent trials, and declaring both roughly doubles n_trials for roughly
one family's worth of real search. That direction of error is CONSERVATIVE —
it makes every DSR harder to pass, never easier — and it is accepted here
deliberately, because the brief requires both hypotheses to be pre-declared
and screened rather than one chosen after the fact. n_trials will NOT be
shrunk back to 12 afterwards on the mirror-image argument; that would be
exactly the post-hoc trial-count laundering this project has rejected before.

WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED, AND IS FIXED NOW AT ONE VALUE EACH:
  rank_fraction      0.40  (top 40% / bottom 40% of the flagged-jump
                            cross-section by signed jump return; the middle
                            20% is dropped as directionally ambiguous)
  portfolio          long_short
  leg_weighting      magnitude (the harness default)
  event lookback     equal to h — the signal at a formation reads jumps in
                     the trailing h trading days, so every detected jump is
                     traded exactly once, at the first formation on or after
                     it, with 0..h-1 days of delay. Consequence, disclosed
                     now: signal freshness is worse at longer h (mean delay
                     ~h/2), so the h dimension confounds horizon with
                     staleness. The alternative (a fixed 5-day event window
                     for every h) would instead throw away 75% of events at
                     h=20. Neither is free; this one is chosen, declared, and
                     not revisited after results.
  min_names_per_leg  5 (harness default) -> a formation needs >= 13 flagged
                     names to produce two disjoint legs of >= 5.

6. UNIVERSE — POINT-IN-TIME, NO STATIC TICKER LIST
============================================================================
sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over(start, end) supplies the candidate
pool and sp500_membership_history.was_member (the harness default
membership_fn) gates every formation date. ticker_universe.SCREENING_UNIVERSE
is NOT used and is not imported. Screening window: 2015-01-07
(MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START) to the run date; a start before that is rejected with
a loud ValueError, since was_member answers False for everyone there.

RESIDUAL SURVIVORSHIP, disclosed now because it is NOT fixed by the above and
is a property of the data, not of this module: that module's own KNOWN LIMITS
record that yfinance returns no price history for ~48% of the tickers that
left the index in the trailing 5 years — precisely the acquired and failed
names — and that a few resolve as RECYCLED tickers belonging to a different
company. The point-in-time gate removes the LOOK-AHEAD half of survivorship
bias (today's membership applied retroactively); it cannot manufacture prices
that no free vendor sells. The count of unpriceable point-in-time tickers
will be reported in the run report as a first-class number.

This bias is directionally relevant HERE in a way worth stating in advance: a
failed company's terminal decline is exactly a large down-jump, so the
missing names are disproportionately down-jump events with catastrophic
forward returns. Their absence should, if anything, FLATTER the short leg of
a continuation book and PENALIZE the short leg of a reversal book.

7. COST MODEL
============================================================================
config.cost_model = "edge_spread" — each ticker's own trailing effective
HALF-spread from spread_estimator.build_edge_half_spread_frame (the validated
Ardia, Guidotti & Kroencke, JFE 2024, EDGE estimator over daily OHLC, full
spread / 2), at the module's COST_MODEL_WINDOW_DAYS = 63 production window,
charged on that ticker's traded notional at each formation. Reused, not
reinvented. cost_bps = 5.0 (DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS) is the per-ticker fallback
where no EDGE estimate resolves; the fallback notional is reported.

config.financing_bps_per_year = 0.0, the harness default and the value every
other equity family in this project uses, because this project has no sourced
securities-borrow feed (a known OPEN paid-data gap). A dollar-neutral equity
long-short is approximately self-financing, but the short leg's borrow is a
real cost that is NOT charged here. Disclosed, not modeled.

NOT MODELED, declared in advance: market impact; commissions; borrow; the
post-jump SPREAD WIDENING that is this family's specific cost hazard. That
last one is the important one — EDGE estimates a TRAILING 63-day spread, so
it prices the stock's normal-times spread, not the wider one it actually
trades at in the days right after a jump. This family's true costs are
therefore UNDERSTATED by an unmeasured amount, and a marginal positive result
must not be read as tradeable.

8. WHAT WOULD MAKE A POSITIVE RESULT HERE FAKE
============================================================================
 1. SHORT-HORIZON REVERSAL IS A KNOWN, SEPARATE ANOMALY. Lehmann (1990) and
    Jegadeesh (1990) document weekly/monthly reversal in ordinary returns
    with no jump test anywhere. A positive H_REV result is only interesting
    if it is about JUMPS rather than about large returns. It is therefore
    pre-declared that the run report must state the realized jump frequency
    and the mean |r| on flagged days, so a reader can judge whether the
    "jump test" is doing anything a plain |return| threshold would not.
 2. THE SIZE DISTORTION OF SECTION 2(b). If the measured jump frequency is
    far above nominal alpha, the detector is mostly firing on fat tails, and
    the family is a return-magnitude sort wearing a jump test's name.
 3. TRIAL COUNT. n_trials = 24 covers only THIS grid. It does not cover the
    literature scan that nominated this family, nor the ~15 other families
    this project has screened. Every DSR reported is an UPPER BOUND.
 4. THIN LEGS. rank_fraction 0.4 of a flagged-only cross-section can be a
    handful of names. avg_names_per_leg and n_skipped_formations are
    reported for every spec and a spec whose legs are near the floor is
    single-name noise, not a cross-section.

9. THE POST-JUMP EVENT STUDY (separate from, and additional to, the screen)
============================================================================
Descriptive, cost-free, and reported alongside the traded specs because the
traded specs alone cannot separate "no effect" from "an effect smaller than
costs". For each detected jump day d with sign s, forward CUMULATIVE LOG
returns are measured over k in {1, 5, 10, 20} trading days STARTING THE DAY
AFTER d (close(d) -> close(d+k)), split into UP-jumps and DOWN-jumps.

BASELINE: the same ticker's own unconditional forward-k distribution over its
NON-jump days, subtracted per ticker before pooling. Ticker-demeaning is what
removes the ordinary drift the brief asks to be isolated away: a stock that
drifted up over 2015-2026 contributes that drift to both its jump days and
its baseline days, and the difference nets it out.

SIGNIFICANCE: a seeded bootstrap (fixed seed, declared in code) resampling,
per ticker, the same NUMBER of non-jump days as that ticker had jump days,
recomputing the pooled ticker-demeaned mean, B times, giving a two-sided
empirical p-value. This matches the brief's "bootstrap/resample non-jump
days" and gives the pooled statistic a null built from the same panel.

PRE-DECLARED READING OF THE EVENT STUDY:
  mean abnormal forward return, up-jumps    > 0  and down-jumps < 0  => CONTINUATION
  mean abnormal forward return, up-jumps    < 0  and down-jumps > 0  => REVERSAL
  anything else (same sign both sides, or both indistinguishable from 0)
                                                                     => NULL
No fourth reading will be invented after the numbers are in.

10. STOPPING RULES
============================================================================
 * The spec grid in section 5 is FROZEN. If, after seeing results, a further
   spec looks attractive, the temptation is to be DOCUMENTED IN THE RUN
   REPORT and not acted on.
 * The direction is FROZEN by being fully crossed: there is no post-hoc flip
   available, because both signs are already declared and both will be
   reported.
 * If the unit tests cannot demonstrate that the section-3 detector fires on
   a synthetic injected jump and stays silent on a synthetic high-volatility
   no-jump path, the honest outcome is "no daily-data-feasible jump test
   could be responsibly implemented", reported as such, with no substitute
   shipped.
 * An honest negative is a complete and acceptable result. No spec will be
   added, no threshold moved, and no horizon introduced to avoid one.
