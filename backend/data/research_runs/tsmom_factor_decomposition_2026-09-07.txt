  A SECOND BIAS, FOUND IN THESE OWN NUMBERS AND DISCLOSED
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PIT is NOT simply the conservative version of IN_SAMPLE. At k =
  [1, 2, 3] the PIT breadth is HIGHER than the in-sample one, which cannot
  be a look-ahead effect. The cause is that PIT betas are estimated
  with error, and that estimation error enters each instrument's
  residual as noise that is largely INDEPENDENT across instruments --
  which lowers measured residual correlation and therefore INFLATES
  measured breadth. So the two numbers bracket the truth from
  opposite directions but neither is clean:
     IN_SAMPLE inflated by construction (orthogonality identity)
     PIT       inflated by estimation noise in the betas
  CONSEQUENCE: the 16.9550 should NOT be read as a conservative
  estimate. It is an optimistic one by an unquantified amount, and it
  sits only 13% above a floor of 15. Quantifying that bias (e.g. by a
  noise-injection null, or by measuring breadth on residuals from
  factors estimated on a disjoint sample) is REQUIRED before this
  gate result is relied on. It is not done here, and this report does
  not claim the gate is settled.

  k values PASSING the floor on PIT: [5, 6]
  k values FAILING the floor on PIT: [1, 2, 3, 4]
  -> the pass is NOT robust to the choice of k. Disclosed, not smoothed.

  SOURCES ACTUALLY READ THIS SESSION (sourcing limits stated)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   [P] Polakow, D. and T. Gebbie, 'How many independent bets are
       there in an emerging market?', arXiv:physics/0601166v3
       (11 Jan 2008). Downloaded and read this session.
       Its Lemma 1.1 is the exact point this whole job turns on:
       'Independence is not separateness ... The square root of N in
       mathematical statistics implies <independence> amongst
       statistical units (here bets) rather than simply the notion
       of <separate bets> as is most often implied.'
       Its Sec 1.2 estimates breadth from the eigenvalue spectrum of
       the return correlation matrix -- the same idea this project
       already uses, though [P] uses the integer Kaiser-Guttman
       count while this project uses sum(l)^2/sum(l^2). NOT adopting
       their estimator; citing the concept only.

   [G] Grinold, R. C. (1989), 'The fundamental law of active
       management', Journal of Portfolio Management 15, 30-37.
       SOURCING LIMIT, STATED PLAINLY: Grinold (1989) itself was NOT
       read. This reference is quoted as it appears in [P]'s own
       bibliography (ref [3]). Treat as a second-hand citation.

   THE FORMULA, AND HOW IT WAS VERIFIED WITHOUT READING [G]:
       IR = IC * sqrt(BR)
       [P] does not print this equation in its extractable text, but
       it gives a worked example in Sec 1.1: 'assume we have a 60%
       chance of getting equity bets correct. A bet on one underlying
       will yield an IR of 0.2, a bet on five underlying securities,
       an IR of 0.45 and a bet on 20 underlying securities, an IR of
       0.90.'  Checking against IR = IC*sqrt(BR) with IC = 0.2:
         sqrt(1)*0.2  = 0.2000  vs printed 0.2
         sqrt(5)*0.2  = 0.4472  vs printed 0.45
         sqrt(20)*0.2 = 0.8944  vs printed 0.90
       The form is therefore CONFIRMED numerically against a real
       source's own printed numbers, not recalled from memory.

   CAVEAT ON BR THAT MUST NOT BE GLOSSED: in [G]'s formulation BR is
   the number of independent bets PER YEAR (decisions), whereas the
   effective-breadth statistic measured here is CROSS-SECTIONAL
   (instruments). [P] itself treats N cross-sectionally. Conflating
   the two is a known ambiguity in the literature and NO numeric IR
   forecast is produced here because of it. Resolving it is a
   prerequisite of any DSR N assumption, not a detail.

  (a) FACTOR-MOMENTUM SIGNAL on the extracted common factor(s)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
      Trade trend on the 6 extracted factor return series
      themselves (each factor is a real, tradable long/short
      portfolio of the 31 instruments -- the eigenvector IS a weight
      vector, which is what makes this implementable at all).

      STATISTICAL REQUIREMENT -- WHY IT IS DIFFERENT:
      This is a 6-bet strategy at most, and PC1 alone
      carries 21.3% of total variance, so it is
      closer to a 1-2 bet strategy in practice. The multi-bet
      law-of-large-numbers argument that CLAUDE.md's whole approach
      rests on DOES NOT APPLY to it. Per [P] Lemma 1.1 the sqrt(N)
      benefit requires independence, and these factors are few.
      Consequence: it cannot borrow statistical credibility from
      diversification and must instead earn it from EFFECT SIZE and
      LENGTH OF HISTORY. Its DSR must therefore be run at a MORE
      CONSERVATIVE (smaller) effective-N than a multi-bet family --
      a small n_local is NOT a licence for a lenient denominator,
      because the trials are few AND the bets are few.
      RECOMMENDATION: treat a pass here as UNRESOLVED by default
      under the project's existing two-tier rule unless it clears
      the most conservative rung of the {n_local,37,362,1031} ladder.

      HONEST PRIOR: 'momentum on the first principal component of a
      futures panel' is close to what the CTA industry already sells
      as its core product. The prior that this is un-arbitraged is
      LOW. Flagged now rather than discovered after a build.

  (b) IDIOSYNCRATIC TSMOM on the residual panel
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
      31 residual series, PIT effective breadth 16.9550 >=
      15.0. This one CAN use the standard
      multi-bet approach and the project's normal DSR treatment,
      because the gate it was blocked on is now (conditionally) met.

      BUT THREE CONDITIONS BEFORE IT IS TREATED AS UNBLOCKED:
      1. COST. The residual retains 62.3% of each
         instrument's variance on average, so restoring the raw risk
         target needs about 1.27x gross exposure, while
         spread and commission are charged on gross notional and do
         not shrink. Cost per unit of risk is therefore roughly that
         multiple higher BEFORE the extra turnover of continuously
         re-hedging 6 factor legs as the eigenvectors drift. Per
         CLAUDE.md cost realism must feed the DSR itself, not sit as
         a side note -- so this is a gating input, not a caveat.
      2. K-DEPENDENCE. The floor is cleared only for k in [5, 6]. A
         design whose feasibility depends on removing exactly the
         RMT-chosen number of factors is fragile; the owner should
         decide whether that is acceptable.
      3. THE BREADTH IS OF THE RESIDUAL, NOT OF A STRATEGY. A high
         effective breadth says the residual series are close to
         independent. It says NOTHING about whether trend-following
         works on them. The gate was only ever a precondition for
         the test to be meaningful, never evidence of an edge.
