1. THE SOURCE, AND THE TWO CORRECTIONS THE VERIFICATION PASS FORCED
============================================================================
The NBER w25084 PDF was fetched from nber.org and text-extracted IN FULL on 2026-09-01. The
published JF text was NOT retrieved (Wiley 403); the bibliographic record (75(3), 1371-1415, DOI
10.1111/jofi.12885) was confirmed independently at RePEc and the AFA issue listing. Every quote
below is from the working paper, which is known to differ from the published abstract.

The build brief for this family carried a headline of "up to 188 basis points per month (~22%
per year)" concentrated in "MD&A, the executive-team section, and litigation/risk-factor
language". Both halves needed correcting, and both corrections were written into the
PRE-REGISTRATION before any result existed:

 CORRECTION 1. 188bp is real but is NOT the base case — it is an "up to", EQUALLY-WEIGHTED,
 RISK-FACTORS-ONLY figure. The paper's own headline, verbatim: "Our key finding is that a
 portfolio that goes long 'non-changers' and short 'changers' earns a statistically significant
 34-58 basis points per month — up to 7% per year (t=3.59) - in value-weighted abnormal returns
 over the following year." Citing 22%/yr as this strategy's expected return would misrepresent
 the source.
 CORRECTION 2. MD&A is where changes CONCENTRATE, not where the returns are. Verbatim: "while
 changes in MD&A section wording do predict large and significant abnormal returns, changes in
 text in the Risk Factors section are even more informative for stock returns", with MD&A's own
 effect "ranging between 11-22 basis per month". This produced the pre-registered scope ordering
 risk_factors > full > mda — which section 5 shows is what actually happened.

3. THE TEXT PIPELINE — TWO REAL DEFECTS FOUND BEFORE THE GRID WAS FROZEN
============================================================================
Recorded in full because both were found by checking against real filings rather than by
reasoning, and the second one is the kind that ships silently.

3.1 INLINE-XBRL CONTAMINATION. Modern filings carry an <ix:header>/<ix:hidden> machine dump of
    every tagged fact. Stripping tags without removing it first yielded text beginning
    "aapl-20250927 false 2025 FY 0000320193 P1Y P1Y P1Y P1Y
     http://fasb.org/us-gaap/2025#LongTermDebtNoncurrent ..." — ~14,000 characters of duration
    codes and taxonomy URIs before any English. Its content varies with how many facts the filer
    tagged that year, so it manufactures "language change" unrelated to language. Removed first,
    unconditionally.

3.2 SECTION EXTRACTION THAT LOOKED RIGHT AND WAS WRONG. The important one: SIZE GUARDS DID NOT
    CATCH IT, ONLY A CONTENT CHECK DID. Matching heading words anywhere in flattened text
    reported 89-100% "coverage" with entirely reasonable section sizes (24-30% of document), and
    the content was wrong:
      * Apple's "risk_factors" began mid-sentence at '." The Company assumes no obligation to
        revise or update any forward-looking statements' — the forward-looking-statements
        disclaimer, reached through a CROSS-REFERENCE rather than the Item 1A heading.
      * J&J's "mda" ENDED inside Item 5, at '...symbol JNJ. As of February 4, 2026, there were
        108,358 record holders of Common Stock' — spanning the wrong region entirely.
    An earlier, cruder variant returned 87-98% of the WHOLE DOCUMENT for GE and Exxon.
    FIX, structural rather than a looser pattern: html_to_text preserves block-level structure as
    NEWLINES and headings must be LINE-ANCHORED, because a real Item heading occupies its own
    block element while a cross-reference sits mid-sentence.
    AFTER THE FIX, re-measured on 42 real 10-Ks from 15 filers: every extracted section opens
    with genuinely correct prose ("An investment in the Company's common stock or debt securities
    involves risks and uncertainties"; "The following summarizes factors that could have a
    material adverse effect on the Company's business"), and sizes are stable year-over-year for
    the same filer (Caterpillar risk factors: 53,582 then 53,565 characters).
    COVERAGE FELL from a false 89-100% to a true 86%/86% on that probe, and to 90.0%/87.4% on the
    full production sample. That trade was pre-registered as acceptable and is the right one:
    correct text at 90% beats wrong text at 100%.

3.3 STOPWORD REMOVAL, DECIDED BY MEASUREMENT. On 20 real consecutive 10-K pairs, raw
    term-frequency cosine over whole documents spanned 0.9965-0.9998 — a spread of 0.0033,
    dominated by function words identical in every filing. This is the boilerplate-dilution
    failure the brief warned about, and it is real. Removing a standard English stopword list
    widened the spread ~11x, to 0.9676-0.9976. Jaccard, being set-based, was unaffected either way
    (0.775-0.959). Stopword removal is therefore part of the frozen tokenization.

3.4 THE REAL-FILING SPOT CHECK THE BRIEF DEMANDED. Boeing, whose FY2019 10-K covers the 737 MAX
    grounding and whose FY2020 10-K covers COVID plus the continuing MAX crisis, against its own
    routine recovery year:
        BA FY2019 -> FY2020 (crisis):  MD&A cosine 0.9474, Jaccard 0.7240
        BA FY2020 -> FY2021 (routine): MD&A cosine 0.9809, Jaccard 0.8420
    The crisis pair scores markedly LOWER on both metrics than the same firm's following routine
    year. The pipeline detects real language change and does not fire on routine re-filing.
    THIS IS A SANITY CHECK ON THE MEASUREMENT, NOT EVIDENCE FOR THE HYPOTHESIS.

3.5 A SURPRISE WORTH RECORDING, because the brief asked for surprises. EDGAR's own reportDate
    field is not trustworthy. Walmart's 10-K accession 0000104169-09-000006 carries
    reportDate = 2009-04-01, identical to its filing date, while all 26 of its other 10-Ks carry a
    sane 42-77 day lag off a January-31 fiscal year end. A design that keyed signal visibility to
    the period end would have silently ingested a nonsense date there. This family is immune by
    construction: availability_date() reads acceptanceDateTime and has no code path to
    report_date at all.

3.6 KNOWN LIMIT, MEASURED: 5 of 358 filings in the verification sample returned HTTP 404 on their
    primary document — all pre-2002, where EDGAR's archive layout names the primary document
    differently ("0001.txt"). Every one predates this sample's 2012 warm-up floor, so none
    affected the production panel. Not fixed; disclosed.

6. WHY THIS IS A NEGATIVE, STATED PRECISELY
============================================================================
 1. THE BAR WAS SET IN ADVANCE AND IS NOT MET. Best DSR 0.7540 against a required 0.95. The DSR
    is the number that already accounts for having searched 36 configurations, and it says there
    is roughly a 75% chance the best spec's true Sharpe exceeds the 0.4019 that the best of 36
    zero-edge trials would produce by luck. That is not a validated edge.
 2. THE DAILY OBSERVATION COUNT MASSIVELY OVERSTATES THE INDEPENDENT INFORMATION. Every spec
    reports 2,926 trading days, but the signal only moves when a firm files, roughly annually,
    and the book only re-forms every holding period. THE BEST SPEC HAS 24 FORMATIONS. Its
    Sharpe's honest t-statistic over an 11.61-year span is +2.06 BEFORE any multiplicity
    correction — and +2.06 is exactly the sort of number that 36 trials produce by chance. The
    family mean t is +0.83.
 3. THE BEST RESULT SITS ON THE THINNEST AXIS. The top four specs are all h126, i.e. the 24-
    formation variants. That is the cell with the least independent information in the whole
    grid, and it is where the largest number appeared. Treat that ordering as fragile.
 4. SECTION SCOPES RANK A DIFFERENT CROSS-SECTION. risk_factors covers 90.0% of pairs and mda
    87.4%, and the misses are systematic by filer, not random. The scope comparison in section 5
    is therefore suggestive rather than clean — the same hedge [CMN20] itself applies to its own
    section table ("suggestive but not definitive evidence on where our return results are most
    pronounced").
 5. COSTS ARE UNDERSTATED. financing_bps_per_year = 0.0, so the short leg's real borrow is not
    charged (the project's standing OPEN paid-data gap). Market impact and commissions are not
    modeled. Turnover here is genuinely low (an annual signal held 1-6 months), so this matters
    less than in any event-driven family here — but it only moves the numbers down.
 6. SURVIVORSHIP CUTS AGAINST THE HYPOTHESIS, WHICH MAKES THE POSITIVE SIGN SLIGHTLY MORE
    INTERESTING AND STILL NOT SUFFICIENT. The 143 unpriceable names are the acquired and failed
    ones — exactly the firms expected to rewrite their risk-factor and litigation language, i.e.
    "changers" belonging in the SHORT leg. Their absence should weaken the measured short side.
    The direction of that bias is disclosed; its size is unknowable with free data.
 7. n_trials = 36 COVERS THIS GRID ONLY. It does not cover the literature scan that nominated
    this family, nor the ~28 other families this project has screened, nor the pipeline decisions
    of section 3 that were made before the grid was frozen. Every DSR above is an UPPER BOUND.

8. THE HONEST ONE-PARAGRAPH READ
============================================================================
On S&P 500 large caps over 2015-2026, using 7,798 real 10-K documents and a point-in-time-correct
filing-language pipeline, the Lazy Prices signal points the way Cohen, Malloy and Nguyen say it
should — 30 of 36 pre-declared specs positive, the strongest in risk-factor language, the weakest
in MD&A, and strengthening with horizon, all three orderings written down before the run. It does
not point that way strongly enough to clear a multiple-comparisons bar set in advance: the best
DSR is 0.75 against a required 0.95, the best t-statistic is +2.06 across 36 trials, and the best
cell has 24 independent formations. The most likely explanation is the one the pre-registration
named first: this is an inattention anomaly tested on the least inattentive universe in existence,
in a sample that post-dates the paper's own publication, with an annual rather than quarterly
signal refresh. A well-documented honest negative, and the family is left where it is.
