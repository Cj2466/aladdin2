THE SOURCE WAS READ, NOT RECALLED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, "Time series momentum", Journal of Financial
Economics 104(2), 2012, pp.228-250. Fetched from the authors' own hosted
copy (w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf),
extracted to text and read directly. Every quotation below is verbatim from
that text.

THE CLAIM IS EXPLICITLY CONDITIONAL, SO CLAUDE.md RULE 4 APPLIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Abstract, p.228:
    "A diversified portfolio of time series momentum strategies across all
    asset classes delivers substantial abnormal returns with little exposure
    to standard asset pricing factors and performs best during extreme
    markets."

  Introduction, p.229:
    "the return to time series momentum tends to be largest when the stock
    market's returns are most extreme -- performing best when the market
    experiences large up and down moves."

THE CENTRAL FINDING, AND THE HONEST PROBLEM IT CREATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
**MOP 2012 does NOT operationalize "extreme markets" as a binary regime.**
This was checked in the paper's own text rather than assumed. Their actual
significance test is CONTINUOUS -- Section 4.3, p.238:

    "the first row of Panel C of Table 3 reports coefficients from a
    regression of TSMOM returns on the market index return and squared
    market index return. While the beta on the market itself is
    insignificant, the coefficient on the market return squared is
    significantly positive, indicating that TSMOM delivers its highest
    profits during the most extreme market episodes. TSMOM, therefore, has
    payoffs similar to an option straddle on the market."

  Table 3 Panel C row 1: MSCI World coefficient -0.01 (t -0.17);
  MSCI World SQUARED coefficient 1.99 (t 3.88).

So there is no drawdown threshold, no volatility percentile, and no crisis
dummy anywhere in MOP's own test of this claim. This project therefore
cannot copy a binary rule from MOP, because MOP does not have one.

**This is the "the source's own definition cannot be pinned down as a binary
rule, so a standard defensible alternative was derived" case, stated plainly
rather than dressed up as a quotation.** It is the main judgment call in
this job and is flagged as such here, in the module docstring, and in the
persisted JSON's `honest_caveat` field.

THE RULE, AND WHY EACH PIECE IS FORCED BY THE SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIMARY (pre-registered): a calendar quarter is IN-REGIME ("extreme") when
the absolute value of the broad market's realized quarterly price return is
at or above the 80th percentile of absolute quarterly returns over all
STRICTLY PRIOR quarters.

  TWO-SIDED, ON MAGNITUDE -- not a one-sided crash rule. MOP say "largest
  during the biggest up AND down market movements", and their significant
  statistic is the SQUARED market return, symmetric by construction. A
  one-sided drawdown rule would test a different claim than the paper makes.

  THE 20% TAIL is MOP's own convention, not a number chosen here. p.240 uses
  "the top 20% most extreme realizations of the TED spread", and for
  sentiment "the top and bottom extremes (20%) of these variables".

  THE S&P 500 is MOP's own index for this specific claim. Section 4.3:
  "Fig. 4 plots the TSMOM returns against the S&P 500 returns." (Table 3's
  regressions use MSCI World; this project has no clean MSCI World series,
  and Fig. 4's S&P 500 is the closer match to the claim being tested.)

  NON-OVERLAPPING QUARTERS because MOP's own Panel C regressions are run on
  "quarterly non-overlapping returns" (Section 4.2).

  DEVIATION LOGGED: the S&P 500 is proxied by SPY, read on its raw close
  (price return, dividends excluded) so it tracks the PRICE index Fig. 4
  uses rather than a total-return variant. SPY history 1993-01-29 to
  2026-09-04, from this project's own price store.

A NULL RESULT IN THE SOURCE THAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT REPRODUCED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The instinctive "crisis regime" is a volatility percentile. MOP TESTED THAT
AND FOUND NOTHING. Section 4.4, p.240:

    "the VIX index to capture the level of market volatility and the most
    extreme market volatility environments... There is no significant
    relationship between TSMOM profitability and market volatility either."

  Table 3 Panel C: VIX t 0.92, VIX-top-20% t -0.10, TED-top-20% t -0.29,
  every sentiment extreme insignificant.

Choosing a VIX / realized-vol percentile as the primary regime would have
reproduced the one operationalization the source reports as NULL while
ignoring the one it reports as significant. That is why the primary rule
keys on the magnitude of the market's realized RETURN, not its volatility.

NO LOOKAHEAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 80th-percentile cutoff is computed on an EXPANDING window of strictly
prior quarters. The full-sample percentile is never used -- it would leak
the future into every early label. A 40-quarter (10-year) burn-in is
required before any label is emitted; earlier quarters are labelled
UNCLASSIFIED and excluded from BOTH buckets rather than being quietly
dumped into the "normal" bucket, which would contaminate it with periods the
rule never judged.

Tested by truncation invariance: classifying progressively truncated
histories must not change any shared label. A "guards the guard" test
confirms a full-sample percentile really would have differed on the same
data, so the invariance test is not passing vacuously.

CONTEMPORANEOUS -- NOT A TRADEABLE SIGNAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MOP's claim is contemporaneous: TSMOM earns most IN THE SAME quarter the
market moves most. Their regression is contemporaneous, not predictive. So
the primary rule is contemporaneous too -- quarter q's label uses quarter
q's own realized return and is knowable only at the END of q.

It is therefore legitimate ONLY for partitioning already-realized TSMOM
returns into two buckets for a split DSR. Using it to size or gate a
position would be lookahead. `assert_not_used_as_a_timing_signal()` raises
on purposes containing timing/overlay/filter/position/sizing/gate.

SECONDARY RULE (explicitly NOT MOP, recorded for completeness)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Trailing drawdown >= 20% from the running peak -- the one-sided "crisis
alpha" framing CLAUDE.md rule 4 names as its motivating example. It is NOT
the primary rule, because MOP's claim is two-sided. Unlike the primary rule
it IS pointwise-causal, so it is the right choice if some later purpose
genuinely needs a same-day-knowable state.

A REAL DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The drawdown rule initially tested (1 - price/peak) >= 0.20, which MISSES an
exact 20% drawdown: 1 - 80/100 evaluates to 0.19999999999999996 in IEEE-754
double precision, so a textbook-exact boundary case fell on the wrong side
of the rule's own stated threshold. Now compared at the price level
(price <= peak * (1 - threshold)), which is exact for that case. Ties are
near-measure-zero on real data, but a rule that is silently wrong exactly AT
its own threshold is precisely what the verification rule exists to catch. A
regression test pins it, including a "guards the guard" assertion that the
naive formulation really would have failed.

THE INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK -- AN EXACT IDENTITY, NOT A SECOND OPINION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a correlation matrix C of size n:

    sum_i lambda_i   = trace(C)   = n            (unit diagonal)
    sum_i lambda_i^2 = trace(C^2) = ||C||_F^2 = sum_ij C_ij^2

    =>  effective breadth = n^2 / sum_ij C_ij^2

Both hold for any symmetric matrix (C = Q L Q^T, Q orthogonal), so the
Frobenius route is not an approximation -- it agrees to floating point, and
disagreement beyond that means one route is broken. A test monkeypatches
numpy.linalg.eigvalsh/eigh/eig to raise, proving the Frobenius path really
uses no eigendecomposition and is therefore a genuinely independent check.

CAUTION RECORDED: the Frobenius identity is well-defined even for a
NON-PSD symmetric matrix. That is a trap, not a convenience -- the
interpretation as "independent bets" needs non-negative eigenvalues. The
minimum eigenvalue is therefore reported next to every number.

STAGGERED INCEPTION -- HANDLED BY REPORTING BOTH ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not all 31 futures roots start in 2013 (RTY from 2017). A strict
dropna(how="any") would discard 2013-2016 for ALL 31 instruments to
accommodate the latest arrival. Three estimates are computed:

  COMMON_WINDOW  dropna(how="any"). Methodologically identical to Steps 1
                 and 1b, therefore the only number strictly comparable to
                 7.10 / 8.11, and the one the gate is evaluated on. Always
                 PSD (a genuine sample correlation matrix).
  PAIRWISE       Each pair uses its own overlap (>= 252 days, else NaN
                 rather than a fabricated thin correlation). Keeps all
                 history; NOT guaranteed PSD, so reported with its minimum
                 eigenvalue.
  CONSERVATIVE   Elementwise max(full-window, trailing-1260) across the
                 whole matrix. ROBUSTNESS ONLY, never the headline -- an
                 elementwise max of correlation matrices is not
                 PSD-preserving.

If the common-window and pairwise estimates land on OPPOSITE SIDES of the
15 floor, `verdict()` returns UNRESOLVED_ESTIMATES_DISAGREE rather than
silently resolving it. Picking whichever is higher is exactly the quiet
choice that would turn a failed gate into a passed one.

THE max(full, trailing-1260) RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reused unchanged from Step 1b, which adopted it after the PCY/EMB confound:
thin/stale early trading depressed a full-window correlation to 0.61 for a
pair measuring 0.93-0.97 in every complete recent year, so a single

ANALYTIC TEST CASES (checked against arithmetic, not against the code)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  identity matrix (n x n)          -> breadth exactly n     (n = 2,3,5,11,31)
  all-ones matrix                  -> breadth exactly 1     (n = 2,3,5,11,31)
  k perfect blocks of size m       -> breadth exactly k
        by hand: each block gives one eigenvalue m and (m-1) zeros, so
        (km)^2 / (k m^2) = k
  equicorrelated, correlation rho  -> n^2 / [(1+(n-1)r)^2 + (n-1)(1-r)^2]
        across a full grid of n in {3,8,31} and rho in {0,0.1,0.35,0.6,0.9}
  plus: breadth always within [1, n]; adding a duplicate column can never
  increase breadth (the property that makes the metric worth using).

A CORRECTION TO THIS TASK'S OWN BRIEF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief anticipated "their Eq. (2)/(3) region" for position sizing. That
is WRONG, and citing it would have been a genuine mechanism-fidelity error.

  Position sizing is **Eq. (5), p.236**:
      r^{TSMOM,s}_{t,t+1} = sign(r^s_{t-12,t}) * (40% / sigma^s_t) * r^s_{t,t+1}

  Eq. (3), p.233, is a DIFFERENT object -- the sign-based predictive
  regression  r^s_t / sigma^s_{t-1} = alpha + beta_h sign(r^s_{t-h}) + e^s_t,
  used to DOCUMENT predictability, not to size a position.

VERIFIED FROM THE PAPER RATHER THAN ASSUMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * The 40% vol target IS correct. p.236, quoted verbatim: "We size each
    position (long or short) so that it has an ex ante annualized volatility
    of 40%. That is, the position size is chosen to be 40%/sigma_{t-1}...
    The choice of 40% is inconsequential, but it makes it easier to
    intuitively compare our portfolios to others in the literature."
  * Annualization is **261**, not 252. Eq. (1), p.233: "the scalar 261
    scales the variance to be annual." Easy to get wrong from memory.
  * delta is DERIVED from the paper's own stated condition, not hard-coded:
    delta/(1-delta) = 60  =>  delta = 60/61 = 0.9836065573770492. Confirmed
    numerically by summing the weight series it describes.
  * Eq. (1) subtracts an exponentially weighted MEAN (rbar_t), which many
    secondary write-ups silently drop. Implemented via the exact identity
    sum_i w_i (r_i - rbar)^2 = E_w[r^2] - rbar^2, so no truncation of the
    infinite sum is involved.
  * Eq. (1) sums over r_{t-1-i} -- STRICTLY LAGGED. sigma_t is knowable at
    t; this is the paper's own stated "lack of look-ahead bias". Enforced
    and tested.
  * Headline parameterization is k=12, h=1 (p.236, quoted).

A NOTATIONAL INCONSISTENCY IN THE SOURCE, FLAGGED NOT PAPERED OVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MOP's prose says the position size is "40%/sigma_{t-1}" while Eq. (5) writes
"40%/sigma^s_t". These denote the same quantity -- the ex ante estimate known
when the position is put on -- because Eq. (1) already defines sigma_t from
returns through t-1. The subscript differs; the information set does not.
Recorded explicitly in the module so a later reviewer does not "fix" this in
the wrong direction and introduce a real off-by-one.

HARNESS CASES -- EVERY ONE WITH A HAND-DERIVED TRUE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * PURE DETERMINISTIC UPTREND -> always LONG. Constant +1%/month gives a
    trailing 12-month return of 1.01^12 - 1 = +12.68% > 0 at every t, so
    sign is +1 at every defined point (29 of 40 months).
  * PURE DETERMINISTIC DOWNTREND -> always SHORT. 0.99^12 - 1 = -11.36%.
  * KNOWN CONSTANT-VOL SERIES -> EXACT TARGET SCALING. A deterministic
    series alternating +c/-c has weighted mean-of-squares exactly c^2 and a
    negligible weighted mean, so sigma_annual = c*sqrt(261). Choosing
    c = 0.20/sqrt(261) makes ex ante vol exactly 20%, and the position size
    must then be exactly 40%/20% = 2.0. Confirmed.
  * Eq. (1) RE-DERIVED BY HAND on a 4-point series with explicit
    d^i/(1+d+d^2) weights in plain Python, independently of pandas' ewm.
  * A CONSTANT return series has EXACTLY zero ex ante vol -- the check that
    the mean is genuinely subtracted. The mean-less variant would instead
    report sqrt(261)*0.01 = 16.16%, and the test asserts that too, so the
    case cannot pass for the wrong reason.
  * SCALE INVARIANCE: multiply every return by k and sigma scales by k while
    40%/sigma scales by 1/k, leaving the vol-targeted position return
    unchanged. This is what vol targeting is FOR, and a wrong annualization
    or a dropped mean breaks it. Checked at k = 0.5, 2, 10 to rtol 1e-10.
  * NO LOOKAHEAD: perturbing ONLY the final return (to +99%) must leave
    every sigma unchanged, including the last; plus truncation invariance.
  * Eq. (5) end to end: uptrend + further +2% with 20% vol gives
    (+1)(2.0)(0.02) = +0.04; downtrend + further -2% gives
    (-1)(2.0)(-0.02) = +0.04. Both hand-derived.
  * sign(0) == 0 -> a flat lookback takes NO position. MOP give no
    tie-break, and inventing one (defaulting ties long) would quietly add a
    long bias.
  * The diversified factor averages over "the St securities that are
    available at time t" (p.236), so an absent instrument reduces S_t rather
    than contributing a zero return.
