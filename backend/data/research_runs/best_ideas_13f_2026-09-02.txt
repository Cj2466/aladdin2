POINT-IN-TIME — THE HEADLINE CORRECTNESS NUMBERS
==============================================================================
Every filing becomes visible on its own FILING_DATE and never one day earlier. The SEC archives are themselves organised by filing window, and SUBMISSION.tsv carries the exact filing date per accession.
  Realized report-period -> filing-date lag across all eligible filings: min 1 days, median 43 days.
  MIN LAG IS NEVER NEGATIVE BY CONSTRUCTION: the parser refuses any submission whose FILING_DATE precedes its own PERIODOFREPORT (counted as filed_before_period_end).
  A filing whose report period was already older than 365 days when filed is refused outright — delinquent filers reporting multi-year-old books are public when filed (so not look-ahead) but do not represent current conviction.
  A manager's view expires 200 calendar days after it was filed, so a filer who stops filing does not haunt the panel.
  Both cross-manager statistics (the aggregate market-weight vector and the activeness cutoff) are computed from the PREVIOUS period's filings, restricted to those filed strictly before the current period ended — so no filing is ever judged against a contemporaneous or later one.
  Tests (a)-(d) named in advance in the pre-registration are implemented in tests/test_cross_sectional_best_ideas.py, two of them against REAL cached SEC archives.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE GRID'S STRUCTURE ACTUALLY SAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 * THE SIGN IS RIGHT, THE SIZE IS NOT. 35 of 36 specs are positive, in the
   paper's documented direction. But the 36 specs are correlated variants of
   ONE idea, so that sign count carries roughly the information of a single
   positive draw — which is precisely what sigma_sr = 0.161 already prices in.
   The DSR, not the sign count, is the number to read.

 * COUNT BEATS SHARE ALMOST EVERYWHERE, AND THAT IS THE UNCOMFORTABLE NUMBER.
   Mean Sharpe +0.398 for `count` against +0.184 for `share`, with `count`
   winning 17 of 18 matched pairs. `share` is the popularity-controlled
   variant, pre-declared precisely to detect the failure mode where "many
   managers call this their best idea" is really "many managers hold this,
   because it is big". That count > share so systematically is direct
   evidence that a MEANINGFUL PART of the raw signal is a popularity/size
   effect rather than conviction.

 * BUT IT IS NOT PURELY A SIZE ARTIFACT, and the honest reading has to say
   both halves. The single best spec in the whole grid IS a `share` spec
   (+0.573, DSR 0.778), beating the best `count` spec (+0.543, DSR 0.748),
   and its own matched `count` counterpart (+0.499). So popularity explains
   much of the average but not the maximum. Neither reading is strong enough
   to matter at a DSR of 0.778 against a 0.95 bar; both are recorded because
   the pre-registration committed to reporting this comparison either way.

 * THE LONGEST HOLD IS THE BEST ONE, which is the one result that sits
   comfortably with the source. h252 gives the best mean (+0.388) against
   h63 (+0.262) and h126 (+0.222). The paper's Figure 4 claims best-idea
   outperformance "does not revert in the months or even years after first
   appearing in the portfolio", and a signal whose best expression is its
   longest hold is at least consistent with that, rather than with a
   short-lived announcement effect. Contrast the sibling asset-growth family,
   where the literature's own rebalance frequency was the WORST hold.

 * PORTFOLIO_TILT IS THE BEST MEASURE (mean +0.364) ahead of conviction
   (+0.273) and market_tilt (+0.235). Read cautiously: portfolio_tilt is the
   measure that leans hardest on the disclosed aggregate-13F capitalisation
   proxy (module section 2), so its edge over conviction — the one EXACT,
   proxy-free measure — is as easily a property of the proxy as of the
   economics.

 * THIRTEEN OF THIRTY-SIX SPECS EXCEED THE >= 0.5 DSR SCREENING FLOOR this
   project has previously used to select forward-validation candidates, while
   NONE come close to this family's own pre-registered 0.95 bar. Reporting
   only the maximum would understate how much of the grid sits above that
   floor. It does not change the verdict — 0.5 is a screening floor, not a
   significance bar. NOTHING FROM THIS FAMILY IS REGISTERED FOR FORWARD
   VALIDATION; that is a separate, later, human decision and this run makes
   no recommendation either way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A DATA-HYGIENE DECISION MADE AFTER PRE-REGISTRATION, DISCLOSED NOT BURIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAX_REPORTING_LAG_DAYS = 365 is NOT in the pre-registration. It was added
during the build, from INGESTION DIAGNOSTICS ONLY and before any backtest
number existed, after the realized filing-lag distribution showed genuine
delinquent filers reporting books up to 3,027 days old. Keying visibility on
FILING_DATE makes those rows harmless as look-ahead (they are public when
filed), but without the bound a 2016 filing of 2008 holdings would enter the
panel as fresh conviction — an economics error, not a timing one. It refused
7,733 filings. No result influenced the choice of 365.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AN INCIDENTAL FINDING WORTH MORE THAN THE BACKTEST: THE 2023 UNITS BREAK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The per-filing VALUE unit classifier (provider section 2) independently
detected a REGIME CHANGE in how 13F filers report the VALUE column, from the
data alone, with no hardcoded date:

    2021q1..2022q4        1.2% - 1.6% of filings in whole dollars
    2023q1               79.9%
    2023q2..2023q4       86.3% -> 89.4%
    2024 .. 2026q2       90.5% -> 96.0%

VERIFIED against the primary source: the CURRENT Form 13F instructions
(https://www.sec.gov/files/form13f.pdf, fetched this build), instruction 8,
state verbatim "Enter values rounded to the nearest dollar", and the
information-table header reads "(to the nearest dollar)".

LABELLED AS INFERENCE, NOT FACT: the break at 2023q1 is consistent with the
form's units convention changing for 2023 reporting, with a multi-year tail
of filers still on the old thousands convention. NO explicit effective-date
statement was located in the fetched instructions, so the CAUSAL attribution
is an inference. The MEASUREMENT above is not — it is counted from the real
archives.

WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND THIS FAMILY: any 13F pipeline that hardcodes "VALUE is
in thousands" — the convention every pre-2023 description of this dataset
states — is wrong by a factor of 1,000 for 80-96% of filings from 2023
onward. Portfolio WEIGHTS survive that error (same-unit ratios), which is
why conviction is exact here regardless; anything comparing value ACROSS
filers does not. This family's aggregate market-weight vector and its $5m
screen both do compare across filers, and both would have been corrupted
over the entire recent half of the sample.
