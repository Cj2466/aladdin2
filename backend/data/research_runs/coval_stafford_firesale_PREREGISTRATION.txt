1. THE SOURCE, READ DIRECTLY — AND TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE BUILD BRIEF
---------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIMARY (published): Coval, Joshua D. and Erik Stafford, "Asset fire sales
(and purchases) in equity markets", Journal of Financial Economics 86(2),
November 2007, pp. 479-512.

CORRECTION 1 (title). The commissioning brief called this "Fire Sales in
Equity Markets". The actual title is "Asset fire sales (AND PURCHASES) in
equity markets". The parenthetical is not decoration: the inflow-driven
PURCHASE leg is half the paper's own tradeable strategy (Section III), and a
build that dropped it would test half the paper.

SOURCE ACTUALLY USED: the authors' own September 2005 working paper, the
pre-publication version of the JFE article, fetched live from the Federal
Reserve Bank of New York's own media library on 2026-09-08:
  https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2005/liquidity/Coval_Stafford.pdf
  sha256 6840a8329de3fa22f0f0cd22e3278f689de89e16fde561fd2ccfead92e836bc2
  41 pages, text extracted with pypdf and read in full.
The published JFE PDF is paywalled (ScienceDirect) and the isiarticles mirror
returned nothing on fetch — same pattern as margin_credit, ipo_lockup and
quarter_end_marking, where the ungated working paper was used and the gap
disclosed. DISCLOSED GAP, NOT GLOSSED: equation numbers, table numbers and
coefficients below are the 2005 working paper's, which may be renumbered or
revised in the 2007 published version. Every number quoted here was read off
the fetched PDF, never from memory.

CORRECTION 2 (the measure itself — the most important correction).
The brief stated: "the paper's actual measure typically weights each stock by
the SUM across all funds holding it of (fund's outflow severity x fund's
ownership share of that stock)". THAT IS NOT THE PAPER'S MEASURE. The paper's
PRESSURE variable is a pure COUNT of funds, unweighted by ownership share or
by flow severity. Eq.(4), quoted verbatim from the fetched PDF (page 10),
with subscripts restored from the paper's own surrounding prose:

    PRESSURE_i,t =
        [ sum_j Buy_{j,i,t}(flow_{j,t} > 5%) - sum_j Sell_{j,i,t}(flow_{j,t} < -5%) ]
        / [ sum_j Own_{j,i,t-1} ]

    "where Buy_j,i,t equals one if fund j increased its holding in stock i
     during quarter t, and zero otherwise, Sell_j,i,t is defined similarly
     based on decreases, and Own_j,i,t-1 equals one if fund j owns stock i at
     the beginning of quarter t."

The paper's own worked example (page 11), which this build reproduces as a
unit test: 47 funds own ABC at the start of January 2003; 13 owners experience
outflows of at least 5%, of which 11 reduce ABC; one fund has a 5% inflow and
increases ABC. PRESSURE = (1 - 11)/47 = -21.3%.

Crucially, the paper CONSIDERED AND REJECTED the share-weighted measure the
brief described, in the paragraph immediately following Eq.(4):

    "An alternative determinant of asset fire sales might be to tabulate the
     number of shares sold due to capital outflows and scale by the number of
     shares outstanding or trading volume. Initial tests that rely such
     measures deliver results that are economically large, but of mixed
     statistical reliability."

Two stated reasons: (i) price pressure needs COMMONALITY across owners, which
a count captures and a share-sum does not; (ii) share-based measures are
highly sensitive to holdings reporting errors. This build implements Eq.(4) as
written. It does NOT implement the brief's share-weighted variant, and that
refusal is a fidelity decision, not an omission.

STRUCTURAL DISTINCTION from this queue's already-closed Lou (2012) FIT
candidate, confirmed against both papers rather than assumed: Lou's Eq.(3) is
a share-weighted average of fund flows across the funds holding a stock
(continuous, severity-weighted, one fund's own flow-induced trading scaled up).
Coval-Stafford's Eq.(4) is an unweighted NET COUNT of constrained buyers minus
constrained sellers among that stock's owners. Different estimand, different
functional form. This is a genuinely separate test, not a re-run.


2. THE PAPER'S OWN NUMBERS (the bar this build is measured against)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Sample: 1980-2004, CDA/Spectrum quarterly mutual-fund holdings + CRSP.
Cutoffs (page 11, footnote 7): fire sale = PRESSURE <= -15%; inflow-driven
purchase = PRESSURE >= +25%; these are "approximately the 5th and 95th
percentiles". Minimum 10 mutual-fund owners required (footnote 6).

Event-time (Table 4 Panel A, page 14): over the fire-sale quarter (months
-2,-1,0) average abnormal return -10.1% (t = -6.94), average pressure ~-27%.
Months +1 to +12: rebound +6.15% (t = 2.01), pressure retreats under 2%.

THE PAPER'S OWN FALSIFICATION CONTROL (Table 4 Panel B): the same widespread
selling but by UNCONSTRAINED funds (pressure computed with the flow condition
REMOVED) produces a significant price drop and NO REVERSAL — "consistent with
voluntary mutual fund trading bringing information into prices". This is the
paper's own way of separating flow-pressure from information, and it is
pre-declared here as a BINDING interpretive check (section 6).

Tradeable strategy (Section III.A, page 17) — and this is what this build
tests, because it is the only part of the paper that is a strategy rather than
an event study: a calendar-time portfolio that "buys all stocks identified as
fire sale stocks within the past year, but not within the most recent
quarter". Footnote 10, verbatim: "We skip a quarter to ensure that the strategy
is feasible and to avoid any potential spillover of forced selling from the
event window to subsequent months." The short leg sells stocks involved in an
inflow-driven purchase over the same past-year-not-past-quarter window.

PRIOR THE PAPER SETS ON ITS OWN RESULT, and this is decisive for how the grid
below is read: at the |flow| > 5% threshold the long-short strategy's
annualized abnormal returns range from 4.8% (t = 0.80) to 10.9% (t = 1.79) —
the paper's own words: "economically large, but of mixed statistical
significance", "weak statistical reliability", "The incentive to provide
liquidity appears to be fairly mild." Only at |flow| > 10% (Table 5.b) does it
become strong: monthly abnormal returns 1.68% (t = 2.22) to 2.64% (t = 3.50).
=> The 10% arm is pre-declared as the PRIMARY arm and the 5% arm as the
secondary one. A null at 5% is NOT evidence against the mechanism; the source
paper is itself null-ish there. A null at 10% IS evidence against it.

The paper's own honest caveat, carried forward (footnote 12): the long-short
strategy is feasible in only 155 of 180 months, so "the abnormal return
estimates overstate the true investment returns by ignoring the capital costs
of standing idle" — Scholes's "fire-station" problem. With a ~6-year window
this build will be even more exposed to it; idle months are handled in
section 4.


3. DATA, AND THE 13F-vs-N-PORT SUBSTITUTION STATED PLAINLY
-----------------------------------------------------------
Coval-Stafford used CDA/Spectrum mutual-fund holdings (quarterly, 1980-2004)
and CRSP mutual-fund flows. Neither is available to this project. The free
substitute is SEC Form N-PORT bulk data, already ingested for the Lou/FIT
family (backend/data/nport_bulk, 2019q4-2026q2).

THIS IS A SUBSTITUTION, NOT AN EQUIVALENCE. Stated limitations:
 (a) SCOPE. The brief framed the original as 13F-based; it is not — the paper
     uses Spectrum MUTUAL FUND holdings, which is much closer to N-PORT than
     13F is. So the scope mismatch is SMALLER than the brief assumed. But
     N-PORT is registered-investment-company-only (~83% of the $39.2tn
     registered-fund industry), it excludes hedge funds and separate accounts,
     and it is not the same database. Ownership counts here are N-PORT fund
     SERIES counts, not Spectrum fund counts, so the -15%/+25% cutoffs are
     applied to a differently-populated denominator. Reported, not assumed away.
 (b) HISTORY. Oct 2019 to now, ~6 years, versus the paper's 25. This is the
     single largest power constraint and cannot be fixed with more work.
 (c) THE 60-DAY PUBLICATION WALL. SEC Release 33-10231: N-PORT data is not
     public until 60 days after the third month of the fund's fiscal quarter.
     The paper's own skip-a-quarter rule (footnote 10) already accommodates
     roughly this, which is a genuine piece of luck for fidelity. Regardless,
     every panel value is gated on the real FILING_DATE of every contributing
     filing, reusing cross_sectional_nport_flow.fund_snapshots_as_of, which was
     built and independently verified for that purpose.
 (d) MERGERS. N-PORT Item B.6 folds merger-driven share issuance into "shares
     sold" with no separating field, corrupting flow identity on ~33.7% of
     fund-quarters (median error 13.4bp). Inherited disclosure from
     nport_flow_definition_resolution_2026-09-05; irreducible, not a bug. It
     adds noise to the flow-threshold classification, biasing toward LESS
     measured signal, not more.
 (e) FLOW DEFINITION — settled, not re-litigated. Item B.6.a minus Item B.6.c
     (external flow), which is algebraically Lou's own Eq.(1) estimand under
     full-reinvestment. Resolved and merged as a1d64b3 on 49,705 real
     fund-quarters. Coval-Stafford define flow only as "capital flows" scaled
     by TNA and do not specify a reinvestment convention, so this project's
     already-resolved definition is adopted unchanged.
 (f) SPLITS. Share counts are split-adjusted before differencing, reusing
     build_split_adjustment/split_factor_on. Without this a 2-for-1 split reads
     as a "Buy" for every holder simultaneously — which would inject fake
     positive PRESSURE into exactly the stocks with the most owners.

UNIVERSE. Two point-in-time universes, both already used by this project:
S&P 500 (get_universe_over) and S&P 600 small-cap (small_cap_membership_history).
NAMED POWER CONCERN, declared in advance: the paper's fire-sale stocks average
~2% of CRSP-listed firms and are not mega-caps. An S&P 500 stock is held by
hundreds of N-PORT series, so a net -15% owner count is mechanically much rarer
there than in the paper's CRSP-wide sample. The S&P 600 arm is therefore the
more faithful universe, and is pre-declared as such. If either universe yields
too few event-stocks to form a portfolio in most months, that is reported as a
POWER FAILURE, not converted into a verdict about the mechanism.


4. THE FROZEN SPEC GRID — 24 SPECS
-----------------------------------
The grid is deliberately the paper's OWN Table 5.a / 5.b structure, so it
cannot be accused of being a search:

  flow threshold  in {5%, 10%}                          (2)  [Table 5.a / 5.b]
  leg             in {long fire-sale, short inflow-driven, long-short}
                                                        (3)  [the 3 panels]
  weighting       in {equal-weight, value-weight}       (2)  [both reported]
  universe        in {sp500, sp600}                     (2)

2 x 3 x 2 x 2 = 24 specs. n_local = 24.

Fixed, NOT searched over (all are the paper's own values):
  - fire-sale cutoff PRESSURE <= -15%; inflow cutoff PRESSURE >= +25%
  - minimum 10 owners per stock-quarter (footnote 6)
  - formation window: fire sale within the past year but NOT the most recent
    quarter (footnote 10)
  - monthly rebalance, calendar-time portfolio
  - minimum 10 firms in a leg for that leg to be held (Section III.A)

IDLE MONTHS. A month in which a leg cannot be formed (fewer than 10 firms)
earns the risk-free rate = 0 for that leg, NOT a dropped observation. Dropping
idle months is exactly the overstatement the paper's footnote 12 warns about.
This is pre-declared because it can only hurt the measured Sharpe.

COSTS. Per CLAUDE.md, cost realism feeds the DSR itself, not a side note. The
project's calibrated edge_spread cost model is applied at the baseline arm.
A zero-cost arm is reported alongside as a robustness arm, so that a negative
cannot be dismissed as a cost-model artifact (the pattern used by ipo_lockup
and quarter_end_marking).

6. BINDING INTERPRETIVE CHECKS, declared before any result exists
------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. THE PAPER'S OWN CONTROL (Table 4 Panel B). An UNCONSTRAINED-pressure arm
    is computed: identical construction with the flow condition removed (any
    net seller counts, distressed or not). The paper predicts a price drop with
    NO reversal there. If the unconstrained arm's long-leg return is equal to
    or LARGER than the constrained arm's, then whatever is being measured is
    not the forced-selling mechanism, and the candidate is reported as
    MECHANISM_NOT_SUPPORTED regardless of how the DSR came out. A passing DSR
    with a failing C1 is a warning, not a discovery.

C2. EVENT-COUNT FLOOR. Report the number of distinct fire-sale and
    inflow-driven stock-quarters found per universe, and the number of months
    each leg was actually formable. If the long leg is formable in under 1/3 of
    months, the arm is reported as POWER-LIMITED and its DSR is not treated as
    informative in either direction.

C3. NO POST-HOC CUTOFF TUNING. The -15%/+25%/5%/10%/10-owner constants are the
    paper's. If they produce empty samples, that fact is REPORTED as a finding
    about N-PORT ownership counts; the cutoffs are not re-tuned to manufacture
    a sample. Any diagnostic re-run with different cutoffs is labelled
    EXPLORATORY and excluded from the DSR grid entirely.

C4. DIRECTION. The long leg must be LONG fire-sale (high-outflow-pressure)
    stocks and SHORT inflow-driven ones. If the profitable direction turns out
    to be the reverse, that is a negative for this mechanism, not a signal with
    a sign flip.

8. WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS CANDIDATE INTERESTING
-----------------------------------------------
Stated in advance so it cannot be rationalized afterwards: a real result here
is the |flow|>10% long-short arm clearing DSR 0.95 at n=24 or better, in the
S&P 600 universe, WITH C1 satisfied (constrained >> unconstrained). Anything
else — including a strong 5% arm with a weak 10% arm, or a strong result whose
unconstrained control is just as strong — is at best UNRESOLVED and most
likely a negative.

Expected outcome, stated honestly and in advance: LOW probability of a pass.
Six years of data, a mega-cap-skewed universe where the paper's own cutoffs
are mechanically hard to reach, a 20-year-old published effect, and a source
paper that is itself only marginally significant at its baseline threshold.
