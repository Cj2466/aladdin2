MECHANISM, in the paper's own words (abstract, verbatim): "Institutional
investors engage in trillions of dollars of regular portfolio rebalancing,
often based on calendar schedules or deviations from allocation targets. We
document that such rebalancing has a market-wide impact and generates
predictable price patterns. When stocks are overweight, funds sell stocks and
buy bonds, leading to a decrease in equity returns of 17 basis points over the
next day. Our results are robust to controls for momentum, reversals, and
macroeconomic information."

2. THE PAPER'S OWN CONSTRUCTION, QUOTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 THE WEIGHT RECURSION (p.10, and identically in Appendix B, p.52-53)

    w_{t+1}(w_t; R^SP_{t+1}; R^10Y_{t+1})
        =  w_t (1 + R^SP_{t+1})
           -----------------------------------------------------
           w_t (1 + R^SP_{t+1}) + (1 - w_t)(1 + R^10Y_{t+1})

"where R^SP_{t+1} and R^10Y_{t+1} indicate the returns earned by the S&P500 and
the 10-year Treasury note, respectively." Target weights are 60/40; at t=0,
w = 60%. "After one period, the deviation from the target equity allocation is
given by w_{t+1}(...) - 60%."

2.2 THE TWO REBALANCING RULES (Appendix B, verbatim)

  THRESHOLD:  w^T_{t+1} = 60%                                if w^T_t - 60% >= delta
                        = w_{t+1}(w^T_t; R^SP, R^10Y)         otherwise
  CALENDAR:   w^C_{t+1} = 60%                                if t is the last business day of the month
                        = w_{t+1}(w^C_t; R^SP, R^10Y)         otherwise

2.3 THE TWO SIGNALS (Appendix B, Eq. B.1 and B.2, verbatim)

    Threshold signal^delta_{t+1} = w_{t+1}(w^T_t; R^SP_{t+1}; R^10Y_{t+1}) - 60%      (B.1)
    Calendar  signal_{t+1}       = w_{t+1}(w^C_t; R^SP_{t+1}; R^10Y_{t+1}) - 60%      (B.2)

NOTE THE STRUCTURE, because it is easy to get wrong: the SIGNAL is always the
DRIFTED deviation, computed by applying the transition function to the previous
state, WHETHER OR NOT a rebalance was triggered. Only the STATE w^j_{t+1} is
reset to 60%. The trigger is evaluated on the PREVIOUS day's state, so a
breach observed at t produces a reset at t+1 (one day of implementation lag).

2.4 THE PREDICTIVE REGRESSION (Eq. 1, p.11, verbatim)

    Ret_{t+1} = gamma0 + gamma1 * Threshold Signal^delta_t + eps_{t+1}                  (1)

"where Ret_{t+1} is the difference between S&P 500 and 10-year Treasury note
futures returns". Both signals "should negatively predict equity market returns
and positively predict Treasury market returns" (p.10), i.e. gamma1 < 0.

2.5 THE ADOPTED THRESHOLD SIGNAL (Eq. 2, p.12, verbatim)

    Threshold Signal_t = (1/N) * SUM_{delta = 0}^{2.5%} Threshold Signal^delta_t        (2)

"we adopt a Threshold signal that is defined as the average of Threshold
signals computed using delta values that span the range 0%-2.5% with increments
of 0.1%." N = 26 values (0.0%, 0.1%, ..., 2.5%).

2.6 THE CALENDAR / WEEK4 REGRESSION (Eq. 3, p.13, verbatim)

    Ret_{t+1} = beta0 + beta1 * Calendar Signal_t
                      + beta2 * Calendar Signal_t * Dummy^{N Days}_t
                      + beta3 * Dummy^{N Days}_t + eps_{t+1}                            (3)

"where Dummy^{N Days}_t is a dummy variable that takes the value of 1 during the
last N-days of a month". Paper's finding, p.13-14 verbatim: "Calendar
predictability peaks in the last four days of the month, consistent with
liquidity-driven trading by pension funds. Moreover, Figure 3 suggests that
funds attempt to minimize market impact by avoiding trades on the very last day
while spreading trades over several days. Therefore, our focus for the rest of
the paper is the interaction between the Calendar signal and the last week of
the month, labeled as week4_t, which corresponds to Dummy^{5 Days}_t."

2.7 THE PAPER'S OWN TRADEABLE STRATEGY (Section 4, p.37, verbatim)

    R^Strategy_{t+1} = (R^SP500_{t+1} - R^10y_{t+1}) * w^Strategy_t

"where the portfolio weight w^Strategy_t is defined as the average of modified
versions of the Threshold and Calendar signals defined in Appendix B (see Eqs.
(B.1)-(B.2)). We modify Threshold signal by rescaling to
-Threshold Signal_t / 1.5% so that the two rebalancing signals have the same
risk contribution to the strategy... The signal is multiplied by -1 because a
positive Threshold value indicates that the S&P 500 is overweight relative to
the 10-year Treasury note.

While the Threshold strategy can take a position on any day of the month, the
Calendar strategy focuses on the end-of-month effect. Therefore, the Calendar
signal is modified to sign(-Calendar Signal_t) if t falls within the last week
of a month, to capture the 'week4' effect. Furthermore, on the first business
day of a new month, the modified Calendar signal is set to
sign(Calendar Signal_{-4}) to capture potential reversal effects (see Figure
4). On any other day, the modified version of the signal is set to zero."

Note the missing minus sign on the first-business-day term is the paper's, not
a transcription error: it is the REVERSAL leg. Corroborated by footnote 27,
p.29 verbatim: "While month-end predictability is strongest, we also find
statistically significant predictability in weeks 1 and 3. In these weeks,
however, the predictive coefficient is positive, consistent with a reversal of
rebalancing pressures."

3.1 DEVIATION D1 — ETFs INSTEAD OF FUTURES. THE BIG ONE.

[HMM26] uses S&P 500 futures (E-mini, from 1997-09-10, its stated data start)
and 10-YEAR TREASURY NOTE FUTURES. This project has no free, roll-adjusted
futures series: the identical gap was scoped and logged THIS SESSION as
paid-data gap P4 (Norgate, ~$270/yr) after free Yahoo futures tickers were
proven to carry undetectable fabricated roll returns
(data/research_runs/tsmom_futures_feasibility_2026-09-05.txt). Nothing here
re-litigates that.

SUBSTITUTE, and it is this project's OWN already-verified data path, not a new
one: SPY (S&P 500 ETF) and IEF (iShares 7-10 Year Treasury Bond ETF), fetched
through YFinanceProvider.get_price_history off the point-in-time price store —
exactly the pair and exactly the call that vol_regime_timing.py's
TARGET_EQUITY_VS_DURATION already uses, and IEF is the same instrument
cross_sectional_bonds.py has used since 2026-08-26. Coverage RE-VERIFIED LIVE
2026-09-06 rather than assumed: SPY 1993-01-29..2026-09-04 (8,458 rows), IEF
2002-07-30..2026-09-04 (6,065 rows), common window 2002-07-30..2026-09-04,
6,065 rows, zero missing tickers.

WHICH DIRECTION DOES IT BIAS THE RESULT? Reasoned, not assumed:

 (a) THE FINANCING LEG LARGELY CANCELS, AND THAT IS THE MAIN POINT. A futures
     return is (approximately) a spot total return MINUS the financing rate; a
     total-return ETF price change is the spot total return. The paper's
     dependent variable and this family's traded spread are both DIFFERENCES of
     an equity leg and a bond leg, so the financing term appears with opposite
     signs and cancels to first order. R_SPY - R_IEF is therefore a close
     analogue of R^SP500futures - R^10Yfutures. The residual is the difference
     in financing spreads between the two futures contracts, which is small and
     has no reason to correlate with a weight-deviation signal.
 (b) EXPENSE RATIOS ARE A ONE-DIRECTIONAL DRAG THAT PARTLY CANCELS TOO. An ETF
     total return is net of its expense ratio; a futures return is not. Both
     legs are charged, and the SPREAD only carries the DIFFERENCE of the two
     fees — a constant of order a few basis points per year. A constant drift
     in the dependent variable cannot generate predictability from a
     mean-reverting weight-deviation signal; it can only shift the intercept
     gamma0, which Eq. (1) estimates freely. It is NOT capable of explaining a
     real effect either way, and it is not capable of manufacturing one.
 (c) THE ONE PLACE THE SUBSTITUTION IS GENUINELY DIFFERENT IS THE SIGNAL'S OWN
     INPUT, AND THERE THE ETF IS ARGUABLY MORE FAITHFUL. The recursion in 2.1
     wants "the returns earned by the S&P500 and the 10-year Treasury note" —
     i.e. what a real 60/40 fund's holdings earned, which is a CASH total
     return, not a futures excess return. Feeding SPY/IEF total returns into
     the recursion is closer to the economics of a real 60/40 rebalancer than
     feeding it futures excess returns would be.
 (d) DURATION MISMATCH, DISCLOSED AND NOT QUANTIFIED. IEF tracks 7-10y
     Treasuries; the 10-year note future's cheapest-to-deliver basket sits in a
     similar but not identical duration band. This makes our bond leg's
     volatility modestly different from the paper's, which changes the SCALE of
     the spread and hence of the weight deviations. It does not change their
     sign or their timing. No adjustment is made and none is claimed.
 (e) TRACKING ERROR AND THE ETF'S OWN CREATION/REDEMPTION FLOW are real and
     unmeasured here. Direction unknown; disclosed rather than assumed benign.

3.2 DEVIATION D2 — THE TRIGGER IS TWO-SIDED, AND THE PAPER CONTRADICTS ITSELF
    ON THIS. RESOLVED ON THE PAPER'S OWN EVIDENCE, BEFORE ANY BACKTEST.

Appendix B's DISPLAYED equation writes the threshold trigger ONE-SIDED:
"w^T_{t+1} = 60% if w^T_t - 60% >= delta". Four separate places in the same
paper say TWO-SIDED:

  * Section 1.2, p.9: "The Threshold methodology rebalances a portfolio back to
    target weights when the weights DEVIATE BEYOND A SET DISTANCE from their
    targets".
  * Figure 2's caption, p.11: "delta indicates the rebalancing range (i.e.,
    portfolio rebalancing occurs when THE DISTANCE of a portfolio weight from
    its target exceeds delta)".
  * Section 1.2's non-linearity test, p.12: "we estimate predictive regressions
    separately for days with |Threshold Signal^delta_t| >= delta, corresponding
    to predicted rebalancing days, and for days with |Threshold Signal^delta_t|
    < delta".
  * Section 1.3, p.14: "The AVERAGE values of the Threshold and Calendar
    signals are POSITIVE". Under a one-sided rule only POSITIVE breaches reset
    the portfolio, so negative deviations drift without bound and the signal's
    mean would be strongly NEGATIVE. The reported positive mean is only
    consistent with a two-sided band.

There is also a structural argument the paper does not make: under the literal
one-sided rule the state never resets on the downside, so the signal is a
function of the entire price path since an arbitrary t=0 and the initial
condition NEVER washes out. No sensible construction has that property.

DECISION, FIXED HERE: the PRIMARY construction uses |w^T_t - 60%| >= delta.
The literal one-sided Appendix-B form is NOT discarded — it is carried as an
explicitly counted arm of the grid (`threshold_1sided`, section 5), so that
resolving this ambiguity costs trials instead of being a free choice made after
seeing which one worked. This also flags a disagreement with the commissioning
brief, which stated the two-sided form as if it were Appendix B's text; the
brief was right about the intent and imprecise about the source, and this
document follows the paper.

3.3 DEVIATION D3 — WINDOW. Paper: 1997-09-10..2023-03-17. This build:
2002-07-30..2026-09-04 (IEF's inception binds the start). Two consequences,
both reported:
  LOST: 1997-2002, including the dot-com unwind, and the paper's own finding
        (Section 3, p.29) that "the signals' predictive power became
        significant in the early 2000s" means the lost years are the WEAK ones
        — this truncation is, if anything, favourable to the hypothesis.
  GAINED: 2023-03-18..2026-09-04, ~3.5 years that are GENUINELY OUT OF SAMPLE
        relative to the paper's estimation window and postdate its March 2025
        first circulation. That is the single most informative slice in this
        build and is pre-declared as fidelity check F7.

3.4 DEVIATION D4 — NO CONTROLS. The paper's headline is the MULTIVARIATE Eq.
(4), with momentum, one-day trailing return, VIX, MOVE, EPU, ADS and a news
sentiment index. This project has ^VIX and ^MOVE in its price store but not
EPU, ADS or the Shapiro-Sudhof-Wilson sentiment index, and a partial control
set is not the paper's specification. The predictive REGRESSIONS reported as
fidelity checks are therefore the paper's UNIVARIATE Eq. (1) and Eq. (3), which
are the paper's own stated forms for exactly those two checks, and are labelled
as univariate. NO multivariate coefficient from Table 1 is claimed to be
replicated. What is traded is a portfolio, and a portfolio has no controls at
all — which is the honest position for a backtest and is why the CONTROL SPEC
in section 5 exists instead.

3.5 DEVIATION D5 — "LAST N DAYS OF THE MONTH" IS READ AS TRADING DAYS.
The paper says "days" and plots "Days to end-of-month" on daily futures
observations; trading days is the only reading consistent with that. Declared,
not discovered.

3.6 DEVIATION D6 — "Calendar Signal_{-4}" in Section 4 IS READ AS LAG 4.
On the first trading day of a new month, t-4 is the fourth-to-last trading day
of the previous month under either reading (relative-to-month-end or
plain-lag), so the two coincide and the interpretation is not load-bearing.
Declared anyway.

3.7 NON-DEVIATION, STATED SO IT IS NOT LATER MISTAKEN FOR ONE. The delta grid
(0%-2.5%, 0.1% steps, N=26) and the 1.5% rescaling constant are taken VERBATIM
from [HMM26] Eq. (2) and Section 4. They are not tuned here, not searched here,
and not chosen with any knowledge of how they perform on SPY/IEF. The paper's
own footnote 10 addresses the full-sample-calibration concern for its own
choice; this build simply inherits the published constants, which is the
strictest available position.

5. THE GRID — 24 PRE-DECLARED SPECS, n_local = 24
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SIX SIGNALS x TWO SIZINGS x TWO HOLDS = 24. The product is asserted at import
against REBALANCING_N_TRIALS = 24 and is never shrunk to however many specs
survive the data floors.

SIGNALS (6):
  threshold          [HMM26] Eq. (2): the average over delta in {0.0%, 0.1%,
                     ..., 2.5%} (26 values) of Threshold Signal^delta from
                     Eq. (B.1), with the TWO-SIDED trigger (D2).
  threshold_1sided   Identical, with Appendix B's LITERAL one-sided trigger
                     (D2). A robustness arm that costs trials on purpose.
  calendar           [HMM26] Eq. (B.2), traded UNCONDITIONALLY, every day.
                     PRE-DECLARED PREDICTION: this cell FAILS. The paper's own
                     Table 1 gives it +0.0553 (0.0709), t = +0.78, and Table 2
                     Panel A Column (1) gives -0.0778 (0.0582) outside
                     month-end. It is in the grid as the matched negative
                     control for `calendar_week4`, not as a candidate.
  calendar_week4     [HMM26] Eq. (B.2) gated to the last 5 trading days of the
                     month (Eq. (3)'s Dummy^{5 Days}, the paper's own adopted
                     week4), PLUS Section 4's first-business-day REVERSAL leg
                     at +sign(Calendar Signal_{t-4}). Zero on all other days.
                     This is the paper's actual calendar claim.
  paper_combined     [HMM26] Section 4 verbatim: w^Strategy_t = mean of
                     (-Threshold Signal_t / 1.5%) and the modified Calendar
                     signal of `calendar_week4`. This is the spec whose
                     published counterpart reports Sharpe 1.11, and it is the
                     one this family exists to test.
  trailing21_ctrl    THE CONTROL, and the most important spec in the grid. The
                     SAME weight-deviation formula (2.1) evaluated on a rolling
                     21-TRADING-DAY window of cumulative SPY and IEF returns
                     with NO trigger and NO calendar: deviation_t =
                     f(60%, cum21 R_SPY, cum21 R_IEF) - 60%. Same units, same
                     scale, same sign convention, matched to the threshold
                     arm's own reported ~16-rebalances-per-year cadence
                     (~16 trading days) and to the calendar arm's ~21-trading-
                     day month. It contains the trailing-return content of the
                     rebalancing signals and NONE of their trigger or calendar
                     structure.
                     PRE-DECLARED READING, FIXED HERE: if `trailing21_ctrl`
                     matches or beats the rebalancing signals, this family
                     measured 21-day cross-asset REVERSAL in a rebalancing
                     costume, and the report says so regardless of the DSR.
                     This is the direct analogue of vol_regime_timing.py's
                     `vix_level` control spec, and it is motivated by the
                     paper's own admission (p.16) that "Momentum and
                     rebalancing signals have a correlation higher than 67%".

SIZINGS (2), both cited to [HMM26] Section 4:
  scaled   position = -signal_t / 1.5%           (the paper's own rescaling)
           For `paper_combined`, `scaled` means w^Strategy_t verbatim (the
           rescaling is already inside it and is not applied twice).
           NOT CLIPPED, because the paper does not clip. The realized maximum
           |position| is reported so the leverage this implies is visible.
  sign     position = -sign(signal_t)            (the paper's own Calendar
           treatment). For `paper_combined`, sign(w^Strategy_t).

HOLDS (2): 1 and 5 trading days.
  h=1  the paper's own horizon. Eq. (1) predicts Ret_{t+1}; a position formed
       at the close of t and held one day IS that regression, traded.
  h=5  an h-SLEEVE OVERLAPPING BOOK: the held position is the equal-weighted
       mean of the last h daily target positions, i.e. h equal sleeves each
       formed on a different day and held h days. This is a genuinely
       different book (not a rescaling), because it smooths a one-day-ahead
       prediction over five days; if the effect is really one-day it should
       DEGRADE here. It also cuts turnover by roughly 1/h, so the pair (1, 5)
       is simultaneously the cost-amortization axis. Declared, including the
       consequence that Sharpe is NOT invariant across it.
  NO h >= 21. A 21-day hold of a next-day prediction is not a slower version
  of this claim, it is a different claim.

DIRECTION IS NOT A FREE PARAMETER. Every spec trades
REBALANCING_DIRECTION = -1 ("equity overweight -> sell equity, buy bonds"),
fixed by [HMM26] p.10 ("both Threshold and Calendar signals should negatively
predict equity market returns and positively predict Treasury market returns")
before any return is computed. Specs whose realized sign is the opposite will
print negative Sharpes and will NOT be flipped. A flipped family is a second
24-trial search and must be counted as one.

WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED, and therefore not in the denominator: the delta grid
(inherited, 3.7), the 1.5% rescaling (inherited, 3.7), the week4 length of 5
days (the paper's own adopted choice, 2.6), the 21-day control window (fixed
a priori at the paper's own reported ~16/yr threshold cadence and ~21-day
month, not tuned), the traded pair (SPY/IEF, inherited from
vol_regime_timing.py), and the formation start (data-bound, 3.3).

docstring measures it: "at 2026-08-26 closes, a one-cent bid/ask on SPY (~$6xx)
is ~0.08bp, on IEF (~$95) ~1.05bp". The binding leg is IEF at ~1.05bp of
half-spread; 2.0bp one-way is set deliberately ABOVE it to cover commission and
slippage. A unit position change trades 2.0 units of gross notional (one long
leg, one short leg), so a flat-to-unit swing costs ~4bp of equity. It is a
DISCLOSED ASSUMPTION, not a measured execution cost, and the breakeven cost
multiple is reported so a reader can see how wrong it would have to be to
matter.

THIS IS THE FAMILY WHERE COSTS BITE HARDEST, AND THAT IS PRE-ACKNOWLEDGED. At
h=1 with `sign` sizing the book can flip from +1 to -1 in a day, trading 4.0
gross notional for ~8bp. The paper trades futures, whose all-in costs are far
below an ETF pair's, and its own cost treatment is one sentence ("Following the
conservative assumptions of Harvey et al. (2018), we estimate that the Sharpe
ratio net of transaction costs remains close to 1") with no turnover figure
tabulated. This build does not inherit that reassurance.

BORROW: REBALANCING_SHORT_BORROW_BPS_PER_YEAR =
borrow_cost.GENERAL_COLLATERAL_BPS_PER_YEAR = 34.0, charged on the SHORT
notional (|position|) and accrued on CALENDAR days / 365 (a weekend costs three
days). SOURCED, not assumed: Beneish, Lee & Nichols, "In Short Supply", JAE
60(1), 2015, p.15 (DCBS=1 fees average <34bp/yr), the conservative envelope
over D'Avolio (2002) Table 3's 17bp value-weighted GC mean. This deliberately
uses the project's sourced GC rate rather than vol_regime_timing.py's unsourced
50bp assumption; SPY and IEF are among the largest ETFs in existence and are
general collateral by any reading of D'Avolio p.273. The long leg is not
separately financed because the book is dollar-neutral and self-financing,
which is the assumption metrics.sharpe_ratio already documents.

PRE-DECLARED SENSITIVITY LADDER — Sharpe AND DSR recomputed on the same 24
specs, NO new trials, only changed returns:
    cost_free    cost_bps = 0.0, borrow = 0.0     (the gross signal, for
                 attribution only — never a verdict input)
    baseline     cost_bps = 2.0, borrow = 34.0    (THE verdict arm)
    stress       cost_bps = 10.0, borrow = 34.0   (~10x the binding IEF
                 half-spread; a deliberate, UNSOURCED stress standing in for
                 market impact and adverse selection on a daily-turnover book,
                 labelled as unsourced)
A verdict that flips anywhere on this ladder is reported as flipping.

CAPACITY / CROWDING (informational, not gating, per CLAUDE.md, since no capital
is live): this is a two-instrument overlay on the two deepest ETFs in the US

front-running 60/40 rebalancing is by construction the most public flow
prediction in existence. Any real edge here has had eighteen months of

8. MECHANISM-FIDELITY CHECKS, FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All are DIAGNOSTICS computed from the same replays or the same price panel.
None adds a spec, and none may be reported as the family's result.

F1  THRESHOLD-DELTA SENSITIVITY — reproduce [HMM26] Figure 2. Estimate Eq. (1)
    by OLS with White (1980) HC0 standard errors for delta = 0.0% to 4.0% in
    0.1% steps, on this project's own SPY/IEF panel, and report gamma1 and its
    t-statistic at every delta.
    PAPER'S SHAPE: gamma1 negative throughout; predictive power "peaks around 2
    percentage points and declines for values of delta above 2.5%" (p.11-12).
    PRE-DECLARED READING: if that shape does not replicate at all, that is an
    important honest finding and is reported as one. It is NOT grounds for
    picking whatever delta did work — the traded grid uses Eq. (2)'s inherited
    0-2.5% average regardless of what F1 shows.

F2  MONTH-END CONCENTRATION — reproduce [HMM26] Figure 3. Estimate Eq. (3) with
    HC0 standard errors for N = 1..10 days-to-end-of-month and report the
    t-statistic of beta2 at each N.
    PAPER'S SHAPE: "Calendar predictability peaks in the last four days of the
    month" and funds "avoid trades on the very last day", i.e. the curve is
    NON-MONOTONIC and N=1 is weaker than N=4. Reproduced as reported, not
    smoothed over.

F3  THE HEADLINE UNIVARIATE REGRESSION — Eq. (1) with the Eq. (2) averaged
    Threshold signal, and Eq. (3) with the Calendar signal. Report gamma1/beta2,
    HC0 t-statistics, R^2, n, and the ONE-STANDARD-DEVIATION effect in basis
    points per day, against the paper's stated 20bp (Threshold) and 19.2bp
    (Calendar) and its |t| > 4 / t = -3.61 / t = -3.75.

F4  REBALANCING FREQUENCY — measure the realized reset rate of the threshold
    recursion (per year, per delta and at the median delta) and of the calendar
    recursion, against the paper's stated ~16/yr and 12/yr (p.14).

F5  SIGNAL SUMMARY STATISTICS — mean sign of both signals (paper: both
    POSITIVE, p.14), AR(1), skewness, and the Threshold/Calendar correlation
    (paper: ~60%, p.14 and Table C.2).

F6  STATIC-TILT CONFOUND — for every spec, OLS beta of its own daily returns on
    the BUY-AND-HOLD SPY-minus-IEF spread, plus the Sharpe of the BETA-HEDGED
    stream (y - beta*x, NOT the OLS residual, which has mean zero by
    construction — the exact trap vol_regime_timing.py documents). A signal
    whose mean position is persistently one-signed is a static tilt, not
    timing. THIS FAMILY IS STRUCTURALLY EXPOSED TO IT: the paper itself says
    the signals' average value is POSITIVE, which under DIRECTION = -1 means
    the book is short equity / long bonds more often than not, and over
    2002-2026 that is a large persistent loser. A positive Sharpe that
    survives beta-hedging is meaningful; one that does not is a tilt.

F7  PAPER-WINDOW SPLIT — every spec's Sharpe reported separately over
    2003-08-01..2023-03-17 (inside [HMM26]'s estimation window) and
    2023-03-18..2026-09-04 (GENUINELY out of sample for the paper, and
    postdating its March 2025 circulation). PRE-DECLARED READING: a result that
    lives entirely in the in-paper window is consistent with the effect having
    been arbitraged after publication OR with it never having been real
    out-of-sample; either way it is not tradeable now and is reported as such.

F8  CRISIS DEPENDENCE — every spec's Sharpe in equal thirds of its own sample,
    and its Sharpe EXCLUDING 2008-09-01..2009-03-31 and 2020-03-01..2020-03-31,
    which are exactly the two windows [HMM26] p.38 excludes (it reports the
    strategy Sharpe falling 1.11 -> 0.90). This family's own precedent for the
    check is vol_regime_timing.py's one-crisis-dependence diagnostic.

F9  BLOCK BOOTSTRAP — circular block bootstrap Sharpe p-value under a zero-mean
    null with block length = max(holding_days, 5), reusing
    vol_regime_timing.block_bootstrap_sharpe_pvalue unmodified rather than
    reimplementing it. Where it disagrees with the daily-count DSR, the
    bootstrap is the one to believe.

F10 THE CONTROL SPEC — section 5's `trailing21_ctrl`, in-grid, with its
    pre-declared reading fixed there.

F11 SYNTHETIC VALIDATION OF THE RECURSION, before it touches real data. The
    weight recursion is a published formula and CLAUDE.md forbids trusting one
    without a known-answer check. [HMM26] footnote 8 supplies the known answer
    verbatim: "If the equity market increases by 10% and the bond market
    remains unchanged, the portfolio value becomes 66 + 40 = $106. The equity

    also from footnote 8: the required rebalancing TRADE is
    0.60 * 0.40 * 10% = 2.4% of portfolio value.

10. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LOW — but NOT as low as this session's other four flow candidates, and the
reasons for both halves of that sentence are recorded so neither can be
retrofitted.

WHY IT IS NOT AS LOW: the source is recent, the author is among the most cited
empiricists in the field, the mechanism is mechanical rather than behavioural
(a target-weight fund MUST rebalance; nobody has to be making a mistake), the
paper reports a Sharpe above 1 with a stated cost treatment, the effect is
documented in exactly the two instruments this project can trade cheaply, and
the paper's own identification (state-dependent non-linearity in Figure D.3,
the week4 concentration, the size/style extension in Section 3.4) is unusually
concrete for a flow story.

WHY IT IS STILL LOW. Seven reasons, each stated so a positive result would have
to survive all of them:

 (1) THE SIGNAL IS MECHANICALLY A TRAILING-RETURN FUNCTION. Every one of these
     signals is a smooth function of past SPY-minus-IEF returns. The paper
     concedes the correlation with momentum exceeds 67% and controls for it in
     a multivariate regression; a TRADED PORTFOLIO cannot control for anything.
     This build's whole defence is one control spec, and the honest expectation
     is that `trailing21_ctrl` performs comparably.
 (2) COSTS. A next-day prediction traded through an ETF pair pays real spread
     every day. The paper trades futures and tabulates no turnover.
 (3) PUBLICITY AND AGE. Circulated March 2025, revised January 2026, presented
     at the AFA 2026 Annual Meeting, and about the single most predictable flow
     in markets. McLean & Pontiff (2016) measure 58% post-publication decay
     across 97 predictors; preservation_score already applies that 0.42
     retention factor here.
 (4) NOT PEER REVIEWED (section 1).
 (5) THE STATIC-TILT TRAP (F6). A mostly-positive signal traded at -1 is a
     persistent short-equity book across a window in which equities beat bonds
     substantially. Either it is a large negative tilt, or the timing has to be
     good enough to overcome one.
 (6) THE INSTRUMENT SUBSTITUTION IS NOT FREE (D1). It is defensible and its
     first-order term cancels, but it is not the paper's data and this build
     cannot rule out that the residual matters.
 (7) EVERY PRIOR FLOW CANDIDATE THIS SESSION CAME BACK NEGATIVE, and three of
     the four came back negative on this project's own DSR bar rather than on
     data availability. The base rate in this project is what it is.

AN HONEST NEGATIVE IS A COMPLETE, USEFUL OUTCOME AND IS THE EXPECTED ONE. The
failure mode this pre-registration exists to prevent is not a negative — it is
a positive manufactured by choosing the delta, the trigger sidedness, the week4
length, the sizing rule, the holding period, the window or the cost model after
seeing which one worked. Every one of those is fixed above.
