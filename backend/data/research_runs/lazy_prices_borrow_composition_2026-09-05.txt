WHAT lazy_prices ACTUALLY SHORTS, AND WHAT BORROWING IT COSTS
2026-09-05. The S2/S3 bracket replaced by a measurement.
==============================================================================

WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 2026-09-05 cost correction (39f7cb5, report edge_cost_correction_2026-09-05
.txt) priced this family's short leg two ways and called neither a measurement:

    S2   every shorted name is general collateral       ->  34 bp/yr
    S3   every shorted name is a special                -> 430 bp/yr

Those two differ by 12.6x, and the difference is the whole result: S2 costs the
family 0.078 Sharpe and leaves it where it was, S3 takes it to 0/36 specs
positive. A 12.6x bracket around a number that decides the outcome is not a
finding, it is a question.

That report's own reading was "the true rate is near the GC end", resting on
D'Avolio p.273: "S&P 500 constituents, provided in excess supply by indexing
lenders, are almost always general collateral", plus the argument that "this
family's short leg is not built from heavily-shorted names".

THE SECOND HALF OF THAT ARGUMENT DOES NOT SUPPORT THE CONCLUSION, and that is
the first finding here. borrow_cost.BorrowSchedule charges the specials rate to
BOTH short-interest tails, not just the high one — deliberately, because
Beneish/Lee/Nichols p.5 document a U-shape ("both extremely high SIR firms and
extremely low SIR firms have a greater probability of being on special"), with
lendable supply rather than borrowing demand driving the low end. So "not built
from heavily-shorted names" does not imply "cheap": a leg that avoids the high
tail can still sit in the low one.

THE ARITHMETIC THAT MAKES THIS CONCRETE. Under the schedule's default 10%
tails, a short leg drawn INDEPENDENTLY of short interest holds ~20% tail names
and costs

    0.20 * 430 + 0.80 * 34 = 113.2 bp/yr

So 113.2, not 34, is the "this family has nothing to do with short interest"
answer. S2's 34 is the answer for a leg that actively AVOIDS BOTH TAILS, which
is a strictly stronger claim than the one the report made. Neither 34 nor 430
had been checked against what the strategy actually holds.

borrow_cost.py itself asks for the check rather than the bracket:
rate_for_percentiles() assigns a rate per name from that name's cross-sectional
short-interest percentile, book_borrow_bps_per_year() averages that schedule
over a real short leg, and worst_case_bps_per_year() is documented as "the
bound to quote when a family cannot compute percentiles at all". This run
computes the percentiles.

WHAT WAS RUN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Script: data/research_runs/run_lazy_prices_borrow_composition.py
Payload: data/research_runs/lazy_prices_borrow_composition_2026-09-05.json

The REGISTERED spec (lazy_jaccard_full_h126_ivol) replayed through the
harness's own run_cross_sectional_backtest on the family's own frozen price
snapshot and frozen filing index — the same data build as the cost-correction
run, so the formations are the same formations — and every formed
FormationRecord.short_tickers list read off directly. 24 formations, 2015-01-08
.. 2026-07-21, 71 to 98 names per short leg.

The short-interest ratio panel is built by the SHORT-INTEREST FAMILY'S OWN
functions, not by anything reimplemented here: FinraShortInterestProvider.
fetch_observations_for_tickers, SecSharesOutstandingProvider.fetch_share_counts,
build_point_in_time_share_count_frame and build_short_interest_panels. Their
point-in-time contract therefore applies unchanged — a value is visible only
from FINRA's own publication date, and the share count is read at the LATER of
the two inputs' public dates.

Percentiles are taken within THIS FAMILY'S OWN ELIGIBLE S&P 500 CROSS-SECTION
on each formation date (every name carrying a jaccard/full value AND a SIR),
then fed to BorrowSchedule.rate_for_percentiles unmodified.

COVERAGE, STATED BEFORE THE RESULT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINRA's free file starts 2017-12-29 and the SEC shares-outstanding frames cache
starts CY2017Q4; this family's window opens 2015-01-07. So:

    24 formations total
    17 measured (2018-07-11 .. 2026-07-21)
     7 with no SIR coverage at all (2015-01-08 .. 2018-01-09)

Within the 17 measured formations, 92.4% of short-leg names carry a SIR on
average (min 84.7%, max 97.9%). The 7 uncovered formations are reported
separately and are NEVER folded into an average as though they were measured.
The measured span covers 71% of the family's formations and includes 2020 and
2022, so it is not a single-regime window.

THE RESULT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                    short leg    long leg    no-tilt baseline
  share of names in EITHER tail       0.1710      0.1745         0.2000
    ... in the HIGH-SIR tail          0.0722         —           0.1000
    ... in the LOW-SIR tail           0.0988         —           0.1000
  median SIR percentile               0.4837      0.4060         0.5000

  (means over the 17 measured formations; short-leg tail share ranges
   0.1098 .. 0.3056 across them, median 0.1667)

READ THE FIRST ROW AGAINST THE LAST COLUMN. lazy_prices' short leg is NOT
tilted toward hard-to-borrow names. It holds FEWER tail names than an
independent draw would (0.1710 vs 0.2000) and essentially the same share as its
own LONG leg (0.1745). The high-SIR tail specifically is under-represented by
about 28% relative to chance (0.0722 vs 0.1000).

The median SIR percentile does move in the expected direction — "changers" are
slightly more shorted than "non-changers", 0.4837 vs 0.4060 — but both sit near
the middle of the distribution, and the shift is far too small to push names
into the expensive decile. The mechanism (underreaction to changed 10-K
language) is, as far as this measurement can see, close to orthogonal to borrow
difficulty. That is the answer to the question the registration's own text left
open in both directions.

WHAT THE SCHEDULE THEN CHARGES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                          bps/yr on the SHORT LEG   financing_
                                        mean   median   min    max  bps_per_year
  S2 assumption (all GC)                34.0    34.0   34.0   34.0      17.00
  MEASURED, schedule's NaN->GC rule     96.3    93.9   75.0  136.5      48.16
  MEASURED, covered names only         101.7   100.0   77.5  155.0      50.87
  no-tilt reference                    113.2      —      —      —       56.60
  S3 bracket (all specials)            430.0   430.0  430.0  430.0     215.00

financing_bps_per_year is B/2 — the config field charges GROSS notional and a
formed long_short book carries gross 2.0, so half the short-leg rate on gross
2.0 is exactly the stated rate on the 1.0 short leg
(borrow_cost.financing_bps_for_long_short_book).

The two MEASURED rows differ only in how the ~7.6% of short-leg names with no
SIR observation are treated: the first follows borrow_cost's own documented
NaN->GC default (justified for an S&P 500 universe by D'Avolio p.273), the
second ignores them. Both land near 100 bp/yr. 96.3 is the figure to carry
forward, because it is the one the schedule actually produces.

ARITHMETIC CHECK, done by hand rather than trusted:
  measured-only  = 0.1710*430 + 0.8290*34 = 73.53 + 28.19 = 101.72   (script: 101.73)
  schedule-default ~ 0.9237*101.72 + 0.0763*34 = 93.96 + 2.59 = 96.55 (script: 96.33;
  the small gap is mean-of-products vs product-of-means across 17 formations)
  financing = 96.3287 / 2 = 48.1644                                   (script: 48.1644)

SO BOTH BRACKETS ARE WRONG, IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  S2 (34 bp/yr)  UNDERCHARGES this leg by ~2.8x. Its premise — every shorted
                 name easy to borrow — requires the leg to avoid both tails,
                 and it does not: 17.1% of it sits in one.
  S3 (430 bp/yr) OVERCHARGES it by ~4.5x. Its premise — every shorted name a
                 special — is contradicted by the same measurement and by
                 Beneish/Lee/Nichols p.17 ("even in the highest SIR decile,
                 less than 30 percent of the stocks are special"), which caps
                 what any SIR-sorted decile can cost.

S3 remains a legitimate BRACKET and this run does not retire it. What it does
retire is treating S3 as the conservative DEFAULT for this family: a cost 4.5x
above the measured one is not caution, it is a different wrong number from the
0.0 it would replace, and it would manufacture a negative as surely as 0.0
manufactures a positive. Capital preservation is not served by either.

WHAT THIS RUN DOES *NOT* ESTABLISH — the limits, stated rather than found later
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. IT IS STILL NOT A BORROW-RATE FEED. Every rate here is D'Avolio's or
    Beneish/Lee/Nichols', mapped onto names by a short-interest proxy both
    papers explicitly warn against (D'Avolio p.285: "the limited use of this
    measure as a proxy for short-sale constraints"). The paid-feed gap stays
    open — PENDING_PAID_DATA_DECISIONS.md, entry P1. 96.3 bp/yr is a better
    approximation than 34 or 430, not a measurement of what a broker would
    actually charge.
 2. THE SCHEDULE OVERCHARGES BY CONSTRUCTION, so 96.3 is itself conservative.
    BLN's "less than 30 percent" means charging a whole decile the specials
    rate prices roughly 70% of it too high. A truer figure for an S&P 500 leg
    is probably well below 96.3 and nearer D'Avolio's 17-20 bp GC mean. The
    direction of the remaining error is toward OVERcharging, which is the safe
    one.
 3. PERCENTILES ARE WITHIN THE S&P 500 CROSS-SECTION, not the whole market.
    D'Avolio's deciles are market-wide, and an S&P 500 top decile contains far
    fewer true specials than a market-wide one. Same direction: overcharges.
 4. SEVEN OF 24 FORMATIONS ARE UNMEASURED (2015-01 .. 2018-01). The 17 that are
    measured are stable (0.11-0.31 tail share, no trend), so extrapolating is
    reasonable — but it is extrapolation, not measurement.
 5. THE STALENESS IN borrow_cost.py APPLIES UNCHANGED. Its samples end 2013;
    securities lending has since electronified and compressed. That makes the
    GC leg of the schedule more likely an overstatement than an understatement
    in 2026.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE LIVE REGISTRATION — AND WHY NOTHING WAS ENACTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Nothing here was applied to lazy_prices_jaccard_full, and the reason is
structural rather than a matter of taste.

financing_bps_per_year is one of config_identity()'s six fields. Any non-zero
value re-hashes config_fingerprint, detect_config_drift then returns a reason,
and the runner's drift gate calls _park_as_drifted — status "spec_drift",
which ACTIVE_STATUSES excludes, permanently and non-reversibly without a human.
Verified directly:

    registered (financing 0.0)   config_fingerprint 2dccbf93...  (matches the live row)
    financing  17.0 (S2)                            e63f283c...  -> parks
    financing  48.16 (measured)                     97d087f7...  -> parks
    financing 215.0 (S3)                            f56f3176...  -> parks

Adopting ANY borrow rate therefore ENDS this registration's forward record.
That is an operational-status change, which is the repo owner's decision and
never a side effect of a cost fix. It is pinned as an executable argument in
tests/test_lazy_prices_cost_basis_switch.py::
test_any_non_zero_borrow_rate_would_park_this_registration.

The number is now on the record so that the decision, when it is made, is made
against 96.3 bp/yr (financing_bps_per_year 48.16) rather than against a 12.6x
bracket. The measured-borrow outcome for the family is in
lazy_prices_cost_basis_switch_2026-09-05.txt.

OPEN, AND EXPLICITLY NOT ACTED ON: this same measurement has never been run for
short_interest_ratio, whose long_short specs short the MOST heavily shorted
names in the index and which borrow_cost.py's own docstring names as the family
where the 0.0 "bites hardest". Its short leg is selected ON the variable this
schedule keys off, so its tail share will be far above 20% and its true rate
far above this family's. That is a separate decision for the repo owner, on a
separate live registration, and is not touched here.
