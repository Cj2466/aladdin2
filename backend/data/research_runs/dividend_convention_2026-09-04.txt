==========================================================================
0. THE DECISION, UP FRONT
==========================================================================
The dividend CONVENTION is flipped to CRSP and is now the default.
The SAME-DAY SPLIT+DISTRIBUTION DROP RULE that the CRSP enum silently
carried is NOT enabled, and no longer defaults on. It stays available as an
explicit opt-in.

Those were one flag before this change. They are two decisions, they point
in opposite directions on the evidence, and shipping them together would
have fixed one real defect while introducing a larger new one on six real
universe members.

2. WHY THE DROP RULE IS NOT SHIPPED — IT IS WRONG 13 TIMES OUT OF 15
==========================================================================
The rule assumes Yahoo DOUBLE-ENCODES one distribution as both a split
ratio and a cash amount, so counting the cash again double-counts it. That
is true for 2 of the 15 events and false for the other 13, where the split
ratio and the cash amount describe TWO DIFFERENT, BOTH-REAL distributions.

  event                split  recorded  split-IMPLIED   what it really was
                       ratio  dividend  value
  DHR  2016-07-05      1.319   27.7037   24.7461        double-encoding  x1
  XRX  2017-01-03      1.518    0.7450    2.9790        double-encoding  x1
  DXC  2015-11-30      1.7412  10.5000   29.2104        SEPARATE cash    x1
  SSP  2015-04-01      1.127    1.0300    3.2049        SEPARATE cash    x1
  RILY 2016-11-25      1.01     0.2500    0.1688        SEPARATE cash    x1
  TR   every March     1.03     ~0.087    ~0.97-1.27    SEPARATE cash   x10

  Totals: 13 SEPARATE-cash events, 2 double-encoding events.

EVERY ONE OF THE 13 IS CONFIRMED AGAINST A COMPANY FILING OR RELEASE, not
inferred from the ratio-versus-amount mismatch (which on its own proves
nothing — DHR's implied 24.75 and recorded 27.70 also differ, and DHR IS a
double-encoding).

TR (Tootsie Roll) is the clearest and most numerous. It declares a regular
QUARTERLY CASH DIVIDEND of $0.08-0.09 and, separately, an annual 3% STOCK
DIVIDEND, and their ex-dates coincide each March. (Tootsie Roll 8-K,
CIK 0000098677 — "declared a regular quarterly dividend of $0.08 per share"
and "also declared a 3% stock dividend".) The 1.03 ratio is the stock
dividend; the $0.087 is the cash. Dropping the cash discards a real
distribution ten times over.

SSP (E.W. Scripps) 2015-04-01 is the second cleanest. Scripps shareholders

CONCLUSION ON THE DROP RULE. Getting a same-day split+distribution right
requires knowing whether the "split" is a genuine share-count change or a
spin-off price factor, and whether the recorded cash is the same value
again or a separate payment. Yahoo's feed does not carry that distinction —
it puts both in one `Stock Splits` column and one `Dividends` column. No
blind rule can be right for all six names. The rule is therefore NOT made a
default: the six affected names keep the SAME-DAY treatment every recorded
number in this project already ran on (their convention still changes, like
every other name's, and each of the four checkable events gets less wrong
for it — see the arms above), and the residual known-bad events are listed
here rather than silently "fixed" in the wrong direction.

==========================================================================
3. THE CONVENTION ITSELF — VERIFIED AGAINST CRSP'S OWN TEXT, EQUATION BY
   EQUATION
==========================================================================
Source re-fetched and re-read for this review, not quoted from the earlier
report: CRSP Data Description Guide, Chapter 5 "CRSP Calculations", pp.
117-119, retrieved 2026-09-04 from
leiq.bus.umich.edu/docs/crsp_calculations_splits.pdf and extracted to text.

  p.117, verbatim: "Price and dividend data are adjusted with the
  calculation: A(t)=P(t)/C(t)" ... "Share and volume data are adjusted with
  the calculation: A(t)=P(t)*C(t)" ... "if t=C0,C(t) = 1.0 / if t>C0 and no
  split events since t-1,C(t) = C(t-1) / if t>C0 and a split event with
  factor f since t-1,C(t) = C(t-1) * f / ... / if t<C0 and a split event
  change C(t+1)*f" ... "The adjustment base date is usually chosen to be
  the last available day of trading."

  p.119, verbatim: "Divamt in period adjusted to end of range = divamt /
  cumfacpr / facpr" ... "Thus, to calculate a total return using adjusted
  prices and dividends, Total Return = (adjprc + (divamt / cumfacpr /
  facpr)) / prev_adjprc -1" where "divamt = Dividend Amount in Period"
  (p.119: "adjusted to the basis at the end of the previous period"),
  "cumfacpr = Cumulative Factor to Adjust Prices over a Date Range",
  "facpr = Factor to Adjust Price in Period".

CHECK 1 — C(t). cumulative_split_factor returns, for each date t, the
product of every ratio whose ex-date is STRICTLY LATER than t, with the
base at the window's last row. That is CRSP's backwards recursion
C(t)=C(t+1)*f, with the base date at the last available day of trading.
PASS. Pinned against a real event: AAPL's 4-for-1 split has ex-date
2020-08-31, so C(2020-08-28)=4 and C(2020-08-31)=1; the reconstruction
returns 499.23 and 129.04 respectively, both correct.

CHECK 2 — A(t)=P(t)/C(t) for prices, A(t)=P(t)*C(t) for volume.
split_adjusted_prices divides every price column by the factor and
multiplies volume by it. PASS, matching the verbatim text in both
directions.

CHECK 3 — the total-return formula. The implementation computes
    growth(t) = (P(t)/C(t) + D(t)/C(t)) / (P(t-1)/C(t-1))
Writing C(t-1) = f(t)*C(t) with f(t) the ratio on date t, this is
    growth(t) = [ f(t)*P(t) + f(t)*D(t) ] / P(t-1)
CRSP's p.119 formula, expanded the same way, is
    growth(t) = [ f(t)*P(t) + divamt ] / P(t-1)
so the two agree exactly iff divamt = f(t)*D(t). CRSP's divamt is stated to
be on the PREVIOUS period's basis; the store's D(t) is on the ex-date
(current) basis; and one old share becomes f(t) new shares, so a payment of
D per new share is f(t)*D per old share. The identity holds. PASS.

  For f(t)=1 — which is 38,138 of the 38,153 distribution events in this
  project's universes, 99.96% — this reduces to (P(t)+D(t))/P(t-1), which
  is not merely CRSP's definition but the arithmetic definition of what a
  buy-and-hold holder earned. It cannot be subtly wrong; it is an identity.
  The 15 exceptions are exactly the same-day events of section 2.

CHECK 4 — missing returns. CRSP p.117: "If ri is missing, that return is
ignored and thus in effect treated as a return of 0." The implementation
fills a missing growth with 1.0 before the cumulative product. PASS.

CHECK 5 — base-date normalisation. The series is rescaled so its value at
the window's last valid row equals the split-adjusted close there. CRSP:
"All data on this date are unadjusted." PASS.

ONE DEVIATION FROM CRSP, FOUND AND NOT FIXED (out of scope, and immaterial
here). CRSP p.117 states "Shares and volumes are only adjusted using stock
splits and stock dividends" — NOT the price factors of spin-offs, rights
and stock distributions. The store adjusts volume by every non-unit entry
in Yahoo's `Stock Splits` column, which conflates the two. It affects the
six section-2 tickers only, and the two places this project actually
consumes volume are insensitive to it: Amihud ILLIQ uses close*volume
(dollar volume, invariant under the reciprocal price/volume scaling) and
the Round-C turnover proxy uses volume divided by its own rolling mean (a
ratio). Recorded here rather than left to be rediscovered.

==========================================================================
5. THE STEELMAN FOR NOT SWITCHING, AND WHY IT DOES NOT HOLD
==========================================================================
ARGUMENT 1 — "Yahoo's convention is self-consistent. Even if the levels are
wrong, every stock is treated identically, so a cross-sectional RANKING is
undisturbed and the strategies are unaffected."

This is the strongest form of the case, and it fails on a closed form.
Writing k = D/P_prev:

    1 + r_YAHOO = P/(P_prev - D) = (P/P_prev)/(1-k)
    1 + r_CRSP  = (P + D)/P_prev = P/P_prev + k
  =>  r_YAHOO = r_CRSP / (1 - k)                        EXACTLY

Yahoo's convention does not shift the level — it MULTIPLIES the day's true
total return by 1/(1 - D/P_prev). It is a leverage applied on ex-dates only,
in proportion to the distribution's size relative to price.

Measured on every ordinary (no same-day split) distribution in both
universes with D/P_prev > 5% — 144 events:
    events with a POSITIVE true return: 78, Yahoo overstated ALL 78
    events with a NEGATIVE true return: 65, Yahoo understated ALL 65
    (the remaining 1 had an exactly-zero true return, GFF 2018-03-28, and is
     unchanged by either convention — as the closed form requires)
    |error|: median 0.1498%, p90 0.9513%, max 9.6078% (KDP)
The sign of the error is set by the sign of the true return on all 143
events that have a sign.  Reproduce with:
    python data/research_runs/run_dividend_convention.py amplify

That is not a neutral level offset a ranking can absorb; it is a distortion
applied only to names paying a large distribution, and any strategy whose
signal correlates with distribution size inherits it as spurious variance.
KDP's day is amplified 6.21x (1/(1-0.839)).

Where the argument DOES have force: aggregated over eleven years the effect
is genuinely small — the median name's cumulative wealth is unchanged to
six decimal places, p5 -0.2339% and p95 +0.1886%. "Small in aggregate" is
accepted here and is why this is a considered change rather than an
emergency. It is not accepted as a reason to keep a formula that is known
to be a leverage on ex-dates.

A latent failure mode worth naming: when D > P_prev the YAHOO denominator
goes non-positive and the day's return is silently dropped as NaN. No such
event exists in this project's universes today (the largest is ELME
2026-01-08 at 84.65% of price) but the CRSP form has no such degeneracy.

ARGUMENT 2 — "Switching introduces NEW risk: is Yahoo's raw dividend/split
data reliable enough to compute CRSP-style adjustments from?"

This argument is CORRECT, and it is the reason the drop rule is not
shipped. Section 2 and section 4 document three concrete unreliabilities:
Yahoo puts genuine splits and spin-off price factors in one column; its
spin-off "dividend" amounts are not restated for later factors while its
ordinary dividends are; and its spin-off ratios are derived from
when-issued prices and can be ~9% off the value that actually traded.

But it does NOT apply to the convention itself. Both conventions read the
SAME D(t) and the SAME f(t) from the same stored rows; the convention is
only the arithmetic that combines them. Where D is unreliable, both
conventions are unreliable, and the switch neither helps nor hurts. The
argument bites only on a change that depends on INTERPRETING the encoding —
which is exactly the drop rule, and exactly why it is left off.

ARGUMENT 3 — "Is CRSP-style really best given only free yfinance data?
Would a simpler convention be equally correct with less implementation
risk?"

The alternatives were enumerated:
  * Keep Yahoo's — shown wrong.
  * Price-only (drop dividends entirely) — a DIFFERENT quantity (CRSP's own
    "Capital Appreciation"), not a simpler version of the same one, and a
    far larger change to every recorded number.
  * Winsorise or exclude large distributions — adds a discretionary
    parameter, still wrong on the small ones, and deletes real events.
  * (P + D)/P_prev — three lines, no parameters.
There is no simpler CORRECT alternative: (P+D)/P_prev is the definition of
a one-period total return. The implementation risk is the lowest of any
option on the list, not the highest.

ARGUMENT 4 — "What if this is subtly wrong? Could the switch be worse than
doing nothing?"

Bounded, and the bound is checkable:
  * For 99.96% of distribution events the new formula is an algebraic
    identity for what a holder earned. There is no room for a subtle error.
  * The remaining 15 same-day events DO move under the convention change —
    stated plainly rather than glossed: DHR's cumulative wealth falls 13.57%,
    XRX's 3.40%, DXC's 2.62%, and the other three by under 0.2%. Those days
    were already wrong under Yahoo and remain wrong; the change makes each of
    them LESS wrong (DHR +61.22% -> +39.36% against a true +2.33%, XRX
    +37.64% -> +32.76% against a true +10.36%, DXC +8.37% -> +6.14%), which
    is why they are not a reason against it. What the shipped change does
    leave untouched is the DROP RULE, so no event moves in the direction the
    evidence says is wrong.
  * Section 6 measures every family and every spec, not just the four live
    registrations.
  * Nothing in app/services/execution/ is touched and
    ExecutionControl.trading_halted stays True, so the worst case is a
    research-record error with no capital consequence, revertible in one
    line.

==========================================================================
7. WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX, STATED PLAINLY
==========================================================================
 * The six same-day split+distribution names (DHR, DXC, XRX, RILY, SSP, TR)
   still carry a wrong single-day return under the shipped default. Section
   2 quantifies each. Fixing them needs a corporate-actions source that
   distinguishes a share-count change from a price factor, which this
   project does not have at any price on its current feed.
 * Yahoo's spin-off ratios and spin-off "dividend" amounts remain
   unreliable inputs; nothing here improves them.
 * CSRA's first-day price, and delisted-security prices generally, remain
   unobtainable — the standing delisted-securities gap.
 * The volume-adjustment deviation from CRSP in section 3 is recorded, not
   fixed.
 * FOUND WHILE MEASURING, NOT CAUSED BY THIS CHANGE: short_interest's
   absolute LEVEL is not reproducible across sessions. The yahoo arm here
   gives si_ratio_hedged_h21 Sharpe +0.42712, its own registration recorded
   +0.4531, and this morning's price-store rollout measured +0.41870 — three
   same-window rebuilds, three levels. The convention delta measured here is
   unaffected (both arms share byte-identical inputs), but the price store
   evidently did not close this family's reproducibility gap, and the
   remaining cause is a non-price input or the store having accumulated more
   tickers between runs. Not diagnosed here, and deliberately not guessed at.
   Recorded in short_interest_forward_registration.py as an open item.
 * price_cache.py's PriceBar table caches a DERIVED adjusted close, so rows
   written before and after this change sit on two different return
   definitions. Smaller than the base-date splice already documented there,
   self-heals on refetch, and disclosed in that module rather than fixed —
   the clean remedy is a one-time DELETE FROM price_bars.
