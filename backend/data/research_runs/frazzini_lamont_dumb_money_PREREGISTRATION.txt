2. THREE CORRECTIONS TO THE BUILD BRIEF
----------------------------------------
The commissioning brief explicitly asked to have its own description checked
against the paper rather than trusted. It is wrong in three ways, the first
of them fundamental.

CORRECTION 1 (THE MEASURE — fundamental). The brief described the stock-level
measure as "likely something like a value-weighted average, across the funds
holding a stock, of each fund's own recent flow (possibly weighted by
ownership share)". IT IS NOT AN AVERAGE OF FUND FLOWS AT ALL. It is a
COUNTERFACTUAL OWNERSHIP DECOMPOSITION, denominated in percent of the stock's
market capitalisation. Section 2, p.301, verbatim:

    "Our central variable is FLOW, the percent of the shares of a given stock
     owned by mutual funds that is attributable to fund flows. This variable
     is defined as the actual ownership by mutual funds minus the ownership
     that would have occurred if every fund had received identical
     proportional inflows, every fund manager chose the same portfolio weights
     in different stocks as he actually did, and stock prices were the same as
     they actually were."

Formally, Eqs. (6), (7) and (8), p.303, quoted verbatim:

    z_jt     = ( SUM_i  x_it  w_ijt  TNA^Agg_t ) / MKTCAP_jt            (6)
    zhat_jt  = ( SUM_i  xhat_it w_ijt TNA^Agg_t ) / MKTCAP_jt           (7)
    FLOW_jt  = z_jt - zhat_jt
             = { SUM_i [ x_it - xhat_it ] w_ijt TNA^Agg_t } / MKTCAP_jt (8)

where (Eqs. 4 and 5, p.303) x_it = TNA^i_t / TNA^Agg_t is fund i's actual
share of the fund sector and xhat_it = dTNA^i_t / dTNA^Agg_t is its
counterfactual share; w_ijt is fund i's portfolio weight in stock j; and
MKTCAP_jt is the firm's market capitalisation.

The counterfactual TNA is built by redistributing the sector's AGGREGATE flows
pro rata (Eqs. 2/11 and 3/12, pp.302 and 320):

    Fhat^i_s   = ( TNA^i_{t-k} / TNA^Agg_{t-k} ) * F^Agg_s        (2) = (11)
    dTNA^i_s   = ( 1 + R^i_s ) dTNA^i_{s-1} + Fhat^i_s            (3) = (12)
    for t-k <= s <= t

So the estimand is "how much of this stock's mutual-fund ownership exists only
because money went to THESE managers rather than pro rata across all of them",
measured in percent of shares outstanding. An average of holder flows, which
is what the brief described, is a different object with different units.

This matters beyond bookkeeping, because it is what makes the candidate
genuinely distinct from its two already-closed neighbours in this queue:
  * Lou (2012) Eq.(3), FIT family, DEFINITE_NEGATIVE: a share-weighted,
    severity-weighted AVERAGE of the flows of the funds holding the stock.
    Units: fraction of shares traded.
  * Coval-Stafford (2007) Eq.(4), firesale family, POWER FAILURE: an
    unweighted NET COUNT of constrained buyers minus constrained sellers,
    scaled by the number of owners. Units: fraction of owners.
  * Frazzini-Lamont (2008) Eq.(8), this family: a DIFFERENCE OF TWO OWNERSHIP
    LEVELS, actual minus counterfactual. Units: percent of market cap.
Three different functional forms of three different objects. This is a
separate test, not a re-run. (It is NOT independent evidence, though — see
section 8.)

CORRECTION 2 (THE DIRECTION AND WHAT IS BEING BOUGHT). The brief said "stocks
held by funds that are receiving heavy inflows ... subsequently UNDERPERFORM,
because the flow-driven buying is uninformed and reverses/fades." The
underperformance direction is right; the stated MECHANISM is not the paper's,
and the paper is unusually explicit about refusing it (p.302):

    "Although we find that sentiment affects stock prices, we do not attempt
     to analyze precisely the mechanism by which sentiment is propagated. ...
     We do not test this hypothesis nor draw a causal link between the price
     impact of individual funds and future stocks returns."

and it names Coval-Stafford as the price-pressure story it is NOT testing.
F-L's own framing is SENTIMENT: FLOW is "a measure of individual investor
sentiment for different stocks" (abstract) and "stocks ranking high in
popularity have low future returns" (p.302) — the stocks may be overpriced
because of mutual-fund buying OR because overall demand is high and fund flows
merely reveal it. This family is therefore pre-registered as a SENTIMENT
/ demand-revelation test, not a forced-trading test. Writing it up as
mechanical flow pressure would misattribute the claim.

CORRECTION 3 (WHAT THE PAPER SAYS ABOUT "SMART MONEY"). The brief called this
"a 'smart money vs dumb money' framing". The paper's actual finding is that
the smart-money effect largely FAILS to appear: p.307, "Table 2 shows no solid
evidence for the smart money effect in stock returns, even at the shorter
horizons where one might expect price momentum to dominate." The 3-month
horizon L/S is +0.033%/mo with t = 0.13. So the 3-month arm is pre-declared
here as a NULL-EXPECTED arm, on the paper's own evidence, exactly as
coval_stafford pre-declared its 5% arm as null-ish. A null at 3 months is not
evidence against this candidate.

4. DATA, AND THE SUBSTITUTION STATED PLAINLY
---------------------------------------------
F-L used CRSP MFDB + Thomson CDA/Spectrum, 1980-2003. Neither is available to
this project. The free substitute is SEC Form N-PORT bulk data, already
ingested for the Lou/FIT and Coval-Stafford families. THIS IS A SUBSTITUTION,
NOT AN EQUIVALENCE, and the gap here is LARGER than it was for coval_stafford
because F-L's construction needs sector-wide aggregates, not just per-stock
holdings.

 (a) HISTORY. Oct 2019 - now, 27 published quarters, versus the paper's 24
     years. This is the single largest constraint and it bites much harder
     here than it did for the previous two N-PORT families, because F-L's
     measure CONSUMES history: a k-quarter FLOW needs k quarters of lookback
     before its first observation. At the paper's own 3-year baseline
     (k = 12) that leaves ~15 formation quarters, i.e. roughly 45 monthly
     returns, for the arm that matters most. Pre-declared as check C2. The
     5-year arm (k = 20) leaves ~7 quarters and is EXCLUDED as infeasible —
     declared here, in advance, as an infeasibility, not tuned away later.

 (b) THE FUND UNIVERSE, AND WHY A NEW FETCH WAS NEEDED. F-L (p.302) restrict
     to "all domestic equity funds". That restriction is load-bearing rather
     than cosmetic in a way that is easy to miss: a bond fund contributes a
     ZERO term to Eq.(6)/(8)'s sum over funds, so it is invisible in the
     per-stock inputs — but it does enter TNA^Agg and F^Agg, and therefore
     shifts EVERY equity fund's counterfactual TNA through Eq.(11). N-PORT
     publishes no fund-type field (verified by enumerating all 32 members of
     the 2024q1 bulk ZIP: FUND_REPORTED_INFO's 47 columns carry assets,
     liabilities, credit spreads and flows but no category; REGISTRANT carries
     only CIK/name/address; MONTHLY_RETURN_CAT_INSTRUMENT is Item B.7
     DERIVATIVE gains and is empty for a long-only equity fund). Neither
     existing N-PORT cache can supply it either, and would fail SILENTLY:
     both were CUSIP-filtered at fetch time, so an equity share computed from
     them means "fraction of net assets in this index", not "fraction in
     common equity". A separate unfiltered streaming pass
     (fetch_nport_fund_asset_categories.py) therefore computes per-fund,
     per-ASSET_CAT portfolio totals.
     FROZEN THRESHOLD, fixed here before any result: a fund-quarter counts as
     a DOMESTIC EQUITY fund if common equity (ASSET_CAT = 'EC') is at least
     80% of the total reported portfolio value. 80% is declared now and not
     tuned; it is a judgement call and is named as one, not presented as the
     paper's. Sensitivity at 50% and 90% is reported as an EXPLORATORY
     diagnostic outside the DSR grid (C3).
     Residual, NOT fixable: N-PORT cannot distinguish DOMESTIC from
     international equity funds, because ASSET_CAT does not encode issuer
     country. So this arm is "equity funds", not F-L's "domestic equity
     funds". Stated, not assumed away.

 (c) COVERAGE / SCOPE. N-PORT is registered-investment-company-only (~83% of
     the registered-fund industry), excluding hedge funds and separate
     accounts. F-L's own coverage was 97% of CRSP market cap and 69% of CRSP
     names. Ours is the S&P 500 and S&P 600 constituent sets only. This is a
     REAL narrowing of the test, not a technicality: F-L's effect is
     concentrated in growth stocks and their sample reaches well below the
     S&P 600 floor, so the cross-sectional FLOW dispersion here will be
     narrower than Table 1 Panel C's by construction. Check C5(b) measures
     this rather than assuming it.

 (d) THE 60-DAY PUBLICATION WALL. SEC Release 33-10231: N-PORT data is not
     public until 60 days after the third month of the fund's fiscal quarter.
     Every panel value is gated on the real FILING_DATE of every contributing
     filing, reusing cross_sectional_nport_flow.fund_snapshots_as_of, which
     was built and independently verified for that purpose. F-L faced a
     similar but smaller lag with Spectrum and handled it by sorting on "the
     latest available FLOW" (p.306); the same words describe what is done
     here, on a longer lag.

 (e) FISCAL-QUARTER MISALIGNMENT — a limitation with no analogue in the
     paper. N-PORT publishes only the third month of each fund's own FISCAL
     quarter, and fiscal year-ends differ across funds, so the reported
     three-month flow windows do not line up on calendar quarters. F-L had
     calendar-quarter CRSP TNA for every fund. Here each filing is assigned to
     the calendar quarter containing its REPORT_DATE, which keeps roughly one
     observation per fund per calendar quarter but means F^Agg_s aggregates
     flow windows that are staggered by up to two months. This adds noise to
     the aggregate and hence to every counterfactual; it is disclosed, not
     corrected, because no correction is available from the published data.

 (f) FLOW DEFINITION — settled, not re-litigated. Item B.6.a minus Item B.6.c
     (external flow), resolved on 49,705 real fund-quarters and merged as
     a1d64b3. F-L's own Eq.(1) infers flow as
     F^i_t = TNA^i_t - (1+R^i_t) TNA^i_{t-1} - MGN^i_t and states it "assumes
     that inflows and outflows occur at the end of the quarter, and that
     existing investors reinvest dividends and other distributions in the
     fund" (p.302) — the SAME full-reinvestment convention Lou assumes, under
     which B.6.a - B.6.c measures the same estimand directly. Adopted
     unchanged.

 (g) MERGERS — irreducible, and it bites F-L specifically. Eq.(1) carries an
     explicit MGN term (increase in TNA due to mergers) precisely because
     merger inflows are not investor decisions, and Appendix A.1 (p.320) adds
     a second merger rule for the counterfactual: "when constructing the
     counterfactual TNA, we also merge the lagged TNA of the two funds when we
     compute the ratio". N-PORT's Item B.6 instructions fold merger-driven
     share issuance into "shares sold" with no separating field, so NEITHER
     merger adjustment can be reconstructed. Inherited disclosure from
     nport_flow_definition_resolution_2026-09-05 (identity violated on 33.7%
     of fund-quarters, median 13.4bp). It injects noise into flow, biasing
     toward LESS measured signal, not more.

 (h) FUND BIRTH AND DEATH. Appendix A.1 (p.320) needs both: a fund created
     within the last k quarters gets counterfactual TNA of zero, and a fund
     dying in s+1 gets Fhat^i_{s+1} = -dTNA^i_s and zero thereafter. F-L read
     these from CRSP's clean fund-level birth/death dates. Here they are
     inferred from PRESENCE in the N-PORT filing record, which conflates a
     genuine death with a missed or late filing. Disclosed; the direction of
     the resulting error is NOT assumed to be conservative.

 (i) MARKET CAP AND SPLITS. MKTCAP_jt in Eq.(6)-(8) is built point-in-time
     from SEC shares-outstanding facts and split-adjusted prices via the
     existing build_point_in_time_market_cap; share counts are split-adjusted
     with build_split_adjustment / split_factor_on before any differencing.

 (j) FUND RETURNS. R^i_s in Eq.(3)/(12) comes from N-PORT MONTHLY_TOTAL_RETURN,
     which is reported per SHARE CLASS, not per series; the existing
     fund_monthly_returns aggregation is reused unchanged.


5. AN AMBIGUITY IN THE PAPER'S OWN EXPOSITION, RESOLVED IN ADVANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Found by reproducing Appendix Table A1 (p.321) cell by cell rather than
reading it. The main text and the appendix table do not describe the same
recursion, and the difference is real:

  MAIN TEXT (Section 2.1, p.302): "In order to compute the FLOW at date t, we
  start by looking at the total net asset value of the fund at date t-k. Then,
  for every date s we track the evolution of the fund's counterfactual TNA
  using [Eqs. 2 and 3], t-k <= s <= t", and the counterfactual is obtained "by
  repeating the counterfactual exercise every quarter t, and storing the
  resulting dTNA^i_t at the end of each rolling window." That is a ROLLING
  WINDOW: for each t, initialise dTNA^i_{t-k} to the ACTUAL TNA^i_{t-k} and
  recurse forward, with the pro-rata share FIXED at t-k weights (Eq. 11's
  subscripts are explicit).

  TABLE A1 does something else. Every one of its 21 published counterfactual
  cells was re-derived by hand here and they reproduce EXACTLY — but only
  under a CONTINUOUS recursion that never restarts, with the pro-rata share
  re-anchored each period. Worked, for Fund 1: 1982 counterfactual TNA is
  1.05*210 + (160/315)*140 = 291.6, published 292. Under the main-text reading
  with a restart at t-k it would be 1.05*160 + 71.1 = 239.1, which is not the
  published number. (The two readings coincide for the first period, and
  Fund 2 happens to be uninformative because its actual and counterfactual
  1981 TNA are both 105.)

RESOLUTION, fixed before any result: the MAIN-TEXT rolling-window reading is
PRIMARY, because it is what Eqs. (2)/(3)/(11)/(12) say with their own
subscripts and it is the only reading under which the phrase "the end of each
rolling window" means anything. The Table A1 continuous-recursion variant is
computed as a pre-declared ROBUSTNESS ARM, reported alongside but NOT included
in the DSR grid — the same status as the zero-cost arm. Table A1 is
nonetheless used as a UNIT TEST of the shared building blocks (Eq. 11's
pro-rata formula, Eq. 12's recursion, the newborn rule, the death rule, and
the "funds existing in both periods" aggregate), all of which are common to
both readings.

Three anomalies in Table A1 itself, recorded because they were checked rather
than assumed to be transcription errors — all three are present IDENTICALLY in
the 2005 NBER working-paper version (Table A.1, p.1740 of the extracted text),
so they date from 2005 and survived JFE review:
  * the fifth column is labelled 1985 with no 1984 column, although the
    arithmetic is a single one-period step from 1983;
  * aggregate TNA for 1983 is printed as 494 where the fund rows sum to 495;
  * the accompanying prose reads "Between 1982 and 1993 Fund 2 dies" (p.320),
    where 1993 is outside the table entirely and 1983 is meant.
None of the three affects the reproduction; they are reported for honesty, not
used to impugn the construction.


6. THE FROZEN SPEC GRID — 24 SPECS
-----------------------------------
Deliberately the paper's OWN Table 2 structure, so it cannot be characterised
as a search:

  horizon k  in {3-month, 6-month, 1-year, 3-year}   (4)  [Table 2 rows]
  leg        in {long Q1, short Q5, long-short Q1-Q5} (3)  [Table 2 columns]
  universe   in {sp500, sp600}                        (2)

4 x 3 x 2 = 24 specs. n_local = 24.

The 5-year row of Table 2 is EXCLUDED for infeasibility (section 4a), declared
here in advance. Its exclusion is conservative in the sense that matters: it
is the paper's own WEAKEST published horizon (t = -1.35).

Fixed, NOT searched over (all are the paper's own choices, Section 3, p.306):
  - quintile sort on the LAST AVAILABLE FLOW as of month t-1
  - VALUE weights, rebalanced monthly ("Portfolios are rebalanced monthly to
    maintain value weights", Table 2 caption)
  - calendar-time portfolios, monthly returns in excess of Treasury bills
  - counterfactual variant: main-text rolling window (section 5)
  - equity-fund threshold: 80% of portfolio value in ASSET_CAT 'EC'

MARKET HEDGING OF THE SINGLE-SIDED LEGS. The long-Q1 and short-Q5 legs are
reported net of the same-weighted return of the eligible universe, exactly as
the coval_stafford family does and for the same stated reason: a Sharpe on a
raw long-only leg over 2021-2026 would mostly measure the equity risk premium
rather than the dumb-money effect. The paper's own inference is drawn from
regression intercepts (Table 3), which is the same intent. The Q1-Q5 leg needs
no adjustment. This can only REMOVE return, never add it.

IDLE MONTHS. A month in which a leg cannot be formed earns 0.0 for that leg,
never a dropped observation.

COSTS. Per CLAUDE.md, cost realism feeds the DSR itself. The project's
calibrated edge_spread half-spread model plus general-collateral borrow on the
short side is the BASELINE arm. A ZERO-COST arm is reported alongside as a
robustness arm so a negative cannot be dismissed as a cost artifact.

8. BINDING INTERPRETIVE CHECKS, declared before any result exists
------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. THE PAPER'S OWN FACTOR CONTROL (Table 3, p.308). A Fama-French three-
    factor regression is run on every long-short spec, reporting alpha and the
    MKT/SMB/HML loadings. The paper's own result is that characteristic
    adjustment roughly halves the effect (-0.85 -> -0.42 %/mo) while the FF3
    alpha survives at -0.74 %/mo with a large negative HML loading on the
    Q5-Q1 portfolio.
    BINDING READING: if a spec's raw spread is large but its FF3 alpha is
    indistinguishable from zero, the candidate is reported as
    SUBSUMED_BY_KNOWN_FACTORS regardless of its DSR. This project does not
    need a re-labelled HML; a passing DSR whose alpha is entirely value
    exposure is a warning, not a discovery.
    SIGN SUB-CHECK: since the tradeable leg here is Q1-Q5, it is the NEGATIVE
    of the paper's reported portfolio, so its HML loading must be POSITIVE if
    the construction matches the paper. A negative HML loading on Q1-Q5 means
    the sort is not reproducing the paper's growth/value tilt.

C2. POWER FLOOR. Report, per horizon and universe, the number of monthly
    return observations and the number of months each leg was formable. Any
    arm with FEWER THAN 36 MONTHLY RETURNS is reported as POWER-LIMITED and
    its DSR is not treated as informative in either direction. The 3-year arm
    is expected to sit near this line (section 4a) — this is declared NOW so
    that a weak 3-year result cannot later be presented as a clean mechanism
    negative, and so a strong one cannot be presented as a clean pass. This is
    the direct lesson of candidate #14, whose DEFINITE_NEGATIVE was honestly a
    power failure.

C3. NO POST-HOC TUNING. The quintile breakpoints, value weighting, monthly
    rebalance, horizon set, 80% equity-fund threshold and the main-text
    counterfactual reading are frozen above. If they produce degenerate or
    empty panels, that is REPORTED as a finding about N-PORT, not fixed by
    re-tuning. Any re-run with different choices — including the 50%/90%
    equity-threshold sensitivity and the Table A1 counterfactual variant — is
    labelled EXPLORATORY or ROBUSTNESS and excluded from the DSR grid.

C4. DIRECTION. The paper's sign is HIGH FLOW -> LOW future returns, so the
    tradeable spec is LONG Q1 (low flow) / SHORT Q5 (high flow) and the
    paper's own Q5-Q1 column must be NEGATIVE. If Q5-Q1 comes out POSITIVE
    (high-flow stocks outperform), that is a NEGATIVE for this mechanism, not
    a signal to be traded with the sign flipped.

C5. CONSTRUCTION VALIDITY, independent of any return — the analogue of
    ipo_lockup's abnormal-volume check, whose whole value was that it
    validated the event construction even though the trade failed. Two
    pre-declared checks against the paper's own Table 2 Panel A and Table 1
    Panel C:
      (a) MONOTONICITY AND WIDENING. Mean FLOW must increase monotonically
          across quintiles Q1 -> Q5 at every horizon, and the Q5-Q1 spread
          must WIDEN with horizon k (the paper: 1.459 / 2.646 / 4.624 /
          10.135 at 3mo/6mo/1yr/3yr). Monotonicity is near-mechanical given a
          quintile sort; the WIDENING is not, and is the real content of the
          check — it is the signature of Fig. 1's finding that flows cumulate.
      (b) SCALE. Report FLOW's pooled mean and standard deviation against the
          paper's 0.54 and 5.61 (Table 1 Panel C), and top-quintile 3-year
          FLOW against the paper's ~+6% of market cap.
    If (a) fails, the family is reported as CONSTRUCTION_NOT_VALIDATED and NO
    return result from it is treated as informative in either direction. A
    scale mismatch in (b) is expected to some degree (section 4c) and is
    reported, not treated as disqualifying on its own.

C6. TABLE A1 UNIT TEST. The counterfactual building blocks must reproduce all
    21 published counterfactual cells of Table A1 to the paper's printed
    rounding, including the newborn rule (Fund 3, 1981) and the death rule
    (Fund 2, 1983: Fhat = -141 from the COUNTERFACTUAL, not the actual, 1982
    TNA). This is a genuine worked example with known answers, so agreement is
    evidence rather than tautology. If it fails, nothing else is run.
