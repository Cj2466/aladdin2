================================================================================
0. WHY THIS RE-AUDIT EXISTS, AND WHAT WAS ALREADY KNOWN WHEN IT WAS DESIGNED
================================================================================
Commit df8a933 (2026-08-30) confirmed that the EDGE estimator
(spread_estimator.py, Ardia/Guidotti/Kroencke JFE 2024) overstates true
half-spreads for liquid mega/large-cap names by roughly 10-40x — the source
paper's own disclosed limitation for that regime, not an implementation bug.
The two hard-exclusions in multi_signal_combination.py that cite the
edge_spread re-audit of 2026-08-28 (commit 214a58c) therefore rest on a
PESSIMISTIC BOUND, not a realistic cost estimate. This re-audit asks, with a
pre-registered realistic cost calibration: do those two exclusions actually
hold?

DISCLOSED PRIOR KNOWLEDGE (unavoidable — it is in the commit history and the
task brief, and pretending otherwise would be false): under the flat 5bp
control, round_c had 14/30 specs clearing psr_vs_zero >= 0.50 and
phase_a_intraday_expanded had 8/212 with positive pooled raw Sharpe; under
edge_spread, round_c kept 4/30 above the PSR bar (best DSR 0.03) and phase_a
had 0/212 positive. NO per-spec result under any scenario declared below was
computed before this document was frozen. The scenario cost levels below are
calibrated ONLY from the external published sources cited in section 2 — none
was chosen by trying values against round_c/phase_a data.

TWO HONEST MONOTONICITY NOTES, stated before running so they cannot be spun
afterwards:
 * For round_c, every flat scenario below sits below the 5bp control, and net
   Sharpe is monotone non-increasing in a flat cost rate, so the clearing
   count under the flat scenarios is expected to be >= 14/30. That is not a
   finding manufactured by this design — it is the direct consequence of the
   published fact that 5bp/side is roughly 2-4x the real cost of trading the
   S&P 500 cross-section (section 2). The genuinely new information from this
   re-audit is (a) the margin and the DSRs at realistic levels, and (b) the
   edge_ranked scenario, where illiquid names are charged MORE than the flat
   rate — which is not monotone relative to the control.
 * For phase_a, the outcome is genuinely two-sided by construction: its 30
   mega/large-cap tickers get CHEAPER than 5bp under every scenario, while
   its 25 mid/small-cap tickers get MORE EXPENSIVE under the mid and high
   scenarios. Nothing about the direction of the pooled result is implied by
   the design.

2. COST CALIBRATION — SOURCES FIRST, LEVELS SECOND
================================================================================
All costs below are ONE-WAY HALF-SPREADS in bps of traded notional — the same
unit as CrossSectionalConfig.cost_bps and INTRADAY_COST_BPS, and the same
"crossing from mid to bid or ask costs half the quoted/effective spread"
convention spread_estimator.build_edge_half_spread_frame documents. Published
spread statistics are FULL spreads unless stated; one-way cost = full / 2.
Commissions and market impact are modeled by NO cost model in this codebase
(disclosed in cross_sectional.py); that convention is kept, not revisited
here, so the comparison to the 5bp control stays like-for-like.

SOURCES (retrieved and read 2026-08-30, primary text in hand, not recalled
from memory):

 (S1) Bjorn Hagstromer, "Bias in the effective bid-ask spread" (Journal of
      Financial Economics, 2021; working-paper PDF retrieved from
      hec.edu/sites/default/files/documents/overestEspr-v12.pdf and
      text-extracted locally). Table 1, all S&P 500 constituents, Dec 7-11
      2015, per-stock dollar-volume-weighted FULL spreads in bps:
        micro-price effective: mean 2.73, p5 1.21, p25 1.85, median 2.53,
                               p75 3.78, p95 6.99
        midpoint effective:    mean 3.22, median 3.02, p95 7.78
        quoted:                mean 3.55, median 3.23, p95 9.13
      Figure 2: for the $30-$120 share-price groups (74% of S&P 500 dollar
      volume), effective spreads average ~2.1 bps.
      => S&P 500 one-way half-spreads: median stock ~1.3bp, p75 ~1.9bp,
         p95 ~3.5bp, in a low-volatility year (2015).

 (S2) Phil Mackintosh (Nasdaq Chief Economist), "Sampling the S&P 500 to
      Minimize Spreads", nasdaq.com, 2024-05-30 (HTML retrieved and read
      2026-08-30 — this properly sources the previously weakly-cited
      "~4.5bp" figure). Cap-weighted average QUOTED spreads: MSFT/AAPL ~1bp;
      10-largest-stock portfolio ~2.5bp; 100-largest ~3.7bp; the whole
      S&P 500 "just over 4.5 bps". All FULL spreads => one-way ~0.5bp
      (mega-caps) to ~2.3bp (whole index, cap-weighted).

 (S3) Phil Mackintosh, "Have Spreads Changed Over Time?", nasdaq.com,
      2021-10-14 (HTML retrieved and read 2026-08-30). Large-cap spreads
      widen with VIX and sit in year-regimes (2020 much wider than 2015);
      small-cap average FULL spreads went from ~36bp to over 50bp across
      2014-2021 (~50% increase volatility-adjusted). "Small cap" there is
      broader/thinner than phase_a's screened mid/small tier, so 36-50bp is
      read as an UPPER anchor for that tier, not its expected level.

 (S4) Tick-floor arithmetic (exact, no source needed beyond the $0.01
      minimum tick): a stock's quoted spread cannot be below one tick, so
      one-way half-spread >= 0.005/price, i.e. 50/price_in_dollars bps.
      For a $100 stock that is 0.5bp; for a $12.50 stock, 4bp. This floor is
      applied per ticker in phase_a's scenarios (section 3).

 (S5) spread_estimator.py's KNOWN LIMITATION block (df8a933): EDGE levels
      are unusable for liquid large-caps but the estimator "remains far more
      trustworthy for ranking tickers by relative cost". That is the sole
      role EDGE plays in this re-audit (the round_c edge_ranked scenario:
      EDGE supplies relative structure, the LEVEL comes from S1/S2).

CALIBRATION FOR ROUND C's UNIVERSE (every S&P 500 member on any day of
2015-2026; the book equal-weights decile/quintile legs, so the EQUAL-weighted
cross-sectional average half-spread is the relevant level, wider than S2's
cap-weighted 2.3bp one-way):
  From S1's stock-level distribution, an equal-weighted mean FULL effective
  spread sits between the median (2.53) and p75 (3.78), call it ~3bp full =
  ~1.5bp one-way, in 2015's tight regime. S3 says post-2018/2020 regimes run
  wider (order +50% volatility-adjusted); the 2015-2026 window also includes
  departed members that skew less liquid than the surviving snapshot.
  Best estimate, one-way, equal-weighted, across the whole window: ~2bp.

CALIBRATION FOR PHASE A's TWO TIERS (its universe is NOT uniformly mega-cap;
tier membership is the module's own pre-existing split, no discretion here):
  * PATTERN_MINING_UNIVERSE_LARGE_CAP (30 mega/large caps, CRM ~$170B to
    NVDA ~$5.1T): S2 puts their FULL quoted spreads at ~1bp (AAPL/MSFT) to
    ~3.7bp (100-largest average). Best estimate one-way: ~1.5bp.
  * PATTERN_MINING_UNIVERSE_MID_SMALL_CAP (25 names, $300M-$10B, screened
    for >= $5M/day dollar volume): between S2's large-cap ~4bp full and
    S3's thin-small-cap 36-50bp full; the liquidity screen puts these names
    well above the small-cap median in tradability. Best estimate one-way:
    ~10bp (= 20bp full), bracketed a factor of 2 in both directions.

3. THE PRE-DECLARED SCENARIOS — FROZEN HERE, NOT REVISABLE AFTER RESULTS
================================================================================
ROUND C (cost_model / cost_bps are the ONLY things that vary; everything else
is CrossSectionalConfig defaults with formation_start = 2015-01-07):

  RC-control : flat_bps, cost_bps = 5.0   (the project's existing control,
               re-run on this fetch for drift comparability; not a scenario)
  RC-low     : flat_bps, cost_bps = 1.0   (2015-regime tight: S1 median stock
               ~1.3bp one-way, S2 cap-weighted ~2.3bp; optimistic bound)
  RC-mid     : flat_bps, cost_bps = 2.0   (best estimate, section 2)
  RC-high    : flat_bps, cost_bps = 3.5   (= 7bp full equal-weighted average
               — above S1's 2015 p95 stock, covering vol-spike regimes and
               the departed-member illiquidity skew; conservative bound)
  RC-edge-ranked : cost_model = "edge_spread" with the half_spread frame
               REPLACED by scale * EDGE_frame, where
                 scale = 2.0bp / (pooled median over all non-NaN cells of the
                         EDGE half-spread frame on dates >= formation_start)
               i.e. EDGE is used ONLY as a relative ranker (S5) and the LEVEL
               is pinned so the median charged cell equals RC-mid's 2.0bp.
               config.cost_bps = 2.0 so the counted NaN fallback charges the
               same calibrated flat rate, not 5bp. No clipping/winsorizing of
               the scaled frame — one scalar, fully mechanical.
               This scenario answers: does charging the book's actual
               (relatively) illiquid names proportionally more overturn the
               flat-rate answer at a realistic level?

PHASE A (per-ticker one-way cost via cost_bps_by_ticker; tier rates below,
each ticker floored at its own tick floor (S4) using the median close over
the same daily-OHLC fetch window the 2026-08-28 re-audit used (850 calendar
days; auto-adjusted closes — an approximation of raw price disclosed here)):

                      large-cap tier   mid/small tier
  PA-control :  flat 5.0bp everywhere (existing control, re-run; not a scenario)
  PA-low     :        0.75bp              5.0bp
  PA-mid     :        1.5bp              10.0bp
  PA-high    :        3.0bp              20.0bp

  Tier membership is PATTERN_MINING_UNIVERSE_LARGE_CAP vs
  PATTERN_MINING_UNIVERSE_MID_SMALL_CAP, verbatim from intraday_patterns.py.
  Rate sourcing: large tier low/mid/high = S2's mega-cap ~1bp full (0.5bp
  one-way, rounded up), S2's 100-largest ~3.7bp full (~1.85bp one-way,
  rounded down to 1.5), and 2x the mid (6bp full — wider than any published
  large-cap average; conservative). Mid/small tier mid = 10bp one-way (20bp
  full, section 2), low/high = factor-2 bracket each way, with the high end
  (40bp full) at S3's thin-small-cap average — conservative for names
  screened at >= $5M/day dollar volume.

NO OTHER SCENARIO will be run against these families in this re-audit. If a

5. PASS/FAIL CRITERION AND VERDICT RULE, FIXED IN ADVANCE
================================================================================
PER-SPEC BAR: psr_vs_zero >= multi_signal_combination.PSR_SELECTION_THRESHOLD
(= 0.50), i.e. the project's own pre-declared combination-eligibility bar —
reused, not reinvented. Stated plainly so nobody dresses it up afterwards:
PSR-vs-zero >= 0.50 is algebraically "in-sample net Sharpe > 0" (that
module's section 2 says the same). Clearing it makes a spec ELIGIBLE for the
combination experiment; it does NOT make it a validated edge —
PREDECLARATION.txt's DSR >= 0.90 significance standard is untouched by this
document, and DSRs are reported for context on every spec.

FAMILY VERDICT RULE:
  * "EXCLUSION NOT SUPPORTED AS RECORDED" if at least one spec clears the
    PSR bar under the MID scenario (the combination pipeline takes exactly
    one best spec per family, so one clearing spec is what makes the family
    load-bearing). Low/high (and edge_ranked for round_c) are reported as
    sensitivity brackets around that headline.
  * "EXCLUSION SUPPORTED" if zero specs clear under EVERY scenario declared
    in section 3 (control excluded — it is the comparability anchor, not a
    scenario).
  * Anything else: reported exactly as it comes out, mid scenario carrying
    the headline, no verdict massaging.
This rule decides only whether the RECORDED exclusion evidence ("does not
survive a realistic cost model") stands. Whether to actually amend
HARD_EXCLUSIONS and re-run the combination thesis is a downstream decision
explicitly OUT OF SCOPE for this re-audit, and multi_signal_combination.py
is not modified by it.

================================================================================
6. KNOWN LIMITATIONS ACCEPTED IN ADVANCE
================================================================================
 * No market impact and no commissions in ANY scenario — same as every cost
   model in this codebase, disclosed, and identical across control and
   scenarios so the comparison is internally honest.
 * Phase A per-ticker costs are time-constant (the existing wiring's own
   documented limitation); round_c's flat scenarios are also time-constant
   by definition. Only RC-edge-ranked varies cost through time, and its
   time-variation inherits EDGE's (noisy) dynamics.
 * The calibration sources are US-equity averages measured 2015-2024, not
   per-name quote data for this exact universe over this exact window; the
   low/high brackets exist precisely because the point estimate is
   uncertain. A real intraday-quote-based per-name cost feed remains the
   properly-closed version of this question and remains on the
   pending-paid-decisions list.
 * yfinance hourly bars cover only ~2 trailing years and the window slides
   daily, so phase_a's absolute numbers are not byte-comparable to the
   archived 2026-08-28 run — the fresh same-fetch control absorbs that,
   exactly as it did on 2026-08-28.
 * Delisted-member price coverage for round_c (~48% of departed members
   unresolvable on yfinance) is unchanged from every prior round and
   FLATTERS the strategy; it is identical across control and scenarios, so
   it does not affect the cost-model comparison, but it caps how much any
   positive here can be trusted in absolute terms.
