--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HYPOTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H: Ranking stocks on their trailing RESIDUAL (factor-orthogonalized) returns
   predicts the cross-section of subsequent returns BETTER, on a risk-adjusted
   basis, than ranking on trailing TOTAL returns — on the point-in-time
   large-cap S&P 500 universe over 2015-2026.

Mechanism claimed by the source literature: momentum's defensible behavioral
core is underreaction to FIRM-SPECIFIC news. Ranking on raw trailing returns
mixes that with a bet on whatever factor loadings the stock happened to carry
during the formation window — a factor-timing bet that is also the documented
source of momentum's crash risk. Orthogonalizing the ranking variable against a
factor model is supposed to isolate the firm-specific part.

Direction: high residual momentum = long, low = short. Standard momentum sign.

This family does NOT claim to adjudicate why momentum exists. A negative here is
not evidence against underreaction in general — only against this particular
sort being tradeable on this universe now.

2. CITATIONS — VERIFICATION STATUS, INCLUDING ONE BRIEF PREMISE THAT FAILED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every citation below was checked against a live-fetched primary or publisher
record during this build (2026-09-02). This project has a documented incident of
a fabricated academic quote, so the rule is absolute: verified-and-cited, or
explicitly labeled unverified. Nothing in between.

VERIFIED — Blitz, David, Joop Huij & Martin Martens, "Residual momentum",
Journal of Empirical Finance 18(3), June 2011, pp. 506-521,
DOI 10.1016/j.jempfin.2011.01.003. Confirmed against TWO independent records
returning identical fields: the Crossref REST API (queried directly) and the
RePEc/EconPapers record. Verbatim from the abstract, fetched from EconPapers:

    "Conventional momentum strategies exhibit substantial time-varying
     exposures to the Fama and French factors. We show that these exposures can
     be reduced by ranking stocks on residual stock returns instead of total
     returns. As a consequence, residual momentum earns risk-adjusted profits
     that are about twice as large as those associated with total return
     momentum; is more consistent over time; and less concentrated in the
     extremes of the cross-section of stocks."

The SSRN preprint of the same paper carries DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2319861. The
published full text is paywalled — SSRN, ScienceDirect and ResearchGate all
returned HTTP 403 to this build. NO NUMBER FROM THE PAPER'S BODY IS QUOTED
ANYWHERE IN THIS FAMILY. The only magnitude attributed to BHM is the abstract's
own qualitative "about twice as large", which is an exact quote.

THE BUILD BRIEF FOR THIS FAMILY GOT THE FACTOR MODEL WRONG, and it is recorded
here rather than quietly fixed. The brief said BHM use "a Carhart-style
multi-factor regression". THEY DO NOT. The model is the Fama-French THREE-factor
model — market, SMB, HML — with NO momentum (UMD) factor. Three independent
checks agree:

  (i)   BHM's own abstract says "the Fama and French factors", not Carhart's.
  (ii)  Hanauer & Windmüller's replication (below) writes the regression out in
        full as a three-factor model and cites BHM for it.
  (iii) Prunier's independent replication (below) states BHM "use the Fama and
        French three factors model to do their regressions on a 36-month
        rolling window".

  This is not a pedantic correction: including UMD would regress the momentum
  factor out of a momentum signal, which is close to circular and would be a
  different hypothesis entirely. The family is built on FF3.

  The brief was RIGHT about the part it flagged as mattering: the exposures are
  TIME-VARYING, estimated by ROLLING regression, not a single static beta. That
  is verified verbatim below.

VERIFIED, AND THE SOURCE OF THIS FAMILY'S EXACT CONSTRUCTION — Hanauer,
Matthias X. & Steffen Windmüller, "Enhanced Momentum Strategies", working paper,
this draft dated August 14, 2019, hosted at wp.lancs.ac.uk (Lancaster
University's Momentum and Herding in Finance conference). The PDF was fetched
and its text extracted locally. It states it follows Gutierrez and Prinsky
(2007), Blitz et al. (2011) and Blitz et al. (2018), and writes the construction
out as two numbered equations. VERBATIM, from the extracted text:

    "Instead of using the individual stocks' raw returns from t − 12 to t − 2,
     we orthogonalize them with respect to a Fama-French three-factor model.
     [...] we [...] regress the past 36 months' returns of all valid stocks
     within the investment universe on country-specific factors of the
     Fama-French three-factor model. Thus, the following time-series model is
     estimated for every stock i and month t using a rolling-window approach:"

    (8)   R_i,t − R_f,t = α_i + β_RMRF,i * RMRF_t
                                + β_SMB,i * SMB_t
                                + β_HML,i * HML_t + ε_i,t

    "The residuals of the time-series regressions, ε̂_i,t, can be interpreted as
     stock-specific idiosyncratic returns for stock i during month t. As in
     Gutierrez and Prinsky (2007), Blitz et al. (2011), and Blitz et al. (2018),
     we calculate the cumulative idiosyncratic return for each stock by scaling
     the 12-2 month idiosyncratic returns with their volatility:"

    (9)   ε̂12−1_i,t  =   SUM_{j=2..12} ε̂_i,t−j
                         ------------------------------------
                         sqrt( SUM_{j=2..12} (ε̂_i,t−j − ε̄_i)^2 )

Their footnote 6, verbatim, establishes that the naming in this literature is
interchangeable: "Gutierrez and Prinsky (2007) and Blitz, Huij, and Martens
(2011) use the terms abnormal return momentum and residual momentum,
respectively, but the definitions are identical."

VERIFIED AND LOAD-BEARING FOR THIS FAMILY'S GRID — Hanauer & Windmüller
footnote 7, verbatim:

    "Chaves (2016) in this regard shows that also a simplified version of
     idiosyncratic momentum that is based on one-factor (market) unscaled
     residuals works. Blitz et al. (2018) confirm that most of the performance
     improvement comes from orthogonalizing returns with the market factor and
     that the inclusion of additional Fama-French factors leads to small further
     improvements as more of the stock specific momentum is isolated."

This is why the grid below carries a market-only (CAPM) residual arm alongside
the FF3 arm. That arm is not a convenience fallback invented because factor data
was awkward to get — it is a specific, published, testable claim, and the grid
is built so this run measures it.

VERIFIED — independent replication corroborating every construction detail:
Prunier, Laurent, "Robustness tests of residual momentum strategies", master's
thesis, Université de Liège, open-access at matheo.uliege.be. PDF fetched and
text extracted locally. Verbatim:

    "Mainly, they use the Fama and French three factors model to do their
     regressions on a 36-month rolling window. Even though both papers note the
     alpha in the regressions have a role of general control for misspecification
     in the model of expected stock return, only Blitz et al. (2011) do not
     include it in their calculation of the residual. Next, they rank the stocks
     on the sum of their twelve last months' residual, adjusted by its standard
     deviation over the same period and excluding the most recent one. The
     zero-cost portfolio is long the top decile and shorts the bottom one, using
     an equally weighted scheme in each decile."

Two details this build would otherwise have gotten wrong, both adopted:
  * THE ALPHA IS NOT ADDED BACK. The residual is the plain OLS residual ε̂,
    with the intercept absorbed into the fit and excluded from the score. So the
    score measures deviation from the stock's OWN 36-month average
    factor-adjusted return, not from zero.
  * DECILE, EQUAL-WEIGHTED legs are BHM's own choice.

Prunier also reports, from his own sensitivity work, that a 36-month window
"looks like the sweet spot", that 24 months is close behind, and that at a
12-month window "there is no strategy with a significant alpha". This is used
below only to justify NOT searching over the regression window; no number of his
is relied on.

UNVERIFIED, AND THEREFORE NOT RELIED ON ANYWHERE: Chaves, D. B. (2016),
"Idiosyncratic momentum: U.S. and international evidence", Journal of Investing
25, 64-76; Gutierrez & Prinsky (2007); Blitz, Hanauer & Vidojevic (2018). These
three are known to this build ONLY through Hanauer & Windmüller's citations of
them (quoted above). Their own texts were not fetched. Nothing in this family's
construction or interpretation depends on them beyond the quoted footnote.

FACTOR DATA — Kenneth R. French's Data Library, "Fama/French 3 Factors"
(monthly), file F-F_Research_Data_Factors_CSV.zip, downloaded directly from
mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu (HTTP 200). The file's own header states it "was created
using the 202606 CRSP database". Coverage 192607-202606. Columns Mkt-RF, SMB,
HML, RF, in PERCENT. This is the actual factor series BHM regress against, from
the source of record, not a proxy.

3. THE HONEST PRIOR, STATED BEFORE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THIS PROJECT HAS ALREADY TESTED TWO CLOSE BEHAVIORAL RELATIVES OF THIS SIGNAL ON
THIS EXACT UNIVERSE AND BOTH CAME BACK CLEAN NEGATIVES: 52-week-high anchoring
and capital-gains overhang (the "Round C patterns" family). Both are, like
residual momentum, price-path anchoring/underreaction stories sorted on a
large-cap point-in-time cross-section. That is real, directly relevant, in-house
prior evidence and it points down.

Three further reasons for LOW expectations, all stated before any number exists:

 * CROWDING AND DECAY. Residual momentum is 15 years published, is a named
   commercial product line at the asset manager two of its three authors worked
   for, and its inputs are one regression on freely-available factor data. This
   sample (2015-2026) is entirely post-publication.

 * THE LARGE-CAP SEGMENT IS THE WORST PLACE TO LOOK. Momentum in general is
   weakest and most arbitraged among the 500 most liquid names in the world.
   BHM's cross-section is the full CRSP universe with hundreds of names per
   decile; this family will have decile legs in the low tens.

 * MOMENTUM'S OWN 2015-2026 RECORD IS POOR, and this window contains at least
   two documented momentum crashes (the 2020 and 2022 reversals). A residual
   construction is claimed to soften exactly that, which is the one respect in
   which this family might differ from its raw control — but softening a crash
   is not the same as producing a positive Sharpe.

PRIOR: a genuine tradeable edge here is UNLIKELY. The interesting outcomes are
(i) an honest negative that looks like the 52-week-high/overhang negatives, in
which case residual momentum is "the same negative in different clothes"; or
(ii) an honest negative in which residual momentum nonetheless BEATS its own raw
momentum control, which would be a weak-but-real corroboration of BHM's
comparative claim without being a tradeable edge. The grid is built so (ii) is
measurable rather than assertable. THERE IS NO RESULT THIS PRE-REGISTRATION IS
HOPING FOR.

4. DATA AND CONSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

UNIVERSE: the IDENTICAL seeded 200-ticker sample as the quality / asset-growth
families (build_quality_sample, seed 20260828) drawn from the point-in-time
S&P 500 union universe, gated per formation by was_member. Deliberately
identical so this family's result is about the same cross-section whose
composition has already been measured in detail. HONEST COST: decile legs in the
low tens against BHM's hundreds, and correspondingly noisy leg returns.

PRICES: yfinance daily adjusted closes (total-return basis, auto_adjust=True),
the same provider every S&P 500 equity family here uses. Monthly returns are
built by resampling to calendar month ends.

FACTORS: Ken French monthly FF3 + RF (section 2). Percent values are divided by
100. The file is CACHED INTO THE REPO at backend/data/fama_french_factors_
monthly.csv so the run is reproducible offline and the exact vintage used is in
git. Tests use synthetic fixtures; the real run uses the real file.

THE REGRESSION, per stock i and per formation month M:

    r_excess[i,m] = R[i,m] - RF[m]

    r_excess[i,m] = a_i + b_i*MktRF[m] + s_i*SMB[m] + h_i*HML[m] + e[i,m]
                                        (the FF3 arm)
    r_excess[i,m] = a_i + b_i*MktRF[m] + e[i,m]
                                        (the CAPM arm)

  estimated by OLS over the W months ending at month M-2, W = 36.

    score[i,M] = SUM_{m=M-12..M-2} e[i,m]  /  stdev_{m=M-12..M-2}( e[i,m] )

  which is exactly Hanauer & Windmüller Eq. 9 up to a constant: their
  denominator is sqrt(SUM of squared deviations), the sample standard deviation
  is that divided by sqrt(n-1), and n is FIXED AT 11 for every stock because
  this family refuses any stock missing any of the 11 residual months. A
  constant common to every name in the cross-section cannot change a ranking,
  so the two are identical sorts. The refusal is what makes that true and is
  therefore a construction requirement, not a data-quality nicety.

  ALPHA IS NOT ADDED BACK (section 2, Prunier). e is the plain OLS residual.

  NOTE ON NON-DEGENERACY, checked before the run and unit-tested: OLS residuals
  sum to exactly zero over the ESTIMATION window. The 11-month scoring window is
  a strict subset of the 36-month estimation window, so its partial sum is NOT
  identically zero and the score has genuine cross-sectional variation. If the
  two windows were ever made equal the signal would collapse to numerical noise
  for every stock. A test pins this.

THE RAW CONTROL ARM: conventional 12-2 total-return momentum — the cumulative
EXCESS return over the same 11 months, NOT residualized and NOT standardized.
This is BHM's own comparison baseline ("total return momentum"). DISCLOSED
LIMITATION, stated now so the results document cannot pretend otherwise: the
control differs from the residual arms in TWO respects at once (no
orthogonalization AND no volatility standardization), because that is what the
literature's baseline is. It is therefore a test of BHM's headline comparison,
NOT a clean one-factor-at-a-time ablation of orthogonalization alone.

POINT-IN-TIME DISCIPLINE — THE ONE PLACE THIS FAMILY COULD CHEAT, AND HOW IT IS
STOPPED. Ken French's factors are published with a real lag: the file used here
carries data through 2026-06-30 and its own archive timestamp is 2026-08-03, a
MEASURED lag of 34 days. A backtest that formed a portfolio at month-end M using
factor data for month M-1 would be using numbers not yet published. Therefore:

  * every monthly score carries an explicit availability date of
    (its last data month's end + FF3_PUBLICATION_LAG_DAYS), with
    FF3_PUBLICATION_LAG_DAYS = 45, conservative against the measured 34;
  * the score panel is built with the existing build_point_in_time_factor_frame
    helper, which forward-fills from availability dates ONLY, never backfills,
    never interpolates, and refuses a value carried past a staleness bound
    (set to 75 days here — tight, because this signal refreshes monthly, unlike
    the 455-day bound the annual-filing families use);
  * the frame rides CrossSectionalData.fundamental_signal, which the harness
    slices to rows <= the formation date, so look-ahead is structurally
    impossible at formation regardless of what this family computes;
  * an assertion in the builder, not merely a comment, refuses to emit any
    observation whose availability date precedes its own data end.

  CONSEQUENCE, DISCLOSED: the 45-day lag means the score in force at a formation
  date generally stops at month M-2, so the realized gap between the last
  scored month and the formation date is ~2 to ~2.5 months rather than BHM's
  ~2. This family is therefore slightly MORE conservative than the source paper,
  never less. The realized median gap is MEASURED and reported per run rather
  than assumed. THE SAME LAG IS APPLIED TO ALL THREE ARMS INCLUDING THE RAW
  CONTROL — the control gets no timing advantage, so the arms differ only in
  the signal, never in when it is allowed to be known.

  The CAPM arm uses French's Mkt-RF, NOT an SPY proxy, for the same reason: so
  the CAPM-vs-FF3 comparison differs in the factor SET alone and not also in the
  data source or the publication lag.

COSTS: 5 bps one-way (DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS), identical to every S&P 500 equity
family here so Sharpes stay comparable. financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 — this
project's standing DISCLOSED optimism about short borrow, not an estimate. Note
this bites harder here than in the annual-refresh families: a monthly-refreshing
signal at a 21-day hold pays turnover 12 times a year.

5. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 18 DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    signal        {total_return_control, capm_residual, ff3_residual}   3
  x conditioning  {raw, industry_neutral}                               2
  x holding days  {21, 63, 126} trading days                            3
  = 18 definitions, portfolio = long_short, DECILE (0.1) throughout.

n_trials = 18 for the deflated Sharpe. This family's OWN denominator, with no
carried trials from another family: it is a fresh hypothesis from an independent
literature, and all three axes were fixed together before any number existed.

WHY 18 AND NOT THE SIBLING FAMILIES' 12. Every axis is load-bearing and none is
decorative:

 * THE SIGNAL AXIS IS THE HYPOTHESIS. BHM's claim is COMPARATIVE — residual
   momentum earns risk-adjusted profits "about twice as large as those
   associated with total return momentum". That claim is untestable without the
   raw control computed on the identical universe, identical costs and identical
   publication lag. The control is not a bonus spec; it is the denominator of
   the actual hypothesis. And splitting the residual arm into CAPM and FF3 is
   the direct test of the verified Blitz-et-al-2018 footnote in section 2.

 * THE CONDITIONING AXIS IS THIS SIGNAL'S SPECIFIC CONFOUND. Residualizing
   against FF3 removes market, size and value exposure. It does NOT remove
   INDUSTRY. Energy names can share a large common residual in an oil rally, so
   a residual-momentum sort can still be an industry bet — the exact failure
   mode that killed the sibling NOA family's apparent positive. That family had
   to spawn its neutral test AFTER seeing a confounded result; asset growth
   fixed that by pre-declaring both conditionings in one grid. This family does
   the same. THE LOAD-BEARING CONSEQUENCE: a raw positive CANNOT be reported as
   this family's finding without its industry-neutral counterpart, computed in
   the same run under the same denominator, agreeing.

 * THE HOLDING AXIS. 21 days is BHM's own monthly rebalance; 63 and 126 test
   decay. Unlike the asset-growth family, a 21-day hold is INCLUDED here and
   that is deliberate: that family excluded it because its ranking variable
   refreshed once a year, whereas this one refreshes MONTHLY, so a monthly hold
   is re-ranking on genuinely new information rather than re-paying turnover on
   an unchanged sort.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT SEARCHED OVER, so it cannot be selected post-hoc:

 * THE REGRESSION WINDOW IS FIXED AT 36 MONTHS. It is BHM's own, verified twice
   in section 2. Prunier's sensitivity work independently found 36 the best of
   {12, 24, 36, 48} — which is precisely why searching over it here would be
   selecting on a dimension the literature has already reported on. One value,
   pre-committed, no search.
 * THE RANK FRACTION IS FIXED AT DECILES (0.1). BHM's own. The sibling families
   carry a quintile robustness variant; this family spends that grid budget on
   the signal axis instead, because the comparative claim is the hypothesis and
   a quintile variant is not.
 * SCORE STANDARDIZATION IS FIXED ON for both residual arms (Eq. 9). An
   unstandardized residual variant is NOT in the grid.
 * long_universe_hedged IS ABSENT, for the asset-growth family's reason:
   hedging against the sector-imbalanced eligible universe would reintroduce
   through the hedge leg the exposure the neutral conditioning removes from the
   ranking, making the two halves of the grid non-comparable.

INDUSTRY-NEUTRAL CENTERING IS THE BUCKET MEDIAN, NOT THE MEAN, fixed in advance:
the residual-momentum score is a ratio whose denominator can be small for a
quiet stock, so the tails are heavy and a bucket mean is dragged by a single
outlier. MEAN CENTERING IS NOT IN THIS GRID. Industry buckets are the sibling
family's frozen 8-bucket SIC partition (sic_to_bucket) on its POINT-IN-TIME SIC
panel built from archived filing SGML headers, and MIN_BUCKET_SIZE = 3 is
inherited: a name whose bucket has fewer than 3 ranked members at a formation is
refused rather than centered.

6. PASS / FAIL THRESHOLD — FIXED NOW, BEFORE ANY RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This family is reported as a VALIDATED EDGE only if BOTH hold:

  (i)  the best RESIDUAL spec's deflated Sharpe (DSR, computed at
       n_trials = 18) exceeds 0.95; AND
  (ii) that spec's INDUSTRY-NEUTRAL counterpart, at the same signal and the
       same holding period, also shows a materially positive Sharpe rather
       than collapsing toward zero.

The 0.95 bar is the same one the asset-growth and quality families used, kept
identical so this family's verdict is comparable to its siblings rather than
graded on its own curve.

THE RAW CONTROL ARM CAN NEVER BE THIS FAMILY'S "FINDING". It is a benchmark. If
total-return momentum were the best spec in the grid, that is reported as what
it is — this family failing to find that residualization adds anything — and not
as a discovered edge.

SEPARATELY, AND REGARDLESS OF (i) AND (ii), the BHM comparative claim is
evaluated and reported: for each of the 6 matched (conditioning, holding) cells,
the residual arms' Sharpe against the control arm's Sharpe. Pre-committed
reading of that comparison:
  * residual arms beating the control in most cells is WEAK corroboration of
    BHM's direction. It is explicitly NOT a claim of tradeability and must not
    be written up as one if (i) fails.
  * "about twice as large" is NOT treated as a quantitative target to be hit.
    With 18 correlated specs on decile legs in the low tens, this sample cannot
    resolve a 2x risk-adjusted ratio, and the results document will say so.

Anything else is an honest negative, or an honest artifact, and is written up as
such. A well-documented honest negative is a FULLY SUCCESSFUL OUTCOME by this
project's standards, and roughly 25 of this codebase's ~28 tested families have
been exactly that.

EVERY ONE of the 18 specs is persisted to the cross_sectional_trial_results
table under family_key "residual_momentum" regardless of outcome — winners,
losers and nulls alike. No spec is dropped, re-run, or re-specified after seeing
its number. If a bug is found after the run, the fix is re-running the WHOLE
grid and replacing the whole run tag, never patching individual rows.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT A NEGATIVE WILL AND WILL NOT LICENSE US TO SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Written now so the results document cannot quietly overclaim later.

A negative here will NOT refute Blitz, Huij & Martens. Their sort is a broad
CRSP-universe sort with hundreds of names per decile over a multi-decade,
largely pre-publication sample. This is ~150-200 large-cap names over ~11.6
years with decile legs in the low tens, entirely post-publication, on the most
arbitraged segment of the market, with a deliberately conservative extra
publication lag. Anomaly decay, absence in the large-cap segment specifically,
and simple lack of statistical power would ALL be consistent with a null here,
and this dataset cannot distinguish between them. Any results document claiming
otherwise is wrong.

Nor will a negative say anything about residual momentum as a RISK-MANAGEMENT
overlay on an existing momentum book, which is what much of the citing
literature (Hanauer & Windmüller) actually studies. This family tests a
standalone long-short sort, not a drawdown overlay.

What a negative WILL establish is the only question that matters for this
project: not tradeable by us, on our universe, now — and that residual momentum
should not be re-tested here without genuinely new data (a materially wider
cross-section, or a longer pre-publication history) or a genuinely different
hypothesis, carrying these 18 trials into the denominator of anything that does.

FINALLY, THE COMPARISON THIS FAMILY OWES ITS SIBLINGS. This project has already
returned clean honest negatives on 52-week-high anchoring and capital-gains
overhang on this same universe. The results document MUST state explicitly
whether residual momentum reads as materially different from those or as the
same negative in different clothes, and must not dodge that question.
