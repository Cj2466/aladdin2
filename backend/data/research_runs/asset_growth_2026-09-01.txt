WHAT THE STRUCTURE OF THE GRID SAYS
-----------------------------------
1. THE SIGN IS RIGHT BUT THE SIZE IS NOT. 11 of 12 specs are positive, i.e.
   low-asset-growth firms did modestly outperform high-asset-growth firms over
   2015-2026 on this universe, in the direction Cooper/Gulen/Schill document.
   The effect is simply far too small, relative to this grid's own noise, to
   call an edge. Direction consistent with the literature; magnitude not
   tradeable.

2. THIS IS NOT A DISGUISED SECTOR BET — the question this family was built to
   answer up front. Comparing each raw spec with its industry-neutral twin at
   the same hold and rank fraction:

       h63  decile    raw +0.215  ->  neutral +0.095   (raw better)
       h126 decile    raw +0.302  ->  neutral +0.227   (raw better)
       h252 decile    raw +0.041  ->  neutral +0.172   (neutral better)
       h63  quintile  raw +0.107  ->  neutral +0.191   (neutral better)
       h126 quintile  raw +0.184  ->  neutral +0.294   (neutral better)
       h252 quintile  raw -0.060  ->  neutral +0.107   (neutral better)

       mean raw +0.132   vs   mean neutral +0.181

   Neutralizing INDUSTRY does not collapse the result; if anything it slightly
   improves it (4 of 6 matched pairs better, +0.05 on average). Contrast the
   sibling NOA family, where the identical adjustment destroyed the finding
   (+0.46..+0.66 raw collapsing to -0.07..+0.30). The correct reading here is
   not "asset growth survives neutralization" — there was no meaningful raw
   positive to survive. It is that this family's negative is a GENUINE NEGATIVE
   ABOUT ASSET GROWTH, not a confound diagnosis. Whatever small signal exists
   lives within industry, not between industries.

   This is the payoff of pre-declaring both conditionings in one grid: the
   answer to "is it a sector bet?" is a pre-registered result, not a follow-up
   study designed after seeing a number.

3. THE LITERATURE'S OWN ANNUAL REBALANCE IS THE WORST HOLD, which is the most
   awkward number here for the hypothesis. h252 is the rebalance frequency CGS
   themselves use, and it produces raw +0.041 at deciles and -0.060 at
   quintiles — the only negative spec in the grid. The middle hold (h126) is
   the best under both conditionings. An annually-refreshing fundamental signal
   whose best expression is a semi-annual rebalance, and whose worst is the
   source paper's own annual one, is a pattern much more consistent with noise
   than with a real effect being harvested.

WHAT THIS RESULT DOES NOT CLAIM
-------------------------------
It does NOT refute Cooper/Gulen/Schill, and the pre-registration committed to

Anomaly decay after publication, absence in the large-cap segment specifically,

with these numbers, and THIS DATASET CANNOT DISTINGUISH BETWEEN THEM.

Worth recording alongside: Hou/Xue/Zhang's large-scale replication already
found this sort attenuated to t = -2.92 on 1967-2014 — below their own t >= 3
hurdle and fully absorbed by their q-factor model. A weak, non-tradeable result
on a later, narrower, more crowded sample is what that trajectory predicts.

What this run DOES establish is the only question that matters for this
project: NOT TRADEABLE BY US, ON OUR UNIVERSE, NOW.

Do not re-test asset growth on this universe without genuinely new data (a
materially wider cross-section, or a longer pre-publication history) or a
genuinely different hypothesis — and carry these 12 trials into the denominator
of anything that does.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D.2  WHAT COOPER/GULEN/SCHILL ACTUALLY DO — verified from a primary source,
     not from memory and not from this report's own summary
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 2008 Journal of Finance article itself is paywalled. The construction was
instead verified from Cooper, Gulen & Ion, "The Use of Asset Growth in Empirical
Asset Pricing Models" (working manuscript, Bocconi copy, dated 2019-05-07) — by
TWO OF THE THREE ORIGINAL AUTHORS, whose Section 2 explicitly "revisit[s] the
asset growth anomaly of Cooper, Gulen, and Schill (2008)" and extends its sample.
Verbatim from that manuscript:

  * "In June of every year t from 1968 to 2016, we sort firms into deciles based
    on their year-over-year growth in total assets, measured in the fiscal year
    ending in calendar year t - 1."

  * (variable appendix, AG) "We form decile portfolios at the end of June of year
    t based on the AG for the fiscal year ending in calendar year t-1. Each
    month, value weighted returns are calculated for portfolios formed in July of
    year t and held until June of year t+1. PORTFOLIOS ARE REBALANCED AT THE END
    OF JUNE OF YEAR t+1."

  * (footnote 3) "Consistent with the extant literature, our sample EXCLUDES
    FINANCIALS and firms with negative book equity."

  * Their regressions throughout predict "future 12-month buy-and-hold returns".

Three things follow that matter here, and only the first was already on record:

  (a) THE REBALANCE IS STRICTLY ANNUAL, and deliberately so. The signal is not
      merely refreshed annually — it is deliberately held STALE by 6 to 18 months
      (June formation on the fiscal year ending the PREVIOUS calendar year) to
      guarantee public availability. The entire predictive claim is stated at the
      12-month horizon. There is no semi-annual variant in the source at all.

  (b) THE LITERATURE EXCLUDES FINANCIALS. THIS FAMILY DID NOT — financials are
      16.8% of its ranked slots, and the report above presents that inclusion as
      a feature ("WITHOUT excluding financials"). This is a SECOND, previously
      undiscussed construct deviation. Total-asset growth for a bank or insurer
      is balance-sheet and leverage expansion, not the q-theory capital
      investment whose over-investment/over-extrapolation story is the claimed
      mechanism. Roughly one ranked slot in six is therefore measuring a
      different economic quantity from the one CGS theorize about.

  (c) THE ANOMALY IS NOT DEAD IN THE LITERATURE, and this is the strongest single
      argument AGAINST this decline, so it is stated plainly rather than buried:
      Cooper/Gulen/Ion extend the sample to June 2016 and report "Across the
      board, we find that asset growth is strongly negatively associated with
      future 12-month stock returns", explicitly including their LARGE-firm
      subsample, and surviving controls for size, BM, momentum, gross
      profitability, NOA, CEI, accruals, ROA, SUE and ABR.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D.3  THE DECISIVE STRUCTURE: THE FLOOR AND THE MECHANISM DO NOT MEET
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Point (c) sharpens the problem rather than softening it. The authors' own
out-of-sample evidence says the effect, if present, should be present AT THE
ANNUAL HORIZON. This grid's annual-horizon specs are:

      ag_neutral_ls_h252            Sharpe +0.172   DSR 0.498
      ag_low_ls_h252                Sharpe +0.041   DSR 0.325
      ag_neutral_ls_h252_quintile   Sharpe +0.107   DSR 0.410
      ag_low_ls_h252_quintile       Sharpe -0.060   DSR 0.213   (grid's only negative)

NOT ONE of the four literature-cadence specs clears the 0.5 floor. The single
best of them misses it by 0.002.

So the family fails on both readings of the joint gate, and this is the core of
the decision:

  * THE SPEC THAT CLEARS THE FLOOR (ag_low_ls_h126, DSR 0.670) IS NOT THE
    LITERATURE'S CONSTRUCTION — it rebalances twice as often as the source
    theory specifies, on a signal the source deliberately holds stale.
  * THE SPECS THAT ARE THE LITERATURE'S CONSTRUCTION DO NOT CLEAR THE FLOOR.

That is precisely the si_dtc shape: the DSR lives at a spec that does not express
the claimed mechanism. There is no third spec to register instead — unlike short
interest, where si_ratio_hedged_h21 existed as a mechanism-faithful alternative
above the floor, here the mechanism-faithful alternatives are all below it.

The report's own contemporaneous reading (section 3 above), written before any
registration question was posed, reached the same conclusion independently: "An
annually-refreshing fundamental signal whose best expression is a semi-annual
rebalance, and whose worst is the source paper's own annual one, is a pattern
much more consistent with noise than with a real effect being harvested." This
decision agrees with that sentence and adds the primary-source verification that
the h252 cadence really is CGS's own and really is the horizon of their claim.

Note also that this family's OWN pre-registration (lines 271-276) used exactly
this reasoning to EXCLUDE a 21-day hold up front: "the ranking variable refreshes
ANNUALLY per firm (one 10-K a year), so a monthly hold re-pays turnover on an
almost unchanged ranking". That argument does not stop at h21. Applied
consistently it makes h252 the most theory-aligned cadence in the grid and h126
the less aligned of the two survivors — and the results run the opposite way.

DSR 0.670 is a bad number. It is a narrow, reversible decision that THIS GRID
does not contain a spec that is both above the floor and faithful to the
mechanism, and that spending live forward-validation calendar time on the one
cadence the source theory does not predict would put the evidence where the
theory isn't.

It would be REVERSED by any of:
  * an h252 (or true June-formation, 12-month-hold) spec clearing the floor on a
    financials-excluded universe — the construct CGS actually test. That is a
    NEW pre-registered run, with these 12 trials carried into its denominator,
    not a re-read of this one;
  * a materially wider cross-section (the report's own standing condition), where
    ~12 names per decile leg stops being the binding constraint;
  * an explicit, argued human decision to treat the informal 0.5 floor as the
    only gate and drop the mechanism-fidelity gate — which would also require
    revisiting the si_dtc rejection, since si_dtc at DSR 0.948 would qualify
    ahead of everything registered to date.
