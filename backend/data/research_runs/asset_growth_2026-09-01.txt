WHAT THE STRUCTURE OF THE GRID SAYS
-----------------------------------
1. THE SIGN IS RIGHT BUT THE SIZE IS NOT. 11 of 12 specs are positive, i.e.
   low-asset-growth firms did modestly outperform high-asset-growth firms over
   2015-2026 on this universe, in the direction Cooper/Gulen/Schill document.
   The effect is simply far too small, relative to this grid's own noise, to
   call an edge. Direction consistent with the literature; magnitude not
   tradeable.

2. THIS IS NOT A DISGUISED SECTOR BET — the question this family was built to
   answer up front. Comparing each raw spec with its industry-neutral twin at
   the same hold and rank fraction:

       h63  decile    raw +0.215  ->  neutral +0.095   (raw better)
       h126 decile    raw +0.302  ->  neutral +0.227   (raw better)
       h252 decile    raw +0.041  ->  neutral +0.172   (neutral better)
       h63  quintile  raw +0.107  ->  neutral +0.191   (neutral better)
       h126 quintile  raw +0.184  ->  neutral +0.294   (neutral better)
       h252 quintile  raw -0.060  ->  neutral +0.107   (neutral better)

       mean raw +0.132   vs   mean neutral +0.181

   Neutralizing INDUSTRY does not collapse the result; if anything it slightly
   improves it (4 of 6 matched pairs better, +0.05 on average). Contrast the
   sibling NOA family, where the identical adjustment destroyed the finding
   (+0.46..+0.66 raw collapsing to -0.07..+0.30). The correct reading here is
   not "asset growth survives neutralization" — there was no meaningful raw
   positive to survive. It is that this family's negative is a GENUINE NEGATIVE
   ABOUT ASSET GROWTH, not a confound diagnosis. Whatever small signal exists
   lives within industry, not between industries.

   This is the payoff of pre-declaring both conditionings in one grid: the
   answer to "is it a sector bet?" is a pre-registered result, not a follow-up
   study designed after seeing a number.

3. THE LITERATURE'S OWN ANNUAL REBALANCE IS THE WORST HOLD, which is the most
   awkward number here for the hypothesis. h252 is the rebalance frequency CGS
   themselves use, and it produces raw +0.041 at deciles and -0.060 at
   quintiles — the only negative spec in the grid. The middle hold (h126) is
   the best under both conditionings. An annually-refreshing fundamental signal
   whose best expression is a semi-annual rebalance, and whose worst is the
   source paper's own annual one, is a pattern much more consistent with noise
   than with a real effect being harvested.

DATA QUALITY — WHAT WAS ACTUALLY VERIFIED, INCLUDING ONE EXPECTATION THAT WAS
WRONG

* THE ENTITY SCALE-BREAK GUARD NEVER FIRED THIS RUN (0 refusals of any kind).
  The pre-registration called it "this family's single most important data
  guard", and that framing turned out to be over-stated for THIS sample — not
  because the hazard is imaginary, but because TWO OTHER, INDEPENDENT defenses
  upstream caught every instance first. Verified directly against the raw
  filings rather than assumed:
    - LINDE: the $9.2M pre-merger shell balance sheet is ABSENT from the
      extraction entirely — the provider's own cross-filing scale-conflict
      guard dropped it (12 conflicts on LIN). Its series starts at FY2018.
    - TECHNIPFMC: the $74,100 shell value IS still present at FY2015, but its
      FY2016 was dropped by that same provider guard (35 conflicts), leaving a
      731-day gap to FY2017 — and _annual_pairs' 250..480-day window refuses
      that pair outright. The shell value survives in the panel but can never
      form a growth rate.
  So the guard is genuine defence-in-depth that this sample did not need. It is
  unit-tested in both directions regardless, because the hazard is real and the
  upstream defences are not guaranteed to catch the next case.

* THE SEVEN MOST EXTREME VALUES ARE ALL REAL, IDENTIFIABLE CORPORATE EVENTS,
  checked ticker by ticker against the filings rather than assumed. These are
  the three most negative and the four most positive observations in the whole

WHAT THIS RESULT DOES NOT CLAIM
-------------------------------
It does NOT refute Cooper/Gulen/Schill, and the pre-registration committed to
saying so in advance. Their sort is a broad-universe sort with hundreds of
names per decile over a multi-decade sample; this is ~150 large-cap names over
11.6 years with decile legs of ~12 (quintile ~24), in a POST-PUBLICATION window
on a factor since institutionalized into two standard asset-pricing models.
Anomaly decay after publication, absence in the large-cap segment specifically,
and simple lack of statistical power in this cross-section are ALL consistent
with these numbers, and THIS DATASET CANNOT DISTINGUISH BETWEEN THEM.

Worth recording alongside: Hou/Xue/Zhang's large-scale replication already
found this sort attenuated to t = -2.92 on 1967-2014 — below their own t >= 3
hurdle and fully absorbed by their q-factor model. A weak, non-tradeable result
on a later, narrower, more crowded sample is what that trajectory predicts.

What this run DOES establish is the only question that matters for this
project: NOT TRADEABLE BY US, ON OUR UNIVERSE, NOW.

Do not re-test asset growth on this universe without genuinely new data (a
materially wider cross-section, or a longer pre-publication history) or a
genuinely different hypothesis — and carry these 12 trials into the denominator
of anything that does.
