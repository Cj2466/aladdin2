2. THE PRESERVATION SCORE — exact definition
==============================================================================
Implemented in app/services/research_lab/preservation_score.py, 21 unit tests in
tests/test_preservation_score.py. For a spec's realized daily net return series r:

    S    = annualized Sharpe of r
    AR   = mean(r) * periods_per_year          (arithmetic, same mean S uses)
    MDD  = max drawdown of cumprod(1+r)        (<= 0)
    C    = AR / max(|MDD|, 0.01)                 Calmar, floored at 1%
    RQ   = sign(S) * sqrt(|S| * |C|)           risk quality: signed GEOMETRIC MEAN
                                               of Sharpe and Calmar
    S1,S2= Sharpe of the first and second half of r, split by position
    stab = clip( min(S1,S2) / |S| , 0, 1 )     stability
    cred = clip(dsr, 0, 1)                     credibility

    preservation_score         = 0.42 * cred * RQ * stab
    preservation_score_no_stab = 0.42 * cred * RQ

WHY 0.42 AND NOT A ROUND 0.5:
  McLean & Pontiff, 'Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?', Journal of Finance 71(1), 2016, pp. 5-32, doi:10.1111/jofi.12365 (26% out-of-sample decay pre-publication; 58% post-publication)
  Their two decay figures are not interchangeable. 26% is pre-publication (overfitting/statistical bias only); 58% is post-publication (arbitrage on top). Every family here re-implements an ALREADY-PUBLISHED anomaly on a public, liquid cross-section, so the applicable point on their timeline is the post-publication one: retention = 1 - 0.58 = 0.42. Note this is a CONSTANT and therefore changes no ranking at all — it exists so the number reads as an expected forward quantity. All re-ranking comes from cred, RQ and stab.

WHY A GEOMETRIC MEAN OF SHARPE AND CALMAR: Sharpe is path-blind (symmetric volatility); Calmar is path-aware but is a single-episode statistic and much noisier. The geometric mean keeps both and halves the exponent on each, so one bad drawdown episode moves the score by the square root of what it would move a pure Calmar ranking.

WHAT THE SCORE DELIBERATELY OMITS: any turnover or holding-period term. The score carries no prior that low turnover is better, precisely so section 4 can TEST that rather than assume it.

INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION OF THE FORMULA, run before this report was written:
  A separate script that does NOT import preservation_score.py recomputed every term (Sharpe, annualized return, max drawdown, Calmar, risk quality, both half Sharpes, decay, stability, credibility and both scores) from first principles in plain numpy, on the SAME captured return series, for all 12 asset_growth specs: 156 comparisons, 0 mismatches at 1e-12.
  A second pass re-ran asset_growth from scratch an hour after the main run and compared against the committed JSON: every metric agreed to a relative ~1e-6. That residual is DATA drift (yfinance restates adjusted closes), not formula error, and is why the reproduction test in section 3 uses a tolerance rather than equality.

4. THE DECISIVE TEST — does low turnover decay less, ON OUR DATA?
==============================================================================
Decay statistic: sharpe_decay = Sharpe(second half of the spec's realized daily series) - Sharpe(first half). NEGATIVE = the edge got worse over the backtest. Halves are split by position; a spec whose half would fall below 126 observations is not measured at all.

Bucket boundary: holding_days >= 63 = low turnover.
Specs with a measurable decay: 175 of 175 rebuilt.

WHAT WAS FIXED BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTED, and what was not — stated because this section is a hypothesis test and this project does not accept an undisclosed specification search. ADDED AFTER SEEING PARTIAL RESULTS, and therefore reported as descriptive rather than as confirmatory tests: the sign-blind |sharpe_decay| panel, the within-family demeaned rank correlations, the positive-Sharpe subset (4d), the what-would-it-cost panel (4e), and the persisted-thirds panel (4f). ==============================================================================
8. THE ANSWER — is a low-turnover preference actually better HERE?
==============================================================================
THE NUMBERS THE ANSWER RESTS ON (all computed above, repeated here):
  Pooled spec-level decay:  low +0.0818 (n=105) vs high +0.0848 (n=70); difference -0.0030, Welch t -0.03 on 158.3 df
  Within-family paired:     4 of 5 families favour low turnover; mean paired difference +0.1321
  Within-family demeaned rank correlation, holding_days vs decay: rho = +0.032
  Families contributing a within-family contrast at all: 5. Equity families rebuilt: 11. Specs measured: 175.

  Pooled vs WITHIN-FAMILY rank correlation with holding_days — the contrast that
  decides the question, because only the second one isolates holding period:
    statistic            pooled rho   demeaned rho
    sharpe_decay             +0.004         +0.032
    max_drawdown             +0.421         +0.059
    stability                +0.204         +0.029
    sharpe_full              +0.526         +0.008

WHAT THAT DOES AND DOES NOT SUPPORT:
  1. THE DIRECT TEST FINDS NOTHING. Pooled over all rebuilt specs the two buckets' mean decay differ by -0.0030 against within-bucket standard deviations near 0.62. That is not a small effect, it is no effect.
  2. THE APPARENT ADVANTAGES ARE A FAMILY ARTEFACT. Low-turnover specs really do have shallower drawdowns and higher Sharpes in the pooled table — but every one of those pooled rank correlations collapses to near zero once each family's own mean is subtracted (table above). Holding period is not doing the work; WHICH SIGNAL is. In this candidate set the slow families are the good families, which is a fact about what got built, not about turnover.
  3. THE ONE PANEL THAT LEANS THE OTHER WAY, reported rather than buried: the within-family paired comparison (4c) has 4 of 5 families favouring low turnover, mean +0.1321. Its standard deviation is 0.3012 — twice the mean — and it rests on 5 families, one of which (liquidity_shock_delta_illiq) contributes four specs a side. The independent persisted-thirds panel (4f) leans the same way and is even thinner: one family with a within-family contrast. Both are SUGGESTIVE. Neither is a basis for a rule.
  4. SIGN-BLIND INSTABILITY POINTS AGAINST THE PREMISE. |sharpe_decay| measures how far a spec's two halves moved apart regardless of direction, which is what 'stability' actually means. The low-turnover bucket's is LARGER, not smaller.

THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE:
  Preferring low-turnover candidates is NOT supported as a selection rule on this project's own data. The direct decay test finds no difference at all; the drawdown and Sharpe advantages that do show up pooled are between-family effects that vanish once family is controlled for; the sign-blind instability measure leans the wrong way. Two thin panels lean toward it and are reported above, but with 5 families contributing a within-family contrast the evidence is SUGGESTIVE AT BEST and closer to inconclusive. It does not justify a turnover term in the score, and none was added.
  DOES IT LEAVE BETTER HIGH-TURNOVER CANDIDATES ON THE TABLE? In THIS candidate set, barely — section 4e shows the top 10 by preservation_score are all low-turnover, and the best high-turnover full-sample Sharpe (+0.6054) is below the best low-turnover one (+0.7740). But that is the SAME family artefact restated: the high-turnover bucket's mean full-sample Sharpe is -0.2970, and 32 of its 70 specs come from jump_drift and same_calendar_month_seasonality alone — two families this project had already recorded as honest negatives before this run existed. The rule would cost little here while resting on nothing, which is the worst way for a heuristic to survive: cheap enough that nothing ever corrects it.
  WHAT IS SUPPORTED INSTEAD, and is why preservation_score is built as it is: measure the PATH directly. The drawdown and stability terms re-rank candidates materially (section 5, Spearman against the raw-DSR order well below 1.0) without any turnover prior at all. They do the work the 'prefer low turnover' heuristic was reaching for — penalising ugly paths and one-half-only edges — on each candidate's own realized returns instead of using holding period as a proxy for them.
  THE LIMIT OF ALL OF THIS, stated plainly: 11 equity families, 5 with a within-family holding-period contrast, one backtest window each, one cost model. A finding either way on that sample would be weak. This one is a null, and nulls on small samples are the weakest result there is — it is a reason not to ADOPT the rule, not proof the rule is false.
