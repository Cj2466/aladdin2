VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FEASIBLE_FREE_WITH_NAMED_LIMITS_DATA_LAYER_ONLY

  The underlying DATA -- option open interest by strike and expiration, for
  both calls and puts, on real index/single-name tickers, right now -- is
  confirmed free and live from TWO INDEPENDENT sources (yfinance and, newly
  checked in this run, Alpaca's existing paper-trading account). That is a
  stronger free-data picture than either prior note had established. But
  the named limits are severe enough to dominate the practical answer, and
  are reported in order of how much they bind:

  (1) NEITHER CITED PAPER'S ACTUAL MEASURE CAN BE BUILT FOR FREE. Both
      Bollen-Whaley's Net Buying Pressure and Barbon-Buraschi's Gamma
      Imbalance directly observe WHICH SIDE OF EACH TRADE THE DEALER WAS
      ON -- Bollen-Whaley via trade-vs-quote-midpoint classification,
      Barbon-Buraschi via broker-dealer-vs-customer volume reported by
      three options exchanges under license. Free retail data supplies
      OPEN INTEREST, not that sign-identification input. A free build is
      therefore not a degraded version of either paper's method -- it is a
      DIFFERENT construction (the popular "GEX" heuristic, which ASSUMES a
      fixed dealer-short/dealer-long sign convention from open interest
      alone) that neither paper actually validates. This is a real,
      material mechanism-fidelity deviation that would need its own
      citation and independent sign-off before any result built on it is
      treated as evidence about the mechanism these two papers document
      (CLAUDE.md Section 4).

  (2) NO HISTORICAL ARCHIVE OF OPTION OPEN-INTEREST-BY-STRIKE EXISTS THAT
      THIS PROJECT CAN REACH FREE, ANYWHERE. Unlike N-PORT (~7 years of
      already-existing public history minable today), this candidate has
      NOTHING to backtest against: yfinance's historical/expired-contract
      chains are confirmed dead (reproduced live in this run, not merely
      repeated from the prior note); Alpaca's contracts endpoint returns
      only the CURRENT open_interest, no history; Alpaca's separate
      historical-bars product only goes back to Feb 2024 and does not carry
      OI at all; OCC's own free batch-download page could not be reached
      live from this environment (UNVERIFIED-FROM-HERE, not confirmed
      unavailable). The only genuinely historical BY-STRIKE OI product
      found is CBOE DataShop's self-serve "Option EOD Summary" (back to
      Jan 2012) -- which is PAID, with a real but unconfigured price. So
      the free path here is FORWARD-ONLY, starting from zero days of
      history today, not "mine what already exists" the way N-PORT was.

  (3) HOW MUCH FORWARD HISTORY WOULD BE ENOUGH IS AN OPEN QUESTION, NOT
      RESOLVED HERE. Barbon-Buraschi's own statistical power comes from an
      intraday, ~3500-stock cross-section this project cannot reach for
      free; a free, index-level, daily-frequency build would need to
      recover power from TIME instead (spanning multiple real gamma-regime
      transitions), and this run could not measure how often those
      transitions actually occur without the very historical data found
      unavailable above. Reasoned to be "years, not weeks" but not pinned
      to a specific number -- named as an open question for whoever designs
      the real forward-validation registration.

  NO IMMEDIATE PURCHASE IS RECOMMENDED. Per CLAUDE.md, the CBOE DataShop and
  OptionMetrics findings are LOGGED (see PAID-DATA GAP section and

Q1. WHAT DO THE TWO CITED PAPERS' CONSTRUCTIONS ACTUALLY NEED?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BARBON & BURASCHI (2021), "GAMMA FRAGILITY" -- PRIMARY SOURCE READ DIRECTLY.
  Working paper PDF downloaded and read directly 2026-09-06
  (http://www.abarbon.com/assets/Barbon_Buraschi_2021_Gamma_Fragility.pdf,
  linked from the authors' own SSRN abstract page, SSRN 3725454).

  Their "Gamma Imbalance" construction, quoted verbatim with page numbers:
    Eq.(1), p.12: "Hedgers Inventory_j = BrokerDealers Inventory_j -
      Customers Inventory_j"
    p.13, unnumbered: "Hedgers Gamma_i = sum_{j written on i} Gamma_j x
      Hedgers Inventory_i"
    Eq.(2), p.13: "Gamma^IB_i(t) = Hedgers Gamma_i x S_i(t) / ADSV_i(t)"
      where S_i(t) is the underlying's closing price and ADSV_i(t) is its
      21-day-rolling average daily share volume.

  DATA THIS ACTUALLY NEEDS, p.10-11 ("II. Data and Summary Statistics",
  Section A "Data Sources"), quoted:
    "Our empirical analysis is based on a number of data sources. To
     construct the inventory of option contracts held by broker/dealers,
     we use option trading data from three Nasdaq exchanges (ISE, GEMX,
     and PHLX). These datasets provide opening and closing volume for
     broker/dealers, proprietary trading desks, and private customers,
     covering the period between January 2010 and May 2020... We obtain
     data on daily options prices, open interest, and greeks from the
     IvyDB dataset provided by OptionMetrics... We merge the dataset
     obtained with the CRSP and TAQ databases by ticker to obtain
     information on daily and intra-daily returns and trading volume for
     each underlying assets. We collect data on market fundamentals from
     the News Analytics dataset by RavenPack..."
  Footnote 8, p.10-11, on coverage -- quoted because it is itself a finding:
    "In this time window, these three exchanges account for a fraction
     between 10% and 20% of the worldwide dollar volume of option contracts
     written on US equity securities. We are currently in the process of
     obtaining additional data from the CBOE C1 exchange. In that way, we
     would extend our coverage to almost 50% of the total dollar volume."
    Even this paper's OWN AUTHORS needed a direct, individually-negotiated
    exchange data relationship to move past 10-20% coverage -- this is not
    a subscription product available to anyone who pays, at any price.
  Sample: 3491 individual U.S. stocks plus 4 broad market indices (options
  side, p.11), narrowing to "four market indices (S&P 500, Dow Jones,
  Nasdaq 100 and Russell 2000) and 2700 individual U.S. equity stocks"
  after the CRSP/TAQ merge (p.11). Period 2010-2020. Frequency: INTRADAY
  (5-minute and 60-minute return autocorrelations are the paper's actual
  dependent variable, p.2/p.6).

  BOLLEN & WHALEY (2004) -- SECONDARY-SOURCED, PRIMARY TEXT PAYWALLED.
  Stated plainly: this run could NOT obtain the original Journal of Finance
  59(2):711-753 full text. Wiley's page is paywalled; the SSRN abstract
  page (papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=319261) gave only the
  abstract, and its PDF-delivery link returned HTTP 403 from this
  environment. What IS independently confirmed, directly:
    * The paper is real. The American Finance Association's own published
      errata for it (fetched and read directly,
      afajof.org/wp-content/uploads/files/clarifications/
      jf-bollen-whaley-errata.pdf) quotes a real corrected equation from
      the paper -- its Eq.(9), the delta-hedged trading-strategy
      abnormal-return formula, unrelated to net buying pressure -- under
      the exact citation "Bollen, Nicolas P.B., and Robert E. Whaley,
      2004... Journal of Finance 59, 711-753."
    * Its Net Buying Pressure (NBP) construction, quoted VERBATIM from a
      citing academic source read directly (not this run's paraphrase),
      EFMA 2015 Annual Meeting conference paper "Net buying pressure and
      option informed trading", Section 2, p.6 (fetched and read directly
      2026-09-06, efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/
      2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0395_fullpaper.pdf):
        "The classical net buying pressure adopted in Bollen and Whaley
         (2004) and Kang and Park (2008) is defined by the difference
         between the number of buyer-motivated contracts and
         seller-motivated contracts. Herein, the difference is computed
         on a series-by-series basis, and is multiplied by the absolute
         value of the option's delta to express demand in index
         equivalent units."
    * A second, independently-phrased, lower-confidence description
      (WebSearch-aggregated, not a single direct quote, reported for
      corroboration only) adds the trade-classification rule: "Bollen and
      Whaley (2004) identify buyer-motivated options as trades executed at
      the price above the midpoint of prevailing bid and ask prices...
      seller-motivated trades are those executed below the midpoint" --
      the standard quote-midpoint trade-classification rule, same family
      as Lee-Ready.
  DATA THIS ACTUALLY NEEDS: per-trade classification (buyer- vs
  seller-motivated, from execution price vs. the prevailing quote midpoint
  AT THE TIME OF EACH TRADE) times that trade's own option delta, computed
  DAILY on a series-by-series (moneyness-bucketed) basis. That is
  trade-and-quote-level option market data -- NOT an end-of-day
  open-interest snapshot. OptionMetrics IvyDB is the data source secondary
  sources consistently attribute to this and follow-on NBP papers found in
  this run's literature search (Taiwan TAIEX options, VIX options).

  THE SHARED FINDING FROM BOTH PAPERS, AND WHY IT MATTERS MOST: neither
  paper's actual measure is the popular retail "GEX" (Gamma Exposure)
  heuristic that assumes a fixed dealer-short/dealer-long sign convention
  from raw open interest alone. Both papers DIRECTLY OBSERVE which side of
  each trade the dealer was on. Free data supplies the OPEN INTEREST half
  of a GEX-style calculation (Q2) but not the sign-identification half
  either paper actually validates -- so any free build here is a named
  DEVIATION from both cited methods, not a degraded-but-equivalent version
  of either.

Q4. HOW MUCH FORWARD-COLLECTED HISTORY WOULD BE ENOUGH? -- REASONED, NOT
    RESOLVED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  This candidate is shaped like a market-timing/regime overlay on an
  index-level instrument (SPX/SPY), not a cross-sectional stock-picker --
  the same reasoning shape run_tsmom_futures_feasibility.py used for its
  15.0-effective-breadth floor, applied here to a genuinely different
  problem (time-series regime persistence, not cross-sectional breadth).
  Named, not settled:
    * Barbon-Buraschi's own statistical power comes from an INTRADAY,
      ~3500-stock cross-section observed daily over 2010-2020 -- a huge
      panel, not a long single-name time series. A free build limited to
      a handful of index-level tickers (SPY/QQQ/IWM) forfeits that
      cross-sectional power entirely and must recover power from TIME
      instead -- spanning multiple distinct volatility/gamma regimes over
      calendar years -- which is a slower, and not necessarily equivalent,
      substitute.
    * A DAILY-frequency signal (what free OI actually supports; neither
      yfinance nor Alpaca supplies the intraday, participant-classified
      data Barbon-Buraschi's own design needs -- Q1/Q2 above) would need to
      span enough REGIME TRANSITIONS (episodes of persistently negative vs.
      persistently positive aggregate dealer gamma) to identify a real
      relationship rather than one episode's artifact. This project has NOT
      measured how often SPX-level aggregate dealer gamma actually flips
      sign in practice -- doing so would itself require the historical data
      Q3 found is not freely reachable -- so a specific required number of
      transitions cannot be stated here. Named as an open question, per
      this run's own instructions, rather than guessed at.
    * This project's own DSR/mechanism-fidelity gate (CLAUDE.md Section 4)
      needs a real sample of trials/returns to compute DSR or
      preservation_score at all. By analogy to this project's other
      regime-conditional work (e.g. vol_regime_timing.py's own multi-year
      windows), a daily SPY-level series would need, at an absolute floor,
      enough trading days to span at least one full high-vol/low-vol cycle
      (order of 1-3 years) before any DSR number is more than noise, and
      substantially more (multiple such cycles) before a conservative-N DSR
      pass could be trusted per CLAUDE.md's two-tier verdict rule. This is
      a REASONED ESTIMATE, explicitly NOT a settled floor the way TSMOM's
      15.0 breadth number was.
    * Bottom line: a defensible DSR-gated verdict on this candidate is
      realistically YEARS AWAY under the free-data path, counted from the
      day real collection begins -- not a "run it on existing data
      tonight" candidate the way N-PORT was.
