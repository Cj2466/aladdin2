SOURCE: Boehmer, Huszar & Jordan, 'The good news in short interest' (Journal of Financial Economics 96(1), 2010, pp. 80-97) — whose abstract reports that 'relatively heavily traded stocks with low short interest experience both statistically and economically significant positive abnormal returns', 'often larger (in absolute value) than the negative returns observed for heavily shorted stocks'. Replicated here on real FINRA bi-monthly short interest and real SEC point-in-time share counts. The paper's full text could not be obtained during this build (SSRN/ScienceDirect/repositories all refused), so every methodological detail used is second-hand and labeled as such in the module docstring — including one detail, its 'heavily traded' conditioning, that could not be verified at all and is therefore NOT replicated

--- WHAT WAS WRONG, AND IS NOW FIXED ---------------------------------------

8. THE TWO "REGRESSION TESTS" FOR THE CSV DEFECT DID NOT TEST IT. Mutation
   testing (36 mutations across the new logic) found that deleting
   `quoting=csv.QUOTE_NONE` — the headline data-defect fix — left the entire
   suite GREEN. Both existing tests place the double quote in the MIDDLE of
   issueName (`ELEMENTS "Dogs of the Dow" Tot`), which Python's csv module
   reads identically under QUOTE_NONE and under the default dialect, so
   neither could ever have caught a regression. The real corrupting value,
   read from shrt20180329.csv line 3881, is `"ELEMENTS ""Dogs of the Dow""`
   — the field BEGINS with a quote. Measured on that fixture, the default
   dialect returns one row with None in every numeric field and silently
   DROPS the following securities. A pinning test carrying the real
   field-initial value has been added and proven to fail without the fix.
   NOTE this also revises the module's characterisation: the silent-corruption
   mode is not merely hypothetical, it is what a field-initial quote does; the
   hard `_csv.Error` happens on 4 of the 208 full files.

9. THE DSR DENOMINATOR WAS UNPINNED — the single most consequential gap.
   Deleting `n_trials_override=SHORT_INTEREST_N_TRIALS` from the production
   entry point left the suite green. Each pass screens only 6 specs, so the
   harness would then infer n_trials=6, and si_dtc_ls_h63 would score DSR
   0.961 instead of 0.948 — flipping this family's verdict from an honest
   negative into a spurious PASS of its own bar. Pinned end to end with a
   fake-provider test that drives run_short_interest_screening itself and
   asserts n_trials==12 on all 12 returned rows.

10. A GENUINE LOOK-AHEAD WAS UNPINNED. Swapping _step_frame's ffill for a
    bfill survived the suite: the existing back-fill test only covers dates
    BEFORE the first observation, which the staleness mask blanks
    independently. Back-filling BETWEEN two observations — handing a
    formation a short-interest value published later — was untested. Pinned.

11. FURTHER UNPINNED BEHAVIOURS, all now covered by proven tests: the SEC
    visibility lag could be set to 0 (a 90-day look-ahead on share counts)
    because the existing test asserted an identity in terms of the constant
    itself; SHORT_INTEREST_MAX_STALENESS_DAYS was tautological the same way;
    the parser could stop reading stockSplitFlag, silently turning the whole
    split guard into dead code; the scale-break threshold could drop to 20x
    (the existing AMZN test used exactly 20.0x, sitting ON the boundary,
    rather than the real 22.3x); the guard's low-side clause could be deleted;
    and the 5bp cost could be set to zero. 12 consequential mutations
    survived initially; all 12 are now caught.
