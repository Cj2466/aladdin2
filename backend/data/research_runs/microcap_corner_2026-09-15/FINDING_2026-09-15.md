# The two published facts that close the A2 loop — and the one calculation that must NOT be believed yet

2026-09-15, after A2. No new sealed-data look was taken for §1–2; §3 is a cost
re-pricing of the already-computed sealed series and is explicitly NOT a verdict.

## 1. "Just use liquid names" — the literature already tested exactly that

**Hou, Xue & Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies", Review of Financial Studies 33(5),
2020, pp. 2019–2133.** Figures below are read verbatim from the authors' own
slide deck (`global-q.org/uploads/1/2/2/6/122679606/slides_replication_2021may.pdf`,
fetched and text-extracted 2026-09-15), not from a summary.

They replicate **452 anomaly variables**. With microcaps mitigated — their words:
"Testing deciles with NYSE breakpoints and value-weights":

| result | verbatim |
|---|---|
| **65%** | "cannot clear the single test hurdle of \|t\| ≥ 1.96, with microcaps mitigated with NYSE breakpoints and value-weights" |
| **52%** | "Most (52%) anomalies fail to replicate irrespective of microcaps … if adjusting for multiple testing" |
| **96%** | "The biggest casualty in the trading frictions category, with 96% failing the single tests" |
| — | "Replicated anomalies size much smaller than originally reported" |

**This is the question I said would need rebuilding on our own panel. It does not.
It is answered, published and peer-reviewed: remove the micro-caps and roughly
two thirds of published anomalies stop being distinguishable from noise.**

## 2. And it confirms, from a source, what A2 had only inferred

Their Table of portfolio weight allocated to microcaps (in %):

| weighting | share of portfolio weight in microcaps |
|---|---|
| NYSE breakpoints + value-weights | **7.19%** |
| NYSE breakpoints + equal-weights | **55.24%** |
| All breakpoints + equal-weights | **57.56%** |

A2 argued that OSAP's equal-weighted construction makes the book micro-cap
dominated. **Sourced: an equal-weighted anomaly portfolio carries 55–58% of its
weight in microcaps.** The A2 inference was right and is no longer an inference.

Their updated Fama-French (2008) Table I, 1967–2016, says why: microcaps are
**2,365 of 3,896 firms (60.7% by count) but only 3.21% of market cap.**

## 3. The pincer, stated plainly

| choice | cost | edge |
|---|---|---|
| keep microcaps (equal-weight, as OSAP builds) | **fatal** — A2: 184–247 bp per reformation vs a 30.6 bp break-even | intact |
| remove microcaps (value-weight, NYSE breakpoints) | fine — 5–9 bp per reformation | **65% no longer clear \|t\| ≥ 1.96** |

Those are the two ends. Neither is a working strategy on its own.

## 4. The escape the numbers point at — AND WHY IT IS NOT YET A RESULT

There is a third axis: **hold longer.** Re-pricing the same sealed series at the
sourced micro-cap cost, with every member forced to one cadence:

| forced cadence | cost/month | Sharpe @184 bp | Sharpe @247 bp | vs 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| monthly | 184–247 bp | −1.840 | −2.732 | FAIL |
| quarterly | 61.5–82.3 bp | −0.090 | −0.387 | FAIL |
| annual | 15.4–20.6 bp | **0.567** | 0.492 | passes at the cheap end only |
| 2-yearly | 7.7–10.3 bp | **0.676** | **0.639** | PASS |
| 3-yearly | 5.1–6.9 bp | **0.712** | **0.688** | PASS |

And the corner is genuinely one big money cannot take: HXZ's own investment-capacity
table puts the equal-weighted anomaly strategy at **$26.6 billion** against
**$2,015.65 billion** value-weighted. A retail account has no impact problem in a
pool that is 60.7% of listed firms and 3.2% of market cap.

### THE TRAP IN THAT TABLE, WRITTEN DOWN BEFORE ANYONE GETS ATTACHED TO IT

**Forcing P=24 or P=36 divides the COST by 24 or 36 while leaving the paper's own
GROSS RETURN untouched. That is not a strategy; it is an upper bound.** The gross
series is the paper's monthly-rebalanced portfolio. Holding that portfolio for two
years without reforming it would earn LESS than the series says, because the signal
decays — and this calculation gives that decay a value of exactly zero.

The 2026-09-13 holding-period result is only partial comfort: it found gross Sharpe
flat across cadences (0.29 monthly vs 0.26 annual), but that compares DIFFERENT
predictors whose own papers rebalance at different speeds. It does not measure the
same predictor held longer, which is the quantity this table needs and does not have.

**So rows 4–6 above are a statement about costs only. Nothing may be built on them
until signal decay at the intended holding period is measured directly.** That
measurement is the next real task, and OSAP's stock-level signal file (209
characteristics) is the cheapest route to it — subject to checking whether it keys
on CRSP permno, which we cannot join without paid data.

## What this changes in the plan

1. The published-anomaly path is **not dead**, but it is much narrower than the
   0.599 headline suggested: micro-cap corner, very slow rebalancing, many members.
2. **Member selection must be re-based.** Ranking by OSAP Sharpe is wrong twice over
   (cost too low for its universe; no replication filter). The right screen is
   anomalies that survive HXZ's replication AND a slow cadence AND our cost curve.
3. The next measurement is **signal decay at long holding periods** — not more
   cost work, and not more literature.
