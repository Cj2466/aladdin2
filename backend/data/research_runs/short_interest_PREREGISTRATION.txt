SHORT INTEREST, THE LONG-SIDE READING — PRE-REGISTRATION
Boehmer, Huszar & Jordan (JFE 96(1), 2010), "The good news in short interest"
Written and committed 2026-09-02, BEFORE any backtest of this family was run.
==============================================================================

This document is the binding statement of what will be tested, on what data,
under what denominator, and by what pass/fail rule. It is committed before the
first result exists so that the grid cannot grow, the rule cannot soften, and
the caveats cannot be discovered afterwards. The results document that follows
it must apply THIS grid and THIS rule, unchanged, or say plainly that it did
not and why.

Candidate #7 of this project's "Seven Candidates" literature review.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE ONE-LINE HYPOTHESIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Heavily-traded US large-cap stocks with LOW short interest earn positive
abnormal returns relative to their own universe, and that long-side effect is
at least as large as the well-known negative effect on heavily shorted names.

Mechanism claimed by the source: short sellers are informed traders, so
persistently low short interest is a read on the informed crowd's ABSENCE of
negative private information — public information the market incorporates only
slowly (an underreaction / inattention story).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WAS VERIFIED ABOUT THE SOURCE, AND WHAT WAS NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFIED, live-fetched 2026-09-02 from RePEc/EconPapers: the bibliographic
record (Boehmer, Ekkehart; Huszar, Zsuzsa R.; Jordan, Bradford — "The good news
in short interest", Journal of Financial Economics 96(1), 2010, pp. 80-97) and
the full abstract verbatim, including the two clauses this candidate rests on:
"relatively heavily traded stocks with low short interest experience both
statistically and economically significant positive abnormal returns" and
"These positive returns are often larger (in absolute value) than the negative
returns observed for heavily shorted stocks."

NOT OBTAINED: the paper's full text. SSRN (abstract page and every
Delivery.cfm variant), ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, the SMU institutional
repository and NUS ScholarBank were each tried and each returned HTTP 403, a
CAPTCHA, or metadata without full text. The Internet Archive holds no
full-text snapshot. THEREFORE EVERY METHODOLOGICAL DETAIL BELOW IS SECOND-HAND
and is labeled with the strength of its support. Nothing is reconstructed from
memory.

  (a) MEASURE: shares short / shares outstanding, a LEVEL not a change.
      STRONG — multiple independent sources, including a later paper by two of
      the three original authors that groups this paper with the literature
      using "the aggregate number of shares shorted for each stock divided by
      shares outstanding, SIR". One dissenting source implies days-to-cover;
      recorded, not ignored (see section 4).
  (b) BREAKPOINTS: extreme percentile tails — the 1st, 5th and 10th
      percentiles on the low side. MODERATE — two independent replications
      agree on exactly this triple.
  (c) REBALANCE: monthly. MODERATE — two independent sources. Horizon: one
      month baseline, with one source reporting alpha persists to ~6 months.
  (d) UNIVERSE/BENCHMARK: NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ, 1988-2005, ~4,400 stocks per
      month; both equal- and value-weighted; Carhart four-factor alphas.
      MODERATE — three sources agree on four-factor.
  (e) "RELATIVELY HEAVILY TRADED": NOT VERIFIED AT ALL. No source found states
      the trading-activity variable, whether the sort is a double sort, or any
      breakpoint. This is the single largest gap in this build and section 4
      states exactly what is done instead. It is NOT replicated and will not be
      claimed as replicated.

Two fabrication traps were hit during the source search and are recorded so
they are not repeated: (1) a search engine's synthesized answer attributed a
"quintiles, skip a month, 12-month holding period" design to this paper; the
actual fetched source shows that text is a DIFFERENT paper describing its own
method while merely citing BHJ. (2) Several citing papers say "Consistent with
Boehmer et al. (2010), Table 9 shows..." where the table is the CITING paper's,
not BHJ's. Neither was used.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PRIOR — WRITTEN BEFORE RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A tradeable edge here is UNLIKELY, for three stated reasons:

  * POST-PUBLICATION, LARGE-CAP, FREE SIGNAL. The paper's sample ends 2005 and
    it published in 2010. This tests 2018-2026 — thirteen years after the
    sample ends — on the 500 most-watched stocks in the world, using a signal
    FINRA gives away twice a month. If this were tradeable at this scale it
    would be the most easily harvested edge in this entire project.

  * THE LONG SIDE MAY BE A JANUARY / SMALL-CAP ARTIFACT. A 2016 dissertation
    (single source, directly on point) reports the lightly-shorted portfolio
    earns "significant average 4-factor adjusted return of 4.55 percent in
    January ... compared to average return of 0.45 percent in non-January", and
    that outside January the long side is SMALLER in absolute value than the
    short side — the direct opposite of the abstract's headline claim.

  * A SUB-$5 PRICE SCREEN MAY MATTER. One source notes a later paper added a $5
    screen and got different results, implying BHJ's result "could have been
    mainly influenced by stocks priced below $5". Every S&P 500 constituent is
    far above $5, so on that reading this universe removes the effect by
    construction.

THE HONEST EXPECTED OUTCOME IS AN HONEST NEGATIVE. That is a complete and
valued result and will be reported as such without hedging.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DATA — REAL ONLY, AND ONE PREMISE THAT DID NOT SURVIVE VERIFICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHORT INTEREST: real FINRA bi-monthly equity short interest files,
https://cdn.finra.org/equity/otcmarket/biweekly/shrt<YYYYMMDD>.csv, fetched and
parsed directly. No synthetic or simulated input touches any persisted number.

THE BUILD BRIEF'S CENTRAL PREMISE WAS WRONG AND IS CORRECTED HERE. The brief
scoped this candidate around FINRA covering exchange-listed securities only
from June 2021 — about 5.2 years — citing FINRA's own catalog-page note.
Verified by downloading and parsing the real files 2026-09-02: exchange-listed
names are present far earlier. shrt20171229.csv carries 3,102 NYSE, 2,355 NNM,
1,433 ARCA, 347 AMEX and 235 BZX rows, and AAPL appears with 45,746,430 shares
short. A full anchor-walk probe of every candidate settlement date from 2014
found 208 files, earliest 2017-12-29, latest 2026-08-14, with a complete 24
cycles in each of 2018-2025 and no interior gaps.

  USABLE WINDOW: 2017-12-29 .. 2026-08-14, ~8.7 years — NOT 5.2.

  THE HONEST LIMIT OF THAT CORRECTION, disclosed rather than glossed: the
  near-certain explanation is that FINRA RETROACTIVELY BACKFILLED consolidated
  files onto this endpoint when it took over consolidated publication in 2021.
  The pre-June-2021 exchange-listed rows are data that WAS public at the time
  (the listing exchanges disseminated their own semi-monthly short interest on
  the same statutory cycle) but not necessarily public AT THIS URL IN THIS FORM.
  An attempt to settle this via the Internet Archive found NO snapshot of any
  pre-2021 file at this path, so the question COULD NOT BE RESOLVED and is left
  open rather than answered by assertion. Nothing in the code depends on which
  answer is right; the disclosure is the deliverable.

  ~8.7 years is still THIN by this project's standards — most families here
  backtest 10+ years — and every result must be read with that stated, not as
  though it carried a decade's confidence. Section 4 explains how the grid size
  was chosen against that constraint.

SHARE COUNTS (the ratio's denominator): real SEC XBRL frames,
dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding, quarterly instantaneous frames
CY2017Q3I..CY2026Q3I — 37 requests covering 4,400-5,300 filers each.

PRICES: real yfinance daily history. UNIVERSE: the full point-in-time S&P 500
union (sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over), 691 names over the window,
gated per formation by was_member. NOT a seeded sample — this family's data
cost is per-CYCLE, not per-ticker.

POINT-IN-TIME DISCIPLINE, both inputs:
  * Short interest becomes readable at settlement + 14 CALENDAR days. FINRA's
    rule is dissemination "seven business days after the reporting settlement
    date"; rather than reimplement a nine-year holiday calendar, this build
    uses a calendar bound chosen to DOMINATE every row of FINRA's own published
    2026 schedule, whose 24 settlement-to-publication gaps range from 9 to 12
    days. 14 exceeds the worst by two days and can only ever make the signal
    STALER than it really was. The bound is unit-tested against all 24
    published rows. Live corroboration: on 2026-09-02 the 2026-08-14 file was
    downloadable and the 2026-08-31 file returned HTTP 403, exactly as a real
    lag implies. Design precedent: Bradford Jordan's own later work states
    "Short interest data is made public eight business days after the mid-month
    reporting settlement date" and uses only mid-month values "to insure public
    availability by the start of the next month".
  * Share counts become readable at their cover-page `end` + 90 CALENDAR days.
    SEC's frames payload carries no `filed` field, so rather than guess a lag
    this build MEASURED one: 7,539 real (end, filed) pairs extracted from this
    project's own 163 cached EDGAR companyfacts give p50 8 days, p95 35, p99 73,
    max 744, with 2.07% exceeding 45 days. 90 dominates the 99th percentile and
    every statutory deadline an S&P 500 filer faces. IT DOES NOT REACH 100%:
    roughly 0.5% of filings (late filings and amendments) exceed it, and for
    those the panel would admit a count some days early. Bounded because the
    affected quantity is a slow-moving DENOMINATOR while the fast-moving
    NUMERATOR is exactly point-in-time.
  * Both panels are forward-filled STEP series — never interpolated, never
    back-filled — and refuse a value carried past a bounded staleness (45 days
    for short interest, i.e. three consecutive missed cycles; 400 days for
    share counts, imported from cross_sectional_buyback).
  * Formation-time look-ahead is structurally impossible regardless: both ride
    CrossSectionalData frames the harness slices to rows <= the formation date.

THE SPLIT GUARD, load-bearing for the ratio. FINRA reports short interest in
RAW shares as of the settlement date; SEC reports share counts in RAW shares as
of a cover date up to a quarter earlier. A split between them corrupts the ratio
by the split factor, and a 2:1 split HALVES it — pushing the name straight into
the low-short-interest LONG leg this family is testing. FINRA flags such cycles
itself (stockSplitFlag == 'S'; measured 348 flagged rows across 11 sampled
cycles of ~20,000 securities). Every flagged observation is REFUSED.

THE COVERAGE ASYMMETRY AND THE MASK THAT ANSWERS IT. The FINRA numerator needs
no ticker map — files are per-date snapshots keyed on the ticker as it existed
that day, so the current-day-map failure that lost XOM from the sibling EDGAR
families has no analogue. The SEC denominator DOES need one, and SEC's
current-day company_tickers.json resolves only 583 of the 691 universe names.
So the two normalizers do not naturally see the same cross-section. THIS FAMILY
MASKS BOTH PANELS TO THEIR COMMON CROSS-SECTION: a ticker-formation is ranked
only where BOTH quantities are computable, in every one of the 12 specs, so the
normalizer axis measures the normalizer rather than a universe difference. The
cost is paid by the days-to-cover half and is measured per run.

RESIDUAL SURVIVORSHIP, stated not hidden: the mask reimports the current-day
ticker-map bias into the whole family. The dropped names are overwhelmingly
index leavers — disproportionately the short/hedge leg's natural candidates —
so the surviving cross-section is better than the real one was. THIS FLATTERS
THE RESULTS.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE PRE-DECLARED GRID — AND THE THIN-SAMPLE REASONING BEHIND ITS SIZE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      normalizer {short_interest_ratio, days_to_cover}     2
    x holding period {21, 63, 126} trading days            3
    x portfolio {long_universe_hedged, long_short}         2
    = 12 definitions, n_trials = 12.

TWELVE, NOT MORE, AND THE REASONING IS THE THIN SAMPLE. ~8.7 years is roughly
2,180 trading days. Every spec is replayed on the WHOLE sample (they are
correlated variants, not disjoint sub-samples), so the thin-sample risk here is
not "each cell is empty" but SEARCH INFLATION: each extra definition raises the
expected maximum Sharpe of pure noise that the best spec must beat. Twelve
matches the sibling asset-growth family exactly, keeps the DSR denominator
honest without inflating it past what the sample can resolve, and — the actual
constraint — every one of the four axes below carries a real hypothesis. Two
defensible axes were CUT to stay at twelve, and are named here so their absence
cannot later look like an oversight:

  * RANK FRACTION IS FIXED AT 0.05 AND IS NOT SEARCHED. Section 1(b) gives the
    paper's three low-side cutoffs: 1st, 5th, 10th percentile. On a ~500-name
    point-in-time universe the 1st percentile is ~5 names, at this harness's
    DEFAULT_MIN_NAMES_PER_LEG floor — a handful of stock picks, not a
    cross-section. The 10th is a decile, not the tail the paper emphasizes. The
    5th is the one of the three that is both a genuine tail and a usable leg
    (~25 names), so it is fixed in advance. Searching all three and reporting
    the best would be a selection this sample cannot afford.
  * NO WITHIN-UNIVERSE TURNOVER SORT. Section 1(e): the paper's own "heavily
    traded" operationalization could not be verified from any source.
    Inventing a double sort and attributing it to the paper would be
    fabrication. See the disclosure below.

WHY THE NORMALIZER IS AN AXIS RATHER THAN A DECISION. Section 1(a) says the
paper's measure is short interest over shares outstanding, and this family
would simply use it — except that the most detailed replication found (a 2020
ZHAW bachelor thesis) reports the two normalizers give DIFFERENT ANSWERS: its
percent-of-shares-outstanding sorts produced significant low-short-interest
alpha while its days-to-cover sorts did not (intercepts insignificant, p =
0.468 and 0.221). A single thesis is weak evidence, but it is evidence that the
choice is load-bearing, and this project has already been burned once by fixing
a conditioning first and finding the confound afterwards (the raw-NOA family,
whose industry-neutral sibling had to be pre-declared retrospectively and carry
9 extra trials). Both normalizers are therefore pre-declared TOGETHER, under
ONE denominator, and neither may be reported without the other.

WHY BOTH PORTFOLIOS, AND WHICH ONE MATTERS. This candidate is specifically the
LONG-SIDE reading. The claim is not that a low-minus-high spread pays — the
heavily-shorted side is the long-known effect — it is that the LOW side ALONE
earns positive abnormal returns, "often larger" than the short side's. A
long_short spread cannot separate those: a spread driven entirely by its short
leg looks identical. long_universe_hedged (long the ranked leg, short an
equal-weighted basket of the whole eligible universe) isolates the long leg's
abnormal return against its own universe, which IS the paper's headline
quantity. Both are in the grid so the comparison can be made.

HOLDING PERIODS {21, 63, 126}. 21 is the paper's OWN monthly rebalance, and a
monthly hold is genuinely appropriate here for a reason no other family in this
project can claim: the ranking variable refreshes TWICE A MONTH, so a monthly
reformation trades on real new information rather than re-paying turnover on an
unchanged ranking (contrast cross_sectional_quality.py, which excludes 21
precisely because its variable refreshes annually). 126 is the ~6-month horizon
over which one source reports the long-side alpha persists. 63 is the house
middle.

THE "HEAVILY TRADED" CONDITIONING IS NOT REPLICATED — THE ONE DESIGN CALL A
HUMAN SHOULD REVIEW. Rather than invent a double sort, this family lets the
UNIVERSE carry that conditioning: the point-in-time S&P 500 is by construction
the most heavily traded segment of the US market. That is an honest
approximation and a WEAK one — a universe restriction is not a within-universe
sort and cannot reproduce a conditional result. A reader wanting the paper's
actual double sort needs its methodology section (JFE 96(1), around pp. 83-86
per one replication's page citation), which no free source served.

COSTS: 5 bps one-way flat on turnover (DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS), identical to every
S&P 500 equity family here so Sharpes stay comparable. financing_bps_per_year =
0.0 — this project's standing DISCLOSED optimism about short borrow, NOT an
estimate. IT BITES HARDER HERE THAN ANYWHERE ELSE IN THIS PROJECT: a family
whose entire subject is short selling is being charged zero borrow. The
long_universe_hedged specs short a broad index-like basket, genuinely cheap to
borrow; the long_short specs short the MOST heavily shorted names in the index,
exactly where borrow is expensive. Any positive long_short result must be read
with that in mind — a further reason the pass rule leans on the long side.

DSR: computed by this project's existing deflated_sharpe.py machinery
(Bailey & Lopez de Prado), n_trials = 12 on every row, no carried trials from
any other family — this is a fresh hypothesis from an independent literature and
all four axes were fixed together before any number was computed.

ONE DISCLOSED LIMITATION OF THE DSR MACHINERY AS USED HERE. The harness takes
exactly one fundamental_signal frame per call, so the two normalizer halves are
screened as two passes of 6 specs each. Both passes are given
n_trials_override=12 — the family's full pre-declared size — so the
multiple-comparisons denominator is honest. But sigma_SR, which the harness
estimates from the sibling Sharpes it can see, is necessarily the standard
deviation of 6 siblings within a pass, not 12 across the family. This is stated
here rather than discovered later; it is a limitation of the estimate's
stability, not of the denominator, and 6 is above deflated_sharpe's own
MIN_TRIALS_FOR_DSR floor of 5.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PASS / FAIL — FIXED NOW, APPLIED UNCHANGED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reported as a VALIDATED EDGE only if BOTH hold:

  (i)  the best spec's deflated Sharpe (DSR, n_trials = 12) clears 0.95; AND
  (ii) that spec is a long_universe_hedged spec, OR its long_universe_hedged
       counterpart at the same normalizer and holding period also clears a
       materially positive Sharpe.

Condition (ii) is what makes this the LONG-SIDE candidate it claims to be. A
long_short-only positive is consistent with the long-known heavily-shorted
effect and does NOT support this paper's distinctive claim; reporting one as a
confirmation of BHJ would be the same category error the sibling NOA family made
when it reported a sector bet as a quality effect.

ANYTHING ELSE IS AN HONEST NEGATIVE and gets written up as one, including the
case where some specs are positive but none clears the bar.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PRE-DECLARED DIAGNOSTICS — REPORTED, NEVER USED TO SELECT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are computed after the grid and reported whatever they show. None is a
spec axis; none can enlarge the search or change which spec is "best".

  * THE JANUARY SPLIT. Mean January vs mean non-January daily return for every
    one of the 12 specs, because section 2 records a claim that BHJ's long side
    is mainly a January effect. Pre-committed here so that a result concentrated
    in January cannot be reported as a general edge, and so that a null result
    cannot be rescued by a January sub-slice afterwards.
  * REALIZED PANEL COVERAGE: cycles resolved, observations used, every refusal
    reason and count, tickers never ranked, mean names ranked per formation, and
    the realized range of both ranking variables.
  * THE MASK'S COST: cells finite in the ratio panel only, in the days-to-cover
    panel only, and in both.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT A NEGATIVE WILL AND WILL NOT CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A negative here will NOT refute Boehmer/Huszar/Jordan. Their sort is a
broad-universe, ~4,400-stock, 18-year sort spanning NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ including
the micro-cap segment where one source says the effect concentrates. This is
~500 large caps over 8.7 years with ~25-name legs, in a post-publication window,
WITHOUT the paper's own verified trading-activity conditioning, and with the
sub-$5 population excluded by construction. Anomaly decay, absence in the
large-cap segment specifically, the missing conditioning, and simple lack of
power are ALL consistent with a null and this dataset cannot distinguish between
them.

What it will establish is the only question that matters here: not tradeable by
us, on our universe, now.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. FORWARD ACCUMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The literature review that produced this candidate flagged it as "best treated
as forward-accumulating rather than immediately backtestable" — the same
epistemic category as the shipped quality_cbop and quality_noa_industry_neutral
families. Whether any spec here is registered for forward validation is decided
AFTER the results exist, against the same standard those two were registered
under (DSR >= 0.5 as a screening floor, never as proof), and the decision — in
either direction — will be stated explicitly in the results document rather than
left as a silent omission. Registering nothing is a legitimate outcome and will
be said out loud if it is the one chosen.
