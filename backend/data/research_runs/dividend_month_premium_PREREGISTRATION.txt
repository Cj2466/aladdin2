============================================================================
0. WHAT IS BEING TESTED
============================================================================
Buying a firm BECAUSE THE CALENDAR SAYS IT IS DUE TO GO EX-DIVIDEND THIS
MONTH — before the dividend is declared, with no knowledge of whether it will
be declared or how large it will be — and shorting other point-in-time index
members against it.

H_DMP  A book that is long the point-in-time S&P 500 members predicted to go
       ex-dividend in the current calendar month, and short a declared
       comparison set of members that are not, earns a positive net Sharpe.

A null is a fully acceptable and (see section 8) a genuinely likely outcome.

2. THE DATA, AND THE THREE DEVIATIONS FROM [HS12] THAT COME WITH IT
============================================================================
DIVIDEND SOURCE: yfinance's corporate-actions feed, fetched once by
data/research_runs/fetch_dividend_calendar.py and cached to
data/dividend_month_ex_date_calendar.json. MEASURED, not assumed: 768
point-in-time candidate tickers requested, 625 resolved prices, 496 paid at
least one dividend, 24,798 ex-dates over 2012-01-03..2026-09-02.

The index of that feed IS the EX-DIVIDEND DATE, which is exactly the date
[HS12] keys on (correction #2 above). Verified live against the known AAPL
2014-08-07 ex-date and KO's quarterly ex-date cadence. Amounts are
SPLIT-ADJUSTED, on the same basis as Yahoo's split-adjusted-but-not-
dividend-adjusted Close, which is why the yield denominator in section 4 is
that Close and NOT the dividend-adjusted close every return in this project
is computed from. Getting that pairing wrong would overstate historical
yields by a per-ticker factor (0.448 for T on 2015-01-07, per this
provider's own measurement).

PRICES: total-return (dividend- and split-adjusted) closes, the same basis
every other equity family here uses. THIS IS NOT A STYLE CHOICE. On the
ex-day the price falls by roughly the dividend; a price-only series would
therefore hand the long leg a mechanical loss on precisely the day this
family is pointed at, and the whole result would be a known artifact of the
price basis. The module asserts the total-return frame is the one the replay
reads.

DEVIATION 1 — UNIVERSE. [HS12] uses all CRSP NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ common shares
(share code 10/11), excluding ADRs, units, closed-end funds and REITs. This
family uses the point-in-time S&P 500
(sp500_membership_history.get_universe_over / get_universe_as_of), a large-
cap subset two orders of magnitude smaller in the cross-section and NOT
share-code screened. ticker_universe.SCREENING_UNIVERSE (a present-day
snapshot) is NOT used and is not imported. Dates after
membership_coverage_end() are masked ALL-FALSE rather than filled with
today's roster.

DEVIATION 2 — FREQUENCY CLASSIFICATION. [HS12] reads a dividend's declared
frequency from CRSP's distribution code (third digit 3=quarterly,
4=semi-annual, 5=annual; 0 and 1 unknown, treated as quarterly; monthly
payers excluded). THIS PROJECT HAS NO CRSP. Frequency is therefore INFERRED,
point-in-time, from the count of the firm's own ex-dates in months t-12..t-1:
>=10 monthly (EXCLUDED, per the paper), 3-6 quarterly, 2 semi-annual, 1
annual, 0 non-payer. This is a genuine approximation of a field the paper
had and this family does not. Measured composition over the formation window
(firm-months): 57,228 quarterly, 1,726 semi-annual, 1,143 annual, 207
monthly, 8,144 non-payer — against the paper's own 89.07% quarterly /
1.49% semi-annual / 0.47% annual / 8.28% unknown. Three tickers are ever
classified monthly and thereby excluded: DOC, O, SLG.

DEVIATION 3 — VALUE WEIGHTING IS NOT REPRODUCED. [HS12] reports equal- and
value-weighted books side by side. This project has no cheap point-in-time
market cap for this universe (the machinery exists in cross_sectional_ivol
but needs share-count history this family does not fetch). The second
weighting axis here is DIVIDEND YIELD, not market cap (section 4). That is a
DEVIATION and is not presented as a replication of the paper's VW book.

RESIDUAL SURVIVORSHIP, DISCLOSED IN ADVANCE AND MEASURED, NOT FIXED: 143 of
the 768 point-in-time tickers resolved no yfinance price at all — the
acquired and the failed (WFM, UTX, XLNX, VIAB, WCG and 138 more). yfinance
sells no delisted history and a real delisted-securities feed is a known OPEN
paid-data item for this project. The sample is thinner and more survivor-
heavy than the real index was. DIRECTION, stated before any result: this is a
long-short book whose short leg is drawn from the same pool as its long leg,
so the missing names would have appeared on both sides at different times and
the net direction of the bias is NOT cleanly signable a priori.

3. THE PREDICTOR, AND ITS MEASURED ACCURACY
============================================================================
THE RULE IS THE PAPER'S OWN, quoted verbatim from [HS12] and re-grepped out
of the local copy of that PDF during this build:

    "We forecast using the following rule: a company has a 'predicted
     dividend' in month t if it paid a quarterly dividend in months t-3,
     t-6, t-9, or t-12, a semi-annual dividend in months t-6 or t-12, an
     annual dividend in months t-12, or a dividend of unknown frequency in
     months t-3, t-6, t-9, or t-12 (excluding the unknown dividends does not
     affect the results)."

Implemented exactly, with frequency inferred per DEVIATION 2. It is
point-in-time by construction: every input is an ex-date at least three
months old at the formation it justifies, so no prediction can read a
distribution that had not yet happened. The module asserts this rather than
trusting it.

MEASUREMENT A — MONTH LEVEL (2026-09-02, real ex-dates, formation window
2015-01..2026-06, NO returns computed). "Predicted" vs "the firm really had
an ex-date that month", over all firm-months:

   rule                                    precision   recall
   HS13 rule (t-3/6/9/12 by frequency)       0.793      0.956   <-- ADOPTED
   naive: ex-date in month t-12 only         0.919      0.909
   t-12 AND t-24                             0.936      0.840
   t-12 OR t-24                              0.869      0.927
   t-3 only (pure quarterly projection)      0.875      0.873
   t-3 AND t-12                              0.966      0.822
   t-12 +/- 1 month                          0.338      0.974

THE ADOPTED RULE IS NOT THE BEST-SCORING RULE ON THIS DATA, and that is
recorded here deliberately rather than discovered later. Four of the six
alternatives have higher precision; the naive t-12 rule beats it on precision
by 12.6 points at a cost of 4.7 points of recall. THE PAPER'S RULE IS ADOPTED
ANYWAY, because this family's job is to test [HS13]'s claim on this universe,
not to find the best dividend-month forecaster this project can build. Tuning
the predictor against a precision score measured on the same sample the
returns come from is one step away from tuning it against the returns
themselves. The alternatives are reported so that a later reader can see the
choice was made in the open; NONE of them will be traded, and if the adopted
rule produces a negative result, "a sharper predictor might have worked" is a
LIMITATION to be written down, not a second run to be made.

Realized leg sizes under the adopted rule: long leg mean 173 names/month
(min 92, max 236); the 'within companies' short pool mean 262 (min 204, max
362). Predicted months containing no actual ex-date: 20.7%.

MEASUREMENT B — DAY LEVEL, for the 'to_ex' window only (section 4, D3). The
predicted ex-DAY is the most recent qualifying source ex-date projected
forward by a WHOLE NUMBER OF WEEKS: 91 calendar days per 3 months of lag (13
weeks), which preserves day of week. Signed error is (actual - predicted) in
calendar days against the nearest actual ex-date by the same firm:

   projection            outside target month   medAbs  meanAbs  meanSgn   +/-0    +/-1    +/-3
   91-day multiples             4.6%              1.0     2.18    +0.35   46.3%   69.2%   79.3%
   30-day multiples             7.2%              1.0     2.88    +1.41    7.1%   50.8%   74.5%

The week-multiple hypothesis is CONFIRMED EMPIRICALLY, not assumed: the
91-day projection hits the exact day 46.3% of the time against 7.1% for
calendar-month arithmetic. This is the same day-of-week-preservation effect
this project already measured for scheduled earnings filings, and 91/182/273/
364 are all multiples of 7 for the same reason. A predicted ex-day that lands
outside its target month (4.6%) DEGRADES to that month's last trading day,
i.e. that name's 'to_ex' window becomes its 'month' window; this is counted
and reported, never silently dropped.

MEASUREMENT B IS FROZEN BY THIS DOCUMENT. The 91-day-multiple projection was
chosen on DATES ALONE with no return in sight and will not be re-tuned
against returns.

4. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 12 SPECS, FROZEN
============================================================================
Three dimensions, fully crossed. This grid is frozen as of this document.

  D1  short leg    3 levels — THE PAPER'S OWN THREE SHORT PORTFOLIOS, from
                   [HS12] Table III, not three variants invented here:
      "between"    every other point-in-time member not predicted to go ex
                   this month, INCLUDING firms that never pay. The paper's
                   'between companies' comparison.
      "within"     ONLY firms that had an ex-date in the last 12 months but
                   are not predicted to have one this month. The paper's
                   'within companies' comparison and ITS OWN HEADLINE RISK
                   TEST: a quarterly payer is long 4 months a year and short
                   the other 8, so fixed factor loadings cancel. [HS12]:
                   "any fixed loadings on risk factors will tend to cancel
                   out, making systematic risk a less likely explanation."
      "one_after"  ONLY firms exactly one month AFTER a predicted dividend
                   (the paper's third comparison). Under the price-pressure
                   mechanism this is the sharpest test, because the short leg
                   sits in the reversal window.
  D2  weighting    2 levels:
      "equal"      the harness's own equal mode; the paper's EW book.
      "yield"      weight PROPORTIONAL to the paper's own dividend-yield
                   measure — "the average from the previous 12 months of
                   dividends payment (in months that included a dividend),
                   divided by" price, quoted verbatim from [HS12], computed
                   against the split-adjusted (not dividend-adjusted) close
                   at formation. Theory-implied rather than generic: [HS12]
                   reports that a one standard deviation increase in dividend
                   yield adds 26.2bp to the interim-period return and 4.5bp
                   to the ex-day return. It is a MAGNITUDE, so no past return
                   sign ever reaches the weights. IT IS NOT THE PAPER'S VW
                   BOOK — see DEVIATION 3.
  D3  window       2 levels:
      "month"      the paper's own unit. On at the close of the last trading
                   day of month t-1, off at the close of the last trading day
                   of month t.
      "toex"       on at the same close, off at the close of the PREDICTED
                   ex-day (section 3 measurement B). Captures the run-up and
                   exits before the reversal.

  3 x 2 x 2 = 12 = DMP_N_TRIALS, asserted in code against the built list and
  used as the DSR n_trials denominator. 12 >= deflated_sharpe.MIN_TRIALS_FOR_DSR (5).

  pattern_id format: dmp_{between|within|one_after}_{equal|yield}_{month|toex}

  PRE-DECLARED PREDICTION ON D3, written before any return exists: under the
  price-pressure mechanism, "toex" should BEAT "month" for the same D1/D2,
  because [HS12]'s own daily decomposition puts +54.2bp of run-up from
  declaration to ex-day and -73.2bp in the 40 days after it, part of which
  falls inside the same calendar month. If "month" beats "toex" across the
  grid, whatever is being measured is not the run-up the mechanism describes.
  This is recorded now so that neither outcome can be presented as a
  discovery afterwards.

THE SHORT LEG IS ALWAYS EQUAL-WEIGHTED whatever D2 says, and always runs the
FULL calendar month whatever D3 says. It is a comparison pool, not a ranked
leg; cross_sectional.py's own rule for its universe hedge is reused rather
than re-decided — "there is no rank cutoff for 'the whole eligible universe'
to weight members' distance from."

WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED, AND IS FIXED NOW AT ONE VALUE EACH:
  forecast rule            [HS12]'s own (section 3); alternatives measured,
                           reported, never traded
  predicted-ex-day lag     91-day multiples (section 3 measurement B)
  price screen             >= $5.00 at the formation close — [HS12]'s own
                           screen ("we also exclude shares with prices less
                           than $5 in the previous month"). On this universe
                           it should almost never bind; how often it binds is
                           MEASURED and reported rather than assumed.
  monthly payers           EXCLUDED — [HS12]'s own exclusion
  min prior ex-dates       4, applied UNIFORMLY to all twelve specs so every
                           spec trades the IDENTICAL population and differs
                           only in short leg, weighting and window. Without
                           that uniformity the sigma_SR feeding the DSR would
                           measure universe differences rather than spec
                           differences. Asserted by a test.
  yield basis              trailing 12 months, mean over months that had a
                           dividend, over the split-adjusted close
  delisting imputation     ON (Shumway, via the harness's own
                           _compute_delisting_positions)
  formation cadence        the last trading day of each calendar month

5. COST MODEL
============================================================================
Cost is charged as ONE-WAY bps on the L1 turnover of the NET book every day.
The ladder is adopted VERBATIM from this project's own sourced calibration in
data/research_runs/edge_cost_reaudit_corrected_PREREGISTRATION.txt section 2
— it was NOT re-derived here:

   0.0bp  no-cost reference, to separate a gross null from a cost kill
   1.0bp  2015-regime tight bound
   2.0bp  BEST ESTIMATE for an equal-weighted S&P 500 book across 2015-2026
   3.5bp  conservative bound (vol spikes, departed-member illiquidity skew)
   5.0bp  = cross_sectional.DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS, the project's existing
          control rate — AND THIS FAMILY'S DECLARED HEADLINE.

NOTED IN ADVANCE, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE PLACE THIS FAMILY IS STRUCTURALLY
BETTER OFF THAN ITS NEAREST SIBLING: this is a MONTHLY-rebalanced book, not
the every-few-days rotation the earnings-announcement family runs. Cost
should NOT be the assumption that decides the verdict here, and if it turns
out to be, that is itself a finding about the "toex" window rather than about
the premium. The full ladder is reported per spec either way.

Financing: 0.0bp/yr — the equity families' shared convention, carrying the
SAME DISCLOSED OPTIMISM documented in cross_sectional.py: this project has no
sourced securities-borrow feed (a known OPEN paid-data item).

NOT MODELLED, declared in advance: market impact, commissions, borrow. True
costs are HIGHER than every column of the ladder.

6. PRE-DECLARED DIAGNOSTICS (reported always; NOT specs; NOT in n_trials)
============================================================================
 1. THE EX-DAY EVENT STUDY — THE MECHANISM TEST, and the reason this family
    is worth running even if the portfolio is a null. Mean universe-hedged
    daily return in event time -20..+40 trading days around every ACTUAL
    ex-date in the formation window, cumulated.
    PRE-DECLARED READING, fixed here: price pressure predicts a POSITIVE
    run-up into day 0 and a NEGATIVE drift over +1..+40 that offsets it.
    [HS12]'s own characteristic-adjusted numbers, which this is compared
    against and does not attempt to reproduce exactly (different universe,
    different sample, no DGTW matching here): +54.2bp total from declaration
    to ex-day (21.6% declaration day, 29.2% interim, 49.1% ex-day) and
    -73.2bp over the 40 days after the ex-day, which the authors describe as
    "large enough to offset the gains during the dividend month".
    IF THERE IS NO REVERSAL IN THIS SAMPLE, that is evidence against the
    price-pressure mechanism as described operating here, and it is to be
    reported as such — including in the case where the portfolio specs are
    positive, which would then be a positive result whose stated mechanism is
    unsupported. Computed with hindsight on ACTUAL ex-dates; it is a
    diagnostic and is never consulted in position formation.
 2. THE PLACEBO CALENDAR — the falsification control. The identical machinery
    with every predicted month shifted +1 month, which for a quarterly payer
    lands in a month it is NOT due to go ex. PRE-DECLARED READING: if the
    placebo earns what the real book earns, the family is reported as a null
    regardless of its headline Sharpe. THE PLACEBO IS A VETO, NOT A TIEBREAK.
    Its own realized "caught an actual ex-date" fraction is reported as proof
    the shift worked.
 3. caught_actual_fraction, per spec: the share of traded firm-months that
    actually contained an ex-date. Computed with hindsight, REPORTING ONLY,
    never consulted in formation — an ex-ante family cannot know it missed.
 4. The full cost ladder of section 5, per spec.
 5. Gross (0bp) Sharpe alongside net, per spec, so a cost kill is
    distinguishable from a gross null.
 6. Subperiod Sharpes over three equal contiguous thirds, per spec.
 7. Long- and short-leg size distributions and the count of one-sided days.
 8. How often the $5 price screen and the outside-month ex-day degradation
    actually bind.

7. THE DECISION RULE — FIXED BEFORE THE RESULT
============================================================================
Applied to the BEST DSR across the twelve specs at the 5.0bp headline:

   (i)  DSR >= 0.95, the bar this project's other 12-spec, literature-
        nominated family in this same wave (asset_growth, 2026-09-01) used
        and the strict end of this project's stated ~0.90-0.95 band; AND
   (ii) that spec's 'within'-short-leg counterpart, at the same weighting and
        window, is also materially positive.

CONDITION (ii) IS THE LOAD-BEARING ONE and it is not a formality. Dividend
payers differ from non-payers in ways [HS12] itself documents — "dividend-
paying stocks have larger market capitalization, and a higher book-to-market
ratio" — so a book that is long payers-in-their-month and short EVERYTHING
ELSE ('between') carries a permanent payer-vs-non-payer tilt. A positive that
appears only under 'between' is a dividend-PAYER result, not a dividend-MONTH
result, and cannot be reported as this family's finding. This is the exact
structural analogue of the industry-neutral half of the asset_growth grid,
and of [HS12]'s own reason for building the 'within companies' portfolio.

A spec that clears on DSR but fails the placebo control (diagnostic 2) is NOT
reported as a pass.

Reporting bands for everything below the bar, unchanged from this project's
existing convention: 0.50 <= DSR < 0.95 possibly interesting but short of the
standard; DSR < 0.50 an honest negative.

8. WHAT WOULD MAKE A POSITIVE RESULT HERE FAKE, AND WHY A NULL IS LIKELY
============================================================================
 1. THE UNIVERSE IS THE WRONG ONE FOR THIS EFFECT, and the paper says so
    itself. [HS12]'s cross-sectional evidence is that ILLIQUID stocks show
    larger run-ups AND larger reversals — a one standard deviation move in
    their liquidity measure changes interim returns by 7.0bp, ex-day returns
    by 4.8bp and the 40-day post-period by 11.7bp. The S&P 500 is the most
    liquid large-cap segment in the world, i.e. precisely where a price-
    pressure effect should be SMALLEST. Their own value-weighted alphas are
    below their equal-weighted ones (+29.2bp vs +37.4bp within-companies),
    which points the same way. This is the single strongest reason to expect
    little or nothing here, and it is a reason about the UNIVERSE, not about
    the paper.
 2. POST-PUBLICATION DECAY — MEASURED ON THIRD-PARTY DATA, NOT ASSUMED, AND
    THE SINGLE MOST DECISION-RELEVANT NUMBER IN THIS DOCUMENT. No published
    US retest of this signal was located (searched specifically; no rebuttal
    or comment paper exists as far as this build could establish). But an
    open-source replication of it does: Chen & Zimmermann's Open Source Asset
    Pricing project publishes monthly long-short returns for 212 predictors,
    including their "DivSeason" implementation of exactly [HS12]'s forecast

    because it sets the quantitative expectation: a BROAD-UNIVERSE,
    EQUAL-WEIGHTED, GROSS-OF-COSTS implementation of this exact rule earns an
    annualized Sharpe of about 0.62 over 2015-2024 with thousands of names
    per leg. This family runs the same rule on ~170 long names against ~260
    short, in the most liquid large-cap segment there is (reason 1 above),

    is written down before any number of this family's own exists.

    THE DECAY IS CONTESTED, NOT SETTLED, and the disagreement is recorded
    rather than resolved in the direction that suits a null. The AUTHORS'
    OWN updated replication (Hartzmark & Solomon, Annual Review of Financial
    Economics 10, 2018, pp. 499-517, retrieved and read) re-runs the strategy
    "including the most recent data" VALUE-WEIGHTED and finds essentially no
    decay: raw 0.276 (t=7.25), FF4 0.282 (t=7.14), FF5 0.332 (t=7.35). The
    most likely reconciliation is equal- versus value-weighting plus sample
    window, and this build could NOT resolve it. Both readings are on the
    table.
    Separately, Ainsworth & Nicholson's 11-country study (Datastream,
    1993-2013, retrieved and read) is a genuinely modern out-of-sample US
    window and it CONFIRMS: +75bp/month four-factor alpha against other
    dividend payers, larger than [HS12]'s own 37bp. It also finds the effect
    significantly NEGATIVE in Australia and insignificant in Japan, Hong
    Kong, Italy and New Zealand — so international out-of-sample is mixed,
    not uniformly supportive.
    McLean & Pontiff (JF 71(1), 2016) measure returns "58% lower
    post-publication" across 97 predictors; that is GENERAL, is not specific
    to this signal, and is used only as background now that a signal-specific
    measurement exists.
 3. THE MAGNITUDE IS SMALL RELATIVE TO THE NOISE THIS SAMPLE CARRIES. The
    paper's within-companies EW alpha is 37.4bp/month on a cross-section of
    thousands. This is ~170 long names against ~260 short over 11.5 years.
 4. THE FREQUENCY CLASSIFIER IS INFERRED, NOT READ (DEVIATION 2). A
    misclassified firm is predicted in the wrong months, which dilutes both
    legs.
 5. 20.7% OF PREDICTED MONTHS CONTAIN NO ACTUAL EX-DATE. Every Sharpe below
    is diluted by exactly that miss rate; caught_actual_fraction reports the
    realized dilution per spec.
 6. NO RISK-FACTOR CONTROL. [HS12] reports FOUR-FACTOR ALPHAS. This family
    reports raw long-short Sharpes with no factor adjustment at all, because
    this project's harness does not run factor regressions. A positive here
    is therefore NOT an alpha and must not be described as one. Condition
    (ii) of section 7 is a structural substitute for factor neutrality, not
    an equivalent of it.
 7. OVERLAPPING AND CLUSTERED RETURNS. Ex-dates cluster hard into the
    Feb/Mar, May/Jun, Aug/Sep and Nov/Dec pairs (measured: ~2,000 ex-dates
    per heavy month against ~930 in Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct), so the long leg's
    composition swings seasonally and consecutive daily returns share most of
    their constituents. The daily observation count feeding the Sharpe and
    the DSR OVERSTATES the independent information. This is a SEPARATE
    caution from the n_trials correction and neither substitutes for the
    other.
 8. TRIAL COUNT. n_trials = 12 covers only THIS grid. It does not cover the
    literature scan that nominated this family, nor the other families this
    project has screened. Every DSR reported is an UPPER BOUND on the honest
    one.
 9. OVERLAP WITH earnings_announcement_premium. Both families are "long a
    predictable corporate event, short the rest of the index", and both
    predict the event from the firm's own past calendar. They are NOT the
    same signal — quarterly earnings and quarterly ex-dates are different
    dates for most firms — but they are not independent either, and the EAP
    family already returned an honest negative on this same universe over
    substantially the same window. That prior applies here.

9. STOPPING RULES

 * The 12-spec grid in section 4 is FROZEN. If, after seeing results, a
   further spec looks attractive, the temptation is to be DOCUMENTED IN THE
   RUN REPORT and not acted on.
 * The forecast rule is FROZEN at [HS12]'s own, including against the six
   alternatives already measured — four of which score better on precision.
   It will not be swapped after seeing returns.
 * The predicted-ex-day projection is FROZEN at 91-day multiples by section
   3's date-only measurement.
 * The headline cost is FROZEN at 5.0bp.
 * n_trials will NOT be shrunk after the fact on any argument that some specs
   are near-mirrors of each other.
 * If the placebo control earns what the real book earns, the family is
   reported as a null regardless of its Sharpe.
 * The ex-day event study is reported WHATEVER IT SHOWS, including the case
   where it contradicts the price-pressure mechanism while the portfolio
   specs look positive.
 * An honest negative is a complete and acceptable result. No spec will be
   added, no window widened, and no cost lowered to avoid one.
