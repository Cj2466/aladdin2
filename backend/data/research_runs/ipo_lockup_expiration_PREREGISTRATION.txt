2. MECHANISM HONESTY — WHAT KIND OF CANDIDATE THIS ACTUALLY IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS NEITHER A MECHANICAL FORCED-FLOW TEST NOR AN INFORMED-POSITIONING
TEST, AND IT WILL NOT BE WRITTEN UP AS EITHER. Both boxes this session's other
candidates used are wrong here, and the source paper's own authors say so.

WHAT IS MECHANICAL ABOUT IT: the date. [FH01] p.471 verbatim — "The terms of
the lockup, including the expiration or 'unlock' date, are disclosed in the IPO
prospectus." The event is scheduled, public, and knowable at the IPO. There is
no forecasting step and no look-ahead risk in the signal itself.

WHAT IS NOT MECHANICAL ABOUT IT: the flow. Nobody is forced to sell. Insiders
CHOOSE whether to use the permission the expiry grants them. [FH01] p.471:
"On the unlock day, insiders are suddenly allowed to sell up to the volume
limits of Rule 144, potentially tripling the public float". Allowed, not
required. This is unlike SEC N-PORT flow-induced trading or a margin-call
liquidation, where the seller has no choice.

WHAT THE AUTHORS THEMSELVES SAY ABOUT THE CAUSE, verbatim, abstract p.471:
"We find limited support for several hypotheses that may explain the abnormal
return, but no complete explanation." Section VI, p.495-496, spells out what
survived and what did not: downward-sloping demand curves get partial support
(the abnormal return is more negative when volume is abnormally high) but
cannot be the whole story, because "we also find a highly significant negative
abnormal return in those cases where the total volume around the unlock day is
less than one percent of the public float" (p.496); worse-than-expected insider
sales get partial support but also cannot be the whole story, because "the
abnormal return remains highly significant when there are no reported insider
sales" (p.496). They explicitly reject the temporary-price-pressure hypothesis
(§III.B p.487: "the abnormal return around the unlock day seems to be
permanent... Thus, we reject the price pressure hypothesis") and the
trades-at-the-bid and trading-cost hypotheses (§III.A p.486, §III.C p.488-489).

THE HONEST ONE-LINE CHARACTERISATION, WHICH IS THE ONE THAT GOES IN THE REPORT:
a PRE-SCHEDULED, PUBLICLY-DISCLOSED SUPPLY-SHOCK RISK WITH A DOCUMENTED BUT
INCOMPLETELY UNDERSTOOD PRICE EFFECT.

AND THE PART THAT MATTERS MOST FOR THIS PROJECT, WHICH THE SOURCE STATES ABOUT
ITS OWN RESULT AND WHICH IS THEREFORE THE HONEST PRIOR RATHER THAN A FINDING OF
THIS BUILD. [FH01] p.472 verbatim: "The abnormal returns around the unlock day
are not large enough to provide short-term profits for traders who must
transact at the bid and ask." And §III.B p.487-488 verbatim: "Figure 4 shows
that the bid and ask prices do not fall far enough to reward the strategy of
selling short at the bid price before the unlock day and then covering at the
ask price after the unlock day. This conclusion also holds in the subsample of
firms that are venture financed."

THE SOURCE PAPER SAYS THIS TRADE DOES NOT WORK NET OF THE SPREAD, ON ITS OWN
SAMPLE, IN ITS OWN ERA. That is not a reason to skip the test — the paper's
1988-1997 Nasdaq spreads (§III.C p.488-489: quoted RELATIVE spread "about 3.35
percent" widening to "about 3.45 percent") are an order of magnitude wider than
anything a 2012-2019 sample faces, so the arithmetic could in principle come
out differently now. But it IS the reason the prior in section 12 is as low as
it is, and it is the reason the cost model in section 9 is the centrepiece of
this build rather than a footnote.

    "We measure the abnormal return as the raw return deflated by the return on
    the CRSP value weighted index (all returns are measured inclusive of
    dividends). We calculate the three-day abnormal return for each firm as

                        +1
        CAR_i =  [  PROD    (1 + R_i,t) / (1 + R_m,t)  ]  -  1            (1)
                       t=-1

    where R_i,t is the simple return on firm i on day t relative to the unlock
    day, and R_m,t is the simple return on the CRSP value weighted market

    "where V_i,T is the trading volume (from CRSP) for firm i on day T. Most

5.3 THE LOCKUP-DATE PROXY. lockup_calendar_date = offer date + 180 CALENDAR
    days; day 0 ("the unlock day") = the FIRST TRADING DAY ON OR AFTER that
    date, because a lockup expiring on a non-trading day first permits selling
    on the next session.

    DEFENSIBLE ON [FH01]'s OWN TABLE I (3.5): 80% of its full sample and 91% of
    its 1996 cohort are exactly 180 days. NOT VERIFIED FOR 2012-2019 — [FEAS]
    Q2 established that SEC EDGAR full-text search could in principle refine
    this but that exact-phrase search under-counts badly (47 hits for "lock-up
    period of 180 days" across 424B4 filings in ALL years since 2001, against
    ~150-200 non-SPAC IPOs per year), so a real per-company extraction needs a
    broad multi-phrasing NLP pass. That is not attempted here. The 180-day
    default is used and the gap is disclosed, in both directions: an incorrect
    unlock date attenuates the measured effect toward zero (it points the trade
    at an ordinary day), so this proxy CANNOT manufacture a positive result,
    only hide a real one.

    THE PROXY IS NOT TAKEN ON FAITH EITHER. F2 and F10 below are two
    independent, falsifiable tests of whether it lands on real unlock events.

5.4 IDENTITY RESOLUTION — THE NON-NEGOTIABLE PART, AND A CORRECTION TO WHAT
    [FEAS] AND THE COMMISSIONING BRIEF RECOMMENDED.

    [FEAS] Q3 and the commissioning brief both recommend resolving identity by
    CIK via SEC's free company_tickers.json, or by verified company-name match.
    THAT IS NOT SUFFICIENT, AND THIS BUILD ESTABLISHED THAT DIRECTLY, LIVE,
    ON A ROW FROM ITS OWN UNIVERSE, BEFORE WRITING ANY SIGNAL CODE:

      ConnectOne Bancorp Inc, ticker CNOB, Ritter offer date 2013-02-11.
      SEC's company_tickers.json (fetched live 2026-09-06, 10,415 entries)
      maps CNOB -> CIK 712771, title "ConnectOne Bancorp, Inc." A name match
      against Ritter's "ConnectOne Bancorp Inc" SUCCEEDS. It is WRONG. CIK
      712771 is CENTER BANCORP's registrant, filing since the 1980s; the 2013
      IPO entity was CIK 1462694. Independently confirmed: Center Bancorp
      (Nasdaq: CNBC) and ConnectOne Bancorp (Nasdaq: CNOB) merged effective
      2014-07-01 with Center Bancorp as the surviving registrant, which then
      renamed itself ConnectOne Bancorp and kept the CNOB symbol (SEC 8-K
      filings under CIK 0000712771; company press releases dated 2014-01-21
      and 2014-06-24). This project's own market-data provider returns CNOB
      price rows from 2012-07-02 onward — i.e. CENTER BANCORP's history, seven
      months BEFORE the ConnectOne IPO this row describes.

      A CIK-plus-name join would have accepted that row and silently attributed
      a different, older, larger bank's price series to a 2013 IPO's lockup
      expiration. This is exactly the manufactured false positive CLAUDE.md's
      zero-fabrication rule exists to prevent, and CIK resolution alone does
      not stop it.

    THE PROTOCOL THAT IS ACTUALLY USED. Three gates, all applied to EVERY row
    of the 1,205-row universe, all counted and reported (F8):

      G1  SEC CIK CROSS-CHECK (informational, never sufficient on its own).
          Look the Ritter ticker up in the committed snapshot of
          https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers.json. Record one of:
          `cik_name_match` (SEC title matches the Ritter name after
          normalisation), `cik_name_mismatch` (SEC maps the ticker to a
          DIFFERENT company today — e.g. P -> "Everpure, Inc.", verified live,
          where Pandora Media held P at its own IPO), or `cik_absent` (no
          current filer holds that ticker — delisted, acquired, or renamed).
          G1 EXCLUDES NOTHING BY ITSELF. `cik_absent` names can still have
          perfectly good historical price data ([FEAS] verified this for
          Foundation Medicine and Hortonworks), and `cik_name_match` can be
          wrong (CNOB, above).

      G2  FIRST-TRADE ANCHOR (THE LOAD-BEARING GATE, applied to every row).
          Prices are requested from 6 CALENDAR MONTHS BEFORE the offer date, so
          that any earlier holder of the symbol is visible. The row is ACCEPTED
          only if the first available price observation in that window falls in
          [offer_date, offer_date + 7 calendar days]. Any observation before
          the offer date REJECTS the row.

          THE BAND IS NOT ARBITRARY AND WAS MEASURED BEFORE BEING FIXED. Over
          the 2013 cohort (193 universe rows, 65 with any price data), the
          distribution of (first observation - offer date) in days is:
          0 -> 38 rows, +1 -> 25 rows, and exactly two outliers, -224 (CNOB)
          and -507 (NVGS, Navigator Holdings). Nothing else. The 0/+1 split
          independently reproduces Ritter's own documentation, which says the
          offer date is "sometimes the first day of trading, and sometimes the
          day before the first day of trading". The +7 upper bound is slack for
          holidays; the measured data uses none of it.

          THE GATE IS CONSERVATIVE BY DESIGN. NVGS is probably NOT a recycled
          ticker — Navigator Holdings appears to have been quoted before its
          2013 NYSE listing, and SEC maps NVGS to CIK 1581804, its own
          registrant. It is rejected anyway, because an IPO-date-anchored event
          study cannot safely use a name whose price series predates its own
          IPO. The gate converts SILENT MISATTRIBUTION into HONEST MISSINGNESS,
          and errs toward missingness.

      G3  SECURITY-TYPE AND NAME CHECK at fetch time, via yfinance's own
          `.info` (longName / shortName / quoteType), applied to every row that
          PASSES G2 and whose G1 status is NOT `cik_name_match` — i.e. exactly
          the rows where the CIK map cannot vouch for the symbol. A resolved
          security whose quoteType is not EQUITY, or whose name bears no
          relation to the Ritter name, is REJECTED. Additionally a random audit
          sample of `cik_name_match` rows is checked the same way and the audit
          result is reported, because CNOB proves that bucket is not clean
          either.

    ALL THREE GATES' OUTCOMES ARE COUNTED AND REPORTED. A build that silently
    dropped rows would be indistinguishable from one that had no gate at all.

5.5 PENNY-STOCK EXCLUSION. [FH01] §I.A excludes 300 "penny stocks with a price
    below five dollars", measured at the offer. Ritter's file carries no offer
    price. This build instead requires the AS-TRADED close on day -6 (the last
    day before the widest event window opens) to be >= $5.00. As-traded, not
    adjusted: [FEAS] documented that Yahoo retroactively split-adjusts Close
    for all history regardless of the auto_adjust flag, so an adjusted level is
    not a price (Beyond Meat's 2019 window shows ~$2,400/share after its 2026
    1-for-30 reverse split). The as-traded series comes from this project's own
    PriceStore, which stores as-traded rows by construction. The filter is
    TRADEABLE — day -6 is known before the day -5 entry — so it introduces no
    look-ahead. It is a DEVIATION in timing from the paper's offer-price rule
    and is listed as D5.

5.6 WHAT THE FREE PATH CANNOT DO, listed so its absence is not mistaken for a
    decision. [FH01]'s exclusions for CARVEOUTS (403 firms), EARNINGS
    ANNOUNCEMENTS within three days of expiration (355 firms) and SEASONED
    EQUITY OFFERS before or within two weeks after the unlock day (331 firms)
    are NOT implemented — no free point-in-time source covers 1,205 small-cap
    names over 2012-2019 at acceptable cost. Direction of the omission: all
    three add unrelated variance around the event date. The paper itself says
    (p.472) "unlock days typically do not coincide with confounding events like
    earnings announcements". Unmodelled noise around the event widens the CAR
    distribution and REDUCES measured Sharpe; it does not create a systematic
    negative CAR. Recorded as D4.

6. SURVIVORSHIP BIAS — FIRST-CLASS, MEASURED, AND OF UNKNOWN DIRECTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS THE LARGEST KNOWN THREAT TO THIS FAMILY'S VALIDITY AND IT IS STATED
HERE, IN THE PRE-REGISTRATION, RATHER THAN IN A FOOTNOTE OF THE RESULT.

WHAT [FEAS] MEASURED. Every one of 6 independently verified delisted/acquired
IPO companies (LinkedIn, Fitbit, Zynga, Pandora Media, The Container Store,
Cloudera) returned ZERO price rows around its own historical lockup window — a
0% hit rate on the highest-confidence "known bad" anchor set. An UNBIASED,
systematically drawn set of 37 ordinary 2012-2019 IPOs hit at 40.5%. Split by
whether the ticker resolves to any data in the last 30 days, the hit rate was
64.3% ("still trading") versus 10.7% ("not") — a ~6x gap, which is the direct
measured signature of the bias.

WHAT THIS BUILD'S OWN FIRST PROBE MEASURED, on the filtered universe rather
than on a sample: of the 193 universe rows with 2013 offer dates, 65 (33.7%)
returned any price data at all over a 2.5-year window.

THE DIRECTION OF THE BIAS IS UNKNOWN AND IS NOT GUESSED HERE. [FH01] Table III
p.481 shows firms whose price had RISEN since the IPO carry a LARGER negative
CAR (-1.7%, n=1,307) than firms whose price had FALLEN (-1.2%, n=641), which
establishes that price trajectory and CAR magnitude are related but says
NOTHING about which trajectory characterises the firms this project's free data
path loses — establishing that would need exactly the history the gap makes
unavailable. Two plausible stories point opposite ways and neither is testable
here: (a) failures delist, failures fell, fallen firms have SMALLER negative
CARs, so losing them biases the measured effect MORE negative (flattering the
trade); (b) survivors are the firms with continuing institutional sponsorship
and deeper liquidity, whose unlock is better absorbed, so losing the others
biases the measured effect LESS negative (penalising the trade). THE BUILD
TREATS THE SIGN AS UNKNOWN.

THE OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE, FIXED HERE BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS. If this
family's DSR CLEARS the bar, that is to be treated as MORE reason for scrutiny,
not less, and the verdict paragraph must say so in those words. A survivorship-
damaged sample of unknown direction can produce a spuriously good number as
easily as a spuriously bad one, and the asymmetry of this project's loss
function (a false positive is the worst outcome; an honest negative is a fine
one) means a pass here would be an UNRESOLVED-at-best result pending a
delisted-inclusive replication, which needs the paid data in gap P2. This is
stated now so it cannot be read as retrospective hedging later.

7. THE TRADEABLE OBJECT, AND THE TWO RETURN STREAMS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 THE TRADE. For each accepted event i with unlock day d0 and window
    [d0+a, d0+1] (a = -1 or -5): enter at the CLOSE of trading day d0+a-1,
    SHORT one unit of notional in the stock, and (in the `spy` benchmark arm)
    simultaneously LONG one unit of notional in SPY. Exit both legs at the
    CLOSE of trading day d0+1. Nothing is rebalanced in between. Every date is
    known months in advance from the offer date alone, so there is no
    forecasting step and no path by which a future return can influence the
    position.

    THE EXPECTED SIGN IS POSITIVE. [FH01]'s CAR is negative; a short earns it.

    UNIT NOTIONAL PER EVENT IS DELIBERATE AND HAS NO FREE PARAMETER. Scaling
    every event by a common capital base K divides both the mean and the
    standard deviation of the resulting return stream by K, leaving the Sharpe
    ratio — and therefore the DSR and the verdict — exactly unchanged. K is set
    to 1 so that no such parameter exists to be tuned. The consequence is that
    the reported "return" is per unit of short-leg notional, not per unit of
    a portfolio's equity; that is a units statement, not an economic claim, and
    the report says so.

7.2 STREAM A — `daily`, THE CALENDAR-TIME PORTFOLIO. For every trading day t
    in the replay window,

      r_daily(t) = SUM over events i active on day t of
                     [ -(R_i,t - R_m,t) ] - costs charged on day t

    where R_m,t is the SPY total return (or 0 in the `raw` arm), and "active"
    means t lies inside i's window. Days with no active event contribute 0.0.
    periods_per_year = 252.

    THIS IS NOT [FH01]'s STATISTIC and is not attributed to them. It is the
    standard way to keep OVERLAPPING event windows from being counted as
    independent draws: on any day where several events overlap, their returns
    are added inside one observation rather than being treated as separate
    observations, so cross-sectional correlation on that day is inside the
    observation instead of inflating the count. It is also what an actual
    implementation would hold. It is declared here as this build's own
    construction choice, with its reason, rather than cited to a paper this
    session has not read.

7.3 STREAM B — `event`, THE EVENT-CLOCK PORTFOLIO. One observation per DISTINCT
    UNLOCK DATE: the sum, over all events unlocking on that date, of the
    complete window trade return

      trade_i = -CAR_i - costs_i,     CAR_i by Equation (1)

    ordered by unlock date. periods_per_year = (number of observations) /
    (years spanned), computed from the realised data rather than assumed.

7.4 WHY BOTH, AND WHAT THE VERDICT READS OFF — THIS IS THE ANSWER TO "WHAT DOES
    n MEAN FOR A ONE-SHOT-EVENT DESIGN", DECIDED BEFORE ANY RESULT EXISTS.

    Two DIFFERENT counts get conflated in a design like this and they must be
    kept apart:

      * THE MULTIPLE-COMPARISONS COUNT (the DSR denominator N) is the number of
        SPECIFICATIONS SEARCHED. It has nothing to do with the event structure.
        It is 24 here (section 8), deflated exactly as in every other family,
        and the pooled ladder in section 10 is unchanged. This build does NOT
        invent a new denominator rule for being event-based, and it does not
        borrow another family's effective-N reasoning either — it uses the
        project-wide Policy D ladder, whose two upper tiers describe this
        project's whole search history and are therefore independent of any one
        family's data shape.

      * THE OBSERVATION COUNT (the n in PSR's sqrt(n-1)) IS where the event
        structure bites, and it is genuinely ambiguous here. Stream A has ~2,000
        daily observations but only ~500 underlying bets; stream B has ~500
        observations, one per unlock date, but each is a compounded multi-day
        return whose non-normality is much stronger. Neither is obviously
        right. Stream A risks OVERSTATING confidence by counting one bet's
        three days as three draws; stream B risks understating the sample and
        overstating skew/kurtosis penalties.

    THE RULE, FIXED NOW: BOTH STREAMS ARE COMPUTED FOR EVERY SPEC AT EVERY
    DENOMINATOR, AND THE VERDICT USES THE WORSE (LOWER) DSR OF THE TWO. A spec
    passes only if it passes on both. This is deliberately conservative, it
    cannot be gamed after the fact because it is a min rather than a choice, and
    it means neither ambiguity in "what is one observation" can be resolved in
    the family's favour.

    A NOTE ON WHY INCLUDING FLAT DAYS IN STREAM A IS NOT A CHEAT, since it is
    the obvious objection. Writing p for the fraction of days with an active
    position, the per-day Sharpe scales roughly as sqrt(p) while n scales as
    1/p, so the product SR*sqrt(n) — which is what the PSR z-statistic is built
    from — is approximately invariant to whether flat days are included. The
    skew/kurtosis correction moves in the CONSERVATIVE direction (a series with
    many exact zeros has higher kurtosis, which reduces z). Flat days are
    therefore included, and the fraction of active days is reported so the
    reader can see it.

7.5 DEGENERACY GUARD. A stream whose standard deviation is zero (a spec that
    never opens a position — possible for a heavily-filtered sub-arm) reports
    Sharpe 0.0 rather than a ratio of floating-point dust, following
    vol_regime_timing.RESIDUAL_DEGENERACY_RATIO's precedent, and is dropped
    from the screen with its spec_id logged.

9. COSTS — INSIDE THE DSR, NOT BESIDE IT, AND BOUNDED ON BOTH SIDES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLAUDE.md requires cost realism to feed the DSR calculation itself. For this
family the cost model is not a formality — it is where the source paper says
the trade dies (section 2). It is therefore bounded on BOTH sides rather than
asserted at one point, and the flat 2bp figure the aggregate-ETF timing
families (rebalancing_pressure_timing, margin_credit_timing) use is NOT copied
here: those families trade SPY, this one shorts individual small-cap stocks
within months of their IPO.

Three arms. Every arm is screened separately and all three are reported.

  cost_free   0 bp, 0 borrow. The GROSS signal. ATTRIBUTION ONLY, NEVER A
              VERDICT INPUT. Its job is to say whether a failure is a
              cost failure or a signal failure.

  cheap       DELIBERATELY OPTIMISTIC LOWER BOUND, not a realistic estimate.
              2.5 bp one-way half-spread on the stock leg, flat; 2.0 bp one-way
              on the SPY leg; general-collateral borrow at 34 bp/yr
              (borrow_cost.GENERAL_COLLATERAL_BPS_PER_YEAR, Beneish/Lee/Nichols
              2015 p.15) accrued on the short notional for the days held.
              WHY 2.5 bp: the US minimum tick is $0.01, so on a $20 stock one
              tick is a 5 bp FULL spread and 2.5 bp one-way. A stock trading at
              a one-tick spread is at the physical floor. Nothing real is
              cheaper. If a spec fails HERE, no dispute about the spread
              estimator can rescue it.

  baseline    THE VERDICT ARM. Per-name, per-day EDGE effective half-spread
              (Ardia, Guidotti & Kroencke, "Efficient Estimation of Bid-Ask
              Spreads from Open, High, Low, and Close Prices", Journal of
              Financial Economics 2024, via this project's
              spread_estimator.build_edge_half_spread_frame with its
              COST_MODEL_WINDOW_DAYS = 63 window), charged one-way on entry and
              one-way on exit of the stock leg; 2.0 bp one-way on the SPY leg;
              HARD-TO-BORROW borrow at 430 bp/yr
              (borrow_cost.HARD_TO_BORROW_BPS_PER_YEAR, D'Avolio 2002 Table 3
              p.286, value-weighted mean fee for specials) on the short
              notional for the days held. Where EDGE returns no usable estimate
              for a name/date the cheap arm's 2.5 bp flat rate is charged
              instead, and the fallback notional is COUNTED and reported.

              WHY HARD-TO-BORROW AND NOT GENERAL COLLATERAL: before the unlock
              day only about a third of the shares are tradable ([FH01] p.471:
              "Only a fraction (typically about one-third) of the outstanding
              shares can trade"), which is the textbook small-float,
              hard-to-borrow situation. Charging general collateral to a short
              in a pre-unlock recent IPO would be the least conservative
              available assumption.

              THE EDGE ESTIMATOR'S OWN KNOWN LIMITATION, DISCLOSED RATHER THAN
              INHERITED SILENTLY. spread_estimator.py documents that EDGE
              OVERSTATES the level by roughly 10-40x for liquid mega/large caps
              (true half-spreads ~0.3-1.5 bp) and states that it "remains far
              more trustworthy ... as a level estimate for wider-spread/
              less-liquid names above roughly 30-50bps true spread". This
              family's universe is recent small-cap IPOs, which is the regime
              the module says the estimator handles well, not the regime it
              says it handles badly. That is a reason to use it here and NOT a
              claim that it is exact. The module's own calibrated builder
              (build_calibrated_half_spread_frame) is deliberately NOT used,
              because its level is pinned to SP500_TARGET_MEDIAN_HALF_SPREAD =
              2 bp, an S&P-500 calibration whose docstring explicitly warns
              against applying it to another universe, and this session has no
              published small-cap-IPO spread calibration in hand to substitute.
              Fabricating one would be worse than using a documented,
              slightly-pessimistic estimator and saying so.

              THE REALISED AVERAGE CHARGED HALF-SPREAD IS REPORTED, in basis
              points, so the reader can judge the level rather than take it on
              trust — and so a future reader with a real spread feed can redo
              the arithmetic.

  BREAKEVEN COST IS REPORTED FOR EVERY SPEC: the one-way stock-leg half-spread
  at which the baseline-arm Sharpe would fall to zero. That is the number that
  makes the cost debate concrete instead of rhetorical.

  NO INTRADAY EXECUTION IS ASSUMED. Both legs transact at the CLOSE. This
  family therefore does not need, and does not claim, any execution capability
  this project lacks.

  CAPACITY, INFORMATIONAL, NOT GATING (per CLAUDE.md). Pre-unlock float in a
  recent small-cap IPO is thin, and borrow may simply be UNAVAILABLE at any
  price for some names — which is a feasibility limit, not a cost number, and
  is not modelled. Stated, not modelled, not gating while no capital is
  deployed.

11. MECHANISM-FIDELITY CHECKS, FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  EQUATION (1) IS COMPOUNDED, NOT SUMMED. A unit test asserts the CAR of a
    hand-computed synthetic case, with a value worked out by hand in the test
    file, and asserts it DIFFERS from the sum-of-differences approximation on
    the same input. Formula-from-memory guard.
F2  THE UNLOCK DAY MUST BE MONDAY-HEAVY. [FH01] §III.C p.488 observes "because
    of the weekend, unlock days tend to fall on Monday". 180 = 25*7 + 5, so an
    offer date on Monday, Tuesday or Wednesday maps to a weekend or Monday and
    therefore to a Monday unlock day: three of five weekday cases. The build
    reports the realised weekday distribution of its own day-0 dates and a unit
    test asserts Monday is the modal weekday. IF THIS FAILS, THE 180-DAY PROXY
    IS NOT LANDING WHERE THE PAPER'S HAND-COLLECTED DATES LANDED and the whole
    construction is suspect.
F3  THE TRADING-DAY MAPPING. A unit test pins "first trading day on or after
    offer + 180 calendar days" on a worked example with a known calendar.
F4  THE IDENTITY GATE ACTUALLY REJECTS. Two tests: (a) a synthetic row whose
    price series begins before its offer date is rejected by G2; (b) a
    REGRESSION PIN on the real verified case — a row with Ritter name
    "ConnectOne Bancorp Inc", ticker CNOB, offer date 2013-02-11 and a price
    series beginning 2012-07-02 must be rejected, and must be rejected even
    though a SEC CIK/name match would accept it.
F5  NO LOOK-AHEAD IN THE COST MODEL. The EDGE half-spread charged to an event
    is computed on a rolling window ENDING at or before day -6, i.e. strictly
    before the widest event window opens. Tested by truncation invariance:
    replacing all data after day -6 with garbage must leave the charged
    half-spread bit-identical.
F6  REPLICATION CHECK AGAINST [FH01]. Report, for the `lockup` x `w_m1_p1` x
    `spy` specs, the event-level mean CAR, median CAR, fraction negative, and
    t-statistic, for `all` / `vc` / `nonvc`, printed next to [FH01]'s -1.5% /
    -2.3% / -0.8% and 63% negative. Section 4's framing applies: divergence is
    expected; a WILD divergence (wrong sign, order of magnitude) indicates a
    bug.
F7  THE PLACEBO MUST BE REPORTED BESIDE THE LOCKUP ARM, always, with the same
    statistics. Section 8's binding sentence applies.
F8  UNIVERSE AND IDENTITY ACCOUNTING IN THE OUTPUT. Every filter step's row
    count (section 5.2), every G1/G2/G3 outcome count, the number of rows lost
    to no-price-data, and the final VC/non-VC/growth-capital split, all
    reported as numbers in the run output rather than described in prose here.
F9  SURVIVORSHIP MEASUREMENT IN THE OUTPUT. The price-availability rate for the
    whole universe and split by G1 status (`cik_name_match` / `cik_name_mismatch`
    / `cik_absent`), so section 6's bias is a measured number in the report and
    not only a paragraph in this document.
F10 ABNORMAL VOLUME, EQUATION (2), IS THE INDEPENDENT VALIDATION OF THE WHOLE
    CONSTRUCTION. Report the three-day average abnormal volume over days -1..+1
    and the day +1 value, for `all` / `vc` / `nonvc`, against [FH01] Table III
    p.481 (+44% all, +75% VC, +15% non-VC) and Figure 3 p.480 (~+80% on day
    +1, settling near +40%). A 180-day proxy that lands on real unlock events
    should show a volume spike even if the return effect has decayed; one that
    lands on ordinary days should show nothing. THIS TEST CAN FAIL WITHOUT THE
    RETURN TEST FAILING, AND IF IT DOES, THE RETURN RESULT MEANS NOTHING —
    that is the point of running it. The same statistic is reported for the
    `placebo` arm, where it should be absent.

12. PASS / FAIL — FIXED BEFORE RESULTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy D, two-tier, applied to the BEST spec by DSR at n_local = 24 within the
BASELINE cost arm, where each spec's DSR at a given N is the MINIMUM of its
stream-A and stream-B DSR at that N (section 7.4):

  DSR at n_local = 24 < 0.95                        -> DEFINITE_NEGATIVE
  DSR >= 0.95 at n_local but < 0.95 at n = 857      -> UNRESOLVED (needs
                                                      forward validation; NOT
                                                      a pass)
  DSR >= 0.95 at the most conservative N measured   -> PASS

0.95 is the VALIDATED-EDGE bar used by every family this session. 0.50 is the
informal screening floor used only to describe whether a spec is worth
mentioning; it is not a verdict.

TWO ADDITIONAL BINDING RULES, BOTH OF WHICH CAN ONLY MAKE THE VERDICT WORSE:

  (i)  THE PLACEBO OVERRIDE (section 8). If the best `placebo` spec's Sharpe
       and CAR are of the same sign and comparable magnitude to the best
       `lockup` spec's, the family is reported as a NEGATIVE on mechanism
       grounds whatever the lockup arm's DSR, because the effect would then not
       be attributable to lockup expiration.
  (ii) THE SURVIVORSHIP CAVEAT (section 6). Any verdict of PASS or UNRESOLVED
       must carry, in the verdict paragraph itself and not in a footnote, the
       measured price-availability rate, the statement that the direction of
       the resulting bias is UNKNOWN, and the statement that a clean
       replication needs the delisted-securities data in gap P2. A passing DSR
       on this family is a reason for MORE scrutiny, not less.

NO REGIME-CONDITIONAL DSR. CLAUDE.md restricts regime-conditional testing to
sources whose claim is explicitly conditional. [FH01]'s claim is
UNCONDITIONAL — Table V p.485 reports negative mean AND median CAR in all ten
years of its sample, and the paper presents the effect as growing over time
rather than as confined to any regime. Year-by-year CARs are reported here as
DESCRIPTIVE diagnostics only and are not a separate gate.

NO FORWARD REGISTRATION IS CREATED BY THIS BUILD, and no live registration's
operational status is touched. That is the repo owner's call under CLAUDE.md
§6.6 and is not made here.

13. DEVIATIONS FROM THE SOURCE, EVERY ONE NAMED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  LOCKUP DATE IS A 180-CALENDAR-DAY PROXY, not the prospectus's actual unlock

D2  BENCHMARK IS SPY, not the CRSP value-weighted index. SPY is a large-cap
    index and this universe is small-cap, so it is an imperfect market proxy —
    which is precisely why the `raw` (unhedged) arm, the paper's own robustness
    choice (p.477), is carried as a grid dimension rather than as an
    afterthought.
D3  UNIVERSE IS RITTER'S IPO-age.xlsx FILTERED AS IN 5.2, not SDC plus
    hand-collected prospectuses. Different vendor, different exclusions,
    different era.
D4  THREE OF [FH01]'s FIVE EXCLUSIONS ARE NOT IMPLEMENTED — carveouts, earnings
    announcements within three days of the unlock day, and seasoned equity
    offers around it (section 5.6). Adds noise; reduces measured Sharpe.
D5  PENNY-STOCK FILTER IS APPLIED AT THE DAY -6 AS-TRADED CLOSE, not at the
    offer price (section 5.5). Tradeable, so no look-ahead; different in timing
    from the paper.
D6  SAMPLE WINDOW IS 2012-2019, entirely outside [FH01]'s 1988-1997 and 11-18
    years AFTER its 2001 publication. McLean & Pontiff (2016) post-publication
    decay of 58% is the applicable prior, and it is already inside
    preservation_score's OOS_RETENTION = 0.42.
D7  SURVIVORSHIP. Only companies whose price history is still retrievable free
    are testable. Measured, first-class, direction unknown (section 6).
D8  THE VC SPLIT USES RITTER's VC == 1 vs VC == 0, with VC == 2 (growth
    capital, established empirically in 5.1) in neither arm. [FH01] uses SDC's
    venture-backed flag, which is a different vendor's classification.
D9  THE TRADEABLE OBJECT (a calendar-time short basket, section 7) IS NOT IN
    THE PAPER. [FH01] reports a cross-sectional CAR and states plainly that it
    is not exploitable at the bid and ask. This build constructs a portfolio in
    order to have a return stream a Sharpe and a DSR can be computed on at all.
D10 COSTS ARE ESTIMATED WITH EDGE FROM DAILY OHLC, not from quoted spreads.
    [FH01] had CRSP closing bid and ask and reports ~3.35-3.45% relative quoted
    spreads for its 1988-1997 Nasdaq sample (§III.C p.488-489); nothing about
    that number transfers to 2012-2019 and it is not used.
D11 ABNORMAL VOLUME USES YAHOO/PriceStore VOLUME, not CRSP volume. Volume
    enters only as a RATIO within one 56-day window (Equation 2), so any
    consistent share basis cancels; the store's as-traded basis is consistent
    by construction.

NON-DEVIATIONS, STATED SO THEY ARE NOT LATER MISTAKEN FOR ONE: the -1..+1 and
-5..+1 windows, Equation (1)'s compounded form, Equation (2)'s -50..-6
normalisation window, the VC/non-VC split, and the existence of a placebo
control are all [FH01]'s own, taken from the pages cited above. None of them is
searched or tuned.

14. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERY LOW probability of a real pass. Lower than any of this session's previous
seven candidates carried into their own builds. Reasons, all known in advance:

  * THE SOURCE PAPER ITSELF SAYS THE TRADE DOES NOT WORK NET OF THE SPREAD, on
    its own sample, in an era whose relative quoted spreads were around 3.4%
    (§III.B p.487-488, §III.C p.488-489, p.472). Modern spreads are far tighter,
    so the arithmetic is not settled a priori — but starting from "the authors
    checked and it was not profitable" is a materially worse prior than any
    previous candidate started from.
  * PUBLICATION AND ATTENTION. [FH01] appeared in the Journal of Finance in
    2001 and is heavily cited; the paper already notes (p.472) that "Upcoming
    unlock dates are now listed on several Web sites and in a weekly table in
    The Wall Street Journal", and its own conclusion (p.495-496) flags the
    change from its sample period, when "investors were almost never reminded
    of upcoming unlock dates", as an opportunity for future comparison. This
    build IS that comparison, twenty-five years later, on a fully-scrutinised
    event. McLean & Pontiff's 58% post-publication decay is the base rate.
  * THE EVENT IS PERFECTLY PUBLIC AND PERFECTLY SCHEDULED. Every mechanism this
    project has found to survive scrutiny anywhere has involved some frictional
    or informational asymmetry. There is none here by construction.
  * SURVIVORSHIP DAMAGE OF UNKNOWN DIRECTION on roughly two thirds of the
    universe (section 6). This makes a clean positive uninterpretable even if
    one appears — which is why section 12(ii) exists.
  * THE TRADE IS A SHORT IN A SMALL-CAP RECENT IPO — the most expensive corner
    of the US equity market to be short in, on both spread and borrow.

  Against all that: the source is peer-reviewed in a top field journal, the
  effect it documents is large (-1.5% over three days, -2.3% for VC-backed),
  the event date needs no forecasting at all, the data is free, and the
  construction has two independent falsifiable validators (F2 weekday, F10
  abnormal volume) that most candidates do not get. It is worth the 24 trials.

  MOST LIKELY OUTCOME, RECORDED SO IT CANNOT BE RETROFITTED: DEFINITE_NEGATIVE
  under the baseline cost arm, with the gross (cost_free) event-level CAR
  showing a materially SMALLER negative than [FH01]'s -1.5% — because of
  post-publication decay and because the 180-day proxy mislabels some events —
  and with the abnormal-volume check (F10) nonetheless showing a real spike,
  confirming that the events are being located correctly and that it is the
  RETURN effect, not the construction, that has gone.
