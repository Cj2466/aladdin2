FORM N-PORT / FLOW-MECHANISM FEASIBILITY -- VAYANOS & WOOLLEY (2013) / LOU (2012) SCOPING -- 2026-09-05
====================================================================================================

Decide whether SEC Form N-PORT is real, usable, free data for a Lou (2012)-style
flow-induced-trading (FIT) measure -- the data layer a Vayanos & Woolley (2013)
momentum-and-reversal-from-flow family would need. Builds NO signal, constructs
NO flow-induced-trading measure, registers nothing, and takes no paid-data
decision.

SCOPE: feasibility scoping ONLY, mirroring the tsmom_futures_feasibility_2026-09-05
run in both rigor and limits. No family module is touched.

WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  This project's fundamentals-based factor line (asset_growth, quality/NOA,
  residual_momentum, best_ideas_13f) has produced a run of honest negatives. The
  working hypothesis is that short/medium-horizon US equity returns are driven
  more by mechanical flow than by fundamentals. Vayanos & Woolley (2013,
  "An Institutional Theory of Momentum and Reversal", Review of Financial Studies
  26(5):1087-1145) model fund-flow-induced trading pressure as the SAME mechanism
  generating both short-run momentum and long-run reversal, building on Lou
  (2012, "A Flow-Based Explanation for Return Predictability", Review of
  Financial Studies 25(12):3457-3489), who built a flow-induced-trading (FIT)
  measure from mutual-fund holdings + flows and showed it forecasts returns.

  A prior, UNVERIFIED project note claimed: "SEC EDGAR hosts Form N-PORT
  (monthly fund holdings + capital-flow data) free since Oct 2019." This run
  checks that claim against the actual SEC rule text, actual EDGAR filings
  pulled live, and the actual Lou (2012) working paper -- not against memory or
  the note's own wording.

VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FEASIBLE_FREE_WITH_NAMED_LIMITS -- NO PAID-DATA GAP LOGGED

  This is the first run in this project's futures/N-PORT/flow scoping line that
  does NOT end in a paid-data recommendation. The prior note's START DATE claim
  (Oct 2019) is CORRECT -- SEC's own bulk dataset states its coverage begins
  "October 2019", and three independently-checked, unrelated fund families' real
  first public NPORT-P filings cluster Nov 18-27 2019. But the note's GRANULARITY
  claim ("monthly fund holdings") is only HALF right: FLOW data genuinely is
  monthly even today, but HOLDINGS data is quarterly, not monthly, in the regime
  that has been continuously in force since 2016 and remains in force today.
  A 2024 rule change that would have made holdings monthly has never taken
  effect (delayed to 2027/2028) and the SEC proposed in Feb 2026 to scrap it
  and keep quarterly holdings permanently. Bulk collection is genuinely
  practical: SEC DERA publishes an official, quarterly, tab-delimited bulk
  dataset (verified live, not assumed from its own documentation) -- no
  per-filing scraping of thousands of XML documents is required. The two raw
  ingredients Lou's construction needs -- per-fund per-security prior-quarter
  holdings, and per-fund period flow -- are BOTH genuinely present and were
  BOTH extracted from two real, consecutive N-PORT filings for the same fund
  in this run. Named limits: (1) only ~7 years of public history (Oct/Nov 2019
  to today) vs. Lou's original ~30-year sample; (2) holdings are quarterly, not
  monthly, matching Lou's OWN original data cadence rather than degrading it,
  but still a real constraint on statistical power for a long-run reversal
  test; (3) coverage is registered-mutual-fund-only (excludes hedge funds,
  pensions, insurance general accounts -- all outside Lou's scope too, so not
  a new gap, but a real boundary on what the measure can capture); (4) a REAL,
  UNRESOLVED METHODOLOGICAL QUESTION was found and is explicitly NOT resolved
  here (see Q3 and PROOF_4): Lou's own Eq.(1) TNA-inferred flow and N-PORT's
  directly-reported Item B.6 flow DISAGREE IN SIGN on the one real fund-quarter
  measured. That is a genuine judgment call for whoever designs the real
  measure, not a data-availability problem, and is logged rather than guessed
  at, per this run's own escalation instruction.

Q1. WHAT IS ACTUALLY PUBLIC, SINCE WHEN, AT WHAT TRUE GRANULARITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [VERIFIED LIVE 2026-09-05 -- SEC Release 33-10231 / IC-32314, "Investment
  Company Reporting Modernization", adopted Oct 13 2016, 81 FR 81870]
    Primary source fetched and converted to text (25,950 lines). Quoted
    verbatim, p.75-76 (internal pagination) / rule text lines 5934-5937:
      "Only information reported for the third month of each fund's fiscal
       quarter on Form N-PORT will be made publicly available, and such
       information will not be made public until 60 days after the end of the
       third month of the fund's fiscal quarter."
    Rationale, quoted verbatim (footnote 225 and accompanying text, and the
    predatory-trading discussion the rule cross-references):
      "the concerns associated with more frequent public disclosure are
       related to the disclosure of portfolio holdings information... the
       risks of predatory trading practices such as front-running and the
       ability of non-investors to reverse engineer and copycat fund's
       investment strategies."
    Original compliance dates (rule text, lines 14601-14742): June 1, 2018 for
    fund complexes >= $1 billion in net assets; June 1, 2019 for smaller
    complexes. A transition rule (lines 14700-14710) kept ALL N-PORT reports
    nonpublic for the first six months after each complex's compliance date,
    with Form N-Q continuing to serve the public in the interim.

  [VERIFIED LIVE 2026-09-05 -- EMPIRICAL: real EDGAR filings, not just rule text]
    Pulled real filing histories (data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK*.json) for
    three UNRELATED, large fund families/vehicle types to find the actual
    first public NPORT-P filing:
      Vanguard Bond Index Funds (CIK 794105, open-end):  first NPORT-P 2019-11-27
      American Funds Fundamental Investors (CIK 39473):  first NPORT-P 2019-11-27
      SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust / SPY (CIK 884394, UIT ETF): first NPORT-P 2019-11-18
      PCM Fund, Inc. (CIK 908187, closed-end fund):       first NPORT-P 2019-11-26
    All four cover the fiscal quarter ended 2019-09-30, filed ~58-60 days
    later, exactly matching the rule's 60-day lag. This also empirically
    confirms N-PORT filer coverage spans all three major registered-fund
    vehicle types: open-end mutual funds, UIT-organized ETFs, AND closed-end
    funds -- not just traditional mutual funds.

  [VERIFIED LIVE 2026-09-05 -- SEC's OWN BULK-DATASET PAGE]
    https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets,

  [VERIFIED LIVE 2026-09-05 -- THE 2024 AMENDMENT THAT NEVER TOOK EFFECT, AND
   ITS OWN 2026 ROLLBACK PROPOSAL -- a live regulatory saga, not settled history]
    Aug 28, 2024: SEC adopted amendments moving to MONTHLY public disclosure
    ("Monthly Form N-PORT's information be made available to the public 60
    days after month end... twelve times per year", per SEC's own statement
    text, https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-statement-
    form-n-port-amendments-082824). Original effective date Nov 17, 2025.
    April 16, 2025 (IC-35538): SEC DELAYED that effective date, before it ever
    took effect, to Nov 17, 2027 (complexes >= $1B) / May 18, 2028 (smaller).
    Feb 18, 2026 (SEC.gov press release 2026-19, fetched directly:
    https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-19-sec-proposes-
    amendments-reduce-burdens-reporting-fund-portfolio-holdings; proposing
    release IC-35962, File No. S7-2026-05): SEC PROPOSED to "reduce the
    publication of reports from monthly to quarterly" for Form N-PORT -- i.e.
    abandoning the 2024 amendment before it ever took effect once -- while
    separately giving filers "an extra 15 days" on the SEC-facing (non-public)
    monthly filing deadline (30 -> 45 days). Comment period: 60 days from
    Federal Register publication (published 2026-02-23, so ~April 24, 2026).
    As of this run's date (2026-09-05) this is a PROPOSAL, not yet finalized.
    NET CONCLUSION: the operative public-disclosure regime today is IDENTICAL
    in substance to the one adopted in 2016 -- quarterly, third-month-only,
    60-day lag. It has never been anything else in practice, and given the
    2026 proposal, may never become anything else. A project plan that assumed
    "monthly holdings, eventually" as a near-term data-quality upgrade would be
    planning against a live proposal to cancel that exact upgrade.

  [ITEM B.6 -- THE ONE GENUINELY MONTHLY FIELD IN THE CURRENT REGIME]
    Form N-PORT (formn-port.pdf), Item B.6, quoted verbatim:
      "Flow information. Provide the aggregate dollar amounts for sales and
       redemptions/repurchases of Fund shares during each of the preceding
       three months... a. Total net asset value of shares sold (including
       exchanges but excluding reinvestment...). b. Total net asset value of
       shares sold in connection with reinvestments... c. Total net asset
       value of shares redeemed or repurchased, including exchanges."
    Because the public (third-month) filing reports this Item for ALL THREE
    months of the quarter retroactively, the public genuinely gets MONTHLY
    flow granularity even though it only gets QUARTERLY holdings granularity.
    Verified in two real filings (PROOF_1 below), not assumed from the form
    text alone.

Q3. CAN A LOU (2012)-STYLE FIT MEASURE ACTUALLY BE CONSTRUCTED FROM THIS?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOU (2012) EQUATIONS, QUOTED FROM THE ACTUAL PAPER, NOT FROM MEMORY.
  Fetched the freely-available working paper directly
  (https://personal.lse.ac.uk/loud/flows.pdf, London School of Economics,
  the author's own site), converted to text, and located the exact equations
  rather than reconstructing them -- per CLAUDE.md's standing rule against
  implementing a published formula from memory.

    Section 2.2 "Fund Flows", Eq.(1):
      flow_i,t = [TNA_i,t - TNA_i,t-1*(1+RET_i,t) - MGN_i,t] / TNA_i,t-1

    Section 3.2 "The Return Pattern", Eq.(3):
      FIT_j,t = [sum_i shares_i,j,t-1 * flow_i,t * PSF_i,t-1]
                / [sum_i shares_i,j,t-1]
      where PSF ("partial scaling factor") comes from Eq.(2)'s panel
      regression (Table II): quoted verbatim, "the coefficient on flow in a
      univariate regression is 0.97 (t=16.82)" for the OUTFLOW sample, and for
      inflows, "managers invest only 62 (t=15.78) cents out of each dollar of
      inflow in their existing holdings."

  WHAT LOU'S CONSTRUCTION NEEDS, MAPPED ONTO WHAT N-PORT ACTUALLY HAS:
    shares_i,j,t-1 (prior-period fund holdings, per stock)  -> FUND_REPORTED_
      HOLDING.tsv / Part C, quarterly snapshot, CUSIP/ISIN/LEI-identified.
      VERIFIED PRESENT (PROOF_2).
    flow_i,t (fund-level period flow)                        -> FUND_REPORTED_
      INFO.tsv / Item B.6, monthly, DIRECTLY REPORTED dollar figures.
      VERIFIED PRESENT (PROOF_1, PROOF_3).
    TNA_i,t / TNA_i,t-1 (for Lou's OWN Eq.(1) definition of flow)
      -> Item B.1.c (netAssets), one snapshot per quarter. VERIFIED PRESENT.
    RET_i,t (fund's own return, for Eq.(1))                   -> Item B.5,
      monthly, by share class. VERIFIED PRESENT.
  Both of Lou's real raw ingredients exist in N-PORT and were both extracted,
  in this run, from two real, consecutive, same-series filings (Vanguard
  Mid-Cap Growth Index Fund, quarters ended 2026-03-31 and 2026-06-30) --
  see run_nport_flow_feasibility.py and its test file for the exact numbers.

  COVERAGE, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSUMED: N-PORT filers (open-end + closed-end
  + UIT ETFs, ex money-market and SBIC) sit against a total US-registered-
  investment-company industry of $39.2 trillion at year-end 2024 (ICI 2025
  Investment Company Fact Book, Chapter 2, Figure 2.2, fetched and converted
  to text directly). Money market funds are 17% of that total (same figure);
  SBICs are a negligible, separately-registered population. So roughly 83%
  of the $39.2 trillion registered-fund industry (~$32.5tn) sits inside
  N-PORT's filer population -- but note the $39.2tn figure ITSELF is the
  registered-fund universe only. Hedge funds, pensions, and insurance general
  accounts are entirely outside it and file (if at all) the confidential
  Form PF, not any N-PORT-derived measure. THIS IS NOT A NEW GAP RELATIVE TO
  LOU (2012): his paper (Section 2.1, "I include all domestic equity mutual
  funds in the sample") was ALWAYS scoped to registered mutual funds, not all
  institutional flow. N-PORT matches that scope; it does not narrow it.

  PROOF_4 -- A REAL, UNRESOLVED METHODOLOGICAL QUESTION, FOUND AND LOGGED,
             NOT RESOLVED. This is the escalation this run's own instructions
             asked for if a genuine judgment call turned up.
    Using the two real, consecutive filings (Vanguard Mid-Cap Growth Index
    Fund, TNA_t-1 = $29,248,363,673.15 as of 2026-03-31, TNA_t =
    $34,819,073,910.65 as of 2026-06-30):
      Lou's Eq.(1), using one share class's compounded quarterly return
      (19.23%): inferred flow_t = -0.185% of TNA (an OUTFLOW, -$53.98mm).
      N-PORT's own Item B.6, summed net (sales+reinvestment-redemption)
      across the quarter's three real monthly figures: +0.015% of TNA (an
      INFLOW, +$4.46mm).
    BOTH estimates are small (each well under 0.2% of TNA for this specific
    large, low-net-flow index fund), but THEY DISAGREE IN SIGN. Plausible,
    non-exhaustive reasons, named rather than chased to a conclusion: (a)
    this run used ONE of the fund's three share classes' return rather than a
    true NAV-weighted blend across all classes; (b) Eq.(1) folds in
    everything that moves TNA net of return -- fee accruals, unrealized/
    realized gain distributions, rounding in the fund's own NAV process --
    while Item B.6 is a narrower, transaction-level definition; (c) Lou
    himself needed Eq.(1) only because CRSP/CDA never gave him a directly-
    reported flow figure -- N-PORT does, so a real build must explicitly
    decide whether to use Lou's original (TNA-inferred) definition for
    fidelity to the published method, or N-PORT's own (directly-reported)
    Item B.6 for probable better data quality, and these are not guaranteed
    to point the same direction, as measured here. NOT RESOLVED IN THIS RUN.
    Reproduced exactly in run_nport_flow_feasibility.py's
    run_illustrative_cross_check() and pinned in
    tests/test_nport_flow_feasibility.py.

  WHAT WAS NOT ATTEMPTED, ON PURPOSE: no FIT value was computed for any stock.
  Eq.(3)'s sum is over EVERY mutual fund holding a stock; this run has real
  data for exactly one fund, illustrating only ONE NUMERATOR TERM of that sum
  (see run_nport_flow_feasibility.py's `illustrative_eq3_numerator_term_for_
  hilton`), explicitly labeled as not a FIT value in both the code and its
  test. Building the actual measure is out of scope per the task brief.

Q4. HONEST FEASIBILITY VERDICT AND NAMED LIMITS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FEASIBLE_FREE_WITH_NAMED_LIMITS. Precisely, in order of how much they bind:

  (1) HISTORY DEPTH. Usable public N-PORT data spans Oct/Nov 2019 to today
      (2026-09), i.e. roughly 28 quarterly snapshots per fund as of this run's
      date. Lou's original sample ran from 1980 to 2006 (his Section 2.1,
      "Mutual Fund Data", states verbatim: "Quarterly mutual fund holdings
      data is obtained from the CDA/Spectrum database for the period of
      1980-2006"), i.e. 27 YEARS. This project's usable N-PORT-only sample is
      roughly 7 years -- real, binding,
      and worst for testing the LONG-RUN (multi-year) reversal half of the
      Vayanos-Woolley claim specifically, since that needs several
      non-overlapping multi-year windows to say anything with power.

  (2) HOLDINGS ARE QUARTERLY, NOT MONTHLY, TODAY AND FOR THE FORESEEABLE
      FUTURE (Q1 above). This MATCHES Lou's own original cadence rather than
      degrading it, so it is not a NEW gap relative to the paper being
      replicated -- but it remains a real constraint on how finely a
      constructed measure can be dated, and the industry-wide move to true
      monthly public holdings that might have relaxed this has been proposed,
      delayed past 2027, and is now itself proposed to be abandoned (Feb 2026
      proposal) -- so no near-term improvement should be assumed or planned
      around.

  (3) SCOPE IS REGISTERED-MUTUAL-FUND-ONLY. Matches Lou's own scope exactly
      (Q3 above); a "flow mechanism" thesis that intends to reach beyond
      mutual funds (hedge funds, pensions) would need a different, likely
      paid or unavailable, data source -- not evaluated further here since it
      is not what this specific construction needs.

  (4) THE PROOF_4 SIGN-DISAGREEMENT IS AN OPEN METHODOLOGICAL QUESTION, NOT A
      DATA GAP. It does not block feasibility; it is a design decision (which
      flow definition to adopt) that a real build must make deliberately and
      state, not one this scoping run should guess at.

  NO PAID-DATA GAP IS LOGGED FOR THIS RUN. Everything measured above --
  the rule history, the real sample filings, the official bulk dataset, and
  the real fund-level flow/holdings figures -- came from sec.gov and
  data.sec.gov at zero cost. If a real build later wants DAILY (rather than
  monthly) flow, share-class-level ETF creation/redemption basket detail, or
  non-mutual-fund institutional flow, that would be a genuinely different,
  separately-evaluated question -- not chased here since it is not what this
  scoping was asked to answer and nothing in Q1-Q4 required it.

WHAT MUST NOT BE DONE ON THE BACK OF THIS RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Do NOT treat "N-PORT has monthly holdings" as true. It does not, has
    never, and per the Feb 2026 SEC proposal may never. Only FLOW is monthly
    in the currently (and likely permanently) operative regime.
  * Do NOT adopt either of PROOF_4's two flow definitions (Lou's Eq.(1) or
    N-PORT's Item B.6) without a deliberate, stated choice -- they disagree
    in sign on the one real fund-quarter measured here, and this run
    explicitly did not resolve which is "right".
  * Do NOT assume this run's coverage/history numbers generalize without
    re-checking closer to a real build: the ~7-year history figure and the
    83%-of-industry coverage figure are both dated 2026-09-05 and will drift.
  * Do NOT read this run's clean bill of health on DATA as a signal about
    whether a Lou/Vayanos-Woolley-style effect actually EXISTS in this
    project's post-2019, US-mutual-fund-only sample. That is an empirical
    question this run does not touch.

WHAT THIS RUN DOES NOT ESTABLISH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  It does not construct a flow-induced-trading measure for any stock, does not
  backtest any signal, and does not decide which of Lou's Eq.(1) or N-PORT's
  Item B.6 flow definition a real build should use (PROOF_4). It does not
  measure whether ~7 years and ~83% AUM coverage are STATISTICALLY sufficient
  for a real DSR-gated result -- that needs the actual construction and
  backtest this run is scoped out of. Its regulatory-history account is
  bounded by what could be fetched live on 2026-09-05 (Federal Register's own
  pages returned bot-blocking redirects from this environment; SEC.gov's own
  final-rule PDFs, press statements, and DERA data pages were used instead and
  are the primary sources quoted above).
