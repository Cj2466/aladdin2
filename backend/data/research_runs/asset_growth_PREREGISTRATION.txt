1. HYPOTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H: Firms with LOW year-over-year growth in TOTAL ASSETS earn higher subsequent
   returns than firms with HIGH total-asset growth, on the point-in-time
   large-cap S&P 500 universe over 2015-2026.

   AG_t = (Assets_t - Assets_{t-1}) / Assets_{t-1}

Direction: low growth = long. The family's signal is the NEGATED growth rate,
so the harness's top-is-long convention puts conservative-investment firms in
the long leg and aggressive-investment firms in the short leg.

Two mechanisms are offered in the literature and this family does NOT claim to
distinguish between them: q-theory (firms invest more when their discount rate
is low, so heavy investment co-occurs with low subsequent expected returns) and
behavioral overinvestment/extrapolation (investors over-extrapolate fast-growing
firms and overvalue them). A negative result here is evidence against neither
story in general — only against the sort being tradeable on this universe now.

2. CITATIONS — VERIFICATION STATUS, INCLUDING TWO PREMISES THAT FAILED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every citation below was checked against a live-fetched primary or publisher
record during this build (2026-09-01) by a verification pass run independently
of the build. This project has a documented incident of a fabricated academic
quote, so the rule here is absolute: verified-and-cited, or explicitly labeled
unverified. Nothing in between.

VERIFIED — Cooper, Michael J., Huseyin Gulen & Michael J. Schill, "Asset Growth
and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns", Journal of Finance 63(4), 2008,
pp. 1609-1651, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01370.x. Confirmed against two
independent records (Crossref API and RePEc) returning identical fields.
Verbatim from the published abstract, fetched identically from both:
    "Asset growth rates are strong predictors of future abnormal returns."
    "Asset growth retains its forecasting ability even on large capitalization
     stocks."
The second sentence is what makes this family testable on an S&P 500 universe
at all, and it is an exact quote, not a paraphrase.

VERIFIED NEGATIVE — the CGS abstract contains NO numbers. No spread return, no
sample period, no horizon claim. Secondary write-ups circulate several
different magnitudes for this paper and they conflict with each other; the
published full text is paywalled (Wiley/SSRN/ScienceDirect all returned 403)
and could not be fetched. THEREFORE: no empirical magnitude is attributed to
CGS anywhere in this family's code or documents. A "19-year horizon" claim that
came in with the build brief was searched for and NOT FOUND in any source; it
is dropped entirely rather than repeated.

VERIFIED WITH TWO CORRECTIONS — Cooper, Michael, Huseyin Gulen & Mihai Ion,
"The use of asset growth in empirical asset pricing models", Journal of
Financial Economics 151, article 103746, 2024 (working paper 2018; SSRN
3026534). The build brief for this family got two things wrong about it, and
both are recorded here rather than quietly fixed:

  (i)  The brief dated it "~2018-2019" as a working paper. It is a 2024 JFE
       article; 2018 is the working-paper version only.
  (ii) The brief said its total-assets-beats-capex finding is "evidence
       favoring the behavioral half" of the mechanism debate. THE PAPER
       EXPLICITLY DISCLAIMS THAT INFERENCE. Its own conclusion states that
       "factor models in themselves cannot help distinguish between behavioral
       and rational determinants of expected return variation". The strongest
       claim it makes is against q-theory as a SOLE explanation, and the
       mechanism its abstract advances is a financing-cost channel — "the
       superior performance of the asset growth factor seems to be attributable
       to its ability to capture aggregate shocks to equity financing costs" —
       which is not a mispricing story at all.

  The paper's ACTUAL total-assets-beats-capex finding IS verified, from the
  published abstract: factor models' "ability to price the cross-section of
  returns decreases significantly when the investment factor is constructed
  using traditional investment measures, or measures that also account for
  investment in intangibles."

  Nothing in this family's construction depends on this citation either way.
  The design decision it would have supported — measure TOTAL asset growth
  rather than a capex proxy — is made on independent grounds: total assets is
  what the primary source sorts on, and it is the one line item this pipeline
  resolves for essentially every filer (section 4).

VERIFIED — Hou, Kewei, Chen Xue & Lu Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies", Review of
Financial Studies 33(5), 2020, pp. 2019-2133 (NBER Working Paper 23394, 2017).
Their investment factor I/A is defined, verbatim from the fetched NBER PDF, as
"the annual change in total assets (Compustat annual item AT) divided by
one-year-lagged total assets" — the SAME construction as this family's AG_t.

VERIFIED — Fama-French 5-factor CMA. From Ken French's own data library
definitions page: "The investment ratio used to form portfolios in June of year
t is the change in total assets from the fiscal year ending in year t-2 to the
fiscal year ending in t-1, divided by t-2 total assets."

VERIFIED, AND IT MATTERS FOR THE PRIOR — HXZ's replication of this specific
anomaly is more fragile than "it survives" suggests. Under their stricter
method (NYSE breakpoints, value-weighted returns, 1967-2014, financials
excluded), their Table 4 reports the high-minus-low I/A decile at -0.46% per
month, t = -2.92. So it is significant at the conventional 5% level — one of
their survivors, not one of the 286 anomalies they find insignificant — but it
falls BELOW the t >= 3 hurdle they themselves advocate. They state directly
that this is "lower in magnitude than -1.05% (t = -5.04) with value-weighted
returns and -1.73% (t = -8.45) with equal-weighted returns reported by Cooper,
Gulen, and Schill (2008)". That quoted pair is HXZ characterizing CGS and is
the ONLY CGS magnitude stated anywhere in this family, deliberately at second
hand. Its alpha against their own q-factor model is 0.07 (t = 0.61), i.e. fully
absorbed — though that is close to mechanical, since I/A IS the q-model's
investment factor.

3. CROWDING — THE HONEST PRIOR, STATED BEFORE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the most heavily-institutionalized factor this project has ever tested,
and that is a reason for LOWER expectations, not higher. It is not merely
published: it is EMBEDDED AS A FACTOR in two of the standard models the
literature itself risk-adjusts against (HXZ's I/A, Fama-French's CMA), and is
consequently a named, deliberately-harvested leg in a large amount of
commercial systematic equity product. Anything a commercial risk model computes
from a balance sheet in one line, on the 500 most liquid names in the world, is
about as crowded as a public anomaly gets.

The crowding argument is not only a priori. Section 2's verified HXZ result
already shows the effect attenuated to t = -2.92 on 1967-2014 — below its own
replicators' significance bar. THIS family tests a window (2015-2026) beginning
after even that sample ends, on exactly the large-cap segment where crowding
should bite hardest, with a cross-section two orders of magnitude smaller.

PRIOR, on the record before any number exists: a genuine tradeable edge here is
UNLIKELY. The two interesting outcomes are (i) an honest negative, or (ii) an
apparent positive that turns out to be a sector bet — the exact failure mode
the sibling NOA family was caught in. The grid below is built so (ii) cannot be
reported as a success by accident.

4. DATA AND CONSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SOURCE: SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts, via the existing
edgar_xbrl_provider.EdgarXbrlProvider — the same fetch path, cache, rate limit
and originally-filed discipline as the sibling quality families. Prices from
yfinance daily history.

WHY THIS FAMILY'S DATA IS THE CLEANEST OF THE THREE XBRL FAMILIES: asset growth
needs exactly ONE line item, us-gaap `Assets`. That is the single universal tag
in this provider's design probe (ASSETS_TAGS is a one-element tuple annotated
"14/14 probe — the one universal tag"), and the sibling 2026-08-28 run measured
2,629 resolved annual `Assets` observations at 100% first-tier resolution, no
fallback tier ever needed. By contrast CbOP needs revenue AND cost-of-goods,
which financials do not tag (that run refused 53 of 168 priced tickers,
dominated by banks), and NOA needs cash plus common equity plus four zeroable
items. So this family ranks a strictly LARGER, less-selected cross-section than
either sibling, and does NOT structurally exclude financials.

THE COST OF THAT SIMPLICITY, and this family's single most important data
guard: total assets is the line item most violently corrupted by entity
discontinuity, and a GROWTH RATE is the worst possible functional form for it —
a ratio of exactly the two numbers a shell-to-operating-company transition
makes incomparable. The sibling verification pass found real cases in this very
sample: TechnipFMC filed 2015/2016 total assets of $74,100 against $28.3B in
FY2017; Linde filed $9.2M against $93.4B. As asset-growth inputs those are
growth rates of roughly +38,000,000% and +1,000,000%, which under any ranking
would pin those names to the short leg's extreme for a full year. This family
therefore imports and relies on ASSETS_SCALE_BREAK_RATIO (100x, from
cross_sectional_quality) and tests it directly.

WHAT THE GUARD DELIBERATELY DOES NOT REMOVE: genuine large M&A. CBOE's real
Bats-merger year was an 11x asset ratio and the sibling run kept it. Asset
growth financed by acquisition is part of the documented effect, not noise to
trim; keeping it is the faithful choice, and it does mean the short leg can be
concentrated in acquirers. The harness's MAX_WEIGHT_MULTIPLE = 3.0 cap bounds
how much any one such name can matter within its leg, which is what makes
magnitude leg-weighting survivable on a variable this right-skewed (asset
growth is bounded below at -100% and unbounded above).

POINT-IN-TIME: inherited unchanged from the sibling pipeline. Each value
becomes visible at the LATEST `filed` date among the observations used (in
practice the current year's 10-K submission date, never the fiscal period end);
values are originally-filed so restatements never rewrite history; the step
frame forward-fills from filing dates only and refuses a value carried past
FUNDAMENTAL_MAX_STALENESS_DAYS (455); look-ahead is structurally impossible
because the frame rides CrossSectionalData.fundamental_signal, which the
harness slices to rows <= the formation date.

UNIVERSE: the IDENTICAL seeded 200-ticker sample as both sibling quality
families (build_quality_sample, seed 20260828) drawn from the point-in-time
S&P 500 union universe, gated per formation by was_member. Deliberately
identical, so this family's result is about the same cross-section whose sector
composition has already been measured in detail, rather than a universe change
wearing a new family's clothes. HONEST COST: decile legs in the low tens
against the source literature's hundreds, and correspondingly noisy leg
returns. This family should rank MORE names than either sibling; the realized
count is measured per run, not assumed.

COSTS: 5 bps one-way (DEFAULT_XS_COST_BPS), identical to every S&P 500 equity
family here so Sharpes stay comparable. financing_bps_per_year = 0.0 — this
project's standing DISCLOSED optimism about short borrow, not an estimate.

5. THE PRE-DECLARED SPEC GRID — 12 DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    conditioning     {raw, industry_neutral}          2
  x holding period   {63, 126, 252} trading days      3
  x rank fraction    {decile 0.1, quintile 0.2}       2
  = 12 definitions, portfolio = long_short throughout.

n_trials = 12 for the deflated Sharpe. This family's OWN denominator, with no
carried trials from any other family, because this family is NOT a follow-up to
a positive found in this codebase — it is a fresh hypothesis from an
independent literature, and both its conditionings were fixed together before
any number was computed.

THE ONE DESIGN DEPARTURE FROM THE NOA PAIR, AND WHY. The sibling NOA family was
built RAW, came back positive (+0.46..+0.66 Sharpe, DSR 0.88..0.97), was
diagnosed post-hoc as a sector-composition artifact, and only then spawned a
separately pre-declared industry-neutral family that had to carry the raw
family's 9 trials into its own denominator (18) to stay honest about the
sequential search. That was handled correctly, but it was still a sequence: the
confound test was designed AFTER seeing the confounded result.

Asset growth carries the same confound risk for the same structural reason —
sector differences in balance-sheet growth are large and obvious, since capital
intensity IS an industry attribute — and this project already knows that.
Pre-declaring the neutral conditioning only after a raw positive would be
re-running a known trap on purpose. So BOTH conditionings are pre-declared here,
in ONE grid, under ONE denominator. The load-bearing consequence: a raw positive
CANNOT be reported as this family's finding without its industry-neutral
sibling, computed in the same run under the same denominator, agreeing. The
sector-artifact check is not a follow-up study, it is half the grid.

INDUSTRY-NEUTRAL CENTERING IS THE BUCKET MEDIAN, NOT THE MEAN, fixed in advance
for a stated reason: asset growth is severely right-skewed (bounded below at
-100%, unbounded above), so a bucket MEAN is dragged around by whichever peer
happened to close an acquisition that year — precisely the observation that
should NOT redefine what "normal growth for this industry" means. The median is
the robust center for a skewed variable, and it directly implements the natural
reading of the neutral hypothesis (growth relative to the industry's TYPICAL
growth). It is also consistent with what the sibling neutral family measured
(median demeaning outranked mean demeaning across its grid). MEAN CENTERING IS
NOT IN THIS GRID — it is not searched over, so it cannot be selected post-hoc.

Industry buckets are the sibling family's frozen 8-bucket SIC partition
(sic_to_bucket) on its POINT-IN-TIME SIC panel, built from archived filing SGML
headers keyed on each 10-K's real filing date — so the classification used at
any formation is the one publicly on file at that date, not today's.
MIN_BUCKET_SIZE = 3 is inherited: a name whose bucket has fewer than 3 ranked
members at a formation is refused rather than centered.

HOLDING PERIODS {63, 126, 252} and the exclusion of a 21-day hold are inherited
verbatim from the sibling reasoning: the ranking variable refreshes ANNUALLY
per firm (one 10-K a year), so a monthly hold re-pays turnover on an almost
unchanged ranking, while the cross-section still refreshes continuously through
the year as staggered fiscal calendars file. 252 is the source literature's own
annual rebalance.

long_universe_hedged IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT: hedging against the whole
(sector-imbalanced) eligible universe would reintroduce through the hedge leg
exactly the between-sector exposure the neutral conditioning removes from the
ranking, making the two halves of the grid non-comparable. The conditioning
axis replaces it as this family's second dimension.

6. PASS / FAIL THRESHOLD — FIXED NOW, BEFORE ANY RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This family is reported as a VALIDATED EDGE only if BOTH hold:

  (i)  the best spec's deflated Sharpe (DSR, computed at n_trials = 12) exceeds
       0.95; AND
  (ii) that spec's INDUSTRY-NEUTRAL counterpart, at the same holding period and
       the same rank fraction, also shows a materially positive Sharpe rather
       than collapsing toward zero.

Anything else is an honest negative, or an honest artifact, and is written up
as such. Condition (ii) is what makes the NOA failure mode unreportable here:
a raw positive that vanishes under industry neutralization is a sector bet, and
this family will say so plainly rather than reporting the raw number.

A well-documented honest negative is a FULLY SUCCESSFUL OUTCOME by this
project's standards, and roughly 25 of this codebase's ~28 tested families have
been exactly that. There is no result this pre-registration is hoping for.

EVERY ONE of the 12 specs is persisted to the cross_sectional_trial_results
table under family_key "asset_growth" regardless of outcome — winners, losers
and nulls alike. No spec is dropped, re-run, or re-specified after seeing its
number. If a bug is found after the run, the fix is re-running the WHOLE grid
and replacing the whole run tag, never patching individual rows.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT A NEGATIVE WILL AND WILL NOT LICENSE US TO SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Written now so the results document cannot quietly overclaim later.

A negative here will NOT refute Cooper/Gulen/Schill. Their sort is a broad-
universe sort with hundreds of names per decile over a multi-decade sample;
this is ~150 large-cap names over ~11.6 years with decile legs in the low tens,
in a post-publication window, on a factor since institutionalized into two
standard models. Anomaly decay after publication, absence in the large-cap
segment specifically, and simple lack of statistical power in this cross-
section would ALL be consistent with a null, and this dataset cannot
distinguish between them. Any results document that claims otherwise is wrong.

What a negative WILL establish is the only question that actually matters for
this project: not tradeable by us, on our universe, now — and that asset growth
should not be re-tested here without genuinely new data (a materially wider
cross-section, or a longer pre-publication history) or a genuinely different
hypothesis, carrying these 12 trials into the denominator of anything that does.
