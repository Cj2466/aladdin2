RESULT: 31/31 instruments chained successfully -- but only after finding and
fixing a REAL defect. The first real-data run failed 3/31.

2a. THE DEFECT (real, found by running, not predicted)
    First run: LE, ZF, ZT all failed with
      "contract 'X' is not priced on both <day-1> and <day>; a roll needs an
       overlap day on which the incoming contract is already quoted"

    Root cause: CME's SPAN archive prints a placeholder Type-82 record for a
    not-yet-configured far contract month, carrying settle == 0.0 AND a bogus
    expiration_date equal to its own trade date. Under the calendar/expiry-
    order roll rule, that self-referential expiry is the SMALLEST of all
    candidates, so the stub looks like "the front month expiring today" and
    the rule tries to roll into a contract with no real price.

    IMPORTANT AND GOOD: chained_contract_daily_returns REFUSED to guess and
    raised, rather than silently emitting a fabricated return. The failure is
    how this was caught. That refusal behaviour is working exactly as
    intended and should not be relaxed.

    The module docstring already described this hazard and the settle==0.0
    remedy, but the FILTER WAS NOT ACTUALLY IMPLEMENTED in
    build_holding_series() -- documentation had run ahead of code.

2b. THE FIX, INDEPENDENTLY VALIDATED BEFORE BEING TRUSTED
    Zero-settle rows are now excluded from roll-rule candidacy (prices are
    retained; only candidacy is filtered), with a diagnostics counter.

    I did not simply accept that this was safe. I recomputed the held-contract
    series for ALL 31 instruments both with and without the filter and diffed
    them date by date. Across the entire 2013-2025 universe exactly THREE
    held-contract dates change:

        LE  2016-06-14   201710 (stub) -> 201606
        ZF  2015-09-25   201612 (stub) -> 201509
        ZT  2015-09-25   201612 (stub) -> 201509

    Every other date's roll decision is bit-identical. The fix is surgical,
    not a blanket loosening that could mask other problems.

    I also verified the zero-settle population directly: 72 zero-settle rows
    total, splitting 3 stubs / 14 already-expired (already excluded by the
    pre-existing not-yet-expired filter) / 55 far-deferred (nowhere near the
    minimum expiry on their date). Only the 3 stubs carry BOTH a zero settle
    and a self-referential expiration_date.

    Test added: tests/test_chain_cme_span_continuous.py.

2c. SPOT-CHECKS ON THE OUTPUT (the requested verification)
    - Synthetic known-answer validation ran first and passed:
      worst_delta = 1.42e-14. The script refuses to touch real data if this
      fails.

    - INTERNAL CONSISTENCY: pct_change(ratio_adjusted_close) reproduces
      chained_daily_return to a worst-case 1.34e-14 across all 31
      instruments. The back-adjusted price series and the return series are
      the same object viewed two ways.

    - NO BACK-ADJUSTMENT DISCONTINUITY: two rolls hand-checked against the

      computed from the SAME contract on both sides (the defining property of
      MOP 2.1 chaining -- a roll gap must never enter a return):
        CL 2020-04-22 (rolled 202005 -> 202006):
            hand 13.78/11.57-1 = 0.191011   script 0.191011
        GC 2016-05-27 (rolled 201605 -> 201606):
            hand 1213.8/1220.4-1 = -0.00540806  script -0.00540806
      Both exact. The back-adjustment introduces no artificial jump of its
      own.

    - ROLL COUNTS ARE STRUCTURALLY SENSIBLE over 12.7 years:
        ~152 rolls: CL BZ NG HO RB GC SI HG PA PL   (monthly cycle)
        ~49-50    : ES NQ YM ZB ZN ZF ZT 6S         (quarterly cycle)
        ~118      : 6A 6B 6C 6E 6J                  (FX, serial months)

    - STAGGERED INCEPTION: RTY is the only staggered instrument and is handled

2d. KNOWN LIMITATIONS, STATED PLAINLY
    (i) CL 2020-04-20 BREAKS RATIO BACK-ADJUSTMENT. The -37.63 settle is real,
        and it is the ONLY negative settlement in the whole universe. Ratio
        returns are not well defined through a negative price: the series
        reports -305.97% on 2020-04-20, then -126.60% on 2020-04-21 even
        though the price ROSE that day (-37.63 -> +10.01), and the adjusted
        close itself goes negative. These two figures are artifacts of the
        ratio convention, not economic returns. Handled explicitly as a
        sensitivity in Job 3 rather than ignored (it does not change the gate
        verdict). A future TSMOM build on CL would need the Panama/difference
        adjustment (already available in the codebase) or explicit
        negative-price handling. FLAGGED, NOT FIXED HERE -- changing the
        adjustment method is a methodology decision, not a cleanup.

   (ii) ROLL RULE DEVIATION FROM MOP, already disclosed: MOP 2012 rolls into
        the MOST LIQUID contract; no free source provides per-contract volume,
        so this uses the calendar front month. Visible consequence: the five
        FX roots roll ~118 times vs 6S's ~50, because CME lists serial months
        for the majors and a pure calendar rule picks them up even though
        liquidity concentrates in the quarterlies. This is a real fidelity gap
        that would need disclosure in any Layer 2 mechanism-fidelity writeup.
