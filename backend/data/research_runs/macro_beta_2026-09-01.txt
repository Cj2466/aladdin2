HEADLINE, STATED BEFORE THE DETAIL SO IT CANNOT BE BURIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The pre-registered test passed 6 of 26. A post-hoc diagnostic then showed that
4 of those 6 are substantially explained by a confound the pre-registration
did not fully guard against, and the pre-registration's own claim of immunity
to it was too broad. After that control only china_fxi survives, at roughly
40% of its headline magnitude (rho +0.199 -> +0.082).

So the honest verdict for this family is NOT "6 drivers work". It is:

  * 20 of 26 pre-registered tests: NO DEMONSTRATED SKILL. Honest negative.
  * 4 of 26 (copper_cper x2, credit_spread x2): passed the pre-registered
    test, then did NOT survive a market-beta control. Reported as a positive
    that failed its first adversarial check, not as a finding.
  * 2 of 26 (china_fxi x2): passed both, at a small magnitude.
  * The lookup table itself was built successfully: 6,526 rows across all 13
    drivers and 502 of 503 tickers, on real data, with zero fetch failures.

The most-significant raw result in the whole run — credit_spread at
rho +0.29, p = 3.5e-10 — is also the one the diagnostic most completely
demolishes: its beta is 97.5% collinear with plain market beta.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE MOST IMPORTANT CAVEAT: THIS IS A CONTEMPORANEOUS TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Stated plainly because "forecast quality" is easy to misread. The statistic
compares a beta fit on window A against returns on the SAME DAY as the window-B
driver move. It is genuinely out-of-sample in the BETA (the beta never saw
window B), but it is NOT a forecast of tomorrow. It answers "when a shock
happens, does a previously-fit beta describe which names move", which is the
question the intended consumer actually asks — and it is NOT tradeable on its
own, because acting on it requires knowing the driver's move for the day.

No claim about tradeability is made here in either direction. No cost model,
no liquidity model and no portfolio exists anywhere in this family.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. A PRE-REGISTRATION CLAIM THAT WAS TOO BROAD — RECORDED, NOT QUIETLY FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pre-registration section 7.3 said, of the per-day Spearman statistic:

    "Spearman is invariant to subtracting a constant from every y in a day, so
     rho_d is AUTOMATICALLY immune to the market-direction confound."

The invariance is real and the sentence is true as literally written. But it is
NARROWER THAN IT SOUNDS, and reading it as "immune to market effects generally"
would be wrong. It immunises against the market LEVEL shift. It does NOT
immunise against the market-BETA channel:

    A stock's beta to copper is largely a proxy for its beta to the MARKET.
    On a day copper falls hard the market usually falls too, and
    high-market-beta names fall most. Orienting by sign(driver move) then
    lines those names up exactly where a positive rank correlation needs
    them — with no copper-specific information involved at all.

This was not spotted when the pre-registration was written. It was spotted when
the results came back and the three passing drivers turned out to be precisely
the three that co-move most with equities — a pattern too on-the-nose to
accept. Section 6 is the resulting check. Recording the gap here rather than
silently patching the document is the point; the pre-registration is unedited.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. POST-HOC DIAGNOSTIC — NOT PRE-REGISTERED, AND ABLE ONLY TO WEAKEN A CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

METHOD (verify_macro_beta_market_confound.py): identical test, except the
market-beta channel is partialled out of BOTH sides cross-sectionally. Market
proxy = the equal-weighted mean return of the universe itself. Each ticker's
market beta is fit over the SAME window A with the SAME estimator; on each
shock day, both the driver beta and the oriented return are replaced by their
cross-sectional residuals after regressing on market beta, and the Spearman is
recomputed. Same one-sided t-test over days, same alpha.

THIS IS POST-HOC. It was designed after seeing the result. It is legitimate to
run in that order ONLY because of its asymmetry: it can undercut a
pre-registered positive, and it CANNOT create a new one (section 6b).

                                  mkt beta)                pass    rho       pass

  copper_cper        shock_days      +0.693     +0.1661    yes     -0.0036    NO
  china_fxi          full_sample     +0.792     +0.1985    yes     +0.0816    yes

  credit_spread      shock_days      -0.964     +0.2816    yes     -0.0023    NO

FINDING: 4 of the 6 pre-registered positives do not survive.

  * credit_spread is the starkest. Its beta is -0.975 correlated with plain
    market beta — the HY-OAS beta essentially IS the market beta with a sign
    flip. Its headline p of 3.5e-10, the strongest number in the entire run,
    falls to rho +0.034 (full_sample) and -0.002 (shock_days). The apparent
    finding was the market-beta channel almost in its entirety.

    HONEST COUNTER-CAVEAT, stated because it cuts against the conclusion just
    drawn: at |corr| = 0.975 the residual driver beta retains only ~5% of its
    original variance, so this partial test has little power BY CONSTRUCTION.
    "Does not survive" here is partly a statement about power, not proof that
    no credit-specific effect exists. What it does establish is that this run
    cannot distinguish a credit-spread effect from a market-beta effect — and
    an indistinguishable finding is not a usable one.

  * copper_cper collapses from +0.207 to +0.041 (and the shock-day variant to
    -0.004). Same story at corr +0.80.

  * china_fxi SURVIVES both variants (+0.082 and +0.079, both under the
    Bonferroni alpha) but at roughly 40% of its headline magnitude. This is
    the only pre-registered positive left standing after the obvious control.
    A rank correlation of 0.08 is small.

6b. THE DIAGNOSTIC ALSO FLIPS NINE NEGATIVES POSITIVE — AND THAT IS NOT A
    FINDING, IT IS A HYPOTHESIS

Under the same market-beta control, nine tests that FAILED the pre-registered
test would pass: oil_uso (both, partial rho +0.135/+0.145), broad_commod_dbc
(both, +0.153/+0.150), rate_dgs10 (both, +0.134/+0.130), breakeven_t10yie
(both, +0.113/+0.103) and dollar_broad (full_sample, +0.105). For oil the raw
statistic was actually NEGATIVE (-0.056) and the control turns it positive —
consistent with the market-beta channel MASKING a driver-specific effect rather
than manufacturing one.

THIS CANNOT BE CLAIMED, AND IS NOT CLAIMED. It is a post-hoc result from a
specification chosen after seeing the data, it carries no correction for that
additional search, and pre-registration section 9 explicitly forbids reading a
negative as evidence that "a different estimator would also fail" — the
symmetric restraint applies here too. It is recorded as a genuinely
interesting HYPOTHESIS for a future, separately-pre-registered family:
"market-beta-orthogonalised macro betas". Nothing downstream may treat these
nine as positives.

Summary line from the diagnostic run: 6/26 pass raw, 11/26 survive the
market-beta control — but the two sets overlap in only 2 tests, and the 9
non-overlapping ones are post-hoc.

7. WHAT THIS RUN LICENSES US TO SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LICENSED:
  * The lookup table is built, on real data, for all 13 drivers and 502 of 503
    names, with documented gates and no fabricated values.
  * Over this specific 252/252 split, 20 of 26 pre-registered tests showed no
    demonstrated out-of-sample skill.
  * Of the 6 that passed, 4 are not distinguishable from a plain market-beta
    effect, and the strongest of them is the least distinguishable.
  * china_fxi retains a small, control-surviving relationship. Suggestive, not
    established — one split, ~26 test days, rho ~0.08.

NOT LICENSED (and the pre-registration fixed these in advance):
  * "These drivers do not affect equities." A univariate daily beta is a weak
    instrument and ~26 test days is a small sample.
  * "The beta table is useless." It is a descriptive artifact and remains one.
    What this run bounds is how much a later phase may LEAN on it.
  * Anything about tradeability, in either direction.
  * Any claim built on section 6b's nine post-hoc positives.

RECOMMENDATION TO THE PHASE THAT CONSUMES THIS TABLE: treat a driver beta as
a description of co-movement, and assume a large part of it is ordinary market
beta unless orthogonalised. For copper, credit spreads, agriculture, broad
commodities, breakeven inflation and 10Y yields the driver beta is 0.74-0.98
correlated with market beta cross-sectionally; picking "top names by |beta|"
on those drivers will largely return a list of high-beta cyclicals.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. RESIDUAL LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * SURVIVORSHIP. Today's 503 constituents, as pre-registered and defended in
    section 4 of that document. Betas measured are the betas OF SURVIVORS.
  * ONE SPLIT. A single 252/252 fit/test division ending 2026-08-31. No
    walk-forward, no second period. Every p-value is conditional on it.
  * SHORT TEST WINDOW. 25-40 shock days per driver. The day is correctly the
    unit of observation, which is why n is small — pooling ticker-days would
    have inflated n to ~13,000 and manufactured significance.
  * UNIVARIATE BY DESIGN. Each beta is a single-driver regression, so the 13
    are not mutually orthogonal and their betas overlap heavily (section 6).
  * PLAIN OLS t-stats on the persisted rows are not HAC/heteroskedasticity-
    robust and are in-sample. They gate nothing.
  * credit_spread has ~3 years of available history, ever, by FRED licence.
  * REGIME. Window B is a single year (2025-08 to 2026-08). A different macro
    regime could give different betas; that is why the table is append-only
    and refit weekly rather than frozen.
