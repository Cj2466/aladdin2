1. CORRECTIONS TO THE DISPATCH BRIEF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. THE MULTIPLIER IS NOT "3-8"; THAT IS THE ROBUSTNESS RANGE, NOT THE HEADLINE.
    The brief says 'their headline "multiplier" estimate' is "roughly $3-8".
    [GK21] abstract, verbatim: "investing $1 in the stock market increases the
    market's aggregate value by about $5." The headline POINT estimates are
    Table 2: M = 7.08 (s.e. 1.86) with one principal component and M = 5.28
    (s.e. 1.10) with two. The range 3.5-8 is a different object -- [GK21]
    Section 4.2 "Robustness", verbatim: "our results are robust to these
    changes in the empirical strategy with multiplier estimates ranging from
    3.5 to 8." So "3-8" is approximately right as a ROBUSTNESS INTERVAL and
    wrong as a headline. [GK22] carries the identical numbers (7.1 / 5.3 /
    "around M = 5" / "ranging from 3.5 to 8"), so the revision changed neither.

C2. THE PAPER'S PRIMARY IDENTIFICATION DOES NOT REQUIRE PROPRIETARY DATA;
    ITS SECONDARY ONE DOES.
    The brief worried the "IV identification strategy cannot be honestly
    approximated with available free data." Split the two:
      Section 4.2 (sector-level, THE headline Table 2) uses ONLY Flow of Funds
        sector holdings of equities -- free. FEASIBLE.
      Section 4.3 (investor-level) uses 13F from FactSet and mutual-fund flows
        from Morningstar -- both paid. NOT feasible, NOT attempted.
    This family implements 4.2 only, and says so.

C3. THE BRIEF'S FRAMING -- "if it's testable at all here, it would be a
    market-timing overlay ... tilt aggregate equity exposure based on a
    measured aggregate-flow signal" -- DOES NOT FOLLOW FROM THE PAPER, AND THE
    PAPER SAYS SO ITSELF. This is the single most important correction and it
    is set out in full in Section 2. It does not make the candidate
    untestable, but it changes what the test MEANS: a null is the paper's OWN
    PREDICTION, not a refutation of it.

C4. "n" IS NOT LIKE THE CROSS-SECTIONAL FAMILIES, BUT NOT FOR THE REASON THE
    BRIEF SUGGESTS. The brief anticipated "far fewer independent time-series
    observations." True -- but the binding constraint measured in Section 9 is
    NOT that the test is underpowered to the point of being untestable. It is
    testable. That was CHECKED NUMERICALLY BEFORE deciding to build (Section

2. THE DECISIVE PROBLEM, STATED BEFORE ANY TEST IS RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PAPER'S ESTIMAND IS CONTEMPORANEOUS, AND ITS PRICE IMPACT IS PERMANENT.
Neither fact is compatible with a return-predictability claim, and BOTH are
the authors' own, not this project's inference.

(a) The headline regression, [GK21] eq. (32), is same-quarter:

        dp_t = M * Z_t + e_t

    Z_t (the granular instrument, eq. 31) and dp_t (the quarter's return) carry
    the SAME time subscript. Nothing is forecast.

(b) The horizon extension, eq. (38), is
        p_{t+h} - p_{t-1} = a_h + M_h * Z_t + c_h * eta_t + d_h * dy_t + eps
    Its left-hand side WINDOW STARTS AT t-1, so M_h is a CUMULATIVE impact that
    already contains the contemporaneous quarter. [GK21] Section 4.2 on the
    result, verbatim: "We find that the cumulative impact is fairly stable over
    time. This is intuitive as sharp reversals would imply a strong negative
    autocorrelation in returns, which is not something that we observe for the
    aggregate stock market at a quarterly frequency."
    M_h approximately flat in h is ARITHMETICALLY the statement that
    E[p_{t+h} - p_t | Z_t] is approximately 0 for h >= 1: no post-shock drift
    to ride, no reversal to fade.

(c) The same section rules out the other direction too, verbatim: "Size-
    weighted sector-specific demand shocks are also not correlated with returns
    at t-1 (that is, h = -1)."

(d) And the authors state the tradability implication themselves. [GK22],
    "How tenets of finance change if the inelastic markets hypothesis is
    correct", verbatim:
        "a one-time, non mean-reverting inflow permanently changes prices (as
         in p = f/zeta), even if it contains no information whatsoever. ...
         The typical empirical strategy to look for reversals as signs of flows
         (rather than information) moving prices does not work in this case. By
         the same logic, we can see large changes in prices but small changes
         in long-horizon expected returns."

    "Large changes in prices but small changes in long-horizon expected
    returns" is, in the authors' own words, the absence of the thing a timing
    overlay would need to harvest.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THIS FAMILY. The Inelastic Markets Hypothesis is a theory
of price FORMATION -- a causal attribution of aggregate volatility to flows --
not a theory of return PREDICTABILITY. There is no alpha leg in the source to
replicate. Accordingly:

  * The registered hypothesis in Section 6 is NOT [GK21]'s claim. It is an
    ADJACENT claim that [GK21] predicts to be FALSE.
  * A null result therefore CONFIRMS [GK21] and is recorded as a DEFINITE
    NEGATIVE FOR TRADABILITY, explicitly NOT as evidence against the inelastic
    markets hypothesis, which this project has no way to falsify and is not
    trying to.
  * A NON-null result would be the surprising outcome and would be treated
    with more suspicion, not less (Section 10).

WHY BUILD AT ALL, THEN. Three reasons, all pre-declared:
  1. The mechanism-fidelity question is real and answerable: CAN this project
     measure the multiplier at all with free data (Section 7, gate G2)? That is
     a genuine, citable finding either way and has never been attempted here.
  2. The permanence claim in (b) is an empirical claim this project can test
     directly and cheaply on its own data, and testing it is the honest way to
     reach the tradability verdict -- rather than asserting it from the paper.
  3. Section 9 shows the test is adequately powered, so declining to run it
     would be a choice not to look.

3. THE SECOND, INDEPENDENT BLOCKER: PUBLICATION LAG (MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Z_t is built from the Fed's Financial Accounts (Z.1), which is quarterly and
published with a long lag. MEASURED from the Fed's own release page on
2026-09-08, verbatim: "Release Date: June 11, 2026   2026:Q1 Release".

  2026:Q1 ends 2026-03-31. Released 2026-06-11. Lag = 72 days.

A quarter is ~91 days, so Z_t lands ~79% of the way through quarter t+1. It is
therefore NOT available at the start of t+1. The first quarter whose ENTIRE
return can be traded on Z_t is t+2.

PRE-DECLARED AVAILABILITY RULE (binding, no exceptions):

    the position held for the whole of quarter t+2 may depend only on
    Z_s for s <= t, i.e. an availability lag of TWO FULL QUARTERS.

This is the conservative reading and is applied uniformly to every spec. Note
it also makes the contemporaneous relationship of eq. (32) untradable by
construction, independently of Section 2 -- these are two separate, each
individually sufficient, obstacles.

REVISIONS ARE A THIRD, UNRESOLVED LOOK-AHEAD. Z.1 is revised, sometimes
substantially, and this study uses the single June-2026 vintage for all
history. A true point-in-time test would need the vintage available at each
date. The Fed does publish prior vintages, but assembling a full PIT panel was
NOT done here. CONSEQUENCE, STATED PLAINLY: the backtest is biased TOWARD the
strategy (it sees final, revised data). A negative result is therefore SAFE
(the bias worked in the strategy's favour and it still failed); a positive
result would be UNINTERPRETABLE without redoing this on PIT vintages, and
Section 10's verdict rule reflects that asymmetry.

D7. WHY NOT THE PROJECT'S OWN N-PORT INFRASTRUCTURE (the brief asked
    explicitly, so this is answered explicitly).
    NOT USED, and the reason is not convenience. cross_sectional_nport_flow.py
    aggregates SEC N-PORT fund-level holdings -- US registered investment
    companies. Aggregating it up would produce a mutual-fund/ETF flow series
    covering, on this same Z.1 data, sectors 65 and 56 only. Those are two of
    sixteen holder sectors. [GK21]'s entire methodological point is that GIV
    needs the WHOLE cross-section of sectors, because the instrument
    Z = q_S - q_E (eq. 31) is a size-weighted MINUS equal-weighted contrast
    that is only defined relative to the full set of holders; computing it on a
    2-sector subset does not approximate it, it computes a different and
    meaningless quantity. [GK21] Section 4.1 also states the requirement
    directly, verbatim: "we need large idiosyncratic shocks, and a few large
    institutions, so that the market is 'granular'". Additionally N-PORT begins
    in 2019, which would cut the sample from ~118 quarters to ~26 and make the
    test genuinely underpowered. So the reuse the brief suggested would have
    been strictly worse on coverage, on fidelity to the estimator, and on
    power. Z.1 is used instead -- which is also, per Section 2.1, the paper's
    OWN source for its headline table, so this is the more faithful choice as
    well as the better-powered one.

5. CONSTRUCTION, EQUATION BY EQUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All equation numbers are [GK21]'s.

S1. Holdings growth, the paper's dq_it, [GK21] Section 4.1 verbatim
    ("Suppose that we have a time series of changes in investors' equity
    holdings, dq_it = (Q_it - Q_i,t-1)/Q_i,t-1"):

        dq_it = (Q_it - Q_i,t-1) / Q_i,t-1

    with Q_it the LM market-value level of sector i's corporate-equity
    holdings at quarter t.

    NOTE, DISCLOSED: because Q is a MARKET-VALUE level, dq_it mixes the
    sector's trading with the market's own return. That is exactly what
    [GK21] intends -- eq. (28) dq_it = -zeta*dp_t + f^nu_it models precisely
    that mixture, and the -zeta*dp_t term IS the valuation effect. The GIV
    contrast in S3 differences it away because it is common across sectors.
    This is why the estimator can work off holdings alone; [GK21] footnote 42
    verbatim: "we can implement the GIV procedure using dq_it, which does not
    require knowledge of holdings in other assets than equities."

S2. Size shares, eq. (27):
        S_i,t-1 = Q_i,t-1 / sum_j Q_j,t-1        (sums to 1 by construction)
        dq_Et = (1/N) sum_i dq_it                (equal-weighted)
        dq_St = sum_i S_i,t-1 * dq_it            (size-weighted)

S3. The granular instrument. Uniform-loadings case, eq. (31):
        Z_t = dq_Gamma,t = dq_St - dq_Et

    General case with non-uniform loadings, eqs. (34)-(35): demean
    cross-sectionally, dq_check_it = dq_it - dq_Et, extract r principal
    components eta_t from the panel of dq_check_it, and run
        dp_t = M * Z_t + beta' * eta_t + e_t
    estimating M as the coefficient on Z_t. r is a grid dimension (Section 8),
    set to 1 and 2 -- exactly the two columns of [GK21] Table 2.

S4. Controls, eq. (37): the regression includes real GDP growth and a time
    trend alongside the extracted latent factors, as [GK21] Section 4.2
    specifies ("The vector eta_t includes GDP growth, a time trend, and one or
    more latent factors"). The time trend's presence is [GK21] footnote 47
    ("We include a time trend as some sectors grew faster in the nineties").

S5. THE TRADABLE SIGNAL. Z_t is standardized to a z-score using ONLY
    information available at the decision date (Section 3's two-quarter lag
    plus a backward-looking window), then mapped to a weight by the position
    rule. Full detail in Section 8. No full-sample moment is ever used to
    standardize; the PCA question is handled explicitly in gate G3.

6. THE REGISTERED HYPOTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H: The granular aggregate equity-flow shock Z_t, lagged to real-world
   availability (two full quarters, Section 3), predicts the subsequent
   quarterly excess return of the aggregate US stock market with enough
   strength to survive costs and this project's DSR ladder.

DIRECTION IS PRE-DECLARED, NOT SEARCHED. The registered sign is POSITIVE
(continuation): a positive size-weighted idiosyncratic demand shock is
registered as predicting a HIGHER subsequent market excess return. Rationale,
and it is a weak one, stated as such: [GK21] Section 3.2 and Proposition 5
have flows that are persistent rather than mean-reverting, and persistent
inflows keep pushing prices up, so IF anything leaks into the following
quarters it should be continuation rather than reversal. The negative
(reversal) direction is NOT in the registered grid. It will be computed and
reported EXPLORATORY-ONLY, clearly labelled, never as a registered verdict --
matching the coval_stafford EXPLORATORY precedent.

RESTATING SECTION 2 SO IT CANNOT BE LOST: [GK21] predicts H IS FALSE. This
family is registered expecting a negative.

7. BINDING GATES (checked BEFORE the verdict; each can stop the family)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
G1. SECTOR-PARTITION INTEGRITY. The 16 series in D2 must actually partition
    the market. Check: for every quarter, compare sum of the 16 against
    LM893064105 ("All sectors; corporate equities; asset").
    PASS if the median absolute relative discrepancy over 1997Q1-2026Q1 is
    <= 2.0%. If it FAILS, the partition is wrong, the size weights S_i are
    wrong, and the family STOPS and reports that -- it does not get patched by
    hunting for a better-fitting sector list, which would be fitting the
    universe to the answer.

G2. MECHANISM FIDELITY -- CAN WE REPRODUCE THE PAPER AT ALL?
    Estimate M by eq. (35) on the paper's OWN sample, 1993Q1-2018Q4, with 1
    and 2 principal components, and compare to [GK21] Table 2 (7.08 and 5.28)
    and to its disclosed robustness range.
    PASS if at least one of the two point estimates falls in [3.5, 8.0], the
    range [GK21] Section 4.2 itself reports.
    This gate is INFORMATIONAL FOR THE TRADING VERDICT but DECISIVE FOR WHAT
    THE FAMILY MAY CLAIM. If G2 fails, this project cannot measure the
    mechanism with free data, and the family reports "mechanism not
    reproducible here" and makes NO claim about the inelastic markets
    hypothesis in either direction. The trading test still runs and is still
    reported, but is then explicitly labelled as a test of a signal whose
    construction could not be validated against its source.
    NOTE: this is a replication on the paper's own dependent variable, not a
    strategy return, and it is deliberately placed BEFORE the strategy so that
    a construction bug is caught by the paper's number rather than by a
    Sharpe.

G3. NO-LOOK-AHEAD IN THE PCA. The general-case GIV (eq. 34) extracts principal
    components from the panel. Doing that once on the full sample and then
    trading on it would leak the future into every historical position.
    BINDING RULE: for every tradable spec, the PCA and every standardization
    moment are computed RECURSIVELY on data through the decision date only.
    The full-sample PCA is used ONLY for the G2 replication, which is not a
    trading result and is labelled in-sample.
    A spec that cannot be computed recursively is dropped, not approximated.

9. COSTS, STREAMS, AND POWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
COST ARMS -- three, exactly as margin_credit_timing.py, verdict on the middle:
    gross      cost_bps = 0    attribution only, NEVER a verdict input
    baseline   cost_bps = 2.0  one-way on SPY notional traded  <- THE VERDICT
    stress     cost_bps = 10.0 one-way, a deliberate unsourced stress
2.0bp one-way is well above SPY's own quoted spread and is the same constant
this project already committed to for its other one-instrument SPY overlay.
Costs enter the return stream and therefore the DSR directly, per CLAUDE.md
("Cost realism must feed into the DSR calculation itself ... not sit as a side
disclosure"), not as a footnote.

TWO STREAMS, VERDICT ON THE OVERLAY -- the rule margin_credit_timing.py
established and the reason is unchanged: a long-only equity timer is long
equities most of the time, so a DSR on the strategy stream would largely be
measuring the equity risk premium and reporting it as a signal.
    strategy_{t+1} = w_t       * (R_SPY - RF)_{t+1} - costs
    overlay_{t+1}  = (w_t - 1) * (R_SPY - RF)_{t+1} - costs   <- THE VERDICT
Both are always computed and always reported. A spec whose strategy Sharpe
beats buy-and-hold but whose overlay DSR fails is a FAIL.

QUARTERLY, NOT MONTHLY OR DAILY. periods_per_year = 4 everywhere. The signal
can change at most once a quarter; marking the same quarterly bet monthly
would multiply the observation count feeding PSR/DSR by 3 without adding one
independent draw. This is the conservative choice and makes the bar harder.

POWER, COMPUTED BEFORE BUILDING (this is the check C4 refers to).
Using this project's own deflated_sharpe.probabilistic_sharpe_ratio and
expected_max_sharpe_under_noise, with Gaussian skew/kurtosis and the null
dispersion sigma_SR = sqrt(1/(T-1)), the MINIMUM ANNUALIZED NET SHARPE needed
to reach DSR >= 0.95 is:

              N=24     N=37     N=362    N=1031
   T=104     0.725    0.761    0.923    0.986
   T=133     0.638    0.670    0.812    0.868

At the expected T (~115-118) the strictest rung demands roughly a 0.9
annualized net Sharpe on the OVERLAY stream. That is a demanding bar for a
quarterly market timer but it is NOT unreachable, so this test is adequately
powered and "underpowered" is NOT available as an excuse for a null. It also
means a null here is INFORMATIVE about tradability at this project's bar --
though it does NOT rule out an edge smaller than ~0.9 Sharpe, and the write-up
must say so rather than overclaiming.

10. VERDICT RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DSR ladder: dsr_policy_n.py, i.e. dsr_n_trials(n_local=24) followed by the
pooled rungs {37, 362, 1031}. Bar 0.95. Two-tier verdict per CLAUDE.md:

  fails even at the most lenient (family-local) N            -> DEFINITE_NEGATIVE
  passes at lenient N, fails at a more conservative N        -> UNRESOLVED
  passes even at the most conservative N measured            -> real pass

preservation_score.compute_preservation_metrics is computed for every spec, no
exceptions, and reported whatever it says.

THE ASYMMETRY REQUIRED BY SECTION 3, PRE-DECLARED. The backtest uses a single
revised Z.1 vintage, which biases IN FAVOUR of the strategy. Therefore:
  * A NEGATIVE verdict is accepted at face value and is final -- the bias ran
    the strategy's way and it still failed.
  * A POSITIVE or UNRESOLVED verdict MAY NOT be reported as a pass. It is
    reported as "POSITIVE, BUT CONFOUNDED BY REVISION LOOK-AHEAD -- requires a
    point-in-time Z.1 vintage panel before it means anything," and the
    point-in-time rebuild is named as required further work. This is fixed
    NOW, before the result is known, so that a favourable number cannot be
    promoted after the fact.

NOTHING IS REGISTERED OR PROMOTED BY THIS FAMILY. Per CLAUDE.md rule 6 no
live/forward-validation status is created or changed here under any outcome.
This is a research recommendation only.

WHAT A NULL DOES AND DOES NOT MEAN, fixed here so it cannot drift later:
  DOES mean  -- the inelastic-markets flow shock, as measurable from free Z.1
                data at realistic publication lag, does not yield a
                market-timing edge at this project's evidentiary bar.
  DOES NOT   -- refute the inelastic markets hypothesis. Section 2 shows a null
     mean       is what [GK21] predicts. A null is CONSISTENT WITH the paper
                being entirely correct.

   Section 5 (S1-S5) was written from [GK21]'s MAIN TEXT, eqs. (27)/(31)/(34)/
   (35). Those are an exposition of the idea. The recipe that actually produces
   Table 2 is Appendix B.2, steps 1-5 (eqs. 67-69), which differs in four ways
   that all matter:
     (i)   the "equal-weight" leg is INVERSE-VARIANCE ("pseudo-equal") weights
           E~_i, winsorised at 1.5/N and renormalised -- not equal weights;
     (ii)  THE CORPORATE SECTOR IS EXCLUDED from the instrument. B.2 step 1
           verbatim: "We exclude the corporate sector in constructing the
           instrument." It is the supply side, whose elasticity zeta_C is
           estimated separately in eq. (71);
     (iii) dq_check is the residual of a WEIGHTED PANEL REGRESSION, eq. (67),
           with sector FE, TIME FE, sector-specific GDP loadings and sector-
           specific trends -- not a simple cross-sectional demeaning;
     (iv)  the principal components are CONTROLS in eq. (69); they do NOT
           residualise Z, which comes straight from eq. (68).
   The tell was that n_pcs=1 and n_pcs=2 gave near-identical multipliers (1.796
   vs 1.846), impossible if the PCs genuinely entered Z. Correcting to B.2 gave
   2.526 / 2.754. The module implements B.2; section 5 above is retained
   unedited as the honest record of what was believed at registration time.

B. THE n_pcs GRID DIMENSION IS DEGENERATE, AND THAT IS CORRECT.
   Under eq. (68) Z is built from the eq. (67) residuals alone, so pcs1 and
   pcs2 are byte-identical (verified: max |Z1 - Z2| = 0.0). The section 8 grid
   therefore contains 12 UNIQUE specs, not 24. n_local is LEFT AT 24 as
   registered: that overstates the search and makes the DSR bar harder, which
   is the conservative direction. [GK21] footnote 70's alternative construction
   makes n_pcs bite and was run as an exploratory arm.

C. SECTOR ADMISSION HAD TO BE DEFINED; SECTION 4/D2's 16 ARE NOT ALL USABLE.
   Not anticipated at registration. Several Z.1 holder sectors are reported as
   EXACTLY ZERO (not missing) for long stretches, and dq = (Q_t - Q_{t-1})/
   Q_{t-1} is undefined on a zero base. The rule adopted -- mechanical, and
   fixed before any return was computed -- is: admit a sector iff its holdings
   are strictly positive in every quarter from one quarter BEFORE the panel
   start onward. From 1993Q1 this admits 12 of 16 and excludes federal
   government (positive only from 2008Q3), hedge funds (0 in 250 of 303
   quarters; [GK21] S2.2 notes the FoF household sector INCLUDES hedge funds,
   so they are not lost), security brokers/dealers (a NET series that goes
   negative), and ETFs (first positive in exactly 1993Q1, so its 1992Q4 base is
   0). Coverage of the admitted set is a median 95.8% of the total market, and
   is REPORTED rather than assumed negligible. Gate G1 is unaffected and still
   passes on all 16, because a zero-holding sector still sums correctly.

D. REAL GDP WAS SOURCED AROUND A BLOCKED ENDPOINT, NOT SUBSTITUTED.
   Section 4/D5 named FRED GDPC1. Both FRED endpoints are unreachable from this
   environment (fredgraph.csv times out; no FRED API key is configured), so the
   SAME series was taken from its primary publisher: BEA NIPA Table 1.1.6 line
   1, series A191RX, quarterly (via the DBnomics mirror, dataset last revised
   2026-08-26). FRED's GDPC1 IS that BEA series republished. This is a sourcing
   route change, NOT a different quantity.

E. THE REALIZED SAMPLE IS SHORTER THAN SECTION 4/D6 PROJECTED.
   D6 expected ~115-118 overlay quarters. The realized overlay sample is 92
   quarters (2003Q2-2026Q1): the 40-quarter rolling standardization window plus
   the two-quarter availability lag cost more than anticipated. The minimum
   annualized net Sharpe needed for DSR>=0.95 is correspondingly higher than
   section 9's table: 0.773 / 0.811 / 0.984 / 1.052 at N = 24 / 37 / 362 / 1031.
   This does NOT change the verdict, because every realized overlay Sharpe is
   NEGATIVE and a negative Sharpe fails at any power level -- but the shortfall
   is recorded rather than glossed, and it does mean the run is even less able
   to detect a small positive edge than registered.
