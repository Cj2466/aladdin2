================================================================================
0. A CORRECTION TO THIS PROJECT'S OWN RECORDED RATIONALE (verified primary source)
================================================================================
The action list motivates this work with: "the IR-bound math discussed tonight
(Qian & Hua: IR is capped by IC/sigma_IC regardless of breadth unless new bets
are genuinely independent) means correlated additions buy almost nothing."

The paper was fetched and read directly for this run rather than restated from
memory:

  Edward Qian and Ronald Hua, "Active Risk and Information Ratio", Journal of
  Investment Management, Vol. 2, No. 3 (2004), pp. 1-15.
  (retrieved from https://www.panagora.com/assets/JOIM-Active-Risk-and-Information-Ratio.pdf)

What the paper actually says, quoting its own numbered equations:

  Eq. (1), p. 1  IR = IC_bar * sqrt(N)          -- Grinold's (1989) law, which
                                                   the paper restates in order
                                                   to depart from it
  Eq. (8), p. 5  alpha_bar = IC_bar * sqrt(N) * sigma_model * dis(R)
  Eq. (9), p. 5  sigma     = std(IC) * sqrt(N) * sigma_model * dis(R)
  Eq. (3), p. 3  IR = IC_bar / std(IC)

and in its own words on p. 6: "The ratio of Eq. (8) to (9) produces Eq. (3);
i.e. IR is the ratio of the average IC to the standard deviation of IC."

TWO THINGS THE PROJECT'S SUMMARY GOT SUBTLY WRONG, both worth fixing in the
record because they change what the result licenses:

 (a) It is not a CAP. sqrt(N) appears in the numerator (Eq. 8) and the
     denominator (Eq. 9) and CANCELS EXACTLY under the paper's assumptions.
     Qian & Hua's IR does not rise with breadth and then get capped -- it does
     not depend on breadth at all. Their contribution is that once IC
     volatility ("strategy risk", std(IC)) is counted as a source of active
     risk, breadth drops out of the IR entirely.

 (b) The clause "unless new bets are genuinely independent" is NOT Qian &
     Hua's. It belongs to Grinold's original fundamental law (Eq. 1 above),
     where breadth means the number of INDEPENDENT forecasts; correlated
     forecasts simply do not add to N there. The two results are different
     arguments and the project's note welded them together.

 (c) ALSO IMPORTANT, and the reason not to lean on this paper too hard:
     Qian & Hua's N is explicitly "the number of stocks within a select
     universe" (p. 1) -- the cross-sectional breadth INSIDE one strategy, not
     the number of strategies in a book. Applying Eq. (3) to "how many
     strategies should this project register" is an extrapolation beyond what
     the paper claims, and is flagged here as such rather than presented as
     the paper's finding.

WHERE THAT LEAVES THE UNDERLYING ARGUMENT: intact, and if anything stronger,
but sourced correctly. Under Grinold, correlated additions do not raise
breadth, so they do not raise IR. Under Qian & Hua, IR is set by the
consistency of the signal (IC_bar/std(IC)) and raising breadth does not raise
it regardless. Both roads lead to the same operational conclusion this project
was already reaching -- accumulating more DSR-qualifying-but-correlated specs
buys close to nothing -- and neither of them needs the measurement below to be
true. What the measurement adds is whether this project's specs ARE correlated,
which is a fact about our data and not about anybody's paper.

1. WHAT WAS INCLUDED, WHAT WAS NOT, AND WHY
================================================================================
INCLUDED -- the five live forward-validation registrations (POP-A). Sourced

    cbop_ls_h63                  quality_forward_registration.CBOP_PATTERN_ID
    noa_neutral_ls_h126_median   quality_forward_registration.NOA_NEUTRAL_PATTERN_ID

    lazy_jaccard_full_h126_ivol  lazy_prices_forward_registration.LAZY_PRICES_PATTERN_ID
    xc_btcbeta_l180_h180         bab_forward_registration.BAB_PATTERN_ID

CRYPTO WAS INCLUDED, deliberately, and here is the reasoning both ways. The
case against was its calendar: the crypto family runs on periods_per_year=365
(cross_sectional_crypto.CRYPTO_PERIODS_PER_YEAR) against 252 for every equity
family, and its formation history starts 2020-11-01 rather than 2015-01-07, so
it is both a different clock and a much shorter one. The case FOR, which won:
(i) it is one of the five things actually registered, and the question asked is
about the registered book, so dropping it would answer a different question;
(ii) correlation is scale-free and calendar-free -- it is computed on the dates
two series SHARE, and estimate_effective_n_from_returns already does exactly
that via pandas .corr(min_periods=60), so no annualization convention enters
the number at all; (iii) the equity/crypto pair is the single most likely place
in this book for a genuinely independent bet to exist, so measuring it is the
most informative thing crypto can contribute.
Its two real costs are stated with the result rather than buried: every
crypto-vs-equity pair drops weekend observations (equities have none, so the
pair keeps only weekdays), and every crypto pair rests on a shorter overlap
than the equity pairs do. Both are reported per-population as the minimum and
median pairwise overlap.

INCLUDED -- the declined-but-qualifying candidates, because a declined
candidate is still perfectly good evidence about return-generating-process
overlap. Correlation does not care whether a spec was registered; it cares
whether two streams move together. POP-B is defined mechanically as every spec
whose PERSISTED dsr clears 0.50 -- the floor this project has actually applied
in its registration decisions (asset_growth was declined because "every h252
spec in this family fails the 0.5 floor"; noa_neutral's paper-faithful cells
were "all below floor"). That rule, not a hand-picked list, is what selects
POP-B, so the population cannot be quietly shaped to produce a nicer answer.
It brings in asset_growth's winning spec (ag_low_ls_h126, DSR 0.670),
residual_momentum's paper-faithful and industry-neutral specs
(rm_ff3_residual_neutral_ls_h21 0.630 and rm_capm_residual_neutral_ls_h21
0.593), the un-neutralized quality_noa family, and short_interest's si_dtc
siblings.

EXCLUDED -- best_ideas_13f. Its own run report records 207.4 minutes of wall
clock for one build (best_ideas_13f_2026-09-02.txt, line 5). This is the same
reason run_preservation_score.py excluded it and the same reason its
preservation_score could not be computed in 20417f8. Its paper-faithful spec
(bi_conviction_count_h63, DSR 0.6275) WOULD have qualified for POP-B on the
rule above, so this is a cost exclusion, not a judgement, and it is the single
largest known gap in this measurement.

EXCLUDED -- the residual_momentum Daniel-Moskowitz overlay (974db04). Two
independent reasons: its DSRs are 0.387/0.283, below the 0.50 rule that defines
POP-B; and it produces a MONTHLY series, which cannot be correlated against
daily series without a resampling choice that would be this analysis's
invention rather than the family's own construct.

EXCLUDED -- eigenportfolio_statarb, dividend_month_premium,
insider_opportunistic, pead_ear, and the intraday families. All have bespoke
replay engines that never call run_cross_sectional_backtest, so the capture
hook cannot see them. This is a property of the hook, not a finding about those
families, and it is the same documented limitation run_preservation_score.py
measured. eigenportfolio_statarb is the notable loss here: as a statistical
arbitrage construct it is the family most likely to be genuinely orthogonal to
the fundamentals-based book, and its absence means POP-C understates signal-type
diversity by at least one type.

2. WHERE THE RETURN SERIES CAME FROM, AND WHAT IS AND IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE
================================================================================
THE "IS ANYTHING PERSISTED?" QUESTION, checked rather than assumed. Earlier
tonight's finding was that cross_sectional_trial_results persists only summary
statistics. That was re-verified directly here by scanning every row's
full_result_json, and it is ALMOST right -- with one real exception that had
not been noticed before:

  multi_signal_combination rows DO persist real return series. Their
  full_result_json carries `daily_returns`, `candidate_daily_returns` (six
  named specs, including cbop_ls_h63 and noa_neutral_ls_h126_median) and a
  precomputed `correlation_matrix`.

That exception is genuinely useful and is used here as an INDEPENDENT CROSS-
CHECK, not as an input: those series were computed on 2026-08-30, by different
code, on a different price vintage, over a TRUNCATED common window
(2020-10-05..2026-08-25, 1479 days, cut to the shortest candidate's history).
Its stored corr(cbop_ls_h63, noa_neutral_ls_h126_median) = +0.0712. If this
run's independently-rebuilt number for the same pair lands near that, the
reconstruction is corroborated by something it did not produce. The comparison
is not exact by construction (different window, different vintage) and is
reported as corroboration, not as a reproduction test.

Everything else had to be rebuilt. The rebuild reuses run_preservation_score.py's
proven hook rather than reimplementing any backtest: every family here routes
through cross_sectional.run_cross_sectional_backtest, which already computes a
`daily_returns` series that its caller then discards. This run wraps that one
function on the module object and records what it already produced. No
family's replay, cost model, universe or spec grid is altered.

THE PRICE-REPRODUCIBILITY BUG, AND WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT. Commit 24f0974
proved YFinanceProvider's uncached auto_adjust=True fetch is not point-in-time:
Yahoo retroactively re-adjusts its whole historical Close series, so the same
nominal window fetched on different days gives different prices. lazy_prices
was fixed with a frozen snapshot; cbop, noa_neutral, residual_momentum,
asset_growth and short_interest all still share the vulnerable path.

That bug bites a CORRELATION study harder than it bites a single Sharpe: if
family A's prices are fetched at one moment and family B's an hour later and
Yahoo re-adjusts in between, the two return series rest on mutually
inconsistent prices and their measured correlation is contaminated by the
inconsistency itself. Two mitigations were applied:

 (1) lazy_prices ran off its OWN committed frozen snapshot
     (DEFAULT_PRICE_SNAPSHOT_DIR + DEFAULT_FILING_INDEX_PATH) -- the
     byte-identical inputs 24f0974 verified reproduce all 36 specs at delta
     exactly 0.0. No live Yahoo or EDGAR call for this family.

 (2) Every other family's price fetch went through a memoizing wrapper
     installed on YFinanceProvider.get_price_history / get_daily_ohlcv inside
     the RUNNER SCRIPT (not in production code -- the same shape as the
     capture hook). The first call for a given (tickers, start, end) fetched
     live; every later call in this run and in any rerun replays the frozen
     answer from disk. So all families in this run share ONE price vintage,
     and re-running the analysis reproduces it exactly.

WHAT MITIGATION (2) DOES NOT FIX, said plainly rather than glossed: the frozen
vintage is whatever Yahoo served at the moment of this analysis. It is NOT the
vintage any family's originally-reported Sharpe was computed on. So every
rebuilt spec's Sharpe is compared against its persisted value and the
agreement is reported per family in the measurement report, spec by spec.

3. STATISTICAL POWER -- THE PART THAT LIMITS EVERY NUMBER BELOW
================================================================================
The nominal observation counts look reassuring: most equity specs carry ~2,926
daily returns, short_interest ~2,169, crypto fewer. Those counts badly
OVERSTATE the information available, and the overstatement is structural, not
incidental.

Every spec in this project holds a cohort for `holding_days` and rebalances
into OVERLAPPING cohorts every day. Consecutive daily returns of an h126 spec
are therefore near-duplicates of one another by construction -- the portfolio
on day t and day t+1 differ by roughly 1/126th of their positions. The count
that governs a correlation's standard error is the number of NON-OVERLAPPING
holding periods, n/h:

    h=21  over 2,926 days  ->  ~139 independent periods
    h=63  over 2,926 days  ->  ~46
    h=126 over 2,926 days  ->  ~23
    h=180 (crypto, 365d/yr) over its shorter history -> fewer still

For a PAIR, the slower leg governs: a pair of h126 specs rests on roughly 23
effectively independent joint observations, not 2,926.

The consequence, stated numerically rather than hand-waved: the Fisher-z
standard error of a correlation estimated from n independent observations is
about 1/sqrt(n-3). At n~23 that is ~0.22. So for the slowest pairs in the
registered book, ANY measured correlation with |rho| below roughly 0.45 is
statistically indistinguishable from zero. A measured +0.07 and a measured
-0.10 carry the same information: "we cannot tell."

This is reported per population in the measurement report as the minimum and
median effective independent periods and the implied standard error. It is a
COARSE, deliberately conservative heuristic (n/h is the standard
non-overlapping-blocks count; it assumes nothing about the autocorrelation's
shape), and it is labelled as such rather than presented as an exact result.

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN THE HEADLINE NUMBER: it means this analysis can
reliably detect LARGE overlap (two specs that are near-duplicates will show
|rho| well above 0.45 and will cluster together), and cannot reliably
distinguish "genuinely independent" from "mildly correlated". Every conclusion
below is written to respect that asymmetry -- strong overlap is a finding,
apparent independence is a failure to reject, and the two are not treated as
equally informative.

THE ONC NUMBER HERE IS NOT A RESULT AND MUST NOT BE QUOTED AS ONE. The module
says so itself, in its own output: "Only 5 clusterable trial(s) (need >=10) --
too few for the cluster count to mean anything." E[K] is bounded into [2, N-1]
= [2, 4] by construction and the mean silhouette of 0.097 is far below Kaufman
& Rousseeuw's 0.25 "no substantial structure found" line. E[K]=2 is what
k-means returns for five points with no structure; it is not a finding that the
book contains two bets.

The honest number for this population is the variance-based one: the five
registered specs behave like roughly 3.9 independent equal-weighted risk
sources, not 5 (mean off-diagonal rho +0.0733; 5/(1+4*0.0733) = 3.866, which

raise N_eff from 3.87 to 4.40 -- so it accounts for 0.53 of the 1.13 total

5. DOES THE CLUSTERING MAKE ECONOMIC SENSE? -- THE FOUR CHECKS THAT MATTER
================================================================================
(1) DO THE TWO residual_momentum VARIANTS CLUSTER TOGETHER, AS EXPECTED? YES,
    and about as tightly as anything in the data: rm_ff3_residual_neutral_ls_h21
    and rm_capm_residual_neutral_ls_h21 form their own cluster with a quality
    t-stat of 224 and a pairwise correlation of +0.766. This is the expected
    answer -- swapping the CAPM residual for the Fama-French-3 residual changes
    the regression used to strip out systematic return, not the bet -- and
    getting the expected answer on a case with a known truth is what licenses
    reading the cases where the answer was not known in advance.

(2) DOES lazy_prices -- THE ONE TEXT-BASED SIGNAL AMONG PRICE/FUNDAMENTAL ONES
    -- COME OUT AS ITS OWN CLUSTER? YES AS A TYPE, NO AS A SINGLE CLUSTER, and
    the distinction is the interesting part.

    It does not form one cluster. It splits in two, along its MEASURE axis:
      Jaccard-on-full-document specs   internal rho +0.933  (cluster 3)
      cosine-on-risk-factors specs     internal rho +0.867  (cluster 2)
      between those two groups         rho +0.363
    So "Jaccard similarity of the whole 10-K" and "cosine similarity of the
    risk-factor section" are, empirically, two different bets -- not two
    parameterizations of one. The family's pre-registered grid was right to
    treat measure and scope as real axes.

    But as a TYPE it is cleanly separate from everything else. Neither
    lazy_prices cluster merges with any price- or fundamentals-based family.
    The largest mean cross-family correlation either group reaches is +0.192
    (cosine-on-risk-factors against si_ratio); the next is +0.176 (the same
    group against cbop). For the REGISTERED Jaccard-full group specifically,
    its correlation against the accounting families is essentially zero:
    +0.031 with noa_low, +0.009 with noa_neutral, +0.003 with asset_growth,
    and its largest link to anything outside lazy_prices is +0.116 (cbop).

    This is the single most encouraging result in the whole run for the
    candidate-sourcing question. The one family built on a genuinely different
    INPUT -- filing text rather than prices or accounting line items -- is also
    the one that shares least with the rest of the book.

(3) DOES THE REGISTERED noa_neutral SPEC STAND APART FROM THE PLAIN NOA FAMILY?
    NO -- and this is the one cross-family overlap large enough to be
    statistically resolvable at this sample size. ONC puts
    noa_neutral_ls_h126_median and noa_neutral_ls_h63_median in the SAME
    cluster as all nine un-neutralized noa_low specs. The correlations:
        mean +0.534, max +0.733 (noa_neutral_ls_h63_median x noa_low_ls_h63)
        registered spec vs noa_low_ls_h126: +0.704
    +0.70 is well above the ~0.53 threshold below which this data cannot
    distinguish a correlation from zero, so unlike almost every other
    cross-family number here, this one is a real measurement rather than a
    failure to reject.

    Independent of 20417f8's mechanism argument, and arriving at the same
    place from a completely different direction: industry-neutralizing NOA did
    not create a distinct bet. It created a lower-DSR version of a bet the
    project already had.

(4) DID THE short_interest MECHANISM-FIDELITY SWAP ACTUALLY BUY A DIFFERENT
    BET? YES, and this is the clearest vindication in the run of the
    methodology in action-list item 2. si_dtc (DSR up to 0.948, rejected as a
    disguised volume effect) and si_ratio (DSR 0.796, registered because it
    matches the paper's measure) land in SEPARATE clusters, and their mean
    cross-correlation is NEGATIVE at -0.169. They are not the same bet with
    different noise; they point different ways. The decision to take the
    lower-DSR, mechanistically-faithful spec was not a cosmetic preference --
    it selected a materially different return stream.

    Worth recording alongside it: si_ratio's tightest cross-family link in the
    entire pool is with cbop (mean +0.442, max +0.493), and cbop x si_ratio is
    also the single largest correlation inside the live registered book
    (+0.391). Both are near but below the resolution threshold, so this is
    flagged as the one thing in the live book worth re-measuring as forward
    data accumulates -- not as an established overlap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 THE AutonomousPortfolioRunner GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The gate is MIN_STRATEGIES_FOR_AUTONOMOUS_PORTFOLIO = 5 graduated strategies
(autonomous_portfolio_runner.py). The state today is 5 REGISTERED, 0
GRADUATED, and one of the 5 recommended for removal pending sign-off. So the
gate is not close to binding and nothing here changes that.

What the measurement adds is that the gate's own stated rationale is about
having something to reject -- its comment reads "Real selection starts once
there is something to reject: ~37% of members are zero-weighted at n=5" and
"It gives the diversification metrics 10 pairwise correlations instead of 3."
That second clause is now measurable rather than assumed, and the answer is
unflattering: at n=5 those 10 pairwise correlations rest on a median of 17
non-overlapping holding periods each. Nine of the ten cannot be distinguished
from zero. A portfolio optimizer handed that correlation matrix is being fed
mostly noise, and will allocate on it as if it were signal.

CONCRETE IMPLICATION, offered for a human decision and not acted on here:
counting to 5 is the wrong readiness test. Five strategies whose pairwise
correlations are individually unmeasurable do not give an optimizer anything
to optimize. If the gate's purpose is to make optimization meaningful, the
condition that actually serves it is a measured effective-N, which this run
now makes computable -- and on today's book that is 3.87 of 5, or 3.08 of 4
if noa_neutral goes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 THE DEEPER QUESTION: CAN THIS PORTFOLIO'S IR SCALE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stated as plainly as the data supports, and no further.

WHAT IS SOLIDLY ESTABLISHED: adding another spec from a family this project
already has buys close to nothing. Within-family correlations run 0.70-0.93,
far above the ~0.53 level below which this sample cannot resolve a correlation
from zero, so this is a real measurement and not an artifact. 42 specs that
each independently cleared the project's own DSR floor collapse to ~9 clusters
and a variance-based ~6.2 effective bets. The 33 "extra" specs are, in
return-generating terms, re-tests of the same eight or nine ideas.

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED, and must not be claimed: that the families are
genuinely independent of one another. Cross-family correlations measure
0.00-0.20, which is BELOW the resolution threshold. That is a failure to
reject, not a demonstration. The asymmetry matters and it is not symmetric in
consequence either -- if the true cross-family correlation were 0.20 rather
than 0.00, a twelve-family book's effective N would be 12/(1+11*0.20) = 3.75
rather than the 7.23 measured. Roughly half. This data cannot rule that out.

SO THE HONEST SUMMARY IS: the ceiling on this portfolio's IR is set by how
many genuinely different RETURN-GENERATING PROCESSES it can find, and today it
has somewhere between about 6 and 9 of them depending on which estimator is
used, out of the 203 specs rebuilt here across 11 families and the 42 of those
that cleared the DSR floor. (203 is this run's coverage, not the project's
total spec count -- the excluded families in section 1 are not in it.) It does
not currently have a breadth problem that more variants can solve, and no
amount of further spec-grid expansion inside the existing families will change
the number.

ONE MORE STRUCTURAL LIMIT, found while assembling this and not previously
recorded anywhere: FOUR screening modules -- cross_sectional_quality (which
produces BOTH the quality_cbop and quality_noa family keys),
cross_sectional_quality_neutral, cross_sectional_asset_growth and
cross_sectional_residual_momentum, i.e. five of the family keys in this study
-- do not merely resemble each other, they screen the IDENTICAL universe. All
of them call build_quality_sample(), which draws a seeded random sample of
QUALITY_SAMPLE_SIZE=200 tickers with QUALITY_SAMPLE_SEED=20260828. Same 200
names, same EDGAR XBRL source, same cost model. Their low measured return
correlation therefore says their SIGNALS are close to orthogonal WITHIN one
shared opportunity set; it does not say they are reaching different
opportunities. Genuine diversification of opportunity would mean a different
universe, a different asset class, or a different data input -- which is
precisely what crypto (xc_btcbeta, most orthogonal member of the live book)
and lazy_prices (filing text, least correlated family in the pool) already
demonstrate, and what the accounting-signal families by construction cannot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3 CANDIDATE #8+ SOURCING -- THE DIRECT ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
YES -- prioritize genuinely different signal TYPES over more variants of what
already exists, and the measurement now supports that with a number instead of
an intuition.

The ranking the data suggests, strongest evidence first:

  1. A DIFFERENT INPUT beats a different formula. lazy_prices (filing TEXT) is
     the least-correlated family in the pool against everything else
     (max mean cross-correlation +0.18). Crypto (a different ASSET CLASS) is
     the most orthogonal member of the live book. Both were cheap wins in
     independence terms. A new accounting ratio computed from the same EDGAR
     XBRL data on the same 200 tickers is, on this evidence, the least
     promising thing the project could build next.

  2. A DIFFERENT UNIVERSE is nearly as good and is currently unexploited. Five
     equity families share one 200-ticker seeded sample. Nothing about that is
     a law of nature; it was a sampling decision.

  3. MORE SPECS INSIDE AN EXISTING FAMILY buy essentially zero and cost real
     multiple-testing budget. This is the firmest finding in the run. It also
     means the DSR denominators are, if anything, too harsh in one specific
     sense the module's own docstring already flags: 42 qualifying trials
     representing ~9 effective ones is exactly the situation E[K] was designed
     to describe. That said, this module ships UNWIRED and this run does not
     change that -- E[K] under-counts genuinely independent trials, and
     under-counting LOWERS the hurdle, which is anti-conservative. Nothing
     here should be used to relax a DSR denominator.

  4. eigenportfolio_statarb IS THE MOST INTERESTING GAP IN THIS MEASUREMENT.
     It could not be included (bespoke replay engine, invisible to the capture
     hook) and it is a statistical-arbitrage construct -- structurally the
     family most likely to be orthogonal to a book made of accounting and
     text signals. Its DSR was 0.0045, a clear honest negative, so this is not
     a suggestion to revive it. It is a note that the ONE structurally
     different thing tried so far could not be measured here, and that if a
     future stat-arb-shaped candidate is built, making it run through
     screen_cross_sectional_universe would let it be measured.

written or read for anything but reference. effective_n_clustering.py remains
UNWIRED -- no pipeline imports it, and deflated_sharpe.py is untouched; no
n_trials denominator anywhere was changed on the strength of these numbers,
which would be anti-conservative for the reasons the module's own docstring
gives. The pending noa_neutral removal remains pending and still needs the
user's explicit sign-off; section 4 adds one input to it (the ~0.79-effective-
bet diversification cost, and the existence of an unreviewed higher-DSR
sibling family) and takes no position on the decision itself.
