1. THE HYPOTHESIS, AND THE SOURCES IT COMES FROM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MECHANISM. A taxable individual (and, per Sikes, a taxable institution) holding
a stock with an accrued capital loss can accelerate the tax benefit of that
loss by realizing it before 31 December rather than after. That produces
non-informational selling pressure late in the calendar year, concentrated in
the stocks with the largest accrued losses, and a price recovery once the
pressure abates at the turn of the year. This is the fourth direct test in this
session of the "flow, not fundamentals" limits-of-arbitrage thesis
(Shleifer-Vishny 1997, Coval-Stafford 2007, Vayanos-Woolley 2013,
Gabaix-Koijen 2021) — after TSMOM (paused on paid-data gap P4), N-PORT
flow-induced trading (built, DEFINITE_NEGATIVE at DSR 0.341) and
options-dealer gamma (scoped, not buildable free, gap P6).

nothing below may lean on the parts that were not read.

3. CONSTRUCTION — EVERY CHOICE CITED TO [PW01], DEVIATIONS NAMED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 THE CALENDAR ANCHOR

[PW01] p.358, verbatim: "We define turn-of-the-year returns following Roll
(1983), Sims (1995), and others as the dividend-inclusive return on the last
trading day in December and the first five days in January."

Let d(-1) be the LAST trading day of December, d(-2) the second-to-last, and so
on. The harness (cross_sectional.py) forms at a date's CLOSE and realizes from
the following day. Capturing the return ON d(-1) therefore requires forming at
the close of d(-2).

  FORMATION DATE = d(-2), the second-to-last trading day of December.

This is independently corroborated by [PW01]'s own control variable, p.359
verbatim: "PRICE is the stock's closing price on the second to last trading day
in December." The paper measures its cross-section on exactly this date.

  HOLDING WINDOW = 6 trading days: d(-1), then the first five trading days of
  January. That is [PW01]'s turn-of-the-year window, exactly.

3.2 THE RANKING VARIABLE

[PW01] p.357-358, verbatim: "Our primary explanatory variable is LOSS, which
measures the tax-loss potential of a stock. It is defined as the percentage
difference between a stock's price at the beginning of a time period (say
July 1) and its price at the end of the period." Equations (1a) and (1b),
verbatim:

    LOSS_July-Dec  = (P_Dec24 / P_July1 - 1)                              (1a)
    LOSS_Jan-June  = (P_June30 / P_Jan1 - 1)                              (1b)

and, immediately after: "LOSS_July-Dec excludes returns on the last five
trading days of December, on the grounds that these returns may already reflect
some of the turn-of-the-year returns. We set LOSS equal to zero if the firm
experienced a capital gain."

  LOSS-WINDOW END for any window that runs into December = d(-6), the last
  December trading day that is NOT among "the last five trading days of
  December". The signal is therefore measured with a FOUR-TRADING-DAY GAP
  before the formation date d(-2). This is [PW01]'s own exclusion, and it is
  strictly conservative with respect to look-ahead.

  THREE PRE-DECLARED WINDOWS, all three from [PW01]'s own regression:
    full_year   last trading day of year Y-1  ->  d(-6) of December Y.
                [PW01] p.361: "Table III reports specifications that relate
                turn-of-the-year returns to losses and gains over the previous
                12 months."
    jul_dec     last trading day of June Y    ->  d(-6) of December Y.  Eq.(1a)
    jan_jun     last trading day of year Y-1  ->  last trading day of June Y.
                Eq.(1b)
  "Beginning of period" is taken as the last close BEFORE the period opens
  (1 January and 1 July are not trading days), so jan_jun and jul_dec exactly
  partition full_year with no gap and no double count.

3.3 THE PRICE BASIS — A DELIBERATE, CITED CHOICE

The signal is built from the SPLIT-ADJUSTED, DIVIDEND-UNADJUSTED close
(CrossSectionalData.price_only_close, supplied by
YFinanceProvider.get_total_and_price_return_closes), NOT from the
total-return close.

REASON: [PW01]'s LOSS is defined on PRICE ("the percentage difference between a
stock's price at the beginning of a time period and its price at the end"), and
a capital loss for US tax purposes IS a price-basis quantity — dividends are
taxed as income in the year received and are not part of the accrued capital
gain or loss. Folding dividends into the ranking variable would measure
something no taxpayer's Schedule D contains. Note that [PW01] uses a
DIVIDEND-INCLUSIVE measure for the turn-of-the-year RETURN (section 3.1 quote)
and a price measure for LOSS; this family does the same — realized returns come
off the harness's total-return `close`, exactly as every other family here.

REPORTED, NOT SEARCHED: the Spearman rank correlation between the price-basis
signal and the total-return-basis signal is computed at every anchor and
reported as a DIAGNOSTIC. It is not a second arm and adds no trials. If the two
bases rank identically the choice is immaterial and the report will say so.

3.4 THE TWO SIGNAL FORMS — THIS IS THE PRIMARY FIDELITY TEST

[PW01] Table III, all six columns (three regimes x with/without firm effects),
read off the extracted text:

    LOSS_Jan-June    -0.084  -0.102  -0.053  -0.057  -0.068  -0.069
                     (.012)  (.012)  (.007)  (.007)  (.013)  (.013)
    LOSS_July-Dec    -0.143  -0.167  -0.151  -0.178  -0.086  -0.118
                     (.015)  (.012)  (.010)  (.017)  (.015)  (.020)
    GAIN_Jan-June    -0.016  -0.006  -0.001  +0.003  +0.003  +0.008
                     (.005)  (.003)  (.003)  (.003)  (.006)  (.003)
    GAIN_July-Dec    -0.017  -0.013  -0.002  -0.011  -0.017  -0.015
                     (.004)  (.003)  (.005)  (.004)  (.004)  (.003)

and [PW01] p.360 verbatim: "Losses in the previous year predict higher
turn-of-the-year returns in each of the three regimes... The absolute effect of
GAIN is much smaller than the effect of LOSS, and we often fail to reject the
null hypothesis that the coefficients on the GAIN variables are zero."

The paper's estimated effect is therefore ASYMMETRIC AT ZERO: it lives entirely
on the loss side. Two signal forms are pre-declared to make that testable:

  lossonly   s_i = max(-R_i, 0)  — [PW01]'s truncated LOSS variable verbatim
             ("We set LOSS equal to zero if the firm experienced a capital
             gain"). Paired with portfolio="long_universe_hedged": LONG the
             top decile (largest accrued losses), SHORT the equal-weighted
             eligible universe. There is NO short-the-winners leg because
             [PW01] estimates no winner effect to short.
  signed     s_i = -R_i — the plain reversal ranking. Paired with
             portfolio="long_short": long the biggest losers, short the
             biggest winners. This is what a generic "losers bounce"
             or long-horizon-reversal story predicts and what the
             tax mechanism does NOT predict.

PRE-DECLARED READING, fixed here: if `signed` beats `lossonly` materially and
consistently, the family measured cross-sectional reversal wearing a calendar,
not tax-loss selling, and the report must say so regardless of the DSR.

3.5 WHAT IS NOT SEARCHED
  RANK FRACTION fixed at 0.10 (deciles) on every spec. [PW01] runs a panel
    regression and sorts nothing, so neither deciles nor quintiles is "the
    paper's"; deciles are this project's own standing convention and are
    declared, not searched.
  LEG WEIGHTING fixed at "magnitude", the harness default and the default of
    every equity family here. [PW01] gives no weighting to follow.
  DIRECTION: no reversed variants. A decile long-short portfolio is
    antisymmetric under direction reversal up to identical costs, so a
    reversed spec would double n_trials while adding zero information — the
    same reasoning cross_sectional_patterns.py and cross_sectional_
    seasonality.py both state.

3.6 THE HOLDING AXIS — 2 VALUES, BOTH FROM SOURCES
  h=6   [PW01]'s exact turn-of-the-year window (section 3.1).
  h=21  approximately the whole of January — the unit in which [RK76]'s
        month-of-year result is stated, and the window inside which [SK14]'s
        "early January" sits. A month-length hold is the coarser, more
        commonly traded version of the same claim.
  No third value. h=63 or longer would be holding a prediction about a window
  that has already closed.

3.7 THE ONE REAL HARNESS DEVIATION, DECLARED IN ADVANCE
cross_sectional.py forms every `holding_days` TRADING days. This family must
form on ONE specific calendar date per year. It therefore sets
cohort_formation_days=1, which makes the harness run `holding_days`
independent staggered sleeves so that EVERY trading day is a formation date in
exactly one sleeve, guaranteeing the December anchor is always hit. The signal
panel is NaN on every non-anchor date, so the other sleeves form nothing and
stay flat.

CONSEQUENCE, stated before the run rather than discovered in it: the harness
blends concurrently active sleeves by equal-weighted average, so the reported
daily return series is EXACTLY 1/holding_days times the economically real
once-a-year book's return series, on every day. Sharpe, Calmar, risk_quality,
stability and preservation_score are all invariant to that constant scaling;
ANNUALIZED RETURN, total_cost_drag and total_financing_drag reported by the
harness are NOT, and are 1/holding_days of the real figures. The report
multiplies them back by holding_days and labels both the raw and de-scaled
number. A unit test pins the exact-scaling property rather than assuming it.

4. THE SIZE CONFOUND — THE DECISION, MADE AGAINST THE PAPERS AND NOT AGAINST THE DSR
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the single most important thing to get right in this family, and the
sources are unambiguous that it cuts AGAINST this project's primary universe.

[PW01] p.362-363 stratifies by size and reports it in Table V. Verbatim: "Keim
(1983) and many others suggest that large turn-of-the-year returns are
concentrated among small-capitalization stocks... The effect of lagged losses
on turn-of-the-year returns is strongest for small- and medium-size firms,
which might be expected given their higher individual as opposed to
institutional ownership. For Regime II, July-December losses have a
statistically significant, larger effect on turn-of-the-year returns than
January-June losses for 8 of the 10 size deciles. The two estimated
coefficients are statistically indistinguishable for the largest and smallest
size classes."

Table V's LARGEST size decile, b3 - b4 by regime:
    Regime I   +0.050 (0.025)      Regime II  -0.006 (0.022)      Regime III  +0.023 (0.018)
None is significantly negative; the Regime II cell — the regime with the
STRONGEST tax incentive in the whole paper — is essentially zero. [SK14]'s
abstract independently frames the whole phenomenon as one in which "small
capitalization stocks earn unusually high returns in early January".

HONEST CONSEQUENCE: this project's primary universe is the S&P 500, i.e. the
top of [PW01]'s largest size decile and above it. That is precisely where both
papers report the mechanism's signature to be WEAKEST or absent.

THE DECISION — option (b), test within size buckets, NOT (a) full-universe-and-
disclose and NOT (c) size-neutralize:

  UNIVERSE 1  S&P 500, point-in-time (sp500_membership_history.was_member),
              formations from 2015-01-07 (MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START). LARGE CAP.
  UNIVERSE 2  S&P 600 small-cap, point-in-time (small_cap_membership_history.

              MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START). SMALL CAP.

The IDENTICAL 16-spec grid runs on both. This is the only in-project test of
[PW01] Table V / [SK14]'s size claim that free data supports, and it is the

WHY NOT SIZE-NEUTRALIZE WITHIN THE S&P 500: the S&P 500's internal size
dispersion is a slice of [PW01]'s top decile. Demeaning inside it would

axis supplying the result (residual_momentum: industry/factor neutralization
supplied 82.7% of the candidate's Sharpe, and it was DECLINED for it). Not
repeating that shape.

WHAT NEITHER UNIVERSE FIXES, stated plainly: neither reaches the micro-caps
where [RK76]-era January effects were largest, and the small-cap universe's own

family cannot and does not claim to test the effect where it is reported to be
strongest.

5. THE GRID AND THE DENOMINATOR — 16 SPECS, 2 UNIVERSES, n_local = 32
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECEMBER-ANCHORED CORE (12):
    window {full_year, jul_dec, jan_jun}  x  form {lossonly, signed}
                                          x  hold {6, 21}
JUNE-ANCHORED PLACEBO (4):
    window {placebo_jan_jun}              x  form {lossonly, signed}
                                          x  hold {6, 21}
    = 16 specs, asserted at import against TAX_LOSS_SPEC_CEILING = 16.

THE PLACEBO IS A MATCHED PAIR, NOT A LOOSE CONTROL, and this is the sharpest
falsification this build contains. `tls_jun_placebo_*` uses the SAME RANKING
VARIABLE as `tls_dec_jan_jun_*` — the accrued price change from the last close
of year Y-1 to the sixth-to-last trading day of a month — and differs ONLY in
which month it is traded at: late June (no tax deadline) instead of late
December (the tax deadline). Anything that makes the December cell work but not
the June cell is about the calendar boundary; anything that works in both is
generic mean reversion and is not this mechanism.

n_local = 32, and that is arithmetic, not conservatism. Running one
pre-declared 16-definition grid on TWO universes makes the set of results from
which a maximum could be reported 16 x 2 = 32. This is exactly the derivation
cross_sectional_small_mid_cap.py section 2 already established for this project
("Re-running an N-definition family on a second universe makes the set of
results that could have been reported N x 2"), applied here with UNIVERSE_
MULTIPLIER = 2. It is passed to screen_cross_sectional_universe explicitly via
n_trials_override for BOTH universes, so the number the DSR uses is the number
stated here.

CROWDING DISCLOSURE (informational, not gating, per CLAUDE.md, since no capital

its ANNUAL turnover cost is trivially small — but 100% of that turnover is
concentrated in the single most-watched, most-crowded calendar window in US
equities, and the price at which the book is established is the close of the
second-to-last trading day of December, one of the thinnest sessions of the
year. The backtest prices the bid-ask component of that at the universe's
ordinary median spread and models NO market impact and NO widening in that
specific window, under either cost model. That is an optimism this family
cannot measure with free data and does not claim to have measured.

7. THE MECHANISM-FIDELITY CHECKS, FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  LOSS/GAIN ASYMMETRY. [PW01] Table III: LOSS coefficients -0.053 to -0.178,
    every one significant; GAIN coefficients -0.017 to +0.008, mostly
    insignificant. PREDICTION: the `lossonly` (universe-hedged) cells carry
    the edge; the `signed` (long-short) cells do not add to it. Grid cells,
    so this costs trials — which is correct, because it is a hypothesis.

F2  CALENDAR PLACEBO. Same ranking variable, June anchor instead of December
    (section 5). PREDICTION: the placebo cells are flat. If they match or beat
    the December cells, this is reversal, not taxes.

F3  DOSE-RESPONSE MONOTONICITY. [PW01]'s equation (2) is LINEAR in LOSS, so
    the tax story predicts a MONOTONE relationship between loss magnitude and
    turn-of-the-year return, not merely a top-decile jump. At each December
    anchor the eligible universe is split into ten buckets by the `full_year`
    lossonly signal and each bucket's realized 6-day excess return over the
    equal-weighted universe is recorded; the report gives all ten bucket means
    and the Spearman correlation between bucket rank and mean excess return.
    A DIAGNOSTIC computed from the same replays — no new specs, no new trials.

F4  SIZE STRATIFICATION. Section 4. Both universes, same grid, doubled
    denominator. PREDICTION FROM THE SOURCES: small-cap > large-cap. A result
    that is strong in the S&P 500 and absent in the S&P 600 is BACKWARDS
    relative to [PW01] Table V and [SK14] and must be reported as such rather
    than banked.

F5  WITHIN-YEAR HALF SPLIT UNDER THE CURRENT TAX REGIME. [PW01] p.359-360
    derives a regime-specific prediction: under a six-month long-term holding
    period the first-half and second-half loss coefficients diverge (b3 < b4
    in Regime II), and "when the long-term holding period is 12 months in
    Regime III, we would expect b3 = b4 < 0. This is the regime with the
    weakest pressure for investors to realize early year losses before
    year-end."
    THE CURRENT US REGIME IS [PW01]'S REGIME III, verified live rather than
    recalled: IRS Topic no. 409 (irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409, page last
    reviewed/updated 25-Feb-2026) states verbatim "Generally, if you hold the
    asset for more than one year before you dispose of it, your capital gain
    or loss is long-term", and "The amount of the excess loss that you can
    claim to lower your income is the lesser of $3,000 ($1,500 if married
    filing separately) or your total net loss", with carryforward. That is a
    12-month holding period with 100% deductibility to a $3,000 limit — the
    same rules [PW01] Table I records for 1988-1996, its Regime III.
    PREDICTION: `jul_dec` and `jan_jun` perform SIMILARLY. A large divergence
    would be evidence AGAINST the tax reading under today's law.

F6  THE 1969/1976 NATURAL EXPERIMENT IS NOT REPLICABLE, AND NO MODERN
    SUBSTITUTE EXISTS IN THIS WINDOW. Stated here, before the run, so it
    cannot later look like a retrofit.
      * [PW01]'s identification rests on the 1969 Tax Reform Act (long-term
        loss deductibility 100% -> 50%) and the 1976 Tax Reform Act (long-term
        holding period 6 -> 9 -> 12 months), both decades before any daily
        price history this project can reach at scale. Not attempted.
      * The two candidate modern events named in this family's brief are BOTH
        outside the data window: JGTRRA 2003 and ATRA 2013 predate
        MEMBERSHIP_DATA_START (2015-01-07).
      * WITHIN 2015-2026 there is no change to the three parameters [PW01]'s
        identification actually uses (long-term holding period, long-term loss
        deduction fraction, loss limit). The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 did
        not alter them — its proposed mandatory-FIFO securities provision was
        dropped from the final bill — and the IRS statement verified above
        gives the same 12-month / $3,000 rules as in 1988. There is therefore
        NO in-window regime break to exploit, and none is manufactured.
    CONSEQUENCE, accepted: this family tests [PW01]'s within-regime PREDICTION
    (F1, F3, F5) and its size prediction (F4) against a calendar placebo (F2).
    It CANNOT test [PW01]'s across-regime identification, which is the part of
    the paper that establishes the effect is tax-driven rather than merely
    seasonal. A positive result here would therefore be consistent with the
    tax story but would not identify it, and the report must say so.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy D's two-tier rule (registration_scorecard.policy_d_verdict) against the
0.95 VALIDATED-EDGE bar, applied to the best spec by DSR at n_local across BOTH
universes:

  DEFINITE_NEGATIVE   best spec's DSR < 0.95 at n_local = 32. Nothing above it
                      can rescue it — DSR is strictly decreasing in N.
  UNRESOLVED          clears 0.95 at n_local but fails at 481 or 857. NOT a
                      pass.
  PASS                clears 0.95 at every N measured.

0.50 is the informal SCREENING FLOOR this project uses to decide whether a
result is even worth further work. It is not a pass.

The DSR that this rule reads is computed on returns already net of the cost
model and the general-collateral borrow charge in section 6.

preservation_score (app/services/research_lab/preservation_score.py) is
computed for EVERY spec in BOTH universes, with no exceptions, and reported
alongside. It is a secondary check, not a gate.

AN ADDITIONAL, DELIBERATELY MORE HONEST STATISTIC IS REPORTED ALONGSIDE THE
DSR, and it is the one a reader should weigh most: the per-anchor (annual)
realized return of each spec, its mean, its standard deviation across years,
and the naive t-statistic on that handful of annual observations. The Sharpe
machinery will report ~2,700 daily observations per spec; the EFFECTIVE
independent sample is ELEVEN turn-of-year events on the S&P 500 (December 2015
through December 2025) and SIX on the S&P 600 (December 2020 through December
2025). No amount of daily-return arithmetic creates independent annual
observations. This is declared here so that a large-looking DSR on a
zero-inflated daily series cannot be reported as if it rested on 2,700 draws.

NO FORWARD-VALIDATION REGISTRATION is created by this run under any outcome.
Registration is a separate, later, human decision by the project owner.

9. THE HONEST PRIOR, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY NUMBER EXISTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERY LOW. Six independent reasons, each stated so that a positive result would
have to survive all of them:

 (1) THE UNIVERSE IS WHERE THE SOURCES SAY THE EFFECT IS NOT. [PW01] Table V's
     largest size decile shows b3-b4 of -0.006 (s.e. 0.022) in the paper's
     strongest regime; [SK14]'s abstract calls it a small-capitalization
     phenomenon. Universe 1 is entirely inside that largest decile. This alone
     would justify expecting nothing there.
 (2) ELEVEN OBSERVATIONS. Universe 1 offers eleven turn-of-year events, Universe
     2 six. A once-a-year effect measured over eleven years has almost no power
     against a plausible effect size, and this is a property of the data window,
     not of the implementation.
 (3) AGE AND PUBLICITY. [RK76] is fifty years old, [PW01] twenty-five, [SK14]
     twelve. The January effect is the single most widely publicized calendar
     anomaly in equities. McLean & Pontiff (2016) measure 58% post-publication
     decay across 97 predictors; preservation_score already applies that
     0.42 retention factor to every candidate here.
 (4) THE MARGINAL HOLDER IS WRONG. S&P 500 shares are held overwhelmingly
     through index funds, ETFs, retirement accounts and other tax-exempt or
     tax-deferred vehicles, for which the December deadline creates no
     incentive at all. [PW01]'s whole identification depends on the marginal
     seller being a TAXABLE individual.
 (5) NO IDENTIFICATION AVAILABLE. Section 7's F6: the part of [PW01] that
     proves the effect is tax-driven cannot be run in this window.
 (6) THE TRADE IS CONCENTRATED IN THE MOST CROWDED WINDOW IN THE CALENDAR, and
     the backtest models no impact and no window-specific spread widening.

AN HONEST NEGATIVE IS THE EXPECTED OUTCOME AND IS A COMPLETE, USEFUL RESULT.
The failure mode this pre-registration exists to prevent is not a negative — it
is a positive manufactured by choosing the window, the price basis, the signal
form, the holding period, the universe or the cost model after seeing which one
worked.
