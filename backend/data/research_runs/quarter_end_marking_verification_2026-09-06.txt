1. THE ONE RESULT THAT MAKES THIS A CLEAN NEGATIVE RATHER THAN A COST STORY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The pre-registration (section 7) put a x0.0 turnover arm on the ladder for
exactly one purpose: to separate "the mechanism is ABSENT" from "the mechanism
is real but UNTRADEABLE". It answered.

    WITH NO TURNOVER CHARGE AT ALL:
      sp500  best DSR@36 = 0.1083  (qem_quarter_end_day0_perf_h126, Sharpe +0.5051)
      sp600  best DSR@36 = 0.1425  (qem_quarter_end_day0_perf_h126, Sharpe +0.4508)

A free-trading version of this strategy still fails the 0.95 bar by a factor of
seven. THIS FAMILY DOES NOT FAIL BECAUSE OF COSTS. It fails because the
daily-close winner-minus-loser signal is not there at the size the bar needs.
Costs then make it worse (baseline 0.0102, double-cost 0.0010), monotonically,
in both universes and on every spec — which is itself a consistency check the
test suite pins.

The x0.0 arm is a DIAGNOSTIC and cannot pass anything; it is reported here for
what it rules out, not as a result.

2. THE TWO BINDING OVERRIDES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i) THE PLACEBO OVERRIDE DID NOT FIRE, and that changes nothing.
    Every one of the twelve `month_placebo` cells has a NEGATIVE per-event
    mean, while the best real cell's is positive, so there is no same-signed
    placebo to compare against and the override is silent. Recorded plainly
    because a silent override is not evidence FOR anything: the family is a
    definite negative on its own DSR regardless.

    What the placebo arm actually shows is worth stating on its own. On the
    S&P 500 it is mildly negative (best -0.2151 Sharpe, per-event mean
    -0.079%); on the S&P 600 it is strongly negative (-1.3519 to -2.6401
    Sharpe, per-event means -0.59% to -1.55%, t as low as -7.32 on 54 events).
    An equal-weighted winner-minus-loser quintile spread traded across an
    ordinary month-end LOSES money, reliably, and loses more the smaller the
    stocks. That is short-term reversal plus the round-trip spread, and it is
    the correct behaviour for a control that is supposed to contain no
    mechanism.

(ii) THE PRE-WEIGHTED QUARTER-END ASYMMETRY held, in the direction the source
    predicted. [CKMR99]'s own QBEG p-values "generally exceed the usual
    rejection levels" (p.9), and here the quarter_end/day1 cells are the
    weakest real cells in both universes: sp500 -0.2293 and -0.2622 Sharpe,
    sp600 -0.6918 and -1.0096. No surprising quarter-end reversal result had
    to be reported.

Pre-registration section 11(2) predicted, from [CKMR99]'s OWN Section IV prose
arithmetic and BEFORE any number existed, that the `day0` arm would fail —

All four negative, in both universes, on both ranking windows. The prediction

4. F1 — THE QUINTILE DOSE-RESPONSE DOES NOT LOOK LIKE THE PAPER'S
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[CKMR99] pp.15-16 (full cross-section, 1993/4-1997/8): WINNER quintile OVAL
+160bp, LOSER quintile OVAL ~0, and "the in-between quintiles are in between,
in exact order". Largest-cap quintile winner OVAL +181bp.

S&P 500, year_end, ranked on the paper's own six-month window (12 events):
    OVAL by quintile (bp), 0 = LOSERS ... 4 = WINNERS
        -60.77   -5.92   +0.04   -0.68   -9.15      universe -15.18
    Spearman(quintile rank, OVAL) = +0.30

The paper's sign is INVERTED at the ends: here the LOSER quintile carries the
large OVAL and the winner quintile is near the universe mean. Decomposed, the
loser quintile earns +47.15bp on day 1 against the winner quintile's -22.89bp —
the turn-of-the-year loser bounce, still alive, and the entire reason the
`day1` arm (long losers, short winners) is the best real cell. The winner
quintile's own OVAL is -9.15bp against the paper's +160bp: the marking
signature the paper measured is not present at this size in this window.

S&P 600, year_end (6 events): the same shape, more strongly — loser-quintile
OVAL -105.13bp, winner -25.06bp, Spearman +0.70.

At QUARTER-ends the profile is nearly flat across quintiles in both universes
(sp500 +28.34 / +20.93 / +16.79 / +24.24 / +36.69 bp against a universe mean of
+25.35bp), i.e. whatever is happening is happening to the whole cross-section
and is not a winner-versus-loser effect at all. At MONTH-ends the profile is
monotone in the WRONG direction (sp500 perf_ptd Spearman -1.00; sp600
perf_h126 -1.00), which is short-term reversal.

Not one of the twelve (universe x period x ranking-window) profiles reproduces
the paper's monotone winner-highest ordering.

5. F2 / F3 — THE ONE PLACE THE SOURCE'S SIGNATURE DOES SURVIVE, AND WHY IT IS
   STILL NOT A TRADE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most interesting finding in the run and it is reported in full,
including the part that cuts against the verdict.

F3, the [CKMR99] Table II analogue (daily cross-sectional slope of each stock's
day-d return on its own day-(d-1) return, that slope series regressed on the
period-start dummies):

                        intercept        YBEG           QBEG           MBEG
    [CKMR99] ALL funds   +0.0355 (0.01)  -0.2418 (1.37) -0.1407 (1.18) -0.0239 (48.81)
    THIS BUILD, sp500    -0.0148 (0.03)  -0.3206 (0.00) -0.0492 (17.61) -0.0338 (13.18)
    THIS BUILD, sp600    +0.0011 (77.37) -0.2230 (0.02) -0.0325 (35.58) -0.0376 (8.21)
    (two-sided p-values in percent, in brackets)

The YBEG loading is strongly negative and highly significant in BOTH
universes, at a magnitude comparable to and slightly larger than the paper's,
while QBEG and MBEG are insignificant in both. That is the paper's own
ordering — year-begin real, month-begin absent — reproduced 30 years later at
the stock level rather than the fund level. The negative intercept was
pre-declared in advance (F3, pre-registration section 8) as the expected
difference between stocks and funds, and it came out negative as declared.

F2b, the Table I analogue (equal-weighted point-in-time universe return minus
SPY, on the six dummies, sp500, coefficients in bp):
    const -0.18 (80.62)  YEND +3.93 (71.51)  YBEG +1.52 (88.79)
    QEND  +0.84 (89.47)  QBEG -11.76 (6.31)  MEND -3.34 (39.24)  MBEG -2.34 (54.81)
    [CKMR99] Table I ALL: YEND +48.03 (0.02), YBEG -32.93 (0.72), QEND +17.36
    (1.82), QBEG -11.21 (12.69), MEND -1.00 (82.53), MBEG +5.68 (21.04).
The QBEG coefficient (-11.76bp) is remarkably close to the paper's (-11.21bp),
and MEND is insignificant in both. But the two coefficients that carry the
paper's headline — YEND (+48bp, p 0.02% then; +3.93bp, p 71.5% now) and YBEG
(-32.93bp then; +1.52bp now) — have gone. The period-END markup leg, which is
the leg the marking-up model is actually about, is not detectable.

F2a (SPY's own return on the same dummies) shows nothing significant anywhere,
so none of the above is a market-wide calendar artefact the long/short book was
sitting inside.

WHY THIS DOES NOT CHANGE THE VERDICT, stated rather than argued around:
  * The tradeable version of F3's YBEG finding IS the `day1` arm, and it earns
    DSR 0.0102 net and 0.0635 gross. A significant regression coefficient on a
    zero-cost, zero-implementation statistic is not an edge; the same effect,
    expressed as a portfolio anyone could hold, is nowhere near the bar.
  * F3's YBEG dummy is 11 or 12 observations out of 2,933. One extreme January
    session can carry it. The pre-registration's section 9 point about
    effective sample size applies to this diagnostic as much as to the specs.
  * The direction is consistent with the turn-of-the-year loser bounce (F1)
    just as much as with marking-up, and this build has no way to separate the
    two. That was declared in advance (section 11(2)) and is not resolved here.

7. WHAT THIS RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT SAY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IT SAYS: a stock-level, daily-close, winner-minus-loser quintile spread traded
across US period-end boundaries in 2015-2026 (S&P 500) and 2020-2026 (S&P 600)
is not an edge — not net of costs, and not gross of them either.

IT DOES NOT SAY that [CKMR99] was wrong, or that marking the close has stopped
happening. The pre-registration's section 2 named three things this build
cannot test and they remain untested:
  * THE PAPER'S PRIMARY RESULT IS FUND-LEVEL and no fund NAV data exists here.
    "80% of funds beat the S&P on the last day of the year... compared with
    only 30% the next day" (p.9) was never within reach of this build.
  * THE EFFECT THE PAPER MEASURES IS INTRADAY — "46 of those bp came in the
    last hour of the year and 44 of them were gone 30 minutes into day 1"
    (p.15). A close-to-close test cannot resolve a last-hour move, and F1's
    flat winner-quintile OVAL is consistent both with the effect having gone
    and with it having moved inside a window this data cannot see.
  * SECTION V'S HOLDINGS TEST needs the disclosed portfolios of the top 10% of
    funds by year-to-date return. Not available.
And the published article — "Leaning for the Tape", Journal of Finance 57(2),
April 2002, 661-693 — was NOT read (Wiley paywall; UPenn repository 403). Its
sample, methodology and numbers may differ from the 1999 working paper this
build was written against, and disagreement with it is possible and not ruled
out.

The pre-registration's section 11(1) reason for a very low prior — that the
effect may BE the location of a print inside the bid-ask spread, which a
trader must cross twice to reach — is neither confirmed nor refuted here. The
x0.0 cost arm shows the signal is too small even before the spread is paid,
which is a different and stronger failure than the spread argument predicted.

NO FORWARD-VALIDATION REGISTRATION was created and no live registration's
operational status was touched.

8. TWO DEFECTS FOUND DURING THE BUILD, BOTH BEFORE ANY RESULT EXISTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recorded because this project's rule is that a check caught late is a warning,
not a footnote.

D1  THE PER-EVENT STATISTIC WAS MISSING THE EXIT CHARGE. cross_sectional.py
    charges a formation's turnover on that formation's FIRST REALIZATION DAY,
    so the liquidation charge lands the session AFTER the book goes flat. The
    first implementation compounded only the sessions the book was exposed on,
    which made every per-event mean net of the ENTRY cost alone and flattered
    every arm — including the month-end placebo whose comparison decides the
    binding override. Fixed (arm_pnl_window: [d0,d1] / [d1,d2] / [d0,d1,d2]),
    pinned by two tests, and the first production run was killed and restarted
    rather than shipping the flattering number.

D2  THE PLACEBO OVERRIDE SELECTED ITS PLACEBO SIDE BY SHARPE. That has a real
    failure mode for a one-sided rule: a placebo cell with a large per-event
    effect but an unremarkable Sharpe would never have been looked at. Now the
    real side is the highest-Sharpe non-placebo cell and the placebo side is
    the LARGEST same-signed per-event mean anywhere in the placebo arm,
    whatever its Sharpe. Four tests cover it. The second production run was
    killed and restarted for this.

Neither defect could have been detected from the numbers, because neither run
had produced any.
