The draft's Table 5 Panel A reports a 54.37% success rate for eta(r1) against a
50.42% Always-Long benchmark, and Table 1 Panel A a slope of +0.069 (t=4.08).
This section says whether that relationship is present at all in 2016-2026,
SEPARATELY from whether a strategy built on it clears a multiple-testing gate.
Those are different questions and only this one is answered by the paper's own
test. It is reported as a diagnostic precisely so it cannot be mistaken for an
extra spec: it adds nothing to N_local and changes no verdict.

TWO THINGS THIS SECTION SHOWS THAT THE DSR LADDER ALONE DOES NOT:
  (a) The paper's HEADLINE relationship is not merely undetected out of sample,
      its point estimate has the WRONG SIGN. beta_r1 = -0.0511 against the
      paper's +0.069, and the gross directional hit rate is 48.68%, BELOW the
      50% coin flip, against the paper's 54.37%. A test can fail to detect a
      real effect; it does not usually estimate it backwards.
      (Note R2 is sign-blind, so r1's 1.711% R2 -- close to the paper's 1.6% --
      is NOT corroboration: it is the fit of a line sloping the other way.)
  (b) The only predictor with a same-sign, marginally-significant slope is r12
      (+0.0956, t=1.79), and the PLACEBO r2 sits right next to it (+0.0703,
      t=1.46). The two are not distinguishable, which is an attribution problem
      for r12 even though the pre-registered placebo override -- which compares
      STRATEGY Sharpes, not regression slopes -- did not fire.
