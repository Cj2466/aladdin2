------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V.1  THE DETERMINISM FLICKER: CAUSE ISOLATED, AND "IDENTICAL" OVERSTATED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The module docstring said the cause "was not conclusively isolated" and left two
candidates open: (a) float non-determinism in np.linalg.lstsq and (b) yfinance
re-serving different adjusted closes. IT IS (b), and the test is direct rather
than inferential: fetching the SAME 168-ticker, 3963-day price panel twice in
succession returns 357,543 differing cells out of 612,016 (58%), maximum
relative difference 1.3e-6. That is six to seven orders of magnitude larger than
double-precision lstsq reordering noise and exactly the size needed to move a
score-panel maximum in its fifth significant figure.

SEPARATELY, the claim that "'byte-identical' is true of every number this family
reports and not true of one range diagnostic" IS TOO STRONG. Across two full
independent re-runs the per-spec Sharpes, DSRs, PSRs, skewness and kurtosis all
move — by up to ~2e-6 absolute on a Sharpe. What is true, and is all that was
ever needed: every reported number is identical at the 3-4 decimals it is
printed to, the ranking of all 18 specs is identical, and every count and the
verdict are identical. This family is reproducible to reported precision but is
NOT bit-reproducible, and cannot be made so while the price half of its inputs
is an unpinned live API and the factor half is pinned in git.

V.2  THE RAW-VS-EXCESS CONTROL CLAIM IS FALSE AS WRITTEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The correction of the control arm from raw to excess returns IS correctly
applied — the code matches the frozen pre-registration, the counterexample test
is sound, and the claimed effect is confirmed: reconstructing the pre-correction
raw control (by zeroing RF) and re-screening the six control specs moves every
Sharpe by at most 0.0002, reproducing the reported +0.163 -> +0.162 and
+0.135 -> +0.134 at printed precision, with no ordering change anywhere.

BUT the justification attached to it in the code was wrong. It claimed raw and
excess "ranked identically in all 152 real monthly cross-sections of the
production run". Measured directly: THE FULL ORDERING DIFFERS IN 81 OF THE 152.
What is true is the part that actually explains the tiny Sharpe move — the
TRADED decile membership differs in 1 of 152 top deciles and 0 of 152 bottom
deciles. Monthly RF (~1e-3) reshuffles near-ties in the middle of the
cross-section and essentially never reaches the tails that are traded. The
module docstring has been corrected.

V.5  ONE POINT-IN-TIME IMPERFECTION WAS NOT DISCLOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The point-in-time discussion covers the publication LAG thoroughly and is
correct. It says nothing about the publication REVISION. Ken French rebuilds the
entire factor history whenever CRSP is refreshed; the committed vintage was
built from the 202606 CRSP database, so a 2015 formation in this backtest ranks
on the 2026-revised values of 2012-2015 factor months rather than the values
published at the time. The 45-day lag makes the release date honest and does
nothing about the revision.

This is small — FF3 revisions are typically in the third decimal of a monthly
percent, well inside the ~1e-6 price-vendor noise documented in V.1 — and it is
not fixable without a vintage archive nobody keeps. It is recorded because it is
a genuine deviation from strict point-in-time and the section that would have
mentioned it did not. Every other input (prices, membership, SIC) is genuinely
as-of-formation; look-ahead through the harness was re-tested and is structurally
impossible.

V.6  MUTATION TESTING: 39 MUTATIONS, 3 CONSEQUENTIAL SURVIVORS, NOW PINNED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

39 mutations were applied one at a time to the residual-momentum module and the
Fama-French provider, from a verified-green baseline, each run against the
family's two test files. The four the builder reported as already fixed (the
publication-lag constant, the two arm-column collapses, the dropped risk-free
rate) were re-tested and all four are genuinely killed. So were, among others:
the scoring window taken from the wrong end, standardising by the 36-month
rather than the 11-month residual std, adding alpha back, publishing a score a
month early, understating an observation's data end, forward-filling stale
factors, accepting partial estimation windows, mean instead of median industry
centering, dropping MIN_BUCKET_SIZE, centering on the whole universe, the
percent/decimal conversion, the sentinel floor, and the monthly-vs-annual row
regex.

THREE MUTATIONS SURVIVED, and all three are now killed by two new pinning tests
(each written first, then proven to fail under the mutation and pass without it):

  S1 — THE QUALIFYING-MONTH GATE could be narrowed to the `rf` column alone and
  nothing failed. The existing test truncates the WHOLE factor frame, so rf
  disappears alongside smb and hml and an rf-only gate still passes it — the
  test was insensitive to the property it was named for. The realistic vintage
  failure is PARTIAL coverage, and under it the control and CAPM arms would be
  scored for a month where FF3 silently returns NaN: exactly the benchmark
  timing advantage this family is built to prevent. Pinned by
  test_a_month_missing_only_ONE_factor_is_refused_for_every_arm.

  S2/S3 — THE INDUSTRY-BUCKET ROW LOOKUP WAS COMPLETELY UNPINNED, and this is a
  LOOK-AHEAD. Replacing `bucket_frame.loc[formation_ts]` with
  `bucket_frame.iloc[-1]`, or `frame.index[-1]` with `frame.index[0]`, both
  survived the whole suite, because every bucket fixture in the test file was
  constant over time. The `.iloc[-1]` variant is not hypothetical: the bucket
  frame is the FULL panel, captured in a closure and never sliced by the harness
  (only the signal frame is truncated), so that mutation would neutralize every
  formation against the industry each name ENDS UP in. Pinned by
  test_industry_buckets_are_read_at_the_formation_date_and_nowhere_else, which
  uses a time-varying bucket frame extending past the formation date.

  THE PRODUCTION CODE WAS CORRECT IN ALL THREE CASES. No result changes; what
  changed is that these properties can no longer break silently.

A fourth mutation (n_trials_override 18 -> 6) also survived and was traced to
being a genuine NO-OP: repool_deflated_sharpe overwrites n_trials, sigma_SR, SR0
and the DSR for every result afterwards, and its default is already pinned at 18
by test_repooling_uses_the_dispersion_of_every_spec_not_just_one_arm. Recorded
rather than pinned twice.

V.8  THE LITERATURE GROUNDING WAS RE-CHECKED AGAINST PRIMARY RECORDS AND HOLDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The citation was re-verified from scratch against BOTH the Crossref REST API
and the RePEc/EconPapers record: Blitz, Huij & Martens, "Residual momentum",
Journal of Empirical Finance 18(3), 2011, pp. 506-521,
doi:10.1016/j.jempfin.2011.01.003. Every field matches, and the abstract quoted
above and in the module docstring is VERBATIM correct, word for word.

THE FACTOR-MODEL CORRECTION IS REAL, and it was the right call. The build brief
said BHM use a "Carhart-style" (four-factor, momentum-inclusive) regression.
Independent sources confirm they do not: the residuals come from 36-month
rolling regressions of EXCESS stock returns on the FAMA-FRENCH THREE FACTORS
(MKT, SMB, HML), with no UMD term. Including UMD would have regressed the
momentum factor out of a momentum signal, so this was load-bearing rather than
pedantic, exactly as the pre-registration argued.

EVERY OTHER CONSTRUCTION DETAIL THIS FAMILY ADOPTED IS ALSO CONFIRMED against a
source independent of the two the build used: a 36-month rolling estimation
window; ranking on the past 12 months of residual returns EXCLUDING THE MOST
RECENT MONTH (i.e. 11 months); the sum standardized by the standard deviation of
the residuals OVER THAT SAME 11-MONTH PERIOD (not over the 36-month estimation
window — a mutation making that substitution was tried and is killed); and a
zero-investment top-minus-bottom DECILE portfolio, EQUALLY WEIGHTED and
REBALANCED MONTHLY. All four match the code.

ONE CHARACTERISATION TO CORRECT. "Scoring window (Eq. 9) 11 months, most recent
month skipped" describes the right net effect but not the mechanism. Within the
36-month estimation window this family's scoring window is the LAST 11 months,
so the estimation window's most recent month is INCLUDED; the skip is produced
instead by the 45-day publication lag, which pushes the usable score month back
to t-2 or t-3. When it lands on t-2 the scoring window is EXACTLY BHM's
t-12..t-2; when it lands on t-3 it is one month staler. Relatedly, the text
above describes BHM's own skip as "~2" months in one place and "~1-2" in
another; BHM skip ONE month (they form on t-12..t-2), so this family's measured
60-day median data age is about twice their skip, not marginally more. The
direction of the disclosure was right — this family is MORE conservative than
the source paper, never less — but the size of the gap was understated.
