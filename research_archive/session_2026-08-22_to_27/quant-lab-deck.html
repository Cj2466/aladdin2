01 — The premise

Not their returns. Their process.

Matching Renaissance Technologies' specific returns is structurally unreachable — decades of proprietary data, a hundred-plus PhDs, and execution infrastructure no individual account can access. What's reachable, and what this whole project is built on, is their process: rigorous theory, honest experimentation, and keeping only what survives real testing.

The one non-negotiable rule. A rigorous validation process — not a bigger search space or more compute — is what separates a real edge from an accident. This isn't a slogan; it was proven numerically (slide 8) and then proven live, in production, on real money-shaped data (slide 9).
  • Explicitly out of scope: the "basket options" tax-shelter structure from the original research material — historically contested, inapplicable to a retail tool.
  • Explicitly out of scope: microstructure/order-book modeling — needs tick data and colocated execution this project has no path to.
  • Explicitly out of scope: live automated trading. Everything here produces signals and research, not an execution system.

03 — Algorithm 1

The walk-forward engine

A strategy-agnostic loop that decides one trading day at a time, using only data available before that day — the single piece of machinery every future signal (pairs, regime detection, whatever comes next) will run through.

The rule, stated exactly

For day t, the fit window is raw_data[t − W : t] — day t itself is structurally excluded. Whatever the strategy decides for day t is computed before day t's own price move is knowable.

This isn't a claim taken on faith — a dedicated test shocks a single day's price by 50% in an otherwise-identical price series and asserts the position, z-score, and hedge ratio decided for that day and every day before it are bit-identical between the shocked and unshocked runs. The shock only shows up in the day's realized return, never in the decision that preceded it.

03 — Algorithm 1, continued

No look-ahead, drawn out

Refit every single day — benchmarked at ~0.93s for a 20-year daily backtest. Computationally trivial, so there's no tradeoff to make: refit as often as rigor calls for.
04 — Algorithm 2

OU / AR(1) pairs mean-reversion

The first real strategy run through the engine. Given two tickers, model their price relationship as mean-reverting, and trade when it looks stretched. Three steps: fit a hedge ratio, fit the mean-reversion dynamics, derive a trading signal.

Step 1

Hedge ratio via OLS on log prices

Step 2

AR(1) fit on the spread — the exact discretization of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

Step 3

Derive θ, μ, σ, and a z-score for how stretched today looks

04 — Algorithm 2, step 1

The hedge ratio

Regress ticker B's log price on ticker A's log price over the fit window. The slope is the hedge ratio — how many dollars of A offset one dollar of B.

log(Bt) = α + β·log(At) + εt // scipy.stats.linregress hedge_ratio = β spreadt = log(Bt) − β·log(At) // the series we'll model as mean-reverting

Log prices, not raw levels — the standard statistical-arbitrage convention, more stationarity-friendly than fitting price ratios directly.

04 — Algorithm 2, step 2

The mean-reversion fit

An AR(1) regression of the spread on its own previous value is exactly the discrete-time form of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process — no approximation, this is the closed-form discretization.

spreadt = a + b·spreadt−1 + ηt // fit via linregress again θ = 1 − b // mean-reversion speed, per trading day μ = a / (1 − b) // long-run equilibrium level σ²eq = var(η) / (1 − b²) // stationary variance of the process zt = (spreadt − μ) / σeq // how many σ from equilibrium, right now
The validity gate: only valid when 0 < b < 1. Outside that range there's no real mean-reversion to model — the pair is marked not_mean_reverting honestly, rather than reporting a fabricated half-life or z-score. (Half-life = ln(2)/θ, when valid.)
05 — Algorithm 3

The trading rule

A deterministic state machine on the z-score. Three positions — long the spread, short the spread, flat — and a transition never jumps directly from long to short. It always passes through flat first.

06 — Algorithms 4 & 5

P&L, costs, and scoring

raw_returnt = positiont · (retB,t − hedge_ratio·retA,t) / (1 + |hedge_ratio|) costt = (cost_bps / 10000) · |positiont − positiont−1| // default 10bps per unit change net_returnt = raw_returnt − costt equityt = equityt−1 · (1 + net_returnt)

Normalized by gross capital deployed 1 + |hedge_ratio| — not 1 + hedge_ratio, which would silently break if two tickers are inversely related.

Sharpe (net & gross)

mean(returns) / std(returns) · √252 — reported both ways so cost erosion is visible, not hidden.

Max drawdown, hit rate, exposure

Standard definitions — hit rate counts only closed trades; an open position at backtest end is shown, not scored.

07 — The honesty problem

A “strong” fit isn't proof

Before this shipped, the exact validity gate above (0 < b < 1) was stress-tested against pure noise. The result is why every methodology note in this app says what it says.

Independent random walks
~97–100%
of trials pass the naive validity gate anyway, with a spuriously "strong" fit-quality score (r² > 0.95) — indistinguishable from a genuine signal by that score alone.
Why

The well-known finite-sample bias of OLS-estimated AR(1) coefficients near a unit root — exactly why real unit-root tests use special critical values instead of a plain threshold.

This is precisely why a formal cointegration test (Augmented Dickey-Fuller) wasn't hand-rolled — doing that correctly needs real critical-value tables, and doing it incorrectly would be worse than not claiming it at all. Instead: the walk-forward, out-of-sample Sharpe is the only number that counts as evidence. A window's fit quality never is, on its own.

09 — Next

Closing the gap

AAPL/GLD is exactly the case a backtest can't resolve on its own — no amount of historical re-testing proves a relationship is real rather than lucky. Only time can.

Phase 0.5 — Forward Validation Gate

Register a backtested configuration, and the system advances it one real trading day at a time from now on — using the exact same no-look-ahead engine, on data that didn't exist when the strategy was built. 126 trading days (~6 months) minimum before a signal is allowed to graduate from "backtested" to "forward-validated." A floor, not a guarantee — and said so, everywhere it's shown.
