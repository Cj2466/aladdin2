STRUCTURAL FINDING (from the complete, fully-pooled Phase A 212-pattern family -- the only dataset with finished per-pattern results; Phase B's 420-pattern pool has not finished running -- 300/318 ticker-tasks done as of the last log line, no final pooled file written yet -- so it cannot be mined structurally today).

Trade frequency, not the underlying economic hypothesis (reversion vs. continuation, which academic family, which session-phase), is what predicts where a pattern lands in the negative-Sharpe distribution. Across all 212 patterns, log(n_trades) correlates -0.64 with pooled annualized Sharpe. Every one of the 212 is measured on the IDENTICAL 499-trading-day pooled equity series (n_observations=499 for literally all 212, confirmed from the raw results), so this is not the familiar "fewer trades -> noisier Sharpe estimate" artifact -- it is the same fixed calendar window, with fewer of its days carrying a paid round-trip cost.

The relationship holds within 8 of the 9 families whose own design sweeps a firing-selectivity threshold: opening-range breakout corr=-0.996 across its 8 thresholds; Bollinger -0.856 across 24; VWAP -0.846 across 30; candlestick -0.651; volume-price-divergence -0.662. The lone non-conforming family, day-of-week seasonality, has no threshold to sweep (it fires on every bar of a given weekday) and its trade count barely moves (5,280-5,720) -- an expected exception, not a violation.

Mechanism, grounded in this run's own numbers: INTRADAY_COST_BPS=5.0 is charged twice per fire-then-flatten cycle (~10bps round trip), and pooled hit rate across all 212 patterns averages 43.9% (median 44.0%; only 5/212 clear 50%) -- a losing-trade-frequency signature, not a skill signature. A pattern gated to a stricter threshold (volume >=4x average vs >=2x) pays that fixed cost drag on fewer of the fixed 499 days, diluting the average daily cost bleed toward zero without individual trades getting any better. This is the most parsimonious explanation for why the 8 positive-Sharpe patterns cluster where they do: they are not more skilled, they are less exposed.

I tested a second candidate regularity first, and it does NOT survive scrutiny -- reported as a negative control. Three families test a "reversal" and "continuation" reading of the SAME signal at IDENTICAL thresholds/phases (trade count matched, isolating direction alone): Gao intraday momentum (reversal avg Sharpe -8.23 vs continuation -7.68 across 8 matched pairs), overnight close-to-open persistence (reversion -5.44 vs continuation -5.17 across 12 matched pairs), and volume climax vs confirmation (-0.98 vs -1.82 across 15 matched pairs, trade counts identical at 1,312.5 average both directions). Only the third shows an asymmetry favoring the "fade the extreme" reading; the other two show the opposite sign or no meaningful difference. A cross-family average that initially looked like "reversal-story families beat continuation-story families" (-3.23 vs -5.53 avg Sharpe) does not replicate under matched, direction-only within-family comparisons -- it was confounded by family identity. Not reported as a finding.

Pre-specifiable hypothesis for a future round: if trade frequency is the dominant driver of where a cost-dominated family's Sharpe sits, holding trade count fixed while varying signal strictness should erase the -0.64 correlation. Concretely: for each threshold sweep, subsample the loosest-threshold variant's fired trades down to the same count as its strictest sibling (e.g. draw ~263 trades at random from the ~2,956 the 2x volume-climax set fires, matching the 4x set's count) and recompute Sharpe on that matched-count subsample, repeated across many random draws for a distribution. If the Sharpe gap collapses once trade count is equalized, that confirms pure cost-dilution with no remaining edge anywhere in this family. If a residual, direction-consistent gap survives trade-count-matching, that residual -- not any single pattern's raw rank -- is the first legitimate candidate for a real signal worth its own fresh, pre-registered trial.
