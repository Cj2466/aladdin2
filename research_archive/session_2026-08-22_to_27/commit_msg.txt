Extend forward validation to cross-sectional strategies, and register one

Forward validation existed only for pairs/momentum: a single ticker or
pair, re-deciding a scalar position every day. Every cross-sectional family
built this week (Bonds, FX, Commodities, Crypto, Buyback, Small/mid-cap,
index-removal, vol-regime-timing) was backward-backtest only, so none of
them could ever start accumulating the one kind of evidence that is
structurally immune to look-ahead and data-snooping: a track record made of
data that did not exist at registration time.

WHAT WAS BUILT

* cross_sectional_forward.py — "tick one real day" for a strategy that
  reforms every holding_days rather than trading daily. Between formations
  it holds the book and realizes its real P&L; on a formation date it
  reforms from that day's live signal computed on real data up to and
  including that day. Realize-then-reform, matching the batch schedule
  where a hold's last day IS the next formation date.

* THE ANTI-DRIFT REFACTOR. _replay_sleeve's loop body was extracted into
  cross_sectional.form_portfolio (eligibility, history view, signal,
  ranking, leg weighting with its fallbacks, net weights, turnover, cost)
  and cross_sectional.realize_formation_day. The forward ticker calls those
  same functions, so it cannot grow a second copy of the harness's
  arithmetic that quietly diverges from the backtest it is validating —
  engine.step_one_day's own rationale, applied to the cross-sectional
  harness. Pure extraction: A/B replay of 139 real production specs across
  7 families produces bit-identical daily returns, formations and cost
  totals against the pre-refactor code.

* REGISTRATION IS A REFERENCE, NOT A COPY. A cross-sectional spec's
  identity includes a signal closure, a data-driven universe rule and a
  whole market config; none of that is expressible as columns, and
  serializing it would be a second declaration of the strategy that can
  drift. So a registration stores family_key + pattern_id and resolves the
  family's OWN spec object at every tick, plus a fingerprint of what those
  resolved to. If the family is edited later, the tick parks the row as
  "spec_drift" rather than accumulating a track record that would be a
  blend of two strategies.

* A PARALLEL TABLE, not a discriminator on forward_validation_registrations.
  No column means the same thing in both, the carry states are incompatible
  shapes, and — decisively — the live pairs runner selects on status alone,
  so sharing a table would make its correctness newly depend on a WHERE
  clause it never had. A separate table makes that impossible by
  construction.

* A separate runner mirroring ForwardValidationRunner's tick shape, wired
  into main.py's lifespan alongside it, building each family's live panel
  once per tick and sharing it across that family's registrations.

* Graduation threshold is max(MIN_FORWARD_VALIDATION_TRADING_DAYS,
  2 x holding_days), not simply 126. At a 180-row hold, 126 days would
  graduate a registration mid-first-hold on a record of one unfinished bet.

THE ONE REGISTRATION MADE, AND WHY IT IS NOT AUTOMATIC

xc_btcbeta_l180_h180 — Frazzini-Pedersen betting-against-BTC-beta from the
28-definition Crypto family — registered today, deliberately and disclosed
(see bab_forward_registration.py, whose reasoning is also persisted onto
the row itself). It survived the adversarial confound check that rejected
this project's two better-looking results of 2026-08-26 (BTC beta ~0.06,
alpha t up to 2.80 jointly against BTC and the eligible-crypto basket, held
under a regime split), and it still LOST on multiple-comparisons grounds as
1 of 28 pre-declared trials. That verdict stands.

The improper next move would be a narrower "BTC-beta only" re-run reporting
a DSR against 6 trials — post-hoc trial-count shrinkage, the exact mistake
this project already caught and rejected once (cross_sectional_patterns_
round_d.py; screen_cross_sectional_universe RAISES on a shrunk denominator
precisely so it cannot be expressed in code). The backward data is fully
used. Forward validation on data that did not exist today is the only
statistically legitimate remaining test, and exactly one spec is registered
so the forward test does not re-import the selection problem it exists to
escape. Graduation will mean only "enough real out-of-sample data to be
worth looking at", never that the signal works.

THE PAIRS/MOMENTUM PATH IS PROVABLY UNCHANGED

forward_validation_runner.py, engine.py, ou_pairs.py, momentum.py,
strategy_registry.py, models/forward_validation.py, routers/
forward_validation.py, schemas/forward_validation.py and both existing
forward-validation test files are byte-identical to main. The only shared
edit is one keyword-only parameter on check_underperformance, defaulted to
TRADING_DAYS_PER_YEAR exactly as metrics.sharpe_ratio's own identical
parameter is, so a 365-day-a-year crypto family is not judged against an
exchange year it does not trade on. A new regression suite pins all of
this: the constants, that the default call is still the 252-day year, that
the live runner's query and snapshot shape cannot see a cross-sectional row,
that the pairs table gained no discriminator column, that tick-by-tick pairs
replay still matches a batch run_walk_forward exactly, and that with both
kinds of registration in one database the pairs runner loads and advances
only its own.

Full suite: 1352 -> 1393 passing (+41), zero failures, zero regressions.
